## A. Neutrality: the site's own voice

### 1 · Four Scripture rows assert what the passages mean, one of them in the first person — **must-fix**

**Where:** §3 table, rows Ef 4:30; Rm 6:3-4; 1 P 3:20; Jn 19:34.
**Problem:** «El Espíritu **nos** ha marcado con un sello "para el día de la redención": el carácter bautismal, que no se borra y no se repite (Catecismo 1272–1274)» — first person plural outside quotation marks: on the site, the site confesses the indelible character. Rm 6: «la inmersión significa esa sepultura y esa resurrección, **y el bautismo la realiza** (Catecismo 1214, 1227)» — "realiza" is the contested claim (the Reformed say "sign and seal", the Baptist "symbolizing"), stated bare with the citation at the end. 1 P 3:20: «El arca de Noé … **prefigura** la salvación por el bautismo»; Jn 19:34: «La sangre y el agua del costado de Cristo **son figuras** del bautismo y de la Eucaristía» — typological readings asserted as fact, then cited. Same pattern as Justification critic 1 and Eucharist critic 1. Rows that attribute in-sentence («Para la Iglesia, Jesús habla del agua…», «para Trento, este texto…», «el Catecismo lo lee como…») are fine.
**Fix:** Ef 4:30: «Para el Catecismo, el sello "para el día de la redención" es el carácter bautismal, que no se borra y no se repite (1272–1274).» Rm 6: «…la inmersión significa esa sepultura y esa resurrección, y el bautismo, enseña el Catecismo, la realiza (1214, 1227).» 1 P 3:20: «El Catecismo lee el arca de Noé … como prefiguración de la salvación por el bautismo (1219).» Jn 19:34: «Para el Catecismo, la sangre y el agua … son figuras del bautismo y de la Eucaristía (1225, citando a san Ambrosio).»

## B. Caricature and the "what counts as an objection" rule

### 7 · The rule is applied by grammar, and the grammar test is not applied evenly: WCF XXVII.4 counts, BF&M VII does not — **must-fix** (decides D's Question 2)

**Where:** §4 method paragraph; OB-3 ("Quién la plantea: reformados"); OB-7 "Status"; "Not carried over", Baptist line and the suggested site line.
**Problem:** D's rule: a text that "rejects or denies" a Catholic proposition counts; one that "only states its own position" does not. Under it OB-7 (Baptist) is "not counted" because BF&M VII "defines baptism as the immersion of a believer" without rejecting anything in words `[verified in D]`. But OB-3 is counted on WCF XXVII.4, «neither of which may be dispensed by any but by a minister of the word, lawfully ordained» `[verified in D]`: a positive statement of the Reformed position in exclusive form ("by any but"), naming no one, exactly the grammar of BF&M VII's «Christian baptism **is** the immersion of a believer». Philaret Q.290 («What is most essential … Trine immersion») is likewise used under OB-6 "for context". So the rule as applied is: an exclusive Reformed sentence is an objection; an exclusive Baptist sentence is "its own position". The visible result is a Catholic baptism page on which the Reformed raise four objections, the Lutherans three, and the Baptists, whose disagreement on baptism every reader knows, none, with a site line saying they describe baptism «sin formular objeciones a la doctrina católica». A Baptist reader will call that concealment; a secular reader will call it a category error. The expert reaches the same recommendation (b) from the Catholic side (his view on Q2); the asymmetry is the critic's reason.
**Fix:** Count OB-7, with the rule reworded as the expert proposes («rejects, denies or defines in exclusive terms»), and apply it by each text's grammar: BF&M VII and NH XIV on subject and mode; AG 6a on mode only («commanded … by immersion»), since «All who repent and believe … are to be baptized» is a sufficient condition `[verified in D]`. OB-3 stays counted under the same reading. If Pedro keeps (a), the site line must not say «sin formular objeciones»: «…describen el bautismo cristiano como "la inmersión de un creyente en agua" (Fe y Mensaje Bautistas 2000, art. VII); no mencionan el bautismo de niños ni la infusión.» Nothing more: what the texts say, not what they omit to object. Either way the Cierre question (finding 22) follows from this one.

### 8 · OB-3 and OB-4 end on canons aimed past the objector — **must-fix**

**Where:** §4 OB-3, closing canon (VII bapt. can. 4); OB-4 answer, second paragraph (VII bapt. can. 10).
**Problem:** (a) OB-3's objection is Westminster's «by any but a minister of the word, lawfully ordained». The closing canon condemns saying that baptism «aun el que confieren los hereges … con intencion de hacer lo que hace la Iglesia, no es verdadero Bautismo» `[verified in D]`. No Westminster text quoted says that; the objection is about who may lawfully baptize, not whether heretics' baptism is true. Ending here tells the reader that the Reformed deny the validity of baptisms outside their church, which the page nowhere shows them saying, and it uses the word «hereges» on a Reformed objection. No canon of Trent defines lay baptism in necessity (that is Florence and Lateran IV, which the answer quotes); can. 10 (sacr.) condemns the opposite extreme (all Christians may administer all sacraments), and can. 11 (intention) is the one point of actual difference the answer itself identifies. Same breach as Justification critic 10 ("the canon that answers asserts a target"), which this dossier fixed in wording («El canon de Trento dice:») but not in the choice of canon. (b) OB-4: «Por eso Trento condena también la idea de que los pecados cometidos después del bautismo se perdonen "con solo el recuerdo, y fe del Bautismo recibido" (can. 10)». LC IV.79 says repentance «is nothing else than a return and approach to Baptism, that we repeat and practise what we began before» `[verified in D]`, and D's own "Quién la plantea" notes the Lutherans keep absolution as a sacrament (Ap XIII.4). Whether can. 10's «sola recordatione et fide» reaches LC IV is the same open question D states honestly for OB-1 (can. 5 vs WCF XXVIII.5) and does not state here; placed in the answer, "también" makes the Formula's author hold the condemned proposition.
**Fix:** (a) End OB-3 on sacr. can. 11 (intention), which the answer already quotes, with a line: «Ningún canon de Trento define quién puede bautizar en caso de necesidad; la Iglesia lo enseña en Florencia y Letrán IV (citados arriba).» If the uniform-ending rule requires a baptism canon, Pedro decides; can. 4 is not it. (b) Drop the can. 10 sentence from the OB-4 answer or add the honest note (dossier and page): «El Catecismo Mayor conserva la absolución como sacramento (Apología XIII.4); si el canon 10 alcanza su "volver al bautismo" no lo dicen los textos citados.»

### 9 · OB-6 ends on «madre y maestra de todas las Iglesias» — **for Pedro** (D's Question 8)

**Where:** §4 OB-6 closing canon (VII bapt. can. 3); D's tone note.
**Problem:** D says can. 3 "answers this objection's charge exactly" and that choosing another would be tone management. It does not answer it: the 1895 charge, re-read, is that «sprinkling or affusion, being privily brought in, came to be accepted by the Papal Church, which still holds fast the innovation» (1896 ed., OCR l. 1222–1224) `[verified: OCR]`, a claim about the mode and its history; can. 3 says the Roman Church «madre y maestra de todas las Iglesias» has true doctrine on baptism. Its content is the Roman primacy, the one doctrine the Orthodox reader came to this page already rejecting; on the mode no canon of Trent speaks (the expert's Q8 view). Ending here is not the Church's answer to the Orthodox on immersion; it is the site picking the one canon whose words restate the papacy. That is escalation by selection, the mirror of tone management, and it is the Eucharist critic's 14 ("mixed is the one option that reads as partisan") seen from the other side.
**Why for Pedro:** uniform rule vs. no canon where none applies. Recommendation: end on can. 2 (true and natural water) with «Ningún canon de Trento define el modo; la ley de la Iglesia admite inmersión o infusión (can. 854)»; keep can. 3 in Qué enseña's "ver más" where D already lists it. If the expert's split (6a mode / 6b validity) is taken, 6b ends on can. 4, which does answer the Oros.

### 10 · OB-7's honest note on «rebautizar» stays in the dossier; the page shows the canon — **must-fix (wording)**

## Verdict

The Catholic doctrine is reported in the Church's words, the grades show their basis (two lines excepted, finding 20), the Fathers stay inside the Catechism, the honest notes are honest, and the objections are built from official texts. The neutrality breaches are in the connective prose and are one-line fixes: a first-person «nos» in a Scripture row, three typological readings and one slogan heading asserted in the site's voice, «con respeto» praising the Church's tone, the Fathers' name for Penance and the second-century dating stated as the site's own. The fairness problem is structural and has three parts. First, the "what counts as an objection" rule is applied by grammar and unevenly: an exclusive Westminster sentence makes an objection, an exclusive Baptist sentence does not, so the one tradition every reader expects to disagree on baptism raises nothing and is said to raise nothing. Second, two answers close on canons aimed past their objector (heretics' baptism against Westminster's ordained minister; «sola recordatione» against a Catechism that keeps absolution), and a third closes the Orthodox mode objection on the canon that restates Roman primacy. Third, the objectors are quoted where they deny and not where they define: the Reformed "sign and seal" is in the texts read and absent from the page, the Baptists speak in machine Spanish while their own Spanish exists, and the Orthodox diversity would be stated from a website citing an unread edition, with one Orthodox text reaching the page on a translation that has no edition. Count OB-7, re-aim the three endings, add WCF XXVIII.1 and the SBC's Spanish, keep the 1848 off the page until it has an edition, and put the two honest notes on screen 04; then a Baptist, a Presbyterian, a Lutheran and an Orthodox reader would each recognize their position on the page, and a secular reader would find no label wider than the canon behind it.
