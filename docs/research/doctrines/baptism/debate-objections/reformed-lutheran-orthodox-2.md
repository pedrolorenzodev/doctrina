## Objections

### E-1 · "Catholics think baptism saves automatically" (ex opere operato; not every baptized person is regenerate)

1. **Objection (neutral):** The Catholic Church teaches that baptism gives grace by the rite performed (*ex opere operato*); the objectors hold that the grace of the sacrament is received only through faith and the work of the Spirit, so baptism does not of itself regenerate or forgive everyone who receives it.
2. **Popular wording:** EN "Catholics believe baptism has power in and of itself to save — that everyone baptized with water is saved in that moment." (Redeemed Zoomer, below.) ES: no Spanish debate of this group found; proposed translation, not heard: «Los católicos creen que el bautismo salva automáticamente, por el agua misma, haya fe o no.»
3. **Debates and apologists:**
   - R. C. Sproul, "Roman Catholic View of Justification (Part 1)", Ligonier, 2020-10-21, 124,466 views, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O0zU6EXtb0I&t=321s — teaching lecture. "protestants have understood this to mean that baptism works as it were automatically" (05:21–05:54). He also reports Rome's reply (05:54): "we don't like to use the word automatic". `[verified: auto captions]`
   - Same author, two short Ligonier Q&As (counted once with the above): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1xR56XFWoO0&t=70s (2019-11-08, 34,986 views): "we can't assume … because somebody [has] been baptized that therefore they are regenerat[e]"; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cX-5xOZ-XX4 (2020-10-05, 22,871 views): "I don't think that it automatically carries forgiveness of sins with it". `[verified: auto captions]`
   - Redeemed Zoomer, "Does Baptism SAVE?", 2023-08-02, 71,309 views, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HtDjeLJnNU4&t=164s — teaching clip from the Reformed side, quotes WLC 161: "Catholics believe baptism has power in and of itself to save" (02:44); "they think everyone who is baptized with water is saved in that moment" (03:17). `[verified: auto captions]`
   - Reused from the first pass (confirmed in `verification.md`): John H. Gerstner (PCA), Ligonier, 2010 (excerpt of *Primitive Theology*); Rev. Paul Doellinger (LCMS), lutheranreformation.org, 2017; Joe Lau (CLC), *Lutheran Spokesman*, 2022. Hodge (1872) and Deterding (1947 thesis) count as support only (historic).
   - **Counting:** 5 independent people (Sproul, Redeemed Zoomer, Gerstner, Doellinger, Lau); of them 2 on video. No recorded Reformed–Catholic or Lutheran–Catholic *debate* on this point was found; in the Cooper–Akin debate (below) the Lutheran side does not raise it.
4. **Catholic answer:** official text — CCC 1128 (read on https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P33.HTM, 2026-10-20): the sacraments act *ex opere operato*, "by virtue of the saving work of Christ"; "Nevertheless, the fruits of the sacraments also depend on the disposition of the one who receives them." Apologetic: Catholic Encyclopedia "Sacraments" (1912, catholic.com; confirmed in `verification.md`, partly). No Catholic video reply to these clips was read (the two Catholic Answers Live clips were blocked). The answer therefore rests on an official text.
5. **Official anchor (objector):** Augsburg Confession XIII (English, bookofconcord.org): "They therefore condemn those who teach that the Sacraments justify by the outward act, and who do not teach that, in the use of the Sacraments, faith which believes that sins are forgiven, is required"; WCF XXVII.3 (opc.org): "The grace which is exhibited in or by the sacraments rightly used, is not conferred by any power in them"; WCF XXVIII.5: nor "that all that are baptized are undoubtedly regenerated". (All confirmed in `verification.md`.) The objection restates these.
6. **Added criteria:** line — Sproul (PCA, Westminster) yes; Gerstner (PCA) yes; Doellinger (LCMS) and Lau (CLC) are Book of Concord bodies, yes; Redeemed Zoomer presents himself as Reformed and cites the Westminster Larger Catechism, but his church is not named in the clip `[memory: not checked]`. Current teaching — yes, *ex opere operato* is CCC 1128; but the popular wording ("automatically", "everyone baptized is saved in that moment") leaves out CCC 1128's second sentence, and Sproul himself says Rome rejects "automatic". The page should state the objection in its strong form (grace by the rite vs grace through faith), not the caricature.
7. **Overlap:** same as OB-2, with OB-1's second half.
8. **Verdict:** PASSES (5 independent, answered by an official text, anchored, both criteria met with the wording caveat). Weak point: only 2 of the 5 are on video and none is a debate.

### E-2 · "Penance is not a second plank: baptism is never shipwrecked"

1. **Objection (neutral):** The Catholic Church calls penance "the second plank" after the shipwreck of baptismal grace; the objectors hold that the baptismal promise is never lost, so repentance and absolution are a return to baptism, not a second sacrament of salvation.
2. **Popular wording:** EN "Lutherans have no need of a second plank of salvation: repentance is a daily return to your baptism." (Cooper.) ES: none heard.
3. **Debates and apologists:**
   - Jordan B. Cooper (LCMS) in debate with Jimmy Akin, "Lutheran Debates Catholic on Justification", Matt Fradd, 2021-05-14, 168,897 views, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jkgsD6zNX2o&t=3759s — after Akin calls confession "the second plank after baptism" (59:55), Cooper: "where the difference does play itself out is going to be in terms of that question of the second … plank of salvation" (62:39); "repentance he would say is a daily return to baptism" (63:14). `[verified: auto captions]`
   - Same author (counted once): "Is there a Difference Between the Lutheran and Roman Catholic Views of Baptism?", 2023-07-20, 8,731 views, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=omlo2z_Z-n8 — "within the Lutheran tradition there is no need for a second plank of Salvation" (00:00). `[verified: auto captions]`
   - Bryan Wolfmueller (LCMS pastor) with a Catholic and an evangelical host, 2024-04-29, 20,567 views, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fo5MfiYS8J4&t=360s — "the Lutheran[s] love to kind of poke at" Aquinas's line that "Penance is the plank that we cling to when the ship of baptism is wrecked" (06:00). `[verified: auto captions]`
   - Not counted: Sproul (O0zU6EXtb0I, 14:39) describes the "second plank" but objects to works of satisfaction, not to the plank image. First pass: 0 sources (DO-RL-7).
   - **Counting:** 2 independent people (Cooper, Wolfmueller).
4. **Catholic answer:** Akin, in the same debate (59:55), presents penance as "the second plank" "as the fathers refer to it"; he does not reply to Cooper's objection. Official text: CCC 1446 (read on https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4C.HTM): "the Fathers of the Church present this sacrament as 'the second plank [of salvation] after the shipwreck which is the loss of grace.'" No apologetic rebuttal found (`[not found]`; one web search).
5. **Official anchor (objector):** Large Catechism IV.81–82 (bookofconcord.org, read 2026-10-20): "St. Jerome wrote that repentance is the second plank by which we must swim forth and cross over after the ship is broken … Thereby the use of Baptism has been abolished so that it can profit us no longer."
6. **Added criteria:** line — both LCMS, yes. Current teaching — yes (CCC 1446 uses the image).
7. **Overlap:** same as OB-4 (first pass DO-RL-7).
8. **Verdict:** FAILS (frequency: 2; answer only stated, not argued). The best Lutheran-specific candidate; one more independent Lutheran voice would carry it.

### E-3 · "What remains after baptism is truly sin" (concupiscence)

1. **Objection (neutral):** Catholic teaching says baptism removes all that is truly sin and that concupiscence left after baptism is not sin in the proper sense; the objectors hold that concupiscence is itself sin.
2. **Popular wording:** EN "The Lutheran articulation is this: concupiscence is sin." (Wolfmueller.)
3. **Debates:** Wolfmueller, fo5MfiYS8J4, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fo5MfiYS8J4&t=1803s (30:03) `[verified: auto captions]`. A Higher Things clip and a "Canon & Creed" clip on the same topic were found but blocked (429). First pass: 0.
4. **Catholic answer:** none read on video.
5. **Anchor:** Apology II (first pass, not re-read).
6. **Verdict:** FAILS (frequency: 1).

### E-4 · "There is no baptism outside the Orthodox Church: Catholic converts must be baptized"

1. **Objection (neutral):** The mysteries exist only within the one visible Church, so a baptism given in the Catholic Church does not incorporate into Christ or give the Spirit's grace; Catholics who become Orthodox are to be received by baptism (or, by economy, have an empty form filled at chrismation); the Catholic teaching since Vatican II of one common baptism among Christians is rejected.
2. **Popular wording:** EN "If we affirm that there is baptism outside the Orthodox Church, we are affirming that people outside the Church are really in the Church." (Trenham.) Also Heers: "We don't have this theology where there's a partial incomplete church … That's all Second Vatican Council nonsense." ES: no Spanish debate found; proposed translation, not heard: «Fuera de la Iglesia ortodoxa no hay bautismo: el católico que se convierte tiene que bautizarse.»

### E-5 · "Catholics were never baptized: baptism means triple immersion"

1. **Objection (neutral):** Baptism is three immersions in the name of the Trinity; the Latin norm of pouring abandons the apostolic form, so it is not baptism.
2. **Popular wording:** EN "You were never baptized. You know why? Because baptism means immersion." (Heers.)

### E-6 · "Rome lets atheists and Muslims baptize"

1. **Objection (neutral):** The Catholic teaching that in necessity anyone, even a non-baptized person, can baptize separates the sacrament from the faith of the Church; it is a medieval development of Augustine's view.
2. **Popular wording:** EN "It's the Roman Catholic position that thinks that atheists and Muslims can baptize you." (Dyer, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=apHcKNuxR0U&t=2s, 00:02–00:34) `[verified: auto captions]`
3. **Target check:** current — CCC 1256 (read on https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P3L.HTM): "In case of necessity, any person, even someone not baptized, can baptize, if he has the required intention."
4. **Verdict:** FAILS (frequency: 1; first pass OD-6 had 0).
