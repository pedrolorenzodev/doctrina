## How sources were counted

- Only authors of the group's traditions count toward frequency: Baptists of any line (convention, Particular/Reformed, fundamentalist) and Assemblies of God writers or AG-owned media. The anchor must still come from BF&M 2000 / New Hampshire or the AG Statement of Fundamental Truths (DECISIONS 2026-10-15). A Reformed Baptist author can raise an objection; the 1689 cannot anchor it.
- Writers of other traditions who raise the same objection (Presbyterian, Dallas-type evangelical, Church of Christ) are listed as "outside the group" and not counted.
- Each source is marked by whether it **names the Catholic Church** or targets "baptismal regeneration" in general (Church of England, Churches of Christ). A general source is counted, since the Catholic teaching is one of its targets, but the flag is kept for the verifier and for Pedro.
- Baptist sources are listed by family, because DECISIONS 2026-10-15 chose the convention line for the column; most debate material comes from Reformed or fundamentalist Baptists and from the 18th–19th-century English Particular Baptists.

## Objections

### BA-1 · Baptism does not regenerate or forgive sins; it symbolizes a salvation already received by faith

1. **Objection:** Regeneration and forgiveness are received by grace through faith; baptism is an act of obedience that pictures a salvation already received, and does not itself cause the new birth or wash away sin.
2. **Where it is raised** (Baptist / AG authors):
   - C. H. Spurgeon (Particular Baptist), *Baptismal Regeneration*, sermon 573, 5 June 1864, Metropolitan Tabernacle Pulpit vol. 10, https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/baptismal-regeneration, section "facts" (about half-way). Target: the Church of England's Prayer Book, which he calls Roman: "Facts, brethren, are against this Popish doctrine" `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes.
   - John Gill (Particular Baptist), *Infant-Baptism, a Part and Pillar of Popery*, London 1766 (2nd ed., Wellcome Collection record), text at https://www.reformedreader.org/rbb/gill/infantbaptism.htm: rejects the claim that baptism and the rites around it "confer grace ex opere operato, by the work done" `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes.
   - Allen Tennison (AG), "What We Believe About Water Baptism", *Influence* magazine (© Assemblies of God), 28 Feb 2024 (Winter 2024 issue), https://influencemagazine.com/en/Practice/What-We-Believe-About-Water-Baptism: "Rather than a necessary step for salvation, water baptism is reserved for those who have already turned to God by faith." Also: baptism is "a representation, but not an impartation, of grace" `[verified]`. Names Rome: no (contrasts "ordinance" with "sacrament").
   - Brandon Rhea (Reformed Baptist), "The Nicene Creed: Baptism for the Remission of Sins" (part 4), Covenant Baptist Theological Seminary, 12 Jan 2026, https://content.cbtseminary.org/?p=39854: "The Catholic Church teaches baptismal regeneration, but Reformed Baptists reject that doctrine." Quotes CCC 1213 and 1263 `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes.
   - Wally Morris (independent Baptist pastor), "Acts 2:38 and Baptismal Regeneration", *Proclaim & Defend* (Foundations Baptist Fellowship International), 27 Nov 2017, https://proclaimanddefend.org/acts-2/: "Either baptism regenerates or baptism does not regenerate"; names the Catholic Church and most Anglican churches as holding it `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes.
   - `Frequency: 5 independent sources` (Spurgeon, Gill, Tennison, Rhea, Morris; 4 Baptist of three lines, 1 AG).
   - Outside the group (not counted): E. Calvin Beisner (PCA), *Christian Research Journal* 28:2 (2004), https://www.equip.org/articles/does-acts-238-teach-baptismal-remission/ `[verified]`; Mike Gendron, Proclaiming the Gospel (affiliation not stated on his page) `[snippet]`.
3. **The other side answers it:** Catholic Answers, tract "Infant Baptism" (unsigned; nihil obstat 10 Aug 2004; page dated 2023-03-21), https://www.catholic.com/tract/infant-baptism: answers the view that baptism is "merely a public manifestation" with 1 Peter 3:21 `[verified]`. Karlo Broussard, "In Baptism, Our Sins Are Forgiven", *Catholic Answers Magazine*, 21 May 2020, https://www.catholic.com/magazine/online-edition/in-baptism-our-sins-are-forgiven (answers Ron Rhodes's reading; uses Acts 22:16) `[verified]`.
4. **Official anchor:** BF&M VII: "It is an act of obedience symbolizing the believer's faith…"; BF&M IV.A: regeneration "is a change of heart wrought by the Holy Spirit through conviction of sin, to which the sinner responds in repentance toward God and faith"; AG 5: "Salvation is received through repentance toward God and faith toward the Lord Jesus Christ"; AG 6a: by baptism believers "declare to the world that they have died with Christ". How it follows: both texts place the new birth in repentance and faith worked by the Spirit, and describe baptism as a symbol or declaration of it, never as its means. Caveat for the page: AG 5 itself uses Titus 3:5's "washing of regeneration", which CCC 1215 reads of baptism; the AG text does not say what the washing is.
5. **Overlap:** partly OB-1 (Reformed: grace and salvation not tied to baptism) and OB-2 (sacraments do not give grace by the rite alone). New in its ground: the Reformed/Lutheran objections keep baptism as a sign and seal or means; this one makes it a symbol of what preceded it.
6. **Verdict:** **PASSES** (5 sources, answered, anchored in BF&M IV.A, VII and AG 5, 6a).

### BA-2 · Acts 2:38: "for the remission of sins" means "because of", not "in order to obtain"

1. **Objection:** In Acts 2:38 the Greek *eis* ("for/unto the remission of sins") can mean "because of" or "on the basis of", so Peter commands baptism of people already forgiven, and the verse does not teach that baptism remits sins.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - A. T. Robertson (Southern Baptist Theological Seminary), *Word Pictures in the New Testament*, vol. 3, *Acts* (1930), on Acts 2:38, read on a Wayback copy of https://www.studylight.org/commentaries/eng/rwp/acts-2.html: "My view is decidedly against the idea that Peter, Paul, or any one in the New Testament taught baptism as essential to the remission of sins" `[verified: Wayback]`. Frames the issue as "sacramental or … evangelical theology"; does not name Rome.
   - Wally Morris, *Proclaim & Defend* (FBFI), 27 Nov 2017, https://proclaimanddefend.org/acts-2/: *eis* "causal" or "temporal", with Mt 3:11 and 12:41; names the Catholic Church `[verified]`.
   - Brandon Rhea, CBTS, 12 Jan 2026, https://content.cbtseminary.org/?p=39854: reads Acts 2:38 against the Catholic causal reading, with 1 Peter 3:21 `[verified]`.
   - `Frequency: 3 independent sources` (all Baptist; no AG source found).
   - Outside the group (not counted): Beisner (PCA), equip.org 2004 `[verified]`; Ron Rhodes, *Reasoning from the Scriptures with Catholics* (Harvest House), as reported by Broussard 2020 `[verified only as Broussard reports it]`. `[memory]`: Julius R. Mantey (Northern Baptist Theological Seminary) argued the causal *eis* in *JBL* 1951–52 against Ralph Marcus; not read, not counted.
3. **The other side answers it:** Karlo Broussard, "In Baptism, Our Sins Are Forgiven", *Catholic Answers Magazine*, 21 May 2020, https://www.catholic.com/magazine/online-edition/in-baptism-our-sins-are-forgiven: "Rhodes gives no argument from the verse itself or the immediate context" `[verified]`. Also Catholic Answers *Bible Navigator*, "Necessity of Baptism", Acts 2:38, https://catholic.com/bible-navigator/necessity-of-baptism/acts238 `[snippet]`.
4. **Official anchor:** BF&M VII ("an act of obedience symbolizing the believer's faith") and IV.A (regeneration through repentance and faith); AG 5 and 6a. How it follows: if forgiveness comes with repentance and faith and baptism symbolizes it, Acts 2:38 must be read so that baptism follows forgiveness. Note: BF&M VII does not print Acts 2:38 among its proof texts (it prints Acts 2:41-42); AG 6a does not either.
5. **Overlap:** new (no official-layer objection reads Acts 2:38). A sub-argument of BA-1; the coordinator may merge it there as the main proof text.
6. **Verdict:** **PASSES** (3 Baptist sources, answered, anchored), with the flag that the anchor is the general doctrine, not a text that reads the verse.

### BA-3 · Infants cannot repent or believe; the New Testament baptizes only believers

1. **Objection:** Every baptism in the New Testament follows hearing, repentance and faith ("repent and be baptized", "believe and be baptized"); infants cannot repent or believe, so infant baptism has no New Testament command or example.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - John Gill, *Infant-Baptism, a Part and Pillar of Popery* (1766), reformedreader.org: no precept or example in Scripture, so paedobaptists fall back on tradition `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes.
   - C. H. Spurgeon, *Baptismal Regeneration* (1864), section on the sponsors: "they promise for the baby what they never did, and never thought of doing for themselves"; "Surely the infant has no desire in the matter" `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes (the doctrine as "Popish").
   - Allen Tennison (AG), *Influence*, 28 Feb 2024: "The Assemblies of God rejects pedobaptism in favor of credobaptism." `[verified]`. Names Rome: no.
   - Brandon Rhea, CBTS, 2026: on the Catholic answer to the infant's lack of faith, "an alien faith is supplied in the child's place" (his description, not CCC wording) `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes.
   - Proclaim & Defend, "Four Reasons Acts 2:38–39 Does Not Imply Infant Baptism", 9 Jan 2025, https://proclaimanddefend.org/2025/01/09/four-reasons-acts-238-39-does-not-imply-infant-baptism/ (repentance precedes baptism; infants cannot repent) `[snippet]` (author not read; not counted until read).
   - `Frequency: 4 independent sources` (3 Baptist, 1 AG).
   - Catholic side as index: the Catholic Answers tract lists this as the objection of "Fundamentalists" ("Baptism requires faith and a conscious decision") `[verified]`.
3. **The other side answers it:** Catholic Answers, tract "Infant Baptism", https://www.catholic.com/tract/infant-baptism: requirements infants cannot meet do not apply to them (as with circumcision); Acts 2:39 "to your children"; household baptisms (Acts 16:15, 33; 1 Cor 1:16); Luke 18:15 *brephe* `[verified]`. Terry Svik, "The Promise Is to You and to Your Children", *Catholic Answers Magazine* (2019), https://www.catholic.com/magazine/print-edition/the-promise-is-to-you-and-to-your-children `[snippet]`.
4. **Official anchor:** BF&M VII: "Christian baptism is the immersion of a believer"; AG 6a: "All who repent and believe on Christ as Saviour and Lord are to be baptized." How it follows: the subject of baptism is defined as a believer (BF&M) or as those who repent and believe (AG); infants are not that subject. Under the official-layer rule, AG 6a is not exclusive on the subject (`../catholic.md` OB-7 Status); as an anchor for a debate objection it is enough that the objection follows from it.
5. **Overlap:** same ground as OB-7 (prepared, not decided), which already drafts this objection from the same texts.
6. **Verdict:** **PASSES** (4 sources, answered, anchored).

### BA-4 · The thief on the cross was saved without baptism, so baptism is not necessary for salvation

1. **Objection:** Jesus promised paradise to the thief on the cross, who was never baptized; so baptism cannot be necessary for salvation.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - Billy Graham (Southern Baptist), "Is baptism necessary for salvation?", *Answers*, Billy Graham Evangelistic Association, undated, https://billygraham.org/answers/is-baptism-necessary-for-salvation: "the thief on the cross had no opportunity for baptism or church membership"; "I think we violate the Scriptures when we make baptism the prime requirement for salvation" `[verified]`. Names Rome: no.
   - Other Baptist or AG sources: **not found** in this run (searches returned Churches of Christ answering it, Enduring Word (Calvary Chapel, outside the group) and a 1982 Ankerberg programme whose transcript is 404).
   - `Frequency: 1 independent source`.
   - Catholic side as index: the objection is answered repeatedly by Catholic apologists (below), a sign it is frequent in debate, but the Baptist/AG sources were not found here.
3. **The other side answers it:** Karlo Broussard, "The Good Thief Isn't Good for Protestant Doctrines", *Catholic Answers Magazine*, 21 Dec 2016, https://www.catholic.com/magazine/online-edition/the-good-thief-isnt-good-for-protestant-doctrines: states the objection ("Dismas was saved without baptism") and answers that God is not bound to the sacraments in extraordinary cases `[verified]`. Catholic Answers Live, "Doesn't the thief on the cross prove sacraments are optional?", https://www.catholic.com/audio/cal/doesn-t-the-thief-on-the-cross-prove-sacraments-are-optional `[snippet]`; Jimmy Akin, https://jimmyakin.com/?p=10377 (baptism of desire; before the New Covenant) `[snippet]`.
4. **Official anchor:** BF&M IV: "There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ as Lord" (no rite named as a condition); AG 5: "Salvation is received through repentance toward God and faith". How it follows: the texts name repentance and faith as the conditions of salvation; the thief is the stock example of both without baptism.
5. **Overlap:** partly OB-1 (Reformed: salvation not so tied to baptism that none can be saved without it). Note: the Catholic answer agrees in part (CCC 1257, Spanish as quoted in `../catholic.md` QE-3 `[verified there]`: «Dios ha vinculado la salvación al sacramento del Bautismo, sin embargo, Él no queda sometido a sus sacramentos»).
6. **Verdict:** **FAILS: frequency** (1 group source found; answered and anchored). Likely a search gap, not absence: the Catholic side answers it often. Second pass suggested (Baptist Press, Lifeway, AG *Pentecostal Evangel* archive).

### BA-5 · Baptism gives grace "ex opere operato", without faith in the one baptized; the baptized who live in sin show it does not regenerate

1. **Objection:** Catholic teaching has baptism give grace by the rite performed, without faith in the one baptized; but grace is received by faith, and the many baptized people who live without faith or in open sin show that the rite does not regenerate.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - Brad Waggoner (former dean, Southern Baptist Theological Seminary; director of research, LifeWay), quoted in "Baptism as requirement for membership set for church vote", *Baptist Press*, Staff, 24 July 2006, https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/baptism-as-requirement-for-membership-set-for-church-vote/ (read on Wayback 20220526132942; live site 403): "The Southern Baptist understanding of baptism stands in conflict with the official doctrine of traditional Roman Catholicism and even some Protestant groups who teach that in the act of baptism, there is the impartation of grace ex opere operato, without preexisting faith" `[verified: Wayback]` (quotation longer than the usual short quote: the verifier may trim it). Names Rome: yes.
   - Brandon Rhea, CBTS, 12 Jan 2026, https://content.cbtseminary.org/?p=39854: "Ex opere operato, or by the work performed, a baby or an adult is born again" (his summary of the Catholic view, which he rejects) `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes.
   - John Gill (1766), reformedreader.org: rejects that the rites "confer grace ex opere operato, by the work done" `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes.
   - C. H. Spurgeon (1864), "facts" section: "Neither in the righteous nor the wicked do we find regeneration wrought by baptism"; "It is a well-known fact that baptized persons have been hanged" `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes ("Popish doctrine").
   - `Frequency: 4 independent sources` (all Baptist; convention line represented by Waggoner).
   - Outside the group (not counted): Peter Leithart (Theopolis, "Sacramental Efficacy") reports the Protestant claim "virtually a magical view" `[snippet]`.
3. **The other side answers it:** Peter Kreeft, *Fundamentals of the Faith* (Ignatius, 1988), ch. 45, pp. 282–286, reposted at https://www.catholiceducation.org/en/religion-and-philosophy/apologetics/the-sacraments.html: states the objection ("To Protestants, sacraments must be one of two things: either mere symbols, reminders, like words; or else real magic") and answers that the sacraments work ex opere operato "not impersonally and automatically like machines" `[verified]`. Catholic Answers, "Infant Baptism" tract, on faith in infants (requirements infants cannot meet do not bind them) `[verified]`.
4. **Official anchor:** BF&M IV.A (regeneration as "a change of heart wrought by the Holy Spirit through conviction of sin, to which the sinner responds in repentance … and faith"); BF&M IV ("There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ as Lord"); AG 5 ("Salvation is received through repentance toward God and faith"). How it follows: if regeneration is the Spirit's work answered by personal repentance and faith, a rite received without the recipient's faith cannot be its cause.
5. **Overlap:** same ground as OB-2 (Reformed/Lutheran: sacraments do not profit without faith; Trent VII can. 8) and the Simon Magus point of OB-1. The Baptist form adds the infant (no faith of one's own) and Spurgeon's "facts".
6. **Verdict:** **PASSES** (4 sources, answered, anchored). Close to OB-2: the coordinator may fold it in as that objection's "frequent in debate" form.

### BA-6 · Infant baptism rests on tradition, not Scripture: "a part and pillar of popery"

1. **Objection:** Infant baptism has no command or example in Scripture and is defended by appeal to unwritten tradition, the same ground the Roman Church gives for its other rites; Scripture alone must judge it.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - John Gill, *Infant-Baptism, a Part and Pillar of Popery* (1766), reformedreader.org: quotes Roman authorities to the effect that "Among apostolical traditions infant-baptism is to be reckoned", and argues it rests on tradition, not Scripture, and sustains national churches `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes.
   - C. H. Spurgeon (1864): the doctrine prepares "stepping-stones to make it easy for men to go to Rome" (about baptismal regeneration, not infant baptism as such) `[verified]`. Names Rome: yes. Counts only partly (his ground is regeneration, not tradition).
   - J. M. Carroll (Southern Baptist, Landmark), *The Trail of Blood* (1931): treats infant baptism and baptismal regeneration as errors of the developing Catholic Church `[snippet: Wikipedia and Goodreads summaries; book not read]`.
   - `Frequency: 1 verified source fully on this ground (+1 partial, +1 snippet)`.
3. **The other side answers it:** Catholic Answers, "Infant Baptism" tract: the practice is attested by Origen, the Council of Carthage (253) and Augustine, and no early writer condemned it (paraphrase of the tract, not its words) `[verified]`. Catholic Answers Magazine, "The Trail of Blood" (review of Carroll), https://www.catholic.com/magazine/online-edition/the-trail-of-blood `[snippet]`.
4. **Official anchor:** BF&M I: Scripture is "the supreme standard by which all human conduct, creeds, and religious opinions should be tried"; AG preamble: "The Bible is our all-sufficient rule for faith and practice." How it follows: a practice defended from tradition must be tried by Scripture, and the objectors find no Scripture for it.
5. **Overlap:** new for Baptism; it belongs as much to the Sola Scriptura doctrine page (BF&M I is already quoted there, `../../sola-scriptura/catholic.md`).
6. **Verdict:** **FAILS: frequency** (1 verified source on this exact ground; answered; anchored). Care for the page: the Fathers named in the Catholic answer must not be assigned to any modern tradition, and Carroll's successionist history is contested by Baptist historians too (Wikipedia summary, `[snippet]`).

### BA-7 · Pouring or sprinkling is not baptism: *baptizo* means to immerse

1. **Objection:** The Greek *baptizo* means to immerse, and baptism pictures burial and resurrection with Christ (Romans 6:4); so pouring or sprinkling water is not baptism.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - Dennis Newkirk (pastor, Henderson Hills Baptist Church, Edmond, Okla.), column in the *Baptist Messenger*, quoted in *Baptist Press*, 24 July 2006 (Wayback 20220526132942): concern for those "under the mistaken conviction that sprinkling is baptism" `[verified: Wayback]`. Names Rome: no (churches "that sprinkle infants").
   - John Gill (1766): sprinkling replaced immersion under Roman influence `[verified, as summarised by the fetch tool; exact words not extracted]`. Names Rome: yes.
   - Allen Tennison (AG), *Influence*, 2024: "The Assemblies of God practices baptism of believers by immersion" `[verified]` (states the practice; does not object to pouring).
   - `Frequency: 2 independent sources` that object (Newkirk, Gill); Tennison states practice only.
   - Catholic side as index: Catholic Answers answers it in a tract and several videos (below), a sign the objection is common.
3. **The other side answers it:** Catholic Answers, tract "Baptism: Immersion Only?" (unsigned, page dated 2022-08-16), https://www.catholic.com/tract/baptism-immersion-only: *baptizo* also means washing; the *Didache*'s "pour water three times upon the head" `[verified]`. Tim Staples, "Is Immersion the Only Way to Baptize?", https://timstaples.com/is-immersion-the-only-way-to-baptize/ `[snippet]`.
4. **Official anchor:** BF&M VII: "Christian baptism is the immersion of a believer in water"; AG 6a: "The ordinance of baptism by immersion is commanded by the Scriptures." How it follows: both define the mode as immersion; the AG text is exclusive on the mode (`../catholic.md` OB-7 Status).
5. **Overlap:** OB-7 (mode) and OB-6a (Orthodox: triple immersion).
6. **Verdict:** **FAILS: frequency** (2 group sources that object; answered; anchored). Likely a search gap (the classic Baptist works on the mode, e.g. Alexander Carson, *Baptism in Its Mode and Subjects*, 1831 `[memory]`, were not read).

### BA-8 · Those baptized as Catholic infants must be baptized as believers

1. **Objection:** Infant sprinkling is not New Testament baptism, so a Catholic who comes to faith is to be baptized by immersion as a believer; Catholics call this rebaptism, Baptists call it the person's first baptism.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - *Baptist Press*, Staff, 24 July 2006 (Wayback): "The vast majority of Southern Baptist churches would require such inquirers to offer evidence of salvation and submit to baptism by immersion" (about members of "churches that sprinkle infants") `[verified: Wayback]`. A news report of practice, not an argument; Catholics are not named.
