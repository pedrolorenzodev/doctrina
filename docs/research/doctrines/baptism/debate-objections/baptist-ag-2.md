## Objections

### D-1 · "Baptism doesn't save you; faith does. Baptism is a sign of the salvation you already have"

1. **Objection (neutral):** Salvation, forgiveness and the new birth are received by faith alone; baptism is a command obeyed afterwards, a public sign of it, and treating it as what saves turns it into a work.
2. **Popular wording:** "Baptism doesn't save you — you get baptized because you're already saved." Variant on the work argument (Ryan, 30:08): "baptism is not my work, it's God's work. Well, is God getting baptized? No, you are." Spanish: not read in a Spanish debate (blocked); site wording to be confirmed from the Spanish debates.
3. **Debates:**
   - *Does Water Baptism Save?*, The Apologetic Dog, 2025-05-26, 193,443 views, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dWFlknJgLIY&t=1708s (28:28–31:12; also 2:08:14). Ryan (NeedGod.net). Quote (28:28): "that's a performance-based gospel." At 2:08:14: "Once you have faith in the gospel ... Go symbolize that."
   - *A Catholic Apologist Challenged Everything I Believe*, Bryce Crawford Podcast, 2026-06-08, 990,240 views, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oNWseAsfcGM&t=4408s (73:28–74:34). Bryce Crawford: "I do not believe that if someone doesn't get baptized that they don't have eternal salvation with Christ."
   - *Trent Horn and Gavin Ortlund on Baptism*, 2022-04-28, 148,262 views, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGwECD_Dv3k&t=266s (4:26–6:02; 18:29–19:02). Gavin Ortlund: baptism gives grace (he rejects "just a symbol") but "i just don't understand it to be the cause of regeneration"; regeneration "starts at faith and is sort of sealed and publicly expressed at baptism". Same core claim (baptism does not regenerate), with a higher view of baptism than BF&M VII.
   - Written sources from the first pass, verified (`verification.md` BA-1): Brandon Rhea (CBTS, 12 Jan 2026), Wally Morris (*Proclaim & Defend*, 27 Nov 2017).
   - Also: a viewer's question read by Heschmeyer, *Does Baptism Save Us?*, 2024-11-14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TfHjFNOfsL8&t=33s ("to say that baptism saves me is saying I play a part in my own salvation"): anonymous, not counted; Heschmeyer calls it "a pretty standard view" (1:06).
   - `Frequency: 5 independent` (Ryan, Crawford, Ortlund; Rhea, Morris), 3 of them in debate.
4. **Catholic answer:** Heschmeyer in the debate, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dWFlknJgLIY&t=431s (7:11–8:17: "no passage anywhere in the Bible ever describes baptism as a work"; Titus 3:5) and 37:53–44:57 (works of the law vs good works); again in T5uo7c3WV9c&t=1220s (20:20–21:58: baptism "is something that's done to you ... It's a passive verb"). Trent Horn to Crawford, oNWseAsfcGM&t=4119s (68:39: "God has bound salvation to the sacraments but he is not bound by them") and to Ortlund, JGwECD_Dv3k&t=1175s (19:35: actual grace before, sanctifying grace at baptism). Rests on official text: CCC 1257 (Horn's sentence is its wording), CCC 1213, 1215 (Titus 3:5), 1250 ("pure gratuitousness of the grace of salvation"); `../catholic.md` QE-2, QE-3.
5. **Anchor:** BF&M VII ("an act of obedience symbolizing the believer's faith"), IV.A, IV ("no salvation apart from personal faith"); AG 5 ("Salvation is received through repentance ... and faith"), 6a ("Thus they declare to the world..."). Follows directly.
6. **Line:** none of the three debaters is in the convention line. Ryan and Crawford: non-denominational evangelicals (labelled). Ortlund: Baptist by conviction; his church was First Baptist Church of Ojai (American Baptist Churches USA), and he is ordained in the Conservative Congregational Christian Conference `[snippet: Wikipedia via search]`. Rhea: Reformed Baptist; Morris: independent Baptist (FBFI). None is AG.
7. **Current teaching:** yes (CCC 1213, 1257, 1263).
8. **Overlap:** first-pass BA-1 and BA-5; partly OB-1, OB-2.
9. **Verdict:** **PASSES**, with the line flag: the anchor is the convention's text, but no SBC or AG debater was found; the arguers are non-denominational evangelicals and non-convention Baptists.

### D-2 · "The thief on the cross was never baptized" (and Cornelius received the Spirit before baptism)

1. **Objection (neutral):** People in Scripture were saved without baptism (the thief on the cross) or received the Spirit before it (Cornelius, Acts 10), so baptism is not what saves.
2. **Popular wording:** "The thief on the cross was never baptized, and Jesus told him, 'Today you will be with me in paradise.'" (Ryan's list, 33:24: "how the thief on the cross was saved, Cornelius in Acts 10 ... all without baptism".)
3. **Debates:**
   - dWFlknJgLIY, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dWFlknJgLIY&t=2004s (33:24). Ryan.
   - JGwECD_Dv3k, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGwECD_Dv3k&t=623s (10:23–11:29; 13:10). Ortlund, on Cornelius: "it looks like cornelius is regenerated and speaking in tongues prior to his baptism"; he says Cornelius is the pattern, not an exception (13:10).
   - Billy Graham, billygraham.org answer page: "the thief on the cross had no opportunity for baptism" (first pass BA-4, CONFIRMED in `verification.md`; undated page).
   - Heschmeyer expects it as standard (7:11: "if I hear about ... the good thief tonight, I'm going to suspect that we're off topic"): index, not counted.
   - `Frequency: 3 independent` (Ryan, Ortlund, Graham), but Ortlund raises Cornelius only and Graham is a written answer, not a debate. Weak.
4. **Catholic answer:** Trent Horn, JGwECD_Dv3k&t=656s (10:56–13:10): Cornelius is "an exception", a "boundary case" to show Gentiles may be baptized; Dismas is an exception to any rule. Heschmeyer, dWFlknJgLIY&t=431s (7:11): God "can save us however he wants to", the question is whether he wills to save through baptism. Karlo Broussard, Catholic Answers, 21 Dec 2016 (first pass, CONFIRMED). Rests on official text: CCC 1257 ("God has bound salvation to the sacrament of Baptism, but he himself is not bound by his sacraments"), 1258–1260 (baptism of blood and of desire); `../catholic.md` QE-3.
5. **Anchor:** BF&M IV ("There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ as Lord"); AG 5. Follows: salvation is tied to faith, which the thief had.
6. **Line:** Ryan non-denominational; Ortlund as in D-1; Billy Graham: Southern Baptist `[memory]`, not checked today.
7. **Current teaching:** partly. It aims at CCC 1257 ("Baptism is necessary for salvation"), which is current; but the same paragraph and 1258–1260 already allow salvation without the rite, so the objection hits only a reading the Church does not hold ("no one is saved without water baptism"). Flag for Pedro.

9. **Verdict:** **FAILS as it stands** (frequency weak: one debate on the thief, one on Cornelius, one non-debate source; current-teaching criterion only partly met). Likely to pass on frequency once the Spanish debates are read (it is the most-anticipated objection on the Catholic side), but the target problem stays.

### D-3 · "Acts 2:38: 'for' the remission of sins means 'because of'"

1. **Objection:** In Acts 2:38 *eis* can mean "because of", as in Mt 12:41 (they repented "at" the preaching of Jonah); Peter tells people already forgiven to be baptized.
2. **Popular wording:** "It's like 'take two aspirin for your headache': you don't take them to get a headache, you take them because you have one." (Ryan, 53:15.)
3. **Debates:** dWFlknJgLIY, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dWFlknJgLIY&t=3098s (51:38–53:15), Ryan. Written: Wally Morris 2017 (first pass, CONFIRMED). Historical support, not frequency: A. T. Robertson, *Word Pictures* (1930) (first pass, CONFIRMED); Julius Mantey (1950s, named by Heschmeyer at T5uo7c3WV9c 9:54, not checked). `Frequency: 2 independent` (Ryan, Morris).
4. **Catholic answer:** Heschmeyer, T5uo7c3WV9c&t=491s (8:11–9:54): the crowd is not yet saved; *eis* "appears some 1,774 times in the Bible ... you know how many times it means caused by? Never." Karlo Broussard 2020 (first pass, CONFIRMED). Apologetics only; the official text behind it is CCC 1226, 1262 (Acts 2:38 read of baptism), not checked today.
5. **Anchor:** none specific; general (BF&M IV.A, VII).
6. **Line / current teaching:** non-denominational + independent Baptist / yes.
7. **Verdict:** **FAILS: frequency (2)**, as in the first pass. Also no specific anchor. Candidate to recheck in the Spanish debates.

### D-4 · "1 Peter 3:21 says 'not the removal of dirt from the body'"

1. **Objection:** 1 Peter 3:21 denies that the water saves: in Noah's story the ark (Christ) saves and the water judges; baptism saves only as "an appeal to God for a good conscience".
2. **Popular wording:** "Keep reading: 'not as a removal of dirt from the body'." (Crawford, 75:40.)
3. **Debates:** dWFlknJgLIY&t=3228s (53:48–54:55), Ryan ("the ark is the salvation. The water is the judgment"); oNWseAsfcGM&t=4540s (75:40), Crawford. `Frequency: 2`.
4. **Catholic answer:** Heschmeyer, T5uo7c3WV9c&t=1023s (17:03–18:09); Horn to Crawford, oNWseAsfcGM&t=4573s (76:13, citing Oscar Cullmann). Official: CCC 1094 and 845 read the ark as figure of baptism / the Church (not checked today).
5. **Anchor:** BF&M VII by inference only. **Verdict:** **FAILS: frequency (2).**

### D-5 · "Mark 16:16 doesn't say 'whoever is not baptized will be condemned'"

1. **Objection:** Mark 16:16 condemns only unbelief, not the lack of baptism; and the longer ending of Mark is probably not original.
2. **Popular wording:** "It says whoever does not *believe* will be condemned — not whoever isn't baptized."
3. **Debates:** dWFlknJgLIY&t=2964s (49:24–50:32; 2:30:42–2:31:16 on the longer ending), Ryan; oNWseAsfcGM&t=4705s (78:25–79:31), Crawford. `Frequency: 2`.
