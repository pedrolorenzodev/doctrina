## Objections

### OD-1 · Pouring or sprinkling is not baptism: baptism is three immersions

1. **Objection:** Baptism is the triple immersion and emersion in the name of the Trinity; the Latin pouring or sprinkling ("aspersion") is an innovation and is not the baptism the Apostles handed down.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - Protopresbyter George D. Metallinos, *I Confess One Baptism* (Greek, Athens 1983; English tr. Priestmonk Seraphim, St. Paul's Monastery, Holy Mountain, 1994), Epilogue, https://www.oodegr.com/english/biblia/baptisma1/epilogos.htm `[verified]`: "For in no way can their 'aspersion' be considered baptism."
   - Archbishop Nikephoros (Theotokis) of Slavonia and Kherson, epistle "Against Baptism by Pouring" (dated 1754 in the page title; printed in *Orthodox Life* 40/1, 1990, pp. 16–19; posted by Orthodox Ethos, 16 Jan 2023), https://www.orthodoxethos.com/post/against-baptism-by-pouring-an-epistle-of-archbishop-nikephoros-slovania-and-kherson-1754 `[verified]`: in the Roman Church, after immersion, "they began to baptize not only by pouring but also by sprinkling".
   - Holy Orthodox Church in North America (HOCNA, outside communion with the canonical churches), pamphlet "The Decree (Ὅρος) … Concerning Latin Pseudo-Baptism, Issued in 1755", undated (file path dated 2012), p. 1, https://www.imoph.org/pdfs/2012/05/23/E3b2a003LatinPs%20Folder/E3b2a003LatinPs.pdf `[verified: PDF text]`: Roman Catholics "do not have Baptism, but sprinkling (ράντισμα)". Depends on Metallinos (cites him, p. 77).
   - Not counted: St. Nikodemos, *Pedalion* (1800), p. 55 n., cited by Metallinos B4 ("do not preserve the three immersions") and by HOCNA `[snippet: not read in an edition]`; Met. Hierotheos of Nafpaktos asked in 2016 that converts "not baptized" by triple immersion be baptized (reported by Demacopoulos, below) `[verified as a report only]`.
   - **Frequency: 3 independent sources** (one, HOCNA, leans on Metallinos).
3. **The other side answers it:** Jimmy Akin, "Why does the Catholic Church pour instead of immerse for baptisms?", Catholic Answers video, 19 Nov 2018, https://www.catholic.com/video/why-does-the-catholic-church-pour-instead-of-immerse-for-baptisms `[verified]`: "both immersion and pouring are valid modes of baptism", citing the Didache. Fr. Charles Grondin, "Are three pours required at baptism?", Catholic Answers Q&A, 24 Jun 2022, https://www.catholic.com/qa/are-three-pours-required-at-baptism `[verified]`: "All that is required is the pouring of water and the trinitarian formula." Neither names the Orthodox objection.
4. **Official anchor:** Philaret, Longer Catechism Q.290 (Schaff II, p. 491) `[verified]`: "What is most essential in the administration of Baptism? Trine immersion in water, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost." Mogila I Q.102 (Overbeck p. 75) `[verified]`: "by being thrice immersed in Water". 1755 Oros (Metallinos' English): "we follow the sacred and divine Apostles who order us to baptize aspirants with three immersions and emersions" `[verified in ../catholic.md]`. 1895 Encyclical §VIII (p. 31): "sprinkling or affusion, being privily brought in, came to be accepted by the Papal Church" `[verified in ../catholic.md]`. How it follows: if trine immersion is "most essential", a rite without it lacks the essential element.
5. **Overlap:** same as OB-6a.
6. **Verdict:** PASSES (3 sources, answered, anchored). Context for the page: the 1895 Encyclical already says this in words, so the debate layer adds little beyond the official one. Inside Orthodoxy the point is contested: Bishop Seraphim of Ottawa (1998, https://www.archbishop-of-ottawa.org/node/2133 `[verified]`): "a baptism by pouring, when necessary, has always been accepted in our Church", against voices "mostly from the outer edges of the Orthodox Church".

### OD-2 · Baptism given outside the Orthodox Church is void: Catholic converts must be baptized

1. **Objection:** There is no baptism outside the one Church; a baptism given among the Latins conveys no grace, so Catholics who become Orthodox are to be received as unbaptized and baptized.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - Metallinos, *I Confess One Baptism* (1983/1994), ch. C.2 "Latins are 'in need of baptism'", https://www.oodegr.com/english/biblia/baptisma1/B5.htm `[verified]`, summarising the Kollyvades and Oikonomos: "Since it is administered in heresy, i.e. outside the Church, it is in itself without substance (Apostolic Canon XLVII)." Epilogue: economia only where a confession "administered baptism with trine immersion" `[verified]`.
   - Fr. Peter Heers, *The Ecclesiological Renovation of Vatican II* (Uncut Mountain Press, 2015), excerpt (Kindle loc. 2478–2482), https://www.orthodoxethos.com/post/an-excerpt-from-the-ecclesiological-renovation-protopresbyter-peter-heers `[verified]`: "They suppose that Baptism can exist outside the unity of the Church and the other mysteries". Fr. John Cox's review quotes ERV2 p. 142: "the Holy Spirit is not given in the 'Baptism' of those outside the Church" (https://blogs.ancientfaith.com/orthodoxyandheterodoxy/2018/12/04/can-you-baptize-without-baptism-review-the-ecclesiological-renovation-of-vatican-ii-by-fr-peter-heers/, 4 Dec 2018) `[verified, secondary quotation]`.
   - Met. Hierotheos (Vlachos) of Nafpaktos, review of Heers (dated Oct 2014; in his *The Orthodox Church and the Rest of the Christian World*, Levadia 2025, pp. 98–110), https://www.orthodoxethos.com/post/erv2-hierotheos-review1 `[verified]`: of Vatican II's view that there are sacraments outside the Church and the Orthodox who accept it, "Both these issues, however, are deviations from the Orthodox faith and the theology of the Fathers of the Church".
   - HOCNA pamphlet (above), p. 1 `[verified]`: Latin sprinkling, administered "outside the Church", "cannot be accepted by economia" (quoting Metallinos p. 77; not independent of him on this sentence).
   - **Frequency: 3 independent sources** (Metallinos, Heers, Hierotheos; HOCNA repeats Metallinos).
3. **The other side answers it:** Fr. Ray Ryland, "Will the Real St. Cyprian Please Stand?", *Catholic Answers Magazine*, 1 Apr 1997, https://www.catholic.com/magazine/print-edition/will-the-real-st-cyprian-please-stand `[verified]`: answers Orthodox and Anglican use of Cyprian; on whether those outside the Church can baptize, "Stephen ruled that they could, if they used the proper form." Jimmy Akin, "Rebaptism & Conversion", 13 Mar 2005, https://jimmyakin.com/2005/03/rebaptism_conve.html `[verified]`: on rebaptizing a validly baptized person, "it would be an insult to the work that the Holy Spirit did in your baptism to rebaptize you" (Protestant case; the Orthodox practice appears only in a reader's comment).
4. **Official anchor:** 1755 Oros (Metallinos' English; Tier 4): "recognize only one Church … It is her Mysteries [i.e. sacraments], and consequently her baptism, that we accept"; heretics' baptisms "give no sanctification to such as receive them"; "We receive those who come over to the Orthodox faith, who were baptized without being baptized, as being unbaptized" `[verified in ../catholic.md; last sentence re-seen today, oodegr par2.htm]`. Also Dositheus XVI (Robertson p. 141): the minister in necessity must "be Orthodox" `[verified]`. How it follows: if only the Orthodox Church's baptism is accepted, a Latin baptism is not one.
5. **Overlap:** same as OB-6b.
6. **Verdict:** PASSES. Greek and Russian usage differ and the page must label it: the 1755 Oros (Constantinople, Alexandria, Jerusalem) requires baptism; Constantinople 1484 and Moscow 1667 receive Latins by chrismation (Dragas 1998, https://mail.myriobiblos.gr/texts/english/Dragas_RomanCatholic_4.html `[verified]`); Dositheus XV speaks of heretics' "valid Baptism" (`../catholic.md`). Today, per G. Demacopoulos (Fordham; https://orthodoxcouncil.org/-/innovation-in-the-guise-of-tradition-anti-ecumenist-efforts-to-derail-the-great-and-holy-council.html, © 2022) `[verified]`: "none of the fourteen autocephalous churches in the Orthodox world officially require baptism for Trinitarian Christians"; he adds that Cyril V's edict "was denounced by other Orthodox leaders at the time". The proponents found are all from the strict (anti-ecumenist) current.

### OD-3 · Vatican II's "common baptism" is a new ecclesiology: there is no partial communion

1. **Objection:** Vatican II's teaching that all the validly baptized are in a real if imperfect communion with the Church (*Unitatis redintegratio* 3, 22) rests on a new theology of baptism; communion in Christ cannot be partial, and unity in a "common Baptism" is impossible.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - Heers, *Ecclesiological Renovation* (2015), excerpt (Kindle loc. 2478–2482) `[verified]`: "Just as there can be no partial Christ, there can be no partial communion in Christ"; "unity in a 'common Baptism' is impossible". Book announcement, OrthoChristian, 25 Sep 2015, https://orthochristian.com/88675.html `[verified]`: UR has "important inconsistencies, historical and ecclesiological". Same author: paper of 23 Mar 2016 (Piraeus), "The Recognition of the Baptism of the Heterodox as the Basis for a New Ecclesiology (In Step with Vatican II)", https://orthochristian.com/back/list/197/538.html `[verified]`.
   - Met. Hierotheos, review of Heers (Oct 2014), above `[verified]`.
   - Not counted: HOCNA, "Baptismal Theology: The glue that binds together the 'Broad Ecumenical World Family'", https://www.imoph.org/Theology_en/E3f2005aBapTheol.pdf `[verified: PDF text]` — it targets Orthodox ecumenists (Zizioulas 1985, Balamand 1993, SCOBA 1999, Moscow 1997), not Catholic teaching.
   - **Frequency: 2 independent sources.**
3. **The other side answers it:** `[not found]` (no Catholic reply to Heers or Hierotheos located). The only critique found is Orthodox (Cox 2018, above).
4. **Official anchor:** partial. 1755 Oros: "recognize only one Church … her baptism, that we accept" (as OD-2). No allowed text speaks of "partial communion" or of baptism as a bond of unity with the non-Orthodox. Line: if only the Orthodox Church's baptism is accepted, a bond of unity through other baptisms cannot exist.
5. **Overlap:** new (bears on QE-10; no OB-n answers it).
6. **Verdict:** FAILS: frequency (2), not answered by the Catholic side. Note: the debate is mainly intra-Orthodox (anti-ecumenists against Orthodox bodies that use the same "baptismal theology").

### OD-4 · The Latin Church separates chrismation and communion from baptism

1. **Objection:** Baptism, chrismation and the Eucharist are one act of initiation, given together even to infants; the Latin practice of confirming years later, by the bishop, and of withholding communion from baptized infants breaks that unity.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - Orthodox Church in America, *The Orthodox Faith*, vol. II *Worship*, "The Sacraments: Chrismation" (catechetical text of the OCA), https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/chrismation `[verified]`: "the Orthodox Church differs from the Roman Catholic and Protestant churches where the two sacraments are often separated"; communion follows "with infants as well as adults".
   - Priest Brendan Pelphrey, "Congratulations! You Are Being Cremated Today!", Pravmir, 28 Jun 2016, https://www.pravmir.com/?p=272646 `[verified]`: "In the West, however, chrismation/confirmation became completely separated from baptism."
   - Dn. Michael Hyatt, *At the Intersection of East and West*, "For the Life of the World, part ten", Ancient Faith Radio, 8 Dec 2009, https://www.ancientfaith.com/podcasts/eastwest/for_the_life_of_the_world_part_ten `[verified: transcript]`: "In the West, these two things got separated".
   - Not counted: Schmemann, *Of Water and the Spirit* (SVS Press, 1974) `[memory]`; Fr. Robert Taft and other Eastern Catholic writers quoted on byzcath.org (Catholic, not Orthodox) `[snippet]`.
   - **Frequency: 3 independent sources** (all describe the difference as a Western departure; none is polemical in tone).
3. **The other side answers it:** Paul Senz, "Restoring the Order of the Sacraments", *Catholic Answers Magazine* online, 24 Apr 2024, https://www.catholic.com/magazine/online-edition/restoring-the-order-of-the-sacraments `[verified]`: explains the history (19th-century France; Pius X, 1910) and says there is "nothing inherently wrong with the current order", while arguing for restoring the older one. It concedes the Eastern practice was the early one, so it answers the charge of error, not the history.
4. **Official anchor:** Mogila I Q.104 (Overbeck p. 77) `[verified]`: "when the Priest anointeth the newly baptized Person with the holy Oil, he becomes endued from above with the Gifts of the Holy Spirit". 1848 Encyclical §5.xii lists among Western novelties "not anointing baptized infants, or their not receiving the Eucharist" `[verified in ../catholic.md; English of unproven provenance]`. Philaret Q.307 ("the baptized believer") gives no timing `[verified]`. How it follows: chrismation is given by the priest to the newly baptized, so a later, separate confirmation departs from it.
5. **Overlap:** partly: the 1848 §5.xii text is in `../catholic.md` "Not carried over", routed to the Confirmation and Eucharist doctrines; not an OB-n.
6. **Verdict:** PASSES (3 sources, answered, anchored in Mogila Q.104; the 1848 text needs an edition). Whether it belongs to Baptism or to Confirmation is the coordinator's call (see notes).

### OD-5 · Infants inherit death, not Adam's guilt: the Latin doctrine of original guilt (and limbo) is wrong

1. **Objection:** Adam's descendants inherit mortality and corruption ("ancestral sin"), not his guilt; the Church baptizes infants to give them new life, not to remit a guilt they do not have, and the Augustinian idea that unbaptized infants are excluded from heaven (limbo) follows from an error.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - John Meyendorff, *Byzantine Theology* (Fordham UP, 1974), p. 146, as quoted by J. Cutsinger, "Restoration to Our Natural State", 10 Feb 2008, https://www.cutsinger.net/?p=890 `[verified, secondary quotation]`: the Church baptizes children "not to 'remit' their yet non-existent sins, but in order to give them a new and immortal life".
   - Kallistos Ware, *The Orthodox Church* (Penguin, 1991), p. 229, same page `[verified, secondary quotation]`: humans "inherit Adam's corruption and mortality, but not his guilt".
   - Abbot Tryphon, "Ancestral Sin versus Original Sin", 30 Nov 2012 (from morningoffering.blogspot.com), https://www.frpeterpreble.com/2012/11/ancestral-sin-versus-original-sin.html `[verified]`: Orthodox "inherit only the results of Adams sin, not his guilt" (sic).
   - Fr. Stephen Freeman, "In Limbo No More", 22 Oct 2006, https://www.glory2godforallthings.com/2006/10/22/in-limbo-no-more/ `[verified]`: the forensic view "creates stumbling blocks for unbelievers who reject a God who would consign an infant to Hell (or less than heaven)".
   - Not counted: John Romanides, *The Ancestral Sin* (Greek 1957; English Zephyr 2002) `[snippet]`.
   - **Frequency: 4 independent sources** (two only through a secondary quotation).
3. **The other side answers it:** `[snippet]` only: catholic.com tract "What the Early Church Believed: Original Sin" and the Catholic Answers audio "Understanding Eastern Orthodoxy (with Fr. Deacon Dozier)" (search results say it reads Symeon's "inheritance of guilt" as a state, not personal guilt). Not read today. The official Catholic text the answer would rest on, CCC 405 (original sin "does not have the character of a personal fault"), is not an apologetic answer.
4. **Official anchor:** No anchor. The allowed texts say the opposite: Dositheus XVI (Robertson pp. 139–141) `[verified]`: baptism "is necessary even for infants, since they also are subject to original sin, and without Baptism are not able to obtain its remission"; "those that are not regenerated, since they have not received the remission of hereditary sin, are, of necessity, subject to eternal punishment". Mogila I Q.102–103 (Overbeck pp. 75–77) `[verified]`: "Baptism is a washing away and rooting out of original Sin"; "it taketh away original Sin in Infants".
5. **Overlap:** new (touches QE-2 and QE-4); not an OB-n, and the official layer of the Orthodox column agrees with the Catholic one here (`../catholic.md`, "Orthodox agreements").
6. **Verdict:** FAILS: no anchor (the 17th-century confessions teach the contrary); Catholic answer only as snippet. A clear case of a frequent modern Orthodox objection that its own symbolic books do not support; Orthodox writers themselves disagree (Pravmir essay `[snippet]`).

### OD-6 · Only an Orthodox may baptize, even in necessity (against "even a pagan or heretic")

1. **Objection:** Florence's teaching that in necessity even a pagan or heretic can baptize, if he keeps the form and intends what the Church does, is wrong: the minister must be Orthodox.
2. **Where it is raised:** no debate or apologetic source found that states it against the Catholic teaching `[not found]`. Heers's 2016 paper argues against "recognizing" heretics' ministrations in general (counted under OD-2). **Frequency: 0.**
3. **The other side answers it:** `[not found]` as a reply to Orthodox; the Catholic texts are in `../catholic.md` QE-7.
4. **Official anchor:** Dositheus XVI (Robertson p. 141) `[verified]`: "by another man, provided he be Orthodox, and have the intention proper to Divine Baptism"; Mogila Q.102 `[verified]`: not repeated "provided the Person who administered the Baptism believed orthodoxly".
5. **Overlap:** part of OB-6b.
6. **Verdict:** FAILS: frequency (0), answer not found. Anchored only.

### OD-7 · The baptismal formula

1. **Objection (tested):** the Latin active formula "I baptize you" differs from the Eastern "The servant of God N. is baptized".
2. **Where it is raised:** `[not found]` in Orthodox debate material. Florence accepts the Eastern formula (`../catholic.md` QE-6). The live formula disputes found are Catholic-internal (CDF 2020 on "We baptize"). **Frequency: 0.**
3. **Answered:** n/a. 4. **Anchor:** none against the Latin formula (Mogila Q.102 requires the words "accurately, and without any Alteration", without naming a Latin formula). 5. **Overlap:** none.
6. **Verdict:** FAILS: frequency, answer, anchor. Kept as a negative result: the formula is not a point of Orthodox–Catholic debate today.

## Notes for the coordinator

1. **Finding: the debate layer adds almost nothing new for the Orthodox column.** The two strong debate objections (OD-1, OD-2) are already OB-6a and OB-6b from the official layer. The one new passer (OD-4) was deliberately routed to Confirmation in `../catholic.md`. The most frequent new objection (OD-5, ancestral sin) fails the anchor because Dositheus XVI and Mogila teach original sin and its remission in infants; if it reached the page it would make the Orthodox column contradict its own symbolic books.
2. **No public debates.** Orthodox–Catholic disagreement on baptism lives in books, articles and catechetical pages, not in formal debates; none with a transcript was found.
3. **Who raises OD-1 and OD-2.** All proponents found belong to the strict, anti-ecumenist current (Metallinos, Heers, Hierotheos, HOCNA). Mainstream Orthodox voices (Bishop Seraphim of Ottawa; Demacopoulos: no autocephalous church officially requires baptism of Trinitarian Christians) speak against rebaptism. The page's "Frecuente en el debate" mark should not suggest this is the Orthodox Church's common practice. The Greek/Russian label is needed for OD-2 (1755 vs 1484/1667).
4. **HOCNA** is outside communion with the fourteen churches (DECISIONS scope: canonical Eastern Orthodoxy). I listed it but never needed it to reach 3; the verifier may drop it without changing a verdict.
5. **Independence.** HOCNA quotes Metallinos; Hierotheos endorses Heers but writes in his own words. The counts above already treat HOCNA as dependent.
6. **Answers are weak in kind.** The Catholic answers to OD-1 (Akin, Grondin) address immersion vs pouring generally, not the Orthodox; Senz (OD-4) concedes the history. Catholic Answers has little addressed to Orthodox baptism specifically; Ryland 1997 is the only one written against Orthodox use of Cyprian.
7. **Verify first:** Meyendorff p. 146 and Ware p. 229 (read only as quoted by Cutsinger); the Oros quotations (Metallinos' English, © ); the 1848 §5.xii edition; Nikephoros' epistle date (1754 per the page title; the author was born in 1731 `[memory]`); Heers ERV2 p. 142 (read only in Cox's review).
8. **Scope question for Pedro:** OD-4 is an objection about initiation; it may belong to Confirmation, not Baptism (as `../catholic.md` already decided for the 1848 text).
