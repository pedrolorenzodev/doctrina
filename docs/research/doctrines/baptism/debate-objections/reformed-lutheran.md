## Objections

### DO-RL-1 · Sacraments do not give grace "ex opere operato": without faith, baptism does not profit
1. **Objection:** The Catholic Church teaches that baptism confers grace by the performed rite itself (*ex opere operato*) on anyone who places no obstacle, whereas the grace of a sacrament is received only by faith in the promise and works only by the Spirit, not by any power in the rite.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - John H. Gerstner (Reformed, PCA), *Primitive Theology*, excerpted as "A Primer on Roman Catholicism, Pt. 2", Ligonier, 28 May 2010, https://www.ligonier.org/posts/primer-roman-catholicism-pt-2 — "She does teach that the sacraments 'work' ex opere operato in their very administration." `[verified]`
   - Charles Hodge (Reformed, Princeton), *Systematic Theology* III (1872), §12 "Efficacy of Baptism", subsection "Doctrine of the Reformed Churches", https://ccel.org/ccel/hodge/theology3.iii.vi.xii.html — the Reformed deny "That baptism conveys grace 'ex opere operato' in the sense which Romanists attach to those words." `[verified]`
   - Michael Lynch (Reformed historian), "Baptismal Regeneration and Ex Opere Operato", *Modern Reformation*, 11 Nov 2020, https://www.modernreformation.org/resources/articles/baptismal-regeneration-and-ex-opere-operato — "universally condemned by the Reformed was their belief that baptism conferred saving grace ex opere operato." `[verified]`
   - Rev. Paul Doellinger (LCMS), "Bible Study with Luther: Genesis 17:1-14", lutheranreformation.org, 3 Nov 2017, https://lutheranreformation.org/?p=1935 — names the Roman Catholic Church as using *ex opere operato* for the view that performing the Mass or Baptism justifies, then quotes AC XIII. `[verified]` (no quotation of the author's own sentence kept: the fetch paraphrased it)
   - Joe Lau (Church of the Lutheran Confession, Immanuel Lutheran College), "We Receive the Benefits of the Sacraments by Faith Alone", *Lutheran Spokesman*, 1 Sep 2022, https://lutheranspokesman.org/2022/09/01/we-receive-the-benefits-of-the-sacraments-by-faith-alone/ — "Without faith, the mere outward performance of a sacrament brings no spiritual benefit." (He names "Catholics and others", not the Church formally.) `[verified]`
   - John G. Deterding, *Lutheran SOLA FIDE vs Roman Catholic EX OPERE OPERATO of the Basis of the Confessions*, B.Div. thesis, Concordia Seminary St. Louis, 1947, https://scholar.csl.edu/bdiv/180, abstract — "Faith is not necessary to receive this grace, but the mere outward ceremony effects it mechanically". `[verified: abstract only]`
   - `Frequency: 6 independent sources` (3 Reformed, 3 Lutheran; different authors and outlets).
3. **The other side answers it:** D. J. Kennedy, "Sacraments", *Catholic Encyclopedia* (1912), §II "(3) Catholic Doctrine", https://www.catholic.com/encyclopedia/sacraments — "Protestants cannot in good faith object to the phrase"; the efficacy "does not depend on anything human, but solely on the will of God as expressed by Christ's institution and promise." `[verified]` Also Catholic Answers audio "Ex Opere Operato" (https://www.catholic.com/audio/ddp/ex-opere-operato) `[snippet]`.
4. **Official anchor:** Augsburg Confession XIII (Triglot Latin p. 48): «Damnant igitur illos, qui docent, quod sacramenta ex opere operato iustificent, nec docent fidem requiri in usu sacramentorum, quae credat remitti peccata.» Apology XIII.18 (Triglot Latin p. 312): «Hic damnamus totum populum scholasticorum doctorum, qui docent, quod sacramenta non ponenti obicem conferant gratiam ex opere operato sine bono motu utentis.» Westminster Confession XXVII.3: "The grace which is exhibited in or by the sacraments, rightly used, is not conferred by any power in them". `[verified in catholic.md OB-2; not re-read today]` The objection is these texts' condemnation, restated by their apologists.
5. **Overlap:** same as OB-2.
6. **Verdict:** PASSES.

### DO-RL-2 · Not everyone baptized is regenerated: regeneration is the Spirit's work, not the water's
1. **Objection:** Scripture and experience show baptized people who are not born again (Simon Magus, Acts 8:13, 23), and the "washing of regeneration" (Titus 3:5) is the Spirit's work, so baptism does not of itself regenerate those who receive it.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - John H. Gerstner, Ligonier 2010 (as in DO-RL-1) — "Rome tries to counter this with the contention that in Titus 3:5 baptism is represented as the washing of regeneration." `[verified]`
   - Charles Hodge, *Systematic Theology* III §12, subsection "Direct Arguments against the Doctrine of Baptismal Regeneration" (URL as in DO-RL-1) — "The baptized as a body remain unchanged in heart and life." `[verified]`
   - *Evangelical Times* staff writer, "Two limbos", 1 Jun 2007, https://evangelical-times.org/two-limbos — "Unwarranted also is the teaching that infants (or adults) can be washed from their sins by baptismal water." `[verified]` Not counted: the author and tradition are not stated (the paper is British evangelical, Reformed-leaning `[memory]`).
   - `Frequency: 2 independent sources` (group-confirmed); 3 with the unattributed *Evangelical Times* piece.
3. **The other side answers it:** Joe Heschmeyer, "Why 'Faith Alone' Is No Answer to 'Baptism Saves You'", *Shameless Popery* (Catholic Answers), 29 May 2025, https://www.catholic.com/audio/sp/why-faith-alone-is-no-answer-to-baptism-saves-you — reads Titus 3 as God's work, not a human deed ("step three of the kind of argument is just flat out wrong"). `[verified: page summary]`
4. **Official anchor:** WCF XXVIII.5: grace and salvation are not so annexed to baptism "as that no person can be regenerated or saved without it, or that all that are baptized are undoubtedly regenerated" (proof text Acts 8:13, 23); WLC 161: the sacraments become effectual "not by any power in themselves … but only by the working of the Holy Ghost". `[verified in catholic.md OB-1; WCF XXVIII.5 opening re-read on opc.org today]` The second half of XXVIII.5 is the objection's claim.
5. **Overlap:** partly OB-1 (its second half).
6. **Verdict:** FAILS (frequency: 2 confirmed group sources; answered and anchored).

### DO-RL-3 · Trent makes baptism, not faith, the instrument of justification
1. **Objection:** The Council of Trent names baptism the instrumental cause of justification (and penance its instrument for those who fall), whereas faith is the only instrument by which a sinner receives Christ's righteousness; making a sacrament the instrument displaces faith alone.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - R. C. Sproul, "What Does the Roman Catholic Church Believe About Justification?", Ligonier, 10 Oct 2023, https://learn.ligonier.org/articles/what-does-roman-catholic-church-believe-about-justification — "Faith, they said, is the sole instrumental cause for our justification" (the Reformers); the recipient "must accept and cooperate in baptism, by which he receives justifying grace"; penance as "a second plank". `[verified]`
   - "Actual Righteousness", *Tabletalk* daily study, March 2002, https://tabletalkmagazine.com/daily-study/2002/03/actual-righteousness/ (no author shown) — Rome "declared that the instrumental cause was baptism"; "The Reformers said the instrumental cause of justification was faith itself." `[verified]` Not counted separately: same ministry as Sproul (Ligonier).
   - Charles R. Biggs, "The Reformed and Post-Reformation Creeds and Councils, Class II: The Council of Trent", *Reformed Perspectives Magazine* 16/14, 30 Mar–5 Apr 2014, https://reformedperspectives.org/articles/cr_biggs/cha_biggs.reformed.02.html — quotes CCC "Justification is conferred in Baptism, the sacrament of faith" against "Faith is an instrument with no power in and of itself." `[verified]`
   - R. C. Sproul, *Faith Alone: The Evangelical Doctrine of Justification* (Baker, 1995), chapter on Trent `[memory]` — not counted (same author; not read).
   - `Frequency: 2 independent sources` (Sproul/Ligonier, Biggs).
3. **The other side answers it:** Joe Heschmeyer, *Shameless Popery*, 29 May 2025 (URL in DO-RL-2): faith and baptism "are not competing causes". `[verified: page summary]` Catholic Answers also lists "Does Baptism Save Us?" (https://www.catholic.com/audio/sp/does-baptism-save-us) `[snippet]`.
4. **Official anchor:** WCF XI.2: "Faith, thus receiving and resting on Christ and his righteousness, is the alone instrument of justification: yet is it not alone in the person justified, but is ever accompanied with all other saving graces, and is no dead faith, but worketh by love." (OPC text, https://opc.org/wcf.html; same wording in the 1647 text `[memory: not checked on the 1894 scan today]`) `[verified]` The objection sets "the alone instrument" against Trent VI ch. 7 (baptism as instrumental cause). Lutherans not counted: AC IV and the BoC also call baptism the means of regeneration, and no Lutheran source found raised this form.
5. **Overlap:** new for the Baptism page (it belongs at least as much to Justification; `justification/catholic.md` not checked for it).
6. **Verdict:** FAILS (frequency: 2 independent; answered, anchored). Probably reaches 3 with more search (Horton, Gerstner on justification, Boettner).

### DO-RL-4 · Infants who die unbaptized are not lost: salvation is not bound to the rite
1. **Objection:** Rome teaches that no one, infant or adult, is saved without baptism, so infants who die unbaptized are excluded from heaven; but God's promise to believers' children does not depend on the water, and elect infants are saved by the Spirit "when, and where, and how he pleaseth".
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - John Calvin, *Institutes* IV.15.20–22 (Beveridge tr.), https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.vi.xvi.html — children who die before baptism "are not excluded from the kingdom of heaven" (§22). `[verified]` (Calvin = a Reformed voice, not the column's text.)
   - B. B. Warfield, *The Development of the Doctrine of Infant Salvation*, New York: Christian Literature Co., 1891, p. 32–33, PDF https://cameronshaffer.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/The-Development-of-the-Doctrine-of-Infant-Salvation.pdf — English theologians "have repudiated the 'cruel judgment' of the Church of Rome as to the fate of infants dying unbaptized" (p. 33); on Trent, p. 14–15. `[verified: OCR of the scan]`
   - Charles Hodge, *Systematic Theology* III §12, subsections "Baptism is a Condition of Salvation" and "Direct Arguments…" (URL in DO-RL-1) — "Baptism, therefore, has the necessity of precept, not that of a means"; he rejects the view that "no unbaptized person, whether infant or adult, is saved", which he attributes to "Romanists and Romanizers". `[verified]`
   - `Frequency: 3 independent sources` (historic theologians; no modern debate source found in the time).
3. **The other side answers it:** Tim Staples, "What's the Deal with Limbo?", *Catholic Answers Magazine* online, 12 Sep 2023, https://www.catholic.com/magazine/online-edition/whats-the-deal-with-limbo — limbo never defined; ITC 2007 calls it "a possible theological hypothesis"; "the Church entrusts these children to our merciful God". `[verified]`
4. **Official anchor:** WCF X.3: "Elect infants, dying in infancy, are regenerated, and saved by Christ, through the Spirit, who worketh when, and where, and how he pleaseth" (OPC text) `[verified]`; WCF XXVIII.5 ("not so inseparably annexed … as that no person can be regenerated or saved without it") `[verified]`. The objection applies XXVIII.5 to infants via X.3. Lutherans do not raise it: AC IX condemns those who "say that children are saved without Baptism" (`catholic.md` "Not carried over") `[verified there]`.
5. **Overlap:** partly OB-1 (OB-1 is the general form; the infant case is not in its text or answer).
6. **Verdict:** PASSES. Caveat for the coordinator: the sources attack Rome's position as it stood before 2007 (damnation or limbo); limbo was never defined (CEC 1261 "we can only entrust them to the mercy of God" `[memory]`), so the objection must be worded against what the Catholic Church teaches now (CEC 1257 necessity), or it would misstate the target.

### DO-RL-5 · Laypeople and midwives may not baptize, even in emergency
1. **Objection:** Baptism belongs to the ministry of the Word; private baptism by laypeople or women in danger of death has no warrant and grows from the false belief that an unbaptized infant is lost.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - John Calvin, *Institutes* IV.15.20–22 (URL in DO-RL-4) — "it is improper for private individuals to take upon themselves the administration of baptism" (§20); also quoted on Heidelblog, "Calvin Against Baptism By The Laity", Apr 2015, https://heidelblog.net/2015/04/calvin-against-baptism-by-the-laity/ (the post is only the quotation). `[verified]`
   - R. Scott Clark, "On the Question of the Validity of Lay Baptism", Heidelblog, Aug 2024, https://heidelblog.net/2024/08/on-the-question-of-the-validity-of-lay-baptism/ — quotes the Genevan ordinance, "If midwives usurp the office of Baptism, they are to be reproved or chastised", and the Venerable Company, "The doctrine which claims that baptism is necessary for salvation is false." `[verified]`
   - Derek W. H. Thomas, "Blog 213: 4.14.17 – 4.15.22", Reformation21 (Blogging the Institutes) — lay baptism in emergencies "suggests that without it, all are lost" `[snippet]` (page body did not load; not counted).
   - `Frequency: 2 independent sources` (Calvin, Clark); 3 with the snippet.
3. **The other side answers it:** Catholic Answers Q&A, "Why Can Anyone Administer Baptism?", https://www.catholic.com/qa/why-can-anyone-administer-baptism `[snippet]` (not read; author and date unknown); also "Baptism in an Emergency", https://www.catholic.com/qa/baptism-in-an-emergency `[snippet]`.
4. **Official anchor:** WCF XXVII.4: baptism and the Supper, "neither of which may be dispensed by any but by a minister of the word, lawfully ordained" `[verified in catholic.md OB-3; opening re-read on opc.org]`; WCF XXVIII.2 ("by a minister of the gospel, lawfully called thereunto"). The objection is XXVII.4 applied to emergencies.
5. **Overlap:** same as OB-3.
6. **Verdict:** FAILS (frequency: 2 verified; answer only as snippet).

### DO-RL-6 · What remains after baptism is truly sin
1. **Objection:** Baptism removes the guilt of original sin, but concupiscence remaining in the baptized is truly sin, pardoned and not imputed; Trent's denial (V can. 5) contradicts Paul (Rom 7).
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - Martin Chemnitz, *Examen Concilii Tridentini* I (1566), on Session V `[memory]` — not read.
   - John Calvin, *Institutes* III.3.10–11 `[memory]` — not read today.
   - Perichoresis 20 (Dec 2020), article on post-Tridentine Catholic theologians and the Protestant doctrine of the remnants of sin, https://doaj.org/article/63c7c72872c246c5b6a516a930d5cf6e `[snippet]` (403; author unknown; academic, not polemic).
   - `Frequency: 0 independent sources` (verified).
3. **The other side answers it:** "Concupiscence", *Catholic Encyclopedia* (1912), https://www.catholic.com/encyclopedia/concupiscence `[snippet]` ("in baptism everything that is really sin is taken away").
4. **Official anchor:** Apology II.35–39 ("Baptism removes the guilt of original sin, although the material … i.e., concupiscence, remains"); WCF VI.5 ("both itself, and all the motions thereof, are truly and properly sin") `[verified in catholic.md OB-5; WCF VI.5 opening re-read on opc.org]`.
5. **Overlap:** same as OB-5.
6. **Verdict:** FAILS (frequency: none verified; searched too little to say it is rare in debate).

### DO-RL-7 · Penance is not a "second plank" after shipwreck: repentance is a return to baptism
1. **Objection:** Calling penance the second plank after the shipwreck of baptism treats baptism as something past and lost; the ship never breaks, and repentance is daily return to one's baptism.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - Edward H. Schroeder (Lutheran, Crossings), "Baptism and Confession", https://crossings.org/baptism-and-confession/ `[snippet]` (not read; date unknown).
   - R. C. Sproul, Ligonier 2023 (DO-RL-3) describes penance as "a second plank" in Trent `[verified]`, but as description inside the justification objection, not as an objection to the image.
   - John Calvin, *Institutes* IV.19.17 `[memory]`; Luther, *The Babylonian Captivity of the Church* (1520) `[memory]`.
   - `Frequency: 0 independent sources` (verified, as an objection in this form).
3. **The other side answers it:** [not found] among modern apologists; Henry VIII, *Assertio septem sacramentorum* (1521), answers Luther on this point `[snippet: evangelizationstation.com pamphlet]`.
4. **Official anchor:** Large Catechism IV.79–82: "Repentance, therefore, is nothing else than a return and approach to Baptism"; "the ship never breaks" `[verified in catholic.md OB-4]`.
5. **Overlap:** same as OB-4.
6. **Verdict:** FAILS (frequency; no modern Catholic apologetic answer found). Not rare in the 16th century; rare in today's debate as far as these searches show.

### DO-RL-8 · The added ceremonies (exorcism, chrism, salt, spittle, candle) are human inventions that obscure baptism
1. **Objection:** Christ instituted water and the Triune name; Rome's added exorcism, chrism, salt, spittle and tapers have no command or promise in Scripture and turn the sacrament into theatre.
2. **Where it is raised:**
   - John Calvin, *Institutes* IV.15.19 (URL in DO-RL-4) — "theatrical pomp, which dazzles the eyes of the simple"; "spittle and other follies". `[verified]`
   - R. Scott Clark, "The Reformed Rejection Of Alleged 'Adiaphora' In Worship In 1578", Heidelblog, Sep 2018, https://heidelblog.net/2018/09/the-reformed-rejection-of-alleged-adiaphora-in-worship-in-1578/ — lists consecrated water, chrism, salt, spittle, candles, exorcism `[snippet]` (not read).
   - Puritan Board thread "Is it Really Water?" (forum; not counted).
   - `Frequency: 1 independent source` (verified); 2 with the snippet.
3. **The other side answers it:** [not found] in modern apologetics; Thomas Aquinas, *ST* III q.71 a.2 defends exorcism before baptism `[snippet]` (a medieval theologian, not an answer to this objection).
4. **Official anchor:** partial. WCF XXI.1: God "may not be worshipped according to the imaginations and devices of men … or any other way not prescribed in the Holy Scripture" (OPC text) `[verified]`. No Westminster text names baptismal ceremonies; the objection follows only by applying XXI.1 to them. Lutherans do not raise it (Luther's 1526 *Taufbüchlein* kept an exorcism `[memory]`; the BoC treats such ceremonies as adiaphora, FC X `[memory]`).
5. **Overlap:** new.
6. **Verdict:** FAILS (frequency, answer; anchor only by inference).

## Notes for the coordinator
- **Result in one line:** the debate layer adds little that is new for Reformed and Lutherans. Their frequent objections are the official ones (OB-1, OB-2), which is expected: both traditions have detailed confessions on baptism. The two genuinely new candidates (DO-RL-3, DO-RL-8) are thin; DO-RL-4 is new mainly as the infant form of OB-1.
- **Lutherans are mostly allies on Baptism.** Modern Lutheran polemic against Rome on baptism is almost entirely about *ex opere operato* (DO-RL-1); on regeneration, necessity and infants they stand with the Catholic Church against Baptists (AC IX). This asymmetry is real, not a search gap, as far as these searches show.
- **Source quality:** the frequent Reformed sources are historic theologians (Calvin, Hodge, Warfield) and Ligonier; formal recorded Reformed–Catholic debates on baptism were not found (the recorded baptism debates surfaced involve Reformed Baptists such as Gavin Ortlund, who are sibling A's group). Counting historic theologians as "debate" is a call for Pedro; under a stricter "modern debate" reading DO-RL-4 drops to 0.
- **DO-RL-4 target problem:** sources attack the pre-2007 position (damnation or limbo for unbaptized infants). Under the neutrality rule, the page must not present limbo as Catholic doctrine; the objection would have to be restated against CEC 1257–1261.
- **Not written as blocks (not searched for lack of time):** the "indelible character" (Calvin and Chemnitz reject it as unbiblical `[memory]`; no Westminster or BoC text found that rejects it, WCF XXVIII.7 "but once to be administered" agrees with the practice, so likely `No anchor`); "Is Roman Catholic baptism valid?" (Hodge 1845 vs Thornwell; Ligonier Q&A https://learn.ligonier.org/qas/is-roman-catholic-baptism-valid `[snippet]`) is an internal Reformed question about recognising Catholic baptisms, not an objection to Catholic teaching; out of scope.
- **Search tool behaviour:** WebSearch returns model summaries that sometimes misattribute (e.g. it presented the Heidelblog 2015 post as Clark's words; it is a Calvin quotation). Every `[verified]` above was fetched directly; summaries alone were tagged `[snippet]`.
- **Tag caveat:** the Lutheran and some Westminster anchor quotations are taken from `catholic.md` (verified there 2026-10-17/18), not re-read today; only the OPC openings were re-read. The verifier should check the anchors against the editions named in `catholic.md`.
