## Conventions and source boundary

- **Column boundary:** the four families in the Baptist map, not a confession binding every Baptist. A = Particular/Reformed; B = General/Free Will; C = Southern Baptist/convention; D = bodies organized around principles. The owner's work order requires these strands to remain visible; it does not settle which the eventual screen presents.
- **A documents:** First London 1644, with the separately printed 1646 revision; Somerset 1656; Second London 1677/1689 (2LCF); Philadelphia 1742, whose additions are printed by McGlothlin; Baptist Catechism c. 1693/1695.
- **B documents:** Standard Confession 1660; Orthodox Creed 1678/9 (OC); northern Freewill Treatise 1834 with explicitly distinguished 1869 variants; NAFWB Treatise 2026. The northern historical text is not substituted for the present NAFWB text.
- **C documents:** New Hampshire in Brown's **1853** eighteen-article form (NH); Abstract of Principles 1858 (AP), an institutional standard; BF&M 1925, 1963 and 2000, the last with the 2023 amendment to VI. McGlothlin's NH presentation combines a base text and variant notes: quotations below explicitly reconstruct 1853 from those notes. It is not a scan of an 1833 original.
- **D documents:** BUGB Declaration of Principle, current official web text; BWA Beliefs Statement, supplementary federation testimony, whose member-body subscription status the map does not establish. Neither is silently treated as an SBC confession.
- **Regional controls:** the mapped UEBE Confession and Principles, CEBA 1987, CNBM doctrines/principles, CNBV distinctive doctrines, Paraguay's BF&M text, and the other mapped subscription/principles evidence are kept distinct from their English-language relatives. Similarity does not prove adoption. Coverage records acquisition limits rather than declaring a body silent from its relative's text.
- **Edition:** historical English quotations use W. J. McGlothlin, *Baptist Confessions of Faith* (Philadelphia: American Baptist Publication Society, 1911), with page and article. His historical introductions are editorial evidence, not confessional teaching. Page images: [Archive.org scan](https://archive.org/details/baptistconfessio00mcgl).
- **Spanish:** the map identifies no public-domain Spanish edition for these historical confessions. UEBE and circulating 1689 translations have no established reusable public-domain licence. Spanish renderings below are made from the original English and labelled **[traducción propia asistida por IA]**, unreviewed by Pedro. Official modern Spanish excerpts, when used, are labelled separately.
- **Tags:** `[verified: scan]` means that particular English wording was read on the printed page during drafting; it does not certify the dossier. `[unverified]` marks web transcriptions, OCR-only material, edition/licence details not independently checked, and translations. There is no verification, critic or apply pass.
- **Copyright:** historical English print is treated as public-domain material under the mapped edition boundary; current BF&M, NAFWB and contemporary declarations receive only short excerpts and official links. The 1963 US nonrenewal claim is not permission for worldwide full-text reuse.
- **Scripture:** only references supplied by the Baptist documents are listed. Adjacent references in a combined article are not automatically assigned to one clause. No independent biblical argument is added.

## Qué enseña

### A — Particular/Reformed: profession, sign and immersion

> “Those who do actually profess repentance towards God, faith in, and obedience, to our Lord Jesus, are the only proper subjects of this ordinance.”
>
> «Quienes profesan efectivamente arrepentimiento para con Dios, fe en nuestro Señor Jesús y obediencia a él son los únicos sujetos propios de esta ordenanza». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

2LCF 29.2, McGlothlin p. 270. English [verified: scan]. Confession: subordinate standard for bodies subscribing to it, not a universal Baptist decree.

> “a sign of his fellowship with him, in his death, and resurrection; of his being engrafted into him; of remission of sins”
>
> «un signo de su comunión con él en su muerte y resurrección; de su incorporación a él; de la remisión de los pecados». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

2LCF 29.1, p. 270, continuous excerpt after the sentence's beginning on p. 269. English [verified: scan].

> “Immersion, or dipping of the person in water, is necessary to the due administration of this ordinance.”
>
> «La inmersión de la persona en agua es necesaria para la debida administración de esta ordenanza». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

2LCF 29.4, p. 270. English [verified: scan]. Philadelphia's corresponding baptism chapter is not numbered 29 after its inserted singing chapter; cite by heading unless a Philadelphia print is checked.

### C — New Hampshire 1853: baptism and church relation

> “Christian Baptism is the immersion in water of a believer, into the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost”
>
> «El bautismo cristiano es la inmersión en agua de un creyente, en el nombre del Padre, del Hijo y del Espíritu Santo». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

NH XIV, McGlothlin p. 306, reconstructed using the explicit 1853 readings in notes 21–22 and omitting the editorial bracketed opening. English components [verified: scan]; reconstruction [unverified]. This is not labelled “1833, article XIV”.

> “with its effect in our death to sin and resurrection to a new life”
>
> «con su efecto en nuestra muerte al pecado y resurrección a una vida nueva». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

NH XIV, p. 306 n. 24, the **1853 replacement**, not the base reading “with its purifying power”. English [verified: scan].

### B — General Baptists: covenant and incorporation

> “a sign of our entrance into the covenant of grace, and ingrafting into Christ, and into the body of Christ, which is his church”
>
> «un signo de nuestra entrada en el pacto de gracia y de nuestra incorporación a Cristo y al cuerpo de Cristo, que es su Iglesia». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

OC XXVIII, McGlothlin p. 144. English [verified: scan]. The document itself also calls baptism a “holy sacrament” (p. 145); its vocabulary must not be replaced with a claim that every Baptist confession avoids that word.

> “those which do really profess repentance towards God, and faith in, and obedience to our Lord Jesus Christ, are the only proper subjects of this ordinance”
>
> «quienes profesan realmente arrepentimiento para con Dios, fe en nuestro Señor Jesucristo y obediencia a él son los únicos sujetos propios de esta ordenanza». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

OC XXVIII, p. 144. English [verified: scan]. Its further directions and the other mapped strands are kept separate below.

### C — Abstract and BF&M, with different authority scopes

> “Baptism is an ordinance of the Lord Jesus, obligatory upon every believer”
>
> «El bautismo es una ordenanza del Señor Jesús, obligatoria para todo creyente». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

AP XV, 1858 text on [SBTS's official page](https://www.sbts.edu/about/abstract/). English [unverified]: official transcription, not a nineteenth-century print check. Its continuation names immersion, the Trinity, fellowship with Christ's death and resurrection, remission of sins and new life; it requires baptism before church fellowship and the Supper. These are AP's own clauses, not an explanation of how baptism causes remission.

> “act of obedience” — BF&M 2000 VII, [official text](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/). [unverified]
>
> «acto de obediencia». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

The present SBC article places this phrase within its description of believers' immersion and its symbolism. Its full wording remains at the official link. BF&M 1925 XIII and 1963 VII are separate historical witnesses; their baptism/Supper articles are located in the 1925 *Annual*, printed pp. 73–74, and the 1963 *Annual*, p. 276. No modern version silently replaces either. [unverified]

### B — Standard Confession and Free Will witnesses

> “first to teach, or preach the Gospel […] and then to Baptise (that is in English to Dip)”
>
> «primero enseñar o predicar el Evangelio […] y después bautizar, es decir, sumergir». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

Standard Confession XI, McGlothlin p. 115. English [verified: scan]. The same article names repentance and faith as the profession of those baptized; its historical polemic is not a description of another communion's current doctrine.

> “This is the immersion of believers in water”
>
> «Esto es la inmersión de creyentes en agua». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

Northern Freewill Treatise 1834, church-ordinances §1, McGlothlin p. 326. English [unverified]: located in the print OCR. The separately printed 1869 revision places baptism at XVII.1 (p. 323 note). The 1834 and 1869 references are not interchangeable.

> “immersion of believers” — NAFWB *Treatise* 2026 II.XVIII.1, printed p. 15 (PDF p. 23), [official PDF](https://nafwb.org/site/wp-content/uploads/NAFWB-Treatise-2026.pdf). [unverified]
>
> «inmersión de creyentes». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

NAFWB's paragraph gives the trinitarian name and a series of things represented by baptism, including Christ's burial and resurrection and the believer's new life. The current document is cited directly and kept distinct from the northern historical Treatise. [unverified]

### A — Earlier confessions and catechesis

> “That Baptisme is an Ordinance of the new Testament, given by Christ, to be dispensed onely upon persons professing faith”
>
> «Que el bautismo es una ordenanza del Nuevo Testamento dada por Cristo, que ha de administrarse sólo a personas que profesan fe». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

First London 1644 XXXIX, McGlothlin p. 185; English [verified: scan]. The article's notes identify later revisions; this quotation preserves the 1644 “onely” rather than silently substituting a later reading.

Somerset 1656 XXIV, pp. 208–209, likewise addresses repentance, faith and immersion. Its literal explanation belongs in Por qué below. The Baptist Catechism's separately identified 1695 e-text addresses baptism at Q97–101; the circulating “Keach” e-text has different numbering. Its wording is not promoted to a scan-checked quotation. [unverified]

## Por qué

### First London: the sign and what it signifies

> “it being a signe, must answer the thing signified, which are these: first, the washing the whole soule in the blood of Christ”
>
> «al ser un signo, debe corresponder a lo significado, a saber: primero, el lavamiento de toda el alma en la sangre de Cristo». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

First London 1644 XL, McGlothlin p. 185; English [verified: scan]. Its own continuation names participation in Christ's death, burial and resurrection, then the resurrection of the saints. This is the confession's stated rationale, not an argument supplied by the dossier.

### Somerset: what immersion represents

> “to signify and represent a washing away of sin”
>
> «para significar y representar el lavamiento del pecado». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

Somerset XXIV, McGlothlin p. 208, OCR locator; English [unverified]. The article associates its directions with Acts 2:38; 8:12,37–38; Romans 6:3–4; Colossians 2:12; Acts 8:16; Matthew 28:19. No additional causal explanation is supplied here.

### The catechism: efficacy as its own question

> “only by the Blessing of Christ, and the working of the Spirit in those that by Faith received them”
>
> «sólo por la bendición de Cristo y la obra del Espíritu en quienes los reciben por la fe». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

Baptist Catechism, 1695-labelled transcription, Q96, answer about baptism and the Supper. English [unverified]: original-language e-text only; a print witness was not collated in this drafting pass. The catechism's own proofs are 1 Peter 3:21; Matthew 3:11; 1 Corinthians 3:6–7; 12:13. Its anomalous transcription at Q100 is not quoted.

### Limits of the other formulations

2LCF 29 and OC XXVIII supply sign-language and scriptural references; AP XV supplies its sign-language; NH XIV supplies its emblem-language; BF&M VII and NAFWB XVIII supply their stated symbolism. These clauses are the documents' explanations. No further account of sacramental causality, an infant's faith or the fate of an unbaptized child is inferred from their silence.
