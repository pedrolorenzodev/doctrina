### B-02 — NH XIV definition and 1853 variants: WRONG (one omitted word)

Visually read McGlothlin (1911) p. 306, including notes 21–24, and p. 301's editorial convention. [Page 306](https://archive.org/details/baptistconfessio00mcgl/page/306/mode/1up), [page 301](https://archive.org/details/baptistconfessio00mcgl/page/301/mode/1up). The draft correctly reconstructs note 21's “immersion in water of a believer, into” and note 22's “Holy Ghost”. However, the draft's “Father, Son, and Holy Ghost” drops the bracketed **[and]** after “Father”. P. 301 says “Additions made in 1853 are enclosed in brackets”. Correct reconstructed clause: “Christian Baptism is the immersion in water of a believer, into the name of the Father [and] Son, and Holy Ghost”. Retain the brackets and explain their editorial function; Spanish becomes “del Padre [y] del Hijo y del Espíritu Santo”. The bracketed opening [We believe] is deliberately omitted before the independently quoted clause, not misattributed to an original Brown facsimile.

### B-03 — NH XIV meaning and church relation: CONFIRMED

Same image p. 306. Matched note 24: “with its effect in our death to sin and resurrection to a new life”. The draft's second quotation is exact. Context: solemn emblem of faith in the crucified, buried and risen Saviour; baptism precedes church relation and the Supper. Note 23 changes “a” to “the” in 1853, so any newly expanded reconstructed passage must use “the”. Spanish checked directly against English; no claim about sacramental causality or infant salvation is warranted by this article. No verse apparatus is printed here.
