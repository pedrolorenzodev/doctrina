## Conventions and source boundary

- **Column boundary:** the four families in the Baptist map, not a confession binding every Baptist. A = Particular/Reformed; B = General/Free Will; C = Southern Baptist/convention; D = bodies organized around principles. The owner's work order requires these strands to remain visible; it does not settle which the eventual screen presents.
- **A documents:** First London 1644, with the separately printed 1646 revision; Somerset 1656; Second London 1677/1689 (2LCF); Philadelphia 1742, whose additions are printed by McGlothlin; Baptist Catechism c. 1693/1695.
- **B documents:** Standard Confession 1660; Orthodox Creed 1678/9 (OC); northern Freewill Treatise 1834 with explicitly distinguished 1869 variants; NAFWB Treatise 2026. The northern historical text is not substituted for the present NAFWB text.
- **C documents:** New Hampshire in Brown's **1853** eighteen-article form (NH); Abstract of Principles 1858 (AP), an institutional standard; BF&M 1925, 1963 and 2000, the last with the 2023 amendment to VI. McGlothlin's NH presentation combines a base text and variant notes: quotations below explicitly reconstruct 1853 from those notes. It is not a scan of an 1833 original.
- **D documents:** BUGB Declaration of Principle, current official web text; BWA Beliefs Statement, supplementary federation testimony, whose member-body subscription status the map does not establish. Neither is silently treated as an SBC confession.
- **Regional controls:** the mapped UEBE Confession and Principles, CEBA 1987, CNBM doctrines/principles, CNBV distinctive doctrines, Paraguay's BF&M text, and the other mapped subscription/principles evidence are kept distinct from their English-language relatives. Similarity does not prove adoption. Coverage records acquisition limits rather than declaring a body silent from its relative's text.
- **Edition:** historical English quotations use W. J. McGlothlin, *Baptist Confessions of Faith* (Philadelphia: American Baptist Publication Society, 1911), with page and article. His historical introductions are editorial evidence, not confessional teaching. Page images: [Archive.org scan](https://archive.org/details/baptistconfessio00mcgl).
- **Spanish:** the map identifies no public-domain Spanish edition for these historical confessions. UEBE and circulating 1689 translations have no established reusable public-domain licence. Spanish renderings below are made from the original English and labelled **[traducción propia asistida por IA]**, unreviewed by Pedro. Official modern Spanish excerpts, when used, are labelled separately.
- **Tags:** `[verified: scan]` means that particular English wording was read on the printed page during drafting; it does not certify the dossier. `[unverified]` marks web transcriptions, OCR-only material, edition/licence details not independently checked, and translations. There is no verification, critic or apply pass.
- **Copyright:** historical English print is treated as public-domain material under the mapped edition boundary; current BF&M, NAFWB and contemporary declarations receive only short excerpts and official links. The 1963 US nonrenewal claim is not permission for worldwide full-text reuse.
- **Scripture:** only references supplied by the Baptist documents are listed. Adjacent references in a combined article are not automatically assigned to one clause. No independent biblical argument is added.

## Qué enseña

### A — 2LCF's affirmative starting point: Christ heads the church

> “The Lord Jesus Christ is the Head of the Church, in whom by the appointment of the Father, all power for the calling, institution, order, or Government of the Church, is invested in a supream & soveraigne manner”
>
> «El Señor Jesucristo es la cabeza de la Iglesia; en él, por designación del Padre, está investido de manera suprema y soberana todo poder para el llamamiento, la institución, el orden o el gobierno de la Iglesia». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

2LCF **26.4**, McGlothlin **p. 265**, scan leaf 279 / zero-based image 278. English [verified: scan]. This clause, rather than its following rejection, opens the dossier. It is the confession of the Particular/Reformed strand, not an unqualified all-Baptist definition.

### The same paragraph's express judgment on the pope

> “neither can the Pope of Rome in any sense be head thereof, but is that Antichrist, that Man of sin, and Son of perdition, that exalteth himself in the Church against Christ, and all that is called God; whom the Lord shall destroy with the brightness of his coming.”
>
> «Tampoco puede el papa de Roma ser en ningún sentido su cabeza, sino que es aquel Anticristo, aquel hombre de pecado e hijo de perdición, que se exalta en la Iglesia contra Cristo y contra todo lo que se llama Dios; a quien el Señor destruirá con el resplandor de su venida». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

2LCF 26.4, same printed p. 265. English [verified: scan]. The full severe clause is retained rather than shortened to a generic rejection of supremacy.

**Document/status boundary:** this is the historical London confession's wording as reprinted in 1911. The CBA's [full-subscription page](https://www.cba1689.com/full-subscription) identifies that confession as its doctrinal standard and speaks of adopting it as a whole [unverified, official web text]. No separate current CBA explanation of **26.4's papal identification** was collated in this pass. This evidence does not establish that every Baptist body, or every body using some London material, makes that identification.

### B — Orthodox Creed's affirmative headship and explicit papal limits

> “Christ, the only head thereof”
>
> «Cristo, su única cabeza». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

OC XXIX, McGlothlin p. 145. English [verified: scan]. The full article's subject is the one holy catholic church, composed of the elect; this is a General Baptist historical witness, not an SBC article.

> “And no decrees of popes, or councils, or writings of any person whatsoever, are of equal authority with the sacred scriptures.”
>
> «Y ningún decreto de papas o concilios, ni escrito de persona alguna, tiene igual autoridad que las Sagradas Escrituras». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

OC XXXVII, McGlothlin p. 152. English [verified: scan]. This directly addresses papal decrees; it was found in the mapped document even though the map's papacy row points only to 2LCF 26.4.

OC XXVIII also explicitly names the pope in its denial of power to alter baptism (p. 145). Its object is:

> “to alter, or change, this ordinance of Christ”
>
> «alterar o cambiar esta ordenanza de Cristo». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

OC XXVIII, p. 145; excerpt from that **negative** clause, not an assertion granting the power. English [verified: scan]. The paragraph contains a historical portrayal of Roman baptismal doctrine; that portrayal is not represented here as another church's current teaching.

**Historical status:** the map places OC in the historic General Baptist tier, not as the present NAFWB confession. No modern Baptist body's adoption of the OC pope clauses is established here. [unverified; mapped adoption boundary]

## Por qué

### 2LCF: invested authority and its printed proof apparatus

The source itself gives the affirmative reason in the first sentence of 26.4: the Father's appointment invests Christ with the church's governing power. Its following sentence makes its historical identification of the pope. Both sentences are quoted above; no further argument about Peter, Rome or an institutional succession is supplied by the drafter.

> “by the appointment of the Father”
>
> «por designación del Padre». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

2LCF 26.4, p. 265. English [verified: scan]. The printed proof references for the two parts differ and are recorded in La Escritura below.

### OC: the relative authority of decrees and Scripture

> “the best interpreter of itself, and is sole judge in controversy”
>
> «su mejor intérprete, y es el único juez en la controversia». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

OC XXXVII, p. 152, referring to Holy Scripture. English [verified: scan]. This precedes the express comparison with papal and conciliar decrees. The excerpt reports OC's rationale; it is not a new argument composed for Baptists.

### What the searched texts do not develop

**sparse:** no sustained chapter on Petrine succession, a juridical definition of universal Roman jurisdiction or the later Roman definitions of papal infallibility was located in the mapped texts searched. The positive headship and scriptural-authority clauses remain those clauses; they are not expanded into answers about Vatican I or later Catholic formulations.

**Cause:** only 2LCF and the newly located OC passages explicitly name the pope among the central doctrinal documents. The other witnesses supply church/Scripture teaching without that specific exposition. Bounds and edition limits are recorded below; the finding is not “Baptists have never addressed the papacy”.

## Cómo se vive

**silent for a specifically papal practice:** no rite, observance or daily practice directed specifically to accepting/rejecting papal jurisdiction is prescribed in the bounded passages searched. Related polity directions are given only under their actual subjects:

> “this power is given to every particular Congregation, and not one particular person, either member or Officer, but the whole”
>
> «este poder se da a cada congregación particular, y no a una persona particular, sea miembro u oficial, sino al conjunto». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

First London 1644 XLII, McGlothlin p. 186, about receiving and excommunicating members. English [verified: scan]. It does not name the pope and is not represented as an explicit answer about Rome.

> “it being of divine authority”
>
> «siendo de autoridad divina». **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** [unverified]

OC XXXIX, p. 154, about the general assembly of church representatives. English [verified: scan]. Its powers of hearing, determining and excommunicating qualify any generic claim that all these historical Baptist documents vest every external matter solely in one congregation.

**map gap for observed practice:** no empirical evidence was collected about how particular Baptists speak of a present pope, engage with Roman Catholics or act in ecumenical settings. No behavioral conclusion is inferred from a seventeenth-century clause.
