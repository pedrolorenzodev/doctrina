## Conventions and source boundary

- **Column boundary:** Eastern Orthodox churches in the map, not Oriental Orthodoxy. The Orthodox Eucharist dossier fixes the shared boundary: received ecumenical councils and canonical corpus; later councils with their reception distinctions; approved seventeenth-century confessions with approval and Orthodox critique; Philaret's Russian synodal catechism.
- **Primacy witnesses:** Constantinople I canon 3, Chalcedon canon 28, Trullo canon 36; Constantinople III's acts concerning Honorius; 879–880; the rejection of Florence in 1484; Dositheus X,XII; Mogila I.82–85; Philaret Q.270–282; the 1848 and 1895 patriarchal-synodal encyclicals. Their authority grades and scope are not flattened.
- **Greek and Slavic/Russian:** the Greek canonical/confessional witness is labelled separately from Philaret's Russian catechesis. Contemporary patriarchal disagreements are not resolved by inventing a single jurisdictional formula.
- **Dositheus recension:** the Greek text printed by Schaff X includes the mortal-head clause that the map associates with Dositheus's 1690 revision. The map's sources disagree about the revision's place/edition numbering (`traditions/orthodox.md` Tier 3; verification C6). Quotations below are of Schaff's actual Greek print, not an assertion that every clause is the unchanged wording signed in 1672 [unverified: recension genealogy not independently collated].
- **Other confession controls:** Mogila's approved Greek revision, not a substitute Latin draft or an English paraphrase; Jeremiah's mapped Answers, not the private confessions of Gennadius/Kritopoulos; Lucaris is not an adopted Orthodox alternative.
- **Owner boundary:** Crete 2016 informs the map and its reception, but its own teaching is not quoted on the doctrine page (DECISIONS 2026-10-15). ROC 2020 is mentioned only. Local statutes, commissions and private authors do not become confessions.
- **Prints:** Schaff II, Dositheus pp. 410–417 and Philaret pp. 488–490, [scan](https://archive.org/details/creedschristendo02scha). Archive catalogue year 1877 and its copyright note's 1896 disagree; this copy's title-page year has not been collated [unverified]. Printed running heads govern citations.
- **Spanish:** no reusable PD Spanish confession edition is identified by the mapped searches. Spanish is translated directly from the Greek print and labelled **[traducción propia asistida por IA]**, unreviewed by Pedro [unverified]. English Philaret is retained without a Spanish translation through English.
- **Tags:** `[verified: scan]` applies only to quoted wording read in the printed image during drafting. Recension history, translations, web-only controls and uncollated approval details remain `[unverified]`. This is no verification, critic or apply pass.
- **Fathers:** ancient authors are witnesses received inside a conciliar/confessional text; no Father is assigned to the modern Orthodox tradition. Historical assertions made by a confession are attributed to it, not certified by the site.
- **Reuse:** historical witnesses follow the map's PD boundary; no fresh international rights audit [unverified]. Modern criticism receives only short excerpts and a link.

## Qué enseña

### Definition: Christ heads the Church

**Philaret Q.272**, Schaff II p. 488, Blackmore English, the Pauline predicate quoted by the catechism:

> is the Head of the Church, and he is the Saviour of the Body [verified: scan]

The named subject is Jesus Christ; the printed reference is Ephesians 5:23. The answer concerns membership of his body and salvation. This positive opening is not framed as an answer to Rome's claims.

### QE-1 · The same Christ governs through the Church's ministers

**Dositheus X**, Greek, Schaff II pp. 410–411:

> αὐτὸς ὁ κύριος ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦς Χριστός ἐστι κεφαλὴ καὶ αὐτὸς τοὺς οἴακας ἔχων ἐν τῇ τῆς ἐκκλησίας κυβερνήσει πηδαλιουχεῖ διὰ τῶν ἁγίων πατέρων. [verified: scan]

> «El mismo Señor Jesucristo es cabeza y él mismo, teniendo el timón del gobierno de la Iglesia, la dirige por medio de los santos padres». [traducción propia asistida por IA] [unverified]

The source's first-person possessive is omitted in this Spanish excerpt; the doctrinal predicate and named subject are retained. The following sentence names bishops as leaders and shepherds of the particular churches, referring their activity to Christ.

### QE-2 · Apostolic ministry and succession

**Philaret Q.274**, p. 489:

> Because she has from the Apostles, without break or change, both her doctrine and the succession of the gifts of the Holy Ghost, through the laying on of consecrated hands. [verified: scan]

The question asks why the Church is called Apostolic. Its teaching and succession clauses are both retained; the dossier does not reduce apostolicity to a historical office list alone.

### QE-3 · The hierarchy's origin

**Philaret Q.277**, p. 490:

> From Jesus Christ himself, and from the descent of the Holy Ghost on the Apostles; from which time it is continued, in unbroken succession, through the laying on of hands, in the Sacrament of Orders. [verified: scan]

Q.276 names the ecclesiastical hierarchy as the institution preserving the succession of apostolic ministry; Q.277 then prints Ephesians 4:11–12.

### QE-4 · Authority extending over the whole Church

**Philaret Q.278**, p. 490, answer to which hierarchical authority can act over the whole Catholic Church:

> An œcumenical Council. [verified: scan]

**Philaret Q.279**, same page, on the chief divisions of the Church:

> Under the Orthodox Patriarchs and the Most Holy Synod. [verified: scan]

The latter is the catechism's dated synodal-era Russian formulation. It is not silently updated into an account of every present governing institution.

### QE-5 · The mortal-head clause in the Greek print

**Dositheus X**, Greek, p. 410:

> ἐπειδὴ θνητὸς ἄνθρωπος καθόλου καὶ ἀΐδιος κεφαλὴ εἶναι οὐ δύναται [verified: scan]

> «Porque un hombre mortal no puede ser cabeza universal y perpetua». [traducción propia asistida por IA] [unverified]

**Status and recension:** this is the clause read in Schaff's Greek witness, associated in the map with the 1690 revision. No new pan-Orthodox adoption finding is claimed; it is not quietly labelled the unaltered 1672 signing text. The source's Christ-head predicate is given above.

### QE-6 · Canonical primacy is a separate positive layer

The map's conciliar loci concern the ordering and privileges of sees: Constantinople I 3, Chalcedon 28 and Trullo 36. They must remain beside, rather than be replaced by, the confession's statement about Christ. Their received rank is distinct from an isolated modern theologian's account (`traditions/orthodox.md` §§4,8) [unverified: map control until print excerpts below].

### QE-7 · The Church's preservation

**Philaret Q.271**, p. 488, the conclusion of its promise-based answer:

> she shall never apostatize from the faith, nor sin against the truth of the faith, or fall into error. [verified: scan]

The subject is the Church. The answer cites promises and the Eastern patriarchs' article 12; it is not an independent statement that every bishop or local synod is incapable of error.

### QE-8 · The order of sees in the received canons

**Constantinople I, canon 3**, Percival, *Seven Ecumenical Councils*, NPNF second series XIV (1900), p. 178, English print:

> The Bishop of Constantinople, however, shall have the prerogative of honour after the Bishop of Rome; because Constantinople is New Rome. [verified: scan]

**Trullo, canon 36**, same edition p. 382, excerpt after its opening renewal of the preceding councils:

> the see of Constantinople shall have equal privileges with the see of Old Rome, and shall be highly regarded in ecclesiastical matters as that is, and shall be second after it. After Constantinople shall be ranked the See of Alexandria, then that of Antioch, and afterwards the See of Jerusalem. [verified: scan]

These are the canons' English print witnesses, not Spanish translated from English. “After”, equal privileges and the order of the sees remain visible together. The editor's notes and epitomes on the same pages are not quoted as the council's text.

## Por qué

### PQ-1 · Christ's continuing government

Dositheus X, pp. 410–412, attributes the Church's government to Christ acting through the bishops and invokes his presence until the end. The Greek Christ-head sentence is quoted above. The text does not infer that episcopal ministry disappears because Christ is head.

**Dositheus X**, Greek, p. 412:

> Ὅτι δὲ ἀλληλοδιαδόχως τὸ τῆς ἐπισκοπῆς μέγα μυστήριον καὶ ἀξίωμα δίδοται μέχρις ἡμῶν, φανερόν. [verified: scan]

> «Es manifiesto que el gran misterio y dignidad del episcopado se transmite por sucesión hasta nosotros». [traducción propia asistida por IA] [unverified]

The source goes on to its own account of Christ's promise, episcopal ministry and sacraments. This dossier does not independently certify the historical succession claims made by the confession.

### PQ-2 · The Church built on apostles and prophets

**Philaret Q.274**, Schaff II p. 489, the scriptural quotation in its answer:

> and are built on the foundation of the Apostles and Prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner-stone. [verified: scan]

The printed reference is Ephesians 2:19–20. The sentence is quoted as the catechism's reason for apostolicity; no separate argument about Peter is added.

### PQ-3 · The ministry for edifying Christ's body

**Philaret Q.277**, p. 490, the end of its printed Ephesians 4:11–12 citation:

> for the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the Body of Christ. [verified: scan]

The origin and succession answer is quoted under QE-3. It is not recast as an argument against ministry itself.

### PQ-4 · The canon's stated reason for privileges

**Chalcedon, canon 28**, Percival 1900 p. 287:

> For the Fathers rightly granted privileges to the throne of old Rome, because it was the royal city. [verified: scan]

The same canon continues that the 150 bishops gave equal privileges to New Rome for the corresponding reason, then states rank after Old Rome and specifies the Pontic, Asian and Thracian ordinations. These are the canon's own reasons and jurisdictional language, not the drafter's theory of what all primacy must mean.

The whole canon was read on its printed page; the adjacent editorial explanations by Bright, Van Espen and the epitome do not become its definition. No Catholic/Orthodox debate layer is appended.

### PQ-5 · The confession's historical witnesses

Dositheus X, p. 413, names succession witnesses for Rome, Antioch, Alexandria, Constantinople and Jerusalem, and cites Tertullian and Eusebius. These are the confession's attributed historical reasons; the ancient writers are not assigned modern confessional membership and the details have not had an independent historical verification pass.

Mogila I.85 is a foundation-of-the-Church locus. Verification F2 explicitly did not establish it as a quotation about the pope. The dossier does not turn “Christ is the foundation” into a papal denial that the source does not state [unverified: mapped locator, Greek not extracted].

## Cómo se vive

### Local ministry and synodal appointment

Dositheus X, pp. 413–414, distinguishes ordination of a priest by a bishop from a bishop's ordination by two or three bishops, then places election within the appropriate synod. The continuation on p. 414 describes the laying on of bishops' hands and invocation of the Holy Spirit [verified: scan, passage read].

This is the confession's normative description. It is not an independent legal description of each Orthodox church's current election statute.

### Distinct episcopal service

**Dositheus X**, Greek, p. 414:

> Καὶ ὁ μὲν ἱερεὺς εἰς ἑαυτὸν σώζει τὴν, ἣν εἴληφε, ἐξουσίαν καὶ χάριν τῆς ἱερωσύνης, ὁ ἐπίσκοπος δὲ καὶ ἑτέροις μεταδίδωσι. [verified: scan]

> «El sacerdote conserva en sí la potestad y gracia del sacerdocio que ha recibido; el obispo también la comunica a otros». [traducción propia asistida por IA] [unverified]

The following priestly list includes baptism, anointing, the unbloody sacrifice, communion, marriage and prayer; p. 415 distinguishes episcopal ordinations and teaching. The dossier does not infer the validity of another church's ministry from this account.

### Where obedience is learned

**Philaret Q.282**, Schaff II p. 490:

> This may be learned from holy Scripture, from the canons of the holy Apostles, the holy œcumenical and provincial Councils, and the holy Fathers, and from the books of Ecclesiastical Rules and Rubrics. [verified: scan]

Q.280 names metropolitans, archbishops and bishops for smaller provinces and cities. Q.281 describes the rank of the Russian Holy Synod in its own historical institutional setting. Neither answer is silently presented as a current worldwide organization chart.

### The canons' specific scope

Chalcedon 28, Percival p. 287, specifies the ordination of metropolitans in the Pontic, Asian and Thracian dioceses, and provincial bishops by their own metropolitan with the province's bishops. Trullo 36, p. 382, renews earlier enactments and gives the see order quoted above [verified: scan, text read].

The quoted texts do not become an uncited decision by this dossier about every present appeal, diaspora territory or inter-patriarchal dispute. The Greek/Slavic categories stay visible without a modern synthesis imposed on them.

### Patriarchal encyclicals and remaining extraction limits

The map locates the 1848 encyclical §§4,12–17 and 1895 §§XV–XVIII, with verification D3 correcting its primacy locators. Their scope is patriarchal-synodal, not a new ecumenical council. The 1848 Greek original and 1895 Greek/English print have not been read in this drafting pass [unverified].

No contemporary reconciliation, adoption or intercommunion policy is invented from those historical replies. Their scriptural and historical arguments remain an explicit sparse layer rather than being reconstructed from memory.

## Grado de autoridad

### Councils before commentary

Constantinople I 3 and Chalcedon 28 are received conciliar canons in the Orthodox map; Trullo 36 is part of its received canonical corpus. Their text is separate from later canonical interpretations, epitomes and Percival's editorial notes. No note by a Western editor is offered as Orthodox conciliar teaching.

The label “received” follows `traditions/orthodox.md` §§2–4 and the map's verification, not a new survey of adoption by every contemporary jurisdiction [unverified: reception scope not re-audited].

### Confession, recension and critique

Dositheus's confession belongs to Jerusalem 1672, with later textual history recorded in the map. The specific mortal-head clause is quoted from Schaff's Greek and carries its 1690-revision caveat. Mogila's received Greek revision has the patriarchal approval act of 11 March 1643, qualified to the text in that language (`verification-orthodox.md` F2) [unverified: act not re-read].

Krivoshein's 1961 [Orthodox critique, §VI](https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Vasilij_Krivoshein/simvolicheskie-teksty-v-pravoslavnoj-tserkvi/) is retained beside approval, as the map requires. It discusses the authority and Western influence of the symbolic texts, not a fresh synodal repeal. The host's editorial introduction and Krivoshein's own article are distinct [unverified: web witness fetched once for the four dossiers].

### Russian catechesis and later acts

Philaret's 1839 synodal catechism speaks within the Russian catechetical setting, with Schaff's editorial question numbers. Its historical “Most Holy Synod” is not a replacement name for all Orthodox patriarchates today (`verification-orthodox.md` F3) [unverified: approval/title not re-read].

The 1848/1895 encyclicals retain their named signatories and patriarchal-synodal grade. The map's disagreement over numbering 879–880 as an additional ecumenical council is not adjudicated here; its reception is not collapsed into the closed list of seven.
