## Objections heard

### P-1 — “Jesús es la roca; la roca es la confesión de Pedro, no Pedro mismo” — PASSES

**Popular wording.** The recurring Spanish form is: “Jesús es esa piedra”; “la piedra es la confesión de Pedro”; or “la piedra de Mateo 16:18 es Cristo”. It often adds that *petros* and *petra* are different and that Matthew 18 and John 20 give the authority to all apostles.

**Independent people.** 3.

1. **Fabio Ubierna** — Protestant evangelical pastor, described by the host as Arminian. “Está claro que Jesús no se refiere a Pedro como la piedra” ([7:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V7_cgWWKiyw&t=433s)); at 14:31–15:18 he says Christ is the only foundation and rock.
2. **Jesús Messi** — says “nosotros los cristianos que no somos católicos” at 48:36; no office stated. “Yo creo que Jesús es esa piedra” ([13:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N_yHoFcuVC8&t=787s)); he also says “hay una sola piedra y ese es nuestro Señor Jesucristo” at 13:26–13:44.
3. **César Vidal** — presented by the Catholic host as a Protestant scholar; affiliation not independently verified. In the rebroadcast clip: Augustine “afirma claramente que la piedra de Mateo 16:18 es Cristo” ([5:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RfQ11RZY3J4&t=300s)). The clip is counted once, not as a new upload and an original.

**Catholic answers heard.** Luis Bazalar argues from the Aramaic *Kepha*, Matthew 16:18–19, Isaiah 22, Luke 22 and John 21; Rodrigo Calvo argues that *Kepha* and the contemporary Greek uses identify Peter as the rock; Católicos por Convicción answers Vidal with Protestant exegetes who identify Peter as the referent while explicitly noting that this textual point alone does not establish the papacy (16:17–16:48).

**Official Catholic answer.** The Catechism joins person and confession: “Pedro, a causa de la fe confesada por él, será la roca inquebrantable de la Iglesia” (CCC 552). *Lumen gentium* 22 says that Christ established Simon alone “como roca y portador de las llaves”, while the apostolic college united to its head also receives the power to bind and loose. The CDF says Peter, despite human weakness, “fue constituido expresamente por Cristo en el primer lugar entre los Doce” and “es la roca sobre la que Cristo edificará su Iglesia” (*El primado del Sucesor de Pedro*, §3). [CCC 552–553](https://www.vatican.va/archive/catechism_sp/p122a3p3_sp.html); [*Lumen gentium* 22](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_sp.html); [CDF §3](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19981031_primato-successore-pietro_sp.html).

**Objecting-tradition anchor.** The Lutheran *Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope* ¶25: “the Church is not built upon the authority of man, but upon the ministry of the confession which Peter made”; ¶23 says the keys are given alike to all apostles. The Orthodox patriarchal encyclical of 1848 §11 reads the rock as Peter's confession and John 21 as restoration after his denial. [Treatise, edition and paragraphs](https://bookofconcord.org/power-and-primacy/treatise-compiled-at-smalcald/). The dossier quotes the 1921 *Concordia Triglotta* edition and records the 1848 edition caveat.

**Overlap with the official layer.** Exact overlap with `papacy/catholic.md` OB-2.

**Verdict.** **PASSES.** Three independent direct voices, multiple Catholic answers, an explicit Lutheran and Orthodox official anchor, and a target the Catholic Church teaches today.

### P-2 — “Ningún hombre es infalible; sólo Cristo o la Escritura son infalibles” — PASSES

**Popular wording.** The debate form collapses personal sin, ordinary mistakes and *ex cathedra* definition into one challenge: every biblical leader failed, corrupt popes existed, and Scripture never says a pope is infallible.

**Independent people.** 3.

1. **Fabio Ubierna** — Protestant evangelical pastor. “Yo no creo que haya ningún hombre que sea infalible” ([53:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V7_cgWWKiyw&t=3194s)); corrupt popes become his concrete test at 1:16:06–1:19:28.
2. **Fito** — evangelical lay believer, no church office. “Ninguno de ellos era infalible. Por lo tanto [...] el Papa tampoco”; “El único infalible es Jesucristo” ([7:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EM4GDBJAa6A&t=468s)). He invokes the Borgias at 15:51–17:24.
3. **Edgar Pacheco** — Methodist. Only Scripture is infallible and 2 Timothy 3:16 says nothing of bishops, councils or popes ([9:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J5DGTTVy8Gs&t=585s)); he asks where papal infallibility appears in Scripture or the early Church at 13:36–14:25.

**Catholic answers heard.** Luis Bazalar and Valentín distinguish the pope's morality from protected doctrinal definition. Santiago Alarcón argues from the Church's indefectibility and the universal pastor. These answers establish that the Catholic side heard and answered the objection, but Valentín's “conforme [a] toda la tradición” formula and Alarcón's argument omit the complete definition's conditions.

**Official Catholic answer.** *Pastor aeternus* ch. 4 limits the charism to the Roman Pontiff when, acting as pastor and teacher of all Christians, by his supreme apostolic authority, he defines a doctrine of faith or morals to be held by the whole Church; it also says the Spirit was not promised to disclose new doctrine. *Lumen gentium* 25 says he does not pronounce “como persona privada” and remains bound to Revelation. CIC 749 §3 says: “Ninguna doctrina se considera definida infaliblemente si no consta así de modo manifiesto.” The CDF separately acknowledges “errores humanos y faltas, incluso graves” in papal history (§15). [*Pastor aeternus* ch. 4, official Latin](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-ix/la/documents/constitutio-dogmatica-pastor-aeternus-18-iulii-1870.html); [*Lumen gentium* 25](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_sp.html); [CDF §15](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19981031_primato-successore-pietro_sp.html).

**Objecting-tradition anchor.** Westminster I.10 makes “the Holy Spirit speaking in the scripture” the supreme judge of controversies; the Formula of Concord, Epitome, Rule and Norm 1 calls the prophetic and apostolic Scriptures “the sole rule and standard” by which teachers and dogmas are judged. The Orthodox patriarchal encyclical of 1895 §XVIII says that, apart from Christ, it knows no one infallible on earth, and cites Peter, Liberius, Zosimus, Vigilius and Honorius. [Formula of Concord, Rule and Norm](https://bookofconcord.org/epitome/rule-and-norm/); Westminster edition: *The Confession of Faith* (Edinburgh: Johnstone, Hunter & Co., 1894), ch. I.10, p. 21, [scan](https://archive.org/details/confessionoffaith00chur/page/20/mode/2up).

**Overlap with the official layer.** Exact overlap with OB-6. The corrupt-popes form overlaps its Orthodox historical examples but has only two independent debate voices, so it remains part of P-2 rather than a separate pass.

**Quality flag.** Do not use the debaters' compressed Catholic definitions as doctrine. The primary texts correct them. The official dossier still marks an apologetic answer to the individual Liberius/Zosimus/Vigilius/Honorius cases as pending.

**Verdict.** **PASSES.** Three independent voices, direct Catholic responses, three allowed official anchors, and a present Catholic dogma.

### P-3 — “El papado no se desprende de Mateo 16 ni aparece como tal en la Escritura o la Iglesia primitiva” — PASSES

**Popular wording.** The recurring form moves from exegesis to institution: even if Peter has a role, the debates ask where Scripture establishes a perpetual Roman office, how its succession is demonstrated, and why early evidence should imply universal jurisdiction rather than honor or later historical growth.

**Independent people.** 3.

1. **Fabio Ubierna** — Protestant evangelical pastor. “Ni tú ni nadie pueden demostrar la sucesión apostólica” ([31:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V7_cgWWKiyw&t=1909s)); “Nadie puede confirmar que Pedro estuvo en Roma” ([36:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V7_cgWWKiyw&t=2194s)); at 54:54–55:51 he describes Rome's prominence as historical and honorific.
2. **Edgar Pacheco** — Methodist. He asks how papal infallibility was transmitted or developed if it is absent from Scripture and the early Church ([13:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J5DGTTVy8Gs&t=816s)); at 38:40–43:39 he challenges the traceability of the claimed oral tradition.
3. **César Vidal** — presented as Protestant; independently unverified. He says that neither Protestant theologians nor Latin churches before the fourth century read Matthew 16 as Rome does, and that such a reading is impossible linguistically, historically and patristically ([4:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RfQ11RZY3J4&t=267s)). This is counted from the direct recorded clip once.

**Catholic answers heard.** Luis Bazalar argues from Matthew 16, Luke 22 and John 21 and invokes Peter's Roman presence through “Babylon” and 1 Clement. Santiago Alarcón argues that Christ gave the Church indefectibility and a universal pastor. Católicos por Convicción answers only the rock's referent and correctly says that its cited Protestant exegesis does not by itself establish the papacy.

**Official Catholic answer.** *Pastor aeternus* chs. 1–2 defines that Christ conferred a primacy of jurisdiction on Peter and that Peter has perpetual successors in that primacy, identified with the Roman Pontiff. The CDF states “como doctrina de fe” that the bishop of Rome succeeds Peter in his universal primatial service (§4), while §3 says the earliest literary and archaeological tradition associates Peter's martyrdom with Rome. The CDF also distinguishes the divinely instituted episcopate and primacy from later church-created organizational forms (§6). [*Pastor aeternus*, official Latin](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-ix/la/documents/constitutio-dogmatica-pastor-aeternus-18-iulii-1870.html); [CDF §§3–7](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19981031_primato-successore-pietro_sp.html).

**Objecting-tradition anchor.** The Lutheran Treatise ¶12 says Roman authority first grew “by human right, that is, by the ordinance of a council”; ¶1 rejects the claim that the Roman Pontiff stands above all bishops by divine right. The Orthodox patriarchal encyclical of 1895 §XV calls Rome's place an honorary presidency attached to the imperial capital, “first among equals”, and §XVII says the East did not recognize Roman jurisdiction during the ecumenical-council centuries. [Treatise](https://bookofconcord.org/power-and-primacy/treatise-compiled-at-smalcald/). The dossier uses E. Metallinos, *Answer of the Great Church of Constantinople to the Papal Encyclical on Union* (Manchester, 1896), §§XV–XVII, [scan](https://archive.org/details/answerofgreatchu0000eust).

**Overlap with the official layer.** Overlaps OB-3 (honor/human right) and OB-4 (Rome and Peter). The debate form groups them because speakers move repeatedly among Matthew 16, succession, Rome and early history.

**Quality flag.** Luis Toro's answer in the 198K video — that Peter's presence in Rome is not Catholic doctrine and is irrelevant — should not be used. It conflicts at least in formulation with Vatican I's Roman succession claim and the CDF's statement of the Roman martyrdom tradition.

**Verdict.** **PASSES.** Three independent direct voices, Catholic answers, explicit Lutheran and Orthodox anchors, and direct opposition to the Church's present teaching.
