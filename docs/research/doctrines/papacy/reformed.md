## Qué enseña

### Definition: the church gathered under Christ

WCF 25.1 describes the universal invisible church as the elect:

> «que han sido, ó son, ó serán reunidos en uno y sometidos á Cristo la cabeza de la Iglesia; y esta es la esposa, la plenitud de El quien llena todas las cosas en todos».

— Monterrey 1880, opening page of chapter XXV, immediately before p. 130 (image n130) [verified: scan].

### QE-1 · Christ's visible church

WCF 25.2 names the visible church:

> «el reino del Señor Jesu-Cristo [...] la casa y familia de Dios».

— Monterrey 1880, same opening page [verified: scan]; numbered proof references and punctuation between the phrases omitted.

The paragraph includes those throughout the world professing true religion together with their children. “Católica ó universal” is this confession's own designation for that church, not a reassignment of its words to a modern Catholic source [verified: scan].

### QE-2 · Christ gives the church its ministry

> «A esta iglesia católica visible, Cristo ha dado el ministerio, los oráculos, y las ordenanzas de Dios; para juntar y perfeccionar á los santos, en la vida presente, hasta el fin del mundo, haciéndoles suficientes para este fin, segun su promesa, por su presencia y Espíritu.»

— WCF 25.3, Monterrey 1880, pp. 130–131 [verified: scan].

### QE-3 · Church government under its king and head

WCF 30.1 is the positive government formulation: Christ, king and head, appoints government in the hands of church officers distinct from the civil magistrate. The Spanish and English print loci are recorded below; no account of current denominational procedures is inferred from it [verified: scan], Monterrey p. 151, quoted under Cómo se vive.

### QE-4 · The historical rejection of a papal head

> «No hay otra cabeza de la iglesia sino el Señor Jesu-Cristo [...] Ni puede el papa de Roma ser la cabeza de ella en ningun sentido; pues es aquel anti-cristo, aquel inícuo, é hijo de perdicion, que se ensalza en la iglesia contra Cristo, y contra todo lo que se llama Dios».

— WCF 25.6, Monterrey 1880, p. 133 [verified: scan]; numbered proof references omitted.

**Status:** this is the historical Westminster paragraph represented here. The [OPC's currently published WCF 25.6](https://opc.org/wcf.html) retains the denial that the pope can be the church's head but ends there; it does not print the historical Antichrist continuation. The page was fetched and read in this drafting pass; present-body adoption and historical change dates have not received a verification pass [unverified]. This note is restricted to the OPC, not all bodies in family A.

### QE-5 · The church remains and can err

WCF 25.5 says of the purest churches:

> «están expuestas á errar en doctrina y práctica».

The same paragraph says:

> «siempre habrá una iglesia en la tierra, para adorar á Dios conforme á su voluntad».

— Monterrey 1880, p. 132 [verified: scan]. These excerpts preserve the distinction between the church's endurance and the purity of particular churches. The intervening historical condemnation remains the confession's language, not an identification supplied by the dossier.

### QE-6 · Synods and councils

Historical WCF 31.3 assigns them ministerial determination of controversies, cases of conscience, worship and government, with submission to determinations consonant with God's Word; 31.4 says they can err (Edinburgh 1894, p. 97) [verified: scan]. It is not the denial of all ecclesial judgment.

## Por qué

### PQ-1 · Christ's kingship

> «Cristo ejecuta el oficio de Rey, sujetándonos á sí mismo, rigiendo y defendiéndonos, y restringiendo y venciendo á todos sus enemigos y los nuestros.»

— WSC 26, Monterrey 1880, p. 174 [verified: scan].

WLC 45 expands that office through officers, laws, censures, grace, preservation and victory; the original was located across Edinburgh 1894, pp. 118–119. It is a related locus, not a separate claim that WLC uses the word “pope” [unverified].

### PQ-2 · Ministry given for gathering and perfecting

The end given by WCF 25.3 is “para juntar y perfeccionar á los santos” (QE-2). Its proof note cites Ephesians 4:11–13; Isaiah 59:21; Matthew 28:19–20 (Monterrey pp. 130–131) [verified: scan]. The dossier does not extend that sentence into an argument the document does not state.

### PQ-3 · The keys entrusted to church officers

> «A estos oficiales, son entregadas las llaves del reino de los cielos, en virtud de lo cual, tienen poder para retener y remitir pecados, para cerrar aquel reino contra los impenitentes, por la palabra y por las censuras; y para abrirlo á los pecadores arrepentidos, por el ministerio del evangelio, y por la remocion de las censuras, segun exigen las circunstancias.»

— WCF 30.2, Monterrey 1880, p. 152 [verified: scan].

The paragraph's printed witnesses are Matthew 16:19; 18:17–18; John 20:21–23; 2 Corinthians 2:6–8 [verified: scan]. This is the confession's own use of the keys texts, without assigning it a fuller interpretation of Peter's office than it prints.

### PQ-4 · Discipline's stated ends

> «Las censuras eclesiásticas son necesarias para corregir y hacer volver sobre sus pasos á los hermanos que ofenden; para impedir que otros cometan semejantes ofensas; para reivindicar el honor de Cristo, y la santa profesion del evangelio».

— WCF 30.3, Monterrey 1880, p. 152 [verified: scan]; excerpt before the paragraph's final reason concerning God's wrath and profanation of the covenant and seals.

The quotation is a disciplinary purpose clause. It is not an account of the pope's actions or of another church's practice.

### PQ-5 · The historical Antichrist clause's own apparatus

WCF 25.6 attaches Colossians 1:18 and Ephesians 1:22 to Christ's headship, and Matthew 23:8–10 and 2 Thessalonians 2:3–5 to the historical rejection (Monterrey p. 133) [verified: scan]. No additional historical identification, calculation or modern apologetic argument is supplied here.

**Status:** the historical clause's present OPC distinction is given immediately under QE-4. The same scoped note applies here; this is not a claim that all present Reformed bodies retain or discard that clause.

## Cómo se vive

### Government in the document's words

> «El Señor Jesus, como Rey y Cabeza de su iglesia, ha establecido en ella un gobierno regido por oficiales eclesiásticos distintos del magistrado civil.»

— WCF 30.1, Monterrey 1880, p. 151 [verified: scan].

### Censures and restoration

WCF 30.2 describes shutting against the impenitent and opening to the repentant through gospel ministry and removal of censures (PQ-3). WCF 30.4 prescribes:

> «primero de la amonestacion, y en seguida, de la suspension del culpable del sacramento de la cena del Señor por algun tiempo, y despues de la excomunion de la iglesia, segun la naturaleza del delito de la persona».

— Monterrey 1880, p. 153 [verified: scan]; excerpt from the instruction to church officers.

These are the standards' disciplinary prescriptions; no claim is made about the frequency of their use today.

### Councils, submission and limits

Historical WCF 31.3 prescribes decisions about controversies, conscience, worship and government, and hearing complaints of maladministration. Its determinations consonant with the Word are received with reverence and submission (Edinburgh p. 97) [verified: scan].

Historical 31.4 qualifies this with possible error. Historical 31.5 confines their proper business to ecclesiastical matters, with the exceptions it states for extraordinary petition or requested advice (same page) [verified: scan]. This is the historical confession, not the full current constitution of every Presbyterian body.

### Specific papal procedures: silent within the bounded corpus

No papal election or pontifical ceremonial is supplied by these standards in the bounded searches below. That silence is not filled with a Catholic ritual, a modern Reformed commentator or a new description by the agent.

The normative-practice section is full for church government and discipline; the narrower papal-procedures subtopic is silent in this corpus. Those two coverage judgments are kept distinct.

## Grado de autoridad

### The headship statement

WCF 25.6 is the confession's own historical assertion. Its harsh language is quoted as such; the OPC reception distinction remains immediately beside it. A confessional polemic is not used as a neutral description of a different tradition.

### The confession's own hierarchy

WCF 1.10 names the Holy Spirit speaking in Scripture as supreme judge (Spanish Monterrey p. 12, read during this tradition's drafting) [verified: scan]. Historical 31.3–4 recognizes ministerial conciliar judgment while denying that councils become the rule of faith (English Edinburgh p. 97) [verified: scan].
