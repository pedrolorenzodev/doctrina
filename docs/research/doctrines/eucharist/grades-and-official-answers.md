## Result in brief

Two Eucharistic propositions are expressly placed in the first paragraph of the `Professio fidei`: the real and substantial presence and the sacrificial nature of the Eucharistic celebration. Transubstantiation and Eucharistic adoration also have Church sources that explicitly call their status dogmatic. No Church source found places an Eucharistic proposition in the second paragraph (“doctrina definitiva”) or names a particular Eucharistic proposition as a third-paragraph, non-definitive teaching. It would therefore be inaccurate to manufacture Eucharistic examples for those two rows.

The strongest official answer to John 6:63 is not CCC 1336 but John Paul II's general audience of 13 September 1989: it says that the hearers understood the announcement materially, that the Spirit makes it intelligible, and that Christ nevertheless gives his body and blood as food and drink. The strongest official answer to the blood prohibition is Florence's `Cantate Domino`: it explicitly says that the Acts 15 prohibition suited the temporary Jewish-Gentile situation and that its effect ceased when its reason ceased.

## 1. The four grades and Eucharistic examples actually stated by Church sources

The governing definitions are CIC 750 §§1–2 and 752 in the official Spanish 1983 Code, as amended by `Ad tuendam fidem`: [Vatican, CIC 747–755](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/esp/documents/cic_libro3_cann747-755_sp.html).

### Important boundary on the second grade

The closest second-grade examples are:

- CDF 1998 §11 on priestly ordination reserved to men: «tal doctrina debe ser tenida en modo definitivo».
- The same paragraph on Leo XIII's `Apostolicae curae`: «la declaración […] sobre la invalidez de las ordenaciones anglicanas» is listed among truths that «deben ser tenidas en modo definitivo».

Both bear on who can validly celebrate the Eucharist, but neither states the grade of QE-6 (“Christ made the apostles priests; only a validly ordained priest consecrates”). They can be shown as nearby sacramental examples only with this boundary.

## 3. John 6:63: «El Espíritu es quien da vida; la carne no sirve para nada»

### What the Catechism does

CCC 1336 quotes John 6:60 («Es duro este lenguaje»), 6:67 («¿También vosotros queréis marcharos?»), and 6:68 («palabras de vida eterna»). It says: «La Eucaristía y la cruz son piedras de escándalo. Es el mismo misterio» and invites reception of the Eucharist in faith. [Official Spanish, CCC 1336](https://www.vatican.va/archive/catechism_sp/p2s2c1a3_sp.html).

**Verdict:** CCC 1336 frames John 6:60–68 Eucharistically, but it neither cites verse 63 nor explains «la carne no sirve para nada». It supports the Catholic reading of the discourse; it does **not** answer this wording by itself. The current research note should say “contextualizes but does not answer”, not “the Catechism answers”.

### Official text that directly answers it

John Paul II, General Audience, **13 September 1989**, §4, “El Espíritu Santo y la Eucaristía”:

> «Los oyentes de Jesús entendieron de modo “material” aquel primer anuncio eucarístico. El Maestro quiso en seguida precisar que su contenido sólo podía aclararse y entenderse por obra del “Espíritu que da vida”. En la Eucaristía Cristo nos da su cuerpo y su sangre como alimento y bebida, bajo las especies del pan y del vino […] Solamente en virtud del Espíritu, que da vida, el alimento y la bebida eucarísticos pueden obrar en nosotros la “comunión”».

[Official Spanish](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/es/audiences/1989/documents/hf_jp-ii_aud_19890913.html).

This directly rejects the proposed inference “verse 63 cancels the literal gift of Christ's flesh”: “flesh” in the objection is a material way of understanding, while the Eucharistic body and blood act salvifically through the life-giving Spirit. This is an official papal catechesis, not a dogmatic definition; the answer should be labelled accordingly.

John Paul II's General Audience of **30 January 1991**, §6, corroborates the point:

> «Tanto la palabra como el sacramento tienen vida y eficacia operativa por el Espíritu Santo»; the same section says the Spirit realizes «la transformación sacramental del pan y del vino en el Cuerpo y Sangre de Cristo».

[Official Spanish](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/es/audiences/1991/documents/hf_jp-ii_aud_19910130.html).

## 4. The ban on consuming blood: Leviticus 17 and Acts 15

### Catechism: the Mosaic food-law premise

CCC 582 says Jesus brings the food-purity law to fulfilment and quotes Mark 7:18–21: «así declaraba puros todos los alimentos». [Official Spanish, CCC 582](https://www.vatican.va/archive/catechism_sp/p122a4p1_sp.html).

This addresses the general premise behind an appeal to Leviticus 17, but it does not mention the Eucharist or Acts 15. By itself it is an indirect answer.

### Florence: the Acts 15 prohibition explicitly declared temporary

Eugene IV / Council of Florence, Session XI, bull `Cantate Domino` (4 February 1442), in the Holy See's official Latin text:

> «Illam etiam apostolorum prohibitionem, ab immolatis simulacrorum et sanguine et suffocato (At 15, 29), dicit, illi tempori congruisse […] Ubi autem eo usque propagata est christiana religio […] illius apostolice prohibitionis causa cessante etiam cessavit effectus.»

It then declares:

> «Nullam itaque cibi naturam condemnandam esse denuntiat […]»

[Official Latin, lines corresponding to the food-law paragraph](https://www.vatican.va/content/eugenius-iv/la/documents/bulla-cantate-domino-4-febr-1442.html). The Vatican's official Italian edition makes the meaning explicit: «il comando degli apostoli di astenersi […] dal sangue e dagli animali soffocati (Atti 15,29) era adatta a quel tempo» and, once its reason ceased, «ne cessò anche l'effetto». [Official Italian](https://www.vatican.va/content/eugenius-iv/it/documents/bulla-cantate-domino-4-febr-1442.html).

**Verdict:** this is a direct official answer to the continuing force of Acts 15:29. The decree says the ban was a temporary measure for the first Jewish-Gentile Church, not a permanent Christian food prohibition. It also says the Mosaic distinction between clean and unclean foods belonged to ceremonial prescriptions that ceased with the Gospel. Applying this specifically to Eucharistic blood is a further inference, but a tightly bounded one: the text removes the claimed permanent dietary prohibition; the positive Eucharistic command comes from Christ's institution and the Church's Eucharistic teaching.

1. QE-2: replace “grade rests on inference” with the express `Mysterium fidei` 10 classification, while preserving the exact Tridentine scope.
2. QE-5: cite the CDF 1982 ARCIC observations and mark Eucharistic adoration as the object of a dogmatic definition.
3. QE-4 and QE-6: keep the inference/unconfirmed-as-separately-classified warning.
4. “Doctrina definitiva” row: retain «Ninguna afirmación eucarística de esta página tiene este grado» unless a proposition-specific source is later found; do not use Anglican orders as if it graded QE-6.
5. “Magisterio auténtico” row: distinguish the category's official definition from the project's application of a document-type default to particular Eucharistic statements.
6. John 6:63: use John Paul II 1989 §4 as the direct official answer; describe CCC 1336 as contextual support only.
7. Blood ban: use `Cantate Domino` as the direct answer to Acts 15 and CCC 582 as general support; state openly that their Eucharistic application is an inference.
