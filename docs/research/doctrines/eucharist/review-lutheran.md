### §1–3 (Qué enseña, Por qué, La Escritura)

#### E1 · The FC's own one-sentence statement of the dispute (Ep VII.2) is missing — **should** (high)
**Where:** §1 Statements, QE-3 and QE-4; §2 intro.
**Problem:** The dossier gives oral eating (QE-3) and the eating of the unworthy (QE-4) as two separate items, without the FC's own explanation of why these two are the test. For a Lutheran theologian they are the shibboleths of the article: the FC says the "subtle Sacramentarians" use "our own words" ("true presence … spiritually through faith") while meaning that "nothing is present and received with the mouth except bread and wine" (Ep VII.4–5). Without Ep VII.2 the reader sees two odd-looking claims instead of the Lutheran confession's chosen criteria. The dossier uses Ep VII.2, 4–5 later in an objection answer (l.556–566), but the tradition page needs it in Qué enseña.
**Fix:** Add Ep VII.2 (the *Status controversiae*) as the lead source of a merged QE-3/QE-4, or as QE-3's first source. English (Ep VII page) `[verified]`: "Whether in the Holy Supper the true body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ are truly and essentially present, are distributed with the bread and wine, and received with the mouth by all those who use this Sacrament, whether they be worthy or unworthy, godly or ungodly, believing or unbelieving; by the believing for consolation and life, by the unbelieving for judgment? The Sacramentarians say, No; we say, Yes." German `tg.txt` l.14754ff (the dossier already cites this range `[verified: e-text]` in its objection answer). Grade: «Fórmula de Concordia (1577), Epítome VII, estado de la controversia». See E15 (opinion) on merging QE-3 and QE-4.

#### E2 · QE-1 site draft attributes the Apology's «sustancialmente» and the FC's «con» to AC X — **should**
**Where:** QE-1, Texto para el sitio.
**Problem:** «verdadera y sustancialmente presentes, y se dan y se reciben con el pan y el vino. Así lo confiesa el artículo X…». AC X (German) says «wahrhaftiglich unter der Gestalt des Brots und Weins … gegenwärtig»; the Latin says «vere adsint et distribuantur vescentibus». «Substantialiter» and «cum illis rebus, quae videntur» are Ap X.54; «mit Brot und Wein» is FC Ep VII.6. A Lutheran reader who knows AC X will notice that the most-quoted article of the tradition is being made to say more than it says (and the AC German/Latin difference is itself a known point; the dossier's modal note is right).
**Fix:** «La Confesión de Augsburgo confiesa que el verdadero cuerpo y la verdadera sangre de Cristo están verdaderamente presentes en la Cena "bajo la forma del pan y del vino" y que allí se distribuyen y se reciben (art. X). La Apología añade que están "verdadera y sustancialmente presentes" y que esta es la doctrina "recibida en toda la Iglesia" (X.54, 57).»

#### E3 · Definition uses «en, con y bajo», which the dossier's own wording note says the site won't use — **should**
**Where:** §1 Definición, Texto para el sitio; vs. the QE-2 wording note (l.90).
**Problem:** The definition rests on SC VI.1–2, which says only «unter dem Brot und Wein». «En, con y bajo» is a later English/Spanish shorthand (correct in substance, from SD VII.35/38), and the QE-2 note itself decides to quote the FC's words. Inconsistent, and the definition would be the one place the reader takes away.
**Fix:** «…el verdadero cuerpo y la verdadera sangre de nuestro Señor Jesucristo, bajo el pan y el vino…» (SC VI.2), and keep «bajo el pan, con el pan, en el pan» for QE-2 where SD VII.35 is quoted. Optional: the term modal can say that «en, con y bajo» is the usual shorthand of SD VII.35/38 `[memory: usual shorthand; not checked in a Spanish Lutheran source]`.

#### E4 · QE-9: the Wittenberg Concord sentence the FC quotes ("permanently united … apart from the use") is the strongest text for the question and is not used — **should** (high)
**Where:** QE-9, Sources; the note on reliquiae (l.190).
**Problem:** The question asked is «¿Sigue presente Cristo en la hostia que se guarda…?». The FC answers it directly in SD VII.14, quoting the Wittenberg Concord as its own: the Lutherans "believe in no transubstantiation … nor hold that the body and blood of Christ are included in the bread *localiter*, that is, locally, or are otherwise permanently united therewith apart from the use of the Sacrament, yet they concede that through the sacramental union the bread is the body of Christ" (English, SD VII page `[verified]`). SD VII.83–87 gives the rule; SD VII.14 gives the application. The dossier's Considered-and-left-out list says the Concord is "used in QE-9/OB-2 as the FC quotes it", but QE-9's sources don't include it.
**Fix:** Add SD VII.14 as QE-9's second source (German locator for the verifier: SD VII.14 in `tg.txt`, near the Wittenberg Concord quotation; not located by line today). Site draft, after the rule: «La Fórmula de Concordia, citando la Concordia de Wittenberg (1536), dice que el cuerpo de Cristo no está "incluido localmente" en el pan "ni unido a él de modo permanente fuera del uso del sacramento".»

#### E5 · QE-9: keep the answer from implying "receptionism" or a doctrine on reliquiae — **should**
**Where:** QE-9 answer draft and the reliquiae note.
**Problem:** Read alone, «fuera de ella … no es sacramento» lets a reader infer either (a) that for Lutherans the presence begins only at reception, or (b) that what remains after the distribution is "mere bread". The BoC teaches neither. (a) is excluded by SD VII.74–75 (not "the eating and drinking or faith of the communicants" produces the presence; the words "operate … so that in all places where the Supper is celebrated according to the institution … the body and blood of Christ are truly present, distributed, and received"), and by SD VII.86, which says the "use" is "not chiefly faith, nor oral participation only, but the entire external visible action" `[verified in D]`. (b) is the question the dossier rightly finds unaddressed; among Lutherans it is debated (the 16th-century Saliger controversy; current practice of reverent consumption of what remains in confessional bodies) `[memory]`. A confessional Lutheran would object to (a) strongly.
**Fix:** In QE-9's draft, after the rule, one cross-reference sentence from the FC: «La misma Fórmula aclara que lo que hace presente a Cristo no es el comer ni la fe del que comulga, sino sus palabras (ver QE-5).» Keep the reliquiae silence as is, and add to the coverage table: «sobre lo que sobra después de la distribución, el Libro de Concordia no se pronuncia (buscado en SD VII.14–15, 73–87, 108; Ep VII.40)». No body statement fills it (DECISIONS 2026-10-15).

#### E6 · QE-8: the Apology's sacrament/sacrifice distinction (Ap XXIV.17–18) is missing; it is the core of the Lutheran answer — **should** (high)
**Where:** QE-8, Sources and draft.
**Problem:** The draft reduces the Lutheran position to "one propitiatory sacrifice; the Supper is a sacrifice of praise". The Apology's argument is first about *direction*: in a sacrament God gives, in a sacrifice we give. That is what the Lutheran means when he calls the Supper a *Sakrament* and refuses to call it a propitiatory *Opfer*; without it, the "eucharistic sacrifice" sentence reads as a concession instead of the conclusion of the distinction.
**Fix:** Add Ap XXIV.17–18 before XXIV.19. English (Ap XXIV page) `[verified]`: "Theologians are rightly accustomed to distinguish between a Sacrament and a sacrifice. … A Sacrament is a ceremony or work in which God presents to us that which the promise annexed to the ceremony offers … A sacrifice, on the contrary, is a ceremony or work which we render God in order to afford Him honor." Latin `tl.txt` l.6066–6070 `[verified: e-text]`: «Sacramentum est ceremonia vel opus, in quo Deus nobis exhibet hoc, quod offert … promissio … Econtra sacrificium est ceremonia vel opus, quod nos Deo reddimus, ut eum honore afficiamus.» Site draft opening: «La Apología distingue: en un sacramento Dios nos da lo que promete; en un sacrificio somos nosotros los que damos a Dios (XXIV.18). Por eso responde: "hubo en el mundo un único sacrificio propiciatorio…".»

#### E7 · QE-6: the Large Catechism's "food of the soul" is missing — **should**
**Where:** QE-6.
**Problem:** QE-6 gives the benefit only as forgiveness. The LC's own exposition adds, as a second line of the same benefit, that the Supper strengthens faith and the new man for the daily battle; it is a central catechetical text for Lutheran practice of frequent communion (and it feeds "Cómo se vive").
**Fix:** Add LC V.23–24. English (LC V page) `[verified]`: "On this account it is indeed called a food of souls, which nourishes and strengthens the new man. … Therefore it is given for a daily pasture and sustenance, that faith may refresh and strengthen itself so as not to fall back in such a battle, but become ever stronger and stronger." German `tg.txt` l.13802 `[verified: e-text]`: «Darum heißt es wohl eine Speise der Seele, die den neün [neuen] Menschen nährt und stärkt.» (e-text «neün»: for the verifier.)

#### E8 · PQ-4 intro: «Cristo … que "está en todas partes"» invites the "ubiquity" caricature; the FC's limits are not shown — **should** (high)
**Where:** §2 intro draft («sentado a la diestra de Dios, que "está en todas partes"») and PQ-4.
**Problem:** In Spanish the relative «que» reads as referring to Christ, so the site would say Lutherans ground the Supper in Christ's body being everywhere. The FC says "God's right hand is everywhere" (Ep VII.12) and explicitly **rejects** that Christ's humanity is "locally extended in all places of heaven and earth" (SD VIII.92; Ep VIII.29). Its positive claim is that Christ "can be, and also is, present where He will" according to his human nature (SD VIII.78). Reformed critics attack "ubiquity"; a Lutheran would want the confession's own limit visible on the tradition page, not only in an objection answer.
**Fix:** Draft: «…sentado a la diestra de Dios —y "la diestra de Dios está en todas partes"—, que según su naturaleza humana "puede estar, y está, presente donde quiere".» Add to PQ-4: SD VIII.78 English (SD VIII page) `[verified]`: "He can be, and also is, present where He will, and especially that in His Church and congregation on earth He is present as Mediator, Head, King, and High Priest"; SD VIII.92 (antithesis) English `[verified]`: "4. Also, that the humanity of Christ is locally extended in all places of heaven and earth; which is to be ascribed not even to the divinity." German SD VIII.92 `tg.txt` l.19295 `[verified: e-text]`: «4. Item, daß die Menschheit Christi in alle Orte des Himmels und der Erde räumlich ausgespannt sei…»; Latin `tl.txt` l.18151 «IV. Quod humanitas Christi in omnia loca coeli et terrae localiter extensa sit…» `[verified: e-text]`. FC VIII stays graded as FC (same authority as VII); a pointer to the Christology page is fine.

#### E9 · PQ-1: the "last will and testament" argument (SD VII.44, 50; Ap XXII.2) is not drawn out — **should**
**Where:** PQ-1.
**Problem:** The FC's reason for literal reading is not only "Christ cannot lie" but that these are the words of a *testament*, spoken by the testator himself, who chose "entirely proper, simple, indubitable, and clear words" and explained them with "Given for you, shed for you" (SD VII.50). The dossier quotes the Apology's testament argument for both kinds (QE-10) but not for the presence, where the FC makes it.
**Fix:** Add SD VII.50 to PQ-1. English (SD VII page) `[verified]`: "here, as in the making of His last will and testament and of His everabiding covenant and union, … He uses not allegorical, but entirely proper, simple, indubitable, and clear words; and in order that no misunderstanding can occur, He explains them more clearly with the words: Given for you, shed for you." German locator for the verifier: SD VII.50 in `tg.txt` (after l.18227, ¶49 starts there `[verified]`).

#### E10 · PQ-5: the Apology's Greek-canon and "Vulgarius" quotes need the Apology's own stated purpose beside them — **should**
**Where:** PQ-5, first bullet.
**Problem:** Shown as is, "the priest clearly prays that the bread may be changed" and "truly changed into flesh" read as the Apology endorsing a change of substance, against SA III.6.5 and SD VII.35. This is a stock Catholic debate point against Lutherans `[memory]`. The Apology says why it cites them, in the next paragraph.
**Fix:** Add Ap X.57's opening as the closing sentence of the bullet (English, Ap X page) `[verified]`: "We have cited these testimonies, not to undertake a discussion here concerning this subject, for His Imperial Majesty does not disapprove of this article, but in order that all who may read them may the more clearly perceive that we defend the doctrine received in the entire Church, that in the Lord's Supper the body and blood of Christ are truly and substantially present…". The dossier's own note ("uses them for the presence, not for transubstantiation") is then the Apology's words, not the agent's.

#### E11 · The grade/subscription line mislabels LCMS and IELA with «"porque"» and trims ELCA's verb — **must-fix**
**Where:** §1 Statements, the fixed line for the tradition page («las del Concilio Luterano Internacional (como la Iglesia Luterana–Sínodo de Misuri o la IELA) suscriben todo el Libro de Concordia "porque" concuerda con la Escritura; otras, como la ELCA, aceptan los demás escritos como "interpretaciones válidas"»).
**Problem:** (1) The quoted «"porque"» is the CELC's wording ("not in so far as, but because", map §1.2–1.3), i.e. WELS's association, not the ILC's. LCMS's constitution says it "accepts without reservation … All the Symbolical Books … as a true and unadulterated statement and exposition of the Word of God" (map §1.2 `[verified there]`); IELA's own statute was not found (map §1.2). Putting a quoted word in the mouth of bodies that did not write it breaks the map's own rule: "The site should quote each body's verb, not assign a label" (§1.3). (2) ELCA's wording is "further valid interpretations of the faith of the Church" (map §1.2); dropping "further" removes the grading the ELCA itself chose. (3) WELS (the body that does say "because") is missing. A confessional Lutheran would object to (1) at once; an ELCA theologian to (2).
**Fix:** «Todas las iglesias luteranas consultadas confiesan la Confesión de Augsburgo no alterada y el Catecismo Menor. Algunas aceptan todo el Libro de Concordia: la Iglesia Luterana–Sínodo de Misuri, "sin reserva", como "exposición verdadera y no adulterada de la Palabra de Dios"; el Sínodo de Wisconsin, "porque" es "una explicación correcta de la verdad bíblica". Otras gradúan: la ELCA acepta la Confesión de Augsburgo como "verdadero testimonio del Evangelio" y los demás escritos como "otras interpretaciones válidas de la fe de la Iglesia".» All wording from map §1.2 rows `[verified there]`; IELA only if its own text is found. Keep "quia/quatenus" only as glossary terms with the map's note that bodies don't label themselves "quatenus".

#### E13 · Spanish of the "use" rule: «nada tiene razón de sacramento» — **should**
**Where:** QE-9, Spanish of SD VII.85.
**Problem:** *ratio* rendered «razón» reads in Spanish as "reason", making the rule obscure. The Triglot English gives "Nothing has the nature of a sacrament".

#### E14 · «Sacramentarios» needs the FC's own definition in the term modal — **should**
**Where:** PQ-4, Ep VII.10 Spanish («contra los sacramentarios»), and every later use.
**Problem:** To a Spanish reader «sacramentarios» suggests people who value sacraments; in the FC it names those who deny the bodily presence, in two kinds (Ep VII.3–5). It is also the FC's name for the Reformed objectors on this page, so it carries the harsh-language status line.
**Fix:** Term modal from Ep VII.3–4 (English `[verified]`): "there are two kinds of Sacramentarians. Some are gross Sacramentarians, who declare … that in the Holy Supper nothing but bread and wine is present … Others, however, are subtle Sacramentarians … who partly speak very speciously in our own words, and pretend that they also believe a true presence … however, that this occurs spiritually through faith." Plus the ST-FC status line the dossier already drafts (see §4 findings).

### §4 Objeciones

#### E16 · OB-5: «la confesión … se conserva, pero como consuelo, no como condición de la fe» misstates AC XXV.1 and misses the real point of disagreement — **must-fix**
**Where:** OB-5, Respuesta luterana, fourth paragraph.
**Problem:** (1) The framing clause is in the site's voice and is not in any quoted text. (2) It contradicts the text the dossier itself uses in CV-4: AC XXV.1, «Non enim solet porrigi corpus Domini nisi antea exploratis et absolutis» (`tl.txt` l.1101 `[verified in D]`); and Ap XXIV.1: the Sacrament "is offered to those who wish to use it, after they have been examined and absolved" (English, Ap XXIV page `[verified]`). For the confessions, examination and absolution before communion are the church's ordinary practice, not an optional comfort. (3) What the confessions actually deny against Trent can. 11 is (a) that faith is not sufficient preparation and (b) that the enumeration of all sins is necessary. (b) is AC XI.1–2 and it is missing. A Lutheran would not sign the paragraph as written.
**Fix:** Replace the paragraph with: «Para las confesiones luteranas, la absolución se conserva antes de la comunión, pero sin la enumeración de todos los pecados. La Confesión de Augsburgo dice: "no se suele dar el cuerpo del Señor sino a los que antes han sido examinados y absueltos" (XXV.1), y la Apología, que el sacramento se ofrece "a los que quieren usarlo, después de haber sido examinados y absueltos" (XXIV.1). Y enseña que "la absolución privada debe conservarse en las iglesias, aunque en la confesión no es necesaria la enumeración de todos los pecados. Porque es imposible, según el Salmo: ¿Quién podrá entender sus errores?" (XI.1–2; Salmo 19:12).» AC XI English (AC XI page) `[verified]`: "Of Confession they teach that Private Absolution ought to be retained in the churches, although in confession an enumeration of all sins is not necessary. For it is impossible according to the Psalm: Who can understand his errors? Ps. 19:12." Latin AC XI: for the verifier (`tl.txt`, AC XI, not located today). Keep the paragraph's end on Ep VII.38 (the antithesis), which is exactly the confession's answer to can. 11.

#### E20 · OB-8: the strongest FC texts against the "body confined in heaven" are the limit on ubiquity and the "where He wills" — **should** (high)
**Where:** OB-8, fourth paragraph («que Cristo esté en el cielo no lo impide»).
**Problem:** The answer gives "the right hand of God is everywhere" and the "various modes" ground, which, without the FC's limits, is exactly what Reformed polemic calls "ubiquity". The FC itself rejects a local extension of Christ's humanity (antitheses SD VIII.92; Ep VIII.29) and grounds the presence in Christ's will and word (SD VIII.78). It also quotes Luther's second mode, "the incomprehensible, spiritual mode, according to which He neither occupies nor vacates space" (SD VII.100, English `[verified]`), as the mode of the presence in the bread (the dossier cites SD VII.93–103 only in PQ-4).
**Fix:** Add, in this order: SD VIII.78 (text in E8) · SD VII.100 (FC quoting the *Large Confession*: "Secondly, the incomprehensible, spiritual mode, according to which He neither occupies nor vacates space, but penetrates all creatures wherever He pleases") `[verified]` · SD VIII.92 (antithesis, text in E8). Grade: FC VIII is FC, same category.

#### E22 · Missing objection: the Catholic claim that Lutheran communities lack "the proper reality of the eucharistic mystery" for lack of Orders — **should**
**Where:** §4, list of objections.
**Problem:** The most frequent Catholic objection to the Lutheran Supper today is not transubstantiation but ministry: Vatican II, *Unitatis redintegratio* 22, says the separated ecclesial communities "have not retained the proper reality of the eucharistic mystery in its fullness, especially because of the absence of the sacrament of Orders" `[memory: wording; to read on vatican.va]`. It is a universal-magisterium text (DECISIONS 2026-10-15: Catholic column = universal magisterium) and it rejects in words. The BoC answers it directly: the presence is not produced by "the merit or recitation of the minister" but by Christ's word (SD VII.74–75, Ep VII.8, in QE-5); the word "is not rendered powerless by the worthiness or unworthiness of the minister" (SD VII.89, in OB-9); and the church's right to call ministers (AC XIV *rite vocatus*; Tr 67–72 `[memory: Tr ¶ numbers]`).
**Fix:** Add OB-11 «Sin el sacramento del Orden no se conserva la plena realidad de la Eucaristía», objector UR 22 (+ Trent XXII can. 2, which the dossier read and set aside for the ministry page), answer from the BoC texts above. Read UR 22 (es/en, vatican.va) and Tr 67–72 before writing. If Pedro prefers to keep ministry for its own doctrine page, at least a cross-link.

**Where:** §6.
**Problem:** (1) The categories are the BoC's own (RN), and the dossier correctly does not use *norma normata* as a BoC term; a Lutheran would accept the table.
