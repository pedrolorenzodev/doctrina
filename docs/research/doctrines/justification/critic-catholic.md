**Where:** §3 table, rows 1 Cor 6:9-10 and Jn 20:22-23; §4 OB-7 answer, first paragraph.
**Problem:** «Pablo excluye del Reino de Dios no solo a los incrédulos sino a los fieles que cometen pecados graves: por eso, para Trento, todo pecado mortal hace perder la gracia.» The first clause is the site reading 1 Cor 6 (that the people excluded are "fieles" who fell is exactly what the Reformed deny: WCF XVII.1 says the justified can neither totally nor finally fall `[verified in D]`); "para Trento" arrives only in the second clause. **Fix:** «Para Trento, Pablo excluye del Reino no solo a los incrédulos sino también a fieles que cometen pecados graves; por eso todo pecado mortal hace perder la gracia (ses. VI, cap. 15).» OB-7: «y lo funda en 1 Corintios 6:9-10, que Trento lee como dicho también de fieles que pecan gravemente». ### 5 · The Cierre's gloss frames the objector as denying any real change — **must-fix (wording)**

**Where:** §2 "Cierre"; D's Question 14.
**Problem:** «Para la Iglesia católica, la justificación cambia de verdad al pecador» is attributed, and the expert says keep it. The problem is the gloss, not the quote: "cambia de verdad" is the agent's framing, and on a page a Lutheran reads it implies the other side holds that justification changes nothing. Their texts say the opposite: SA III.13.1 «we acquire a new and clean heart» and FC Ep III.11 the faith that justifies «never is alone, but always has with it love and hope» `[verified in D]`. The "legal fiction" trope is the standard caricature of imputation; the site must not lean on it even inside «Para la Iglesia católica». The dispute is not real change vs none; it is whether the justice by which one is justified is inherent in the justified. Trent's quoted words say exactly that.
**Fix:** Drop the gloss and let Trent carry the line: «Para la Iglesia católica, la justicia que justifica está en el justificado: por la justicia de Dios, enseña Trento, "no solo quedamos reputados justos, sino que con verdad se nos llama así, y lo somos" (ses. VI, cap. 7); Dios, dice el Catecismo, "nos hace interiormente justos" (Catecismo 1992).» Q14's answer stays "keep".

## Verdict

The Catholic doctrine is reported in the Church's words, the grades show their basis, the Fathers stay inside the Catechism, and the objections are built from the official texts of the traditions that raise them. The breaches are in the connective prose (two Scripture rows and an answer that state what Paul and Christ did, one "we" in OB-6, the Fathers' name for Penance in the site's voice, and a Cierre gloss that frames the objector as denying any real change) and they are one-line fixes. The fairness problem is on the Lutheran side and has three parts: the reader hears Trent condemn "faith as confidence alone" and "the will as something inanimate" without ever hearing the Lutheran define faith as trust (Ap IV.48, WLC 72) or reject the stone-and-wax image (SD II.89); OB-6 opens with a text Trent also condemns, so the Formula looks confused; and the answers use the LWF's "juntos confesamos" against Book-of-Concord objections and then close on canons the Church herself said (DJ 41) do not reach that teaching. Add the three texts, reorder OB-6, keep the Declaration's Catholic paragraphs in the answers and its joint ones in QE-9 with DJ 41 after each canon, and redo the Lutheran Spanish from the Latin already in hand; then a confessional Lutheran, a Presbyterian and a Baptist would each recognize their objection on the page, and a secular reader would find the labels no stronger than the canons behind them.
