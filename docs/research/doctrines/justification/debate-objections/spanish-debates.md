## Objections heard

### J-1 — God justifies through faith alone, not through works

1. **Neutral formulation.** Justification is received through faith as its sole instrument. Works are the necessary fruit or evidence of living faith, but neither the ground nor an additional instrument of justification.
2. **Popular wording heard.** “La fe sola justifica”; “el hombre es justificado por la fe aparte de las obras de la ley.”
3. **Independent speakers.**
   - Carlos Veloz says Christ's work is fully sufficient and received “totalmente solo por la fe” at [28:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=1698s), then cites justification apart from works at [33:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=2024s). In the slower dialogue he calls faith the sole means while insisting Christians do perform good works at [20:54–28:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T8Fo-HFoNDg&t=1254s).
   - Edgar Pacheco calls faith the sufficient instrumental cause of total justification at [13:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=828s) and reads Romans 4:5 as excluding works from that instrument at [19:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=1140s).
   - Salvador Barragán says a person is justified not by works of the law but “solo por la fe” at [36:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOIDksoeeqc&t=2164s) and repeats “la fe sola justifica” at [43:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOIDksoeeqc&t=2613s).
4. **Catholic answer heard.** Dante Urbina rejects works apart from grace as a basis of salvation and cites Trent at [08:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=488s). José Placencia agrees entry into grace is not earned but argues grace-enabled obedience matters to final salvation at [01:03:29–01:13:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T8Fo-HFoNDg&t=3809s).
5. **Official objector anchors.** Apology IV.73 and IV.86 says faith alone justifies while excluding merit, not the Word or sacraments ([Book of Concord](https://bookofconcord.org/defense/of-justification/)). Westminster Confession XI.2 calls faith “the alone instrument of justification” and immediately says it is accompanied by other saving graces and works through love ([OPC edition](https://opc.org/wcf.html)); Westminster Larger Catechism 73 makes works fruits rather than the basis.
6. **Catholic delimitation.** Trent VI, chapter 8 and canon 9 affirm gratuity and reject confidence in faith understood apart from required dispositions. CCC 1991 says justification is acceptance of God's righteousness through faith in Christ ([Catechism §§1987–2029](https://www.vatican.va/archive/catechism_sp/p3s1c3a2_sp.html)). The disagreement is not “grace versus self-salvation,” but whether faith is the sole instrument and how grace-enabled renewal relates to final salvation.
7. **Dossier overlap.** Official-layer objection 1; also supplies the popular form behind objections 3 and 6.
8. **Verdict.** **PASS — 3 independent named speakers; Catholic answer heard; Lutheran and Reformed anchors; exact current Catholic target.**

### J-2 — Justification is forensic and imputed, not an infused transformation

1. **Neutral formulation.** God pardons and declares the believer righteous by crediting Christ's righteousness; interior renewal belongs to sanctification and must not become the basis of the verdict.
2. **Popular wording heard.** “Acto judicial”; “declarado justo”; “por imputación”; Catholic accounts “mezclan santificación y justificación.”
3. **Independent speakers.**
   - Carlos Veloz announces a case for imputation at [35:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=2130s) and charges the Catholic position with mixing sanctification and justification at [01:14:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=4466s).
   - Edgar Pacheco defines justification as legal or declarative, without inherent or progressive merit, at [15:03–15:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=903s) and contrasts external righteousness with ontological change at [18:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=1116s).
   - Salvador Barragán says God declares the believer just and that the person receives righteousness at [43:33–44:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOIDksoeeqc&t=2613s).
4. **Catholic answer heard.** Nelson Clemente describes justifying faith as involving interior renewal and active obedience at [29:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=1781s). His later “initial/media/final” vocabulary is his own presentation, not an official Catholic formula.
5. **Official objector anchors.** Westminster Confession XI.1 says God justifies “not by infusing righteousness” but by pardoning and accounting people righteous for Christ and imputing his obedience and satisfaction ([OPC edition](https://opc.org/wcf.html)). New Hampshire Confession V says Christ's righteousness is freely imputed through faith (William J. McGlothlin, ed., *Baptist Confessions of Faith*, 1911, p. 299). Baptist Faith and Message IV.B calls justification God's gracious and full acquittal ([SBC text](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/)).
6. **Catholic delimitation.** Trent VI, chapter 7 and canon 11 describe justification as remission and sanctification or renewal through received grace. CCC 1999–2000 defines sanctifying grace as participation in divine life and a habitual gift ([Catechism §§1996–2000](https://www.vatican.va/archive/catechism_sp/p3s1c3a2_sp.html)).
7. **Dossier overlap.** Official-layer objection 2.
8. **Verdict.** **PASS — 3 independent named speakers; Catholic answer heard; Reformed and Baptist anchors; exact current Catholic target.**

### J-3 — Merit and cooperation turn grace into a debt and detract from Christ's sufficient work

1. **Neutral formulation.** No human work can merit pardon or eternal life before God. Making grace-enabled works meritorious for eternal life appears to make God a debtor and add human merit to Christ's sufficient accomplishment.
2. **Popular wording heard.** “Le agregás a la única obra de Cristo tus propios méritos y obras”; merit “convierte a Dios en deudor.”
3. **Independent speakers.**
   - Carlos Veloz says the Catholic account adds personal merits and works to Christ's one work at [01:29:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=5396s) and answers “no” when asked whether works are required for eternal life at [01:32:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=5528s).
   - Edgar Pacheco argues merit *de condigno* turns grace into a transaction and God into a debtor at [24:57–25:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=1497s).
   - Salvador Barragán says adding works contradicts the passive divine act at [44:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOIDksoeeqc&t=2685s). His later claim that Catholic practices “trample” Christ's blood is not retained as neutral wording.
4. **Catholic answer heard.** Dante Urbina says God does not need human help; God graciously includes works performed in Christ at [01:35:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=5735s). José Placencia locates Christian obedience within grace and the Spirit at [01:07:18–01:13:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T8Fo-HFoNDg&t=4038s).
5. **Official objector anchors.** Westminster Confession XVI.5 says even the best works cannot merit pardon or eternal life because of the disproportion with glory and the infinite distance between creature and Creator ([OPC edition](https://opc.org/wcf.html)). Apology IV.73 excludes an “opinion of merit” ([Book of Concord](https://bookofconcord.org/defense/of-justification/)).
6. **Catholic delimitation.** CCC 2007 says there is no strict merit by a creature before God; §§2008–2011 ground Christian merit in prior grace and adoption, and say no one can merit initial grace ([Catechism §§2006–2011](https://www.vatican.va/archive/catechism_sp/p3s1c3a2_sp.html)). Trent VI, chapters 5, 8, and 16 and canons 4 and 32 distinguish unmerited initial justification from grace-enabled cooperation and reward.
7. **Dossier overlap.** Official-layer objections 3 and 6.
8. **Verdict.** **PASS — 3 independent named speakers; Catholic answer heard; Lutheran and Reformed anchors; corrected against current Catholic teaching.**

### J-4 — Justification is definitive; James 2 shows or confirms an existing faith

1. **Neutral formulation.** God's justifying verdict is complete rather than progressive or repeatedly increased. James 2 describes the public vindication, perfection, or evidential fruit of the faith by which Abraham was already justified, not an additional instrument.
2. **Popular wording heard.** “Una vez y para siempre”; “acto único, definitivo y completo”; James's works “confirmaron lo que ya había ocurrido.”
3. **Independent speakers.**
   - Carlos Veloz says believers have been justified once for all at [01:27:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=5238s); on James, works show a living faith and salvation already received at [01:44:20–01:45:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=6260s).
   - Edgar Pacheco says “ya fuimos justificados” in a unique, definitive, complete act at [20:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=1226s), then interprets James's works as evidence rather than the declarative act at [52:22–55:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=3142s).
   - Salvador Barragán calls Genesis 15:6 a complete, punctual, definitive action at [01:58:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOIDksoeeqc&t=7114s) and says James confirms what had already occurred rather than progressive justification at [01:59:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOIDksoeeqc&t=7168s).
4. **Catholic answer heard.** Dante Urbina asks where Scripture says justification happens once for all and appeals to the same *dikaioō* vocabulary in James at [01:41:14–01:44:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=6074s). Nelson Clemente distinguishes an unrepeatable initial act from subsequent Christian life at [40:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=2401s), but his extended staging terminology is not adopted.
5. **Official objector anchors.** Westminster Confession XI.1–2 presents justification as God's act received through faith, while XI.5 allows restoration without falling from the state of justification ([OPC edition](https://opc.org/wcf.html)). Westminster Larger Catechism 73 distinguishes faith's receiving role from accompanying works. Baptist Faith and Message IV.B calls justification a “full acquittal” ([SBC text](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/)); New Hampshire V similarly describes peace and favor through imputed righteousness (McGlothlin 1911, p. 299).
6. **Catholic delimitation.** Trent VI, chapter 10 and canon 24 speak of growth in received justice; chapters 14–16 address restoration and perseverance. Joint Declaration §§15–17 locates the shared basis in God's forgiving and renewing action; §§25–27 records different Catholic and Lutheran emphases on faith, renewal, and works ([Vatican Spanish text](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/chrstuni/documents/rc_pc_chrstuni_doc_31101999_cath-luth-joint-declaration_sp.html)).
7. **Dossier overlap.** Official-layer objections 4 and 5; James 2 is the recurrent debate vehicle.
8. **Verdict.** **PASS — 3 independent named speakers; Catholic answer heard; Reformed and Baptist anchors; current Catholic target.**

### J-5 — In conversion the person receives God's action passively and does not cooperate until renewed

1. **Neutral formulation.** The Holy Spirit alone effects conversion. Before regeneration the person contributes no spiritual capacity or cooperation; only once renewed can the person respond in the new life.
2. **Popular wording heard.** “El hombre no coopera”; “solamente somos espectadores de la gracia divina”; passive-voice verbs place God as actor.
3. **Independent speakers.**
   - Carlos Veloz says the person has no prior spiritual “budget” with which to collaborate with God at [02:02:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=7321s).
   - Edgar Pacheco calls the believer a passive recipient at [16:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=1005s) and says “solamente somos espectadores de la gracia divina” at [02:08:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=7700s).
   - Salvador Barragán argues from the passive voice that God justifies while the person does not cooperate at [40:44–42:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOIDksoeeqc&t=2444s) and says “el hombre no hace nada” at [44:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOIDksoeeqc&t=2654s).
4. **Catholic answer heard.** Dante Urbina answers that grace-enabled cooperation gives glory to Christ rather than competing with him at [01:42:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmMV212gzqQ&t=6130s). Nelson Clemente argues for a real, grace-enabled response rather than autonomous merit at [29:41–42:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eg9dl70R-gY&t=1781s).
5. **Official objector anchors.** Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration II.89–90 says conversion is entirely the Holy Spirit's work and the unregenerate person works nothing but undergoes it, while denying that the person is literally stone or block ([Book of Concord](https://bookofconcord.org/solid-declaration/free-will/)). Westminster Confession X.2 says the person is altogether passive in effectual calling until quickened and renewed ([OPC edition](https://opc.org/wcf.html)). Luther's Small Catechism, Creed III says a person cannot by personal reason or strength believe in or come to Christ ([Book of Concord](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/the-creed/)).
6. **Catholic delimitation.** Trent VI, chapter 5 and canon 4 affirm prevenient grace and deny self-movement toward justice, while rejecting that the will does not cooperate at all. CCC 1993 says justification establishes cooperation between grace and freedom ([Catechism §1993](https://www.vatican.va/archive/catechism_sp/p3s1c3a2_sp.html)). CDF response to the Joint Declaration §3 says Catholic *cooperatio* is itself an effect of grace and is not “merely passive” ([Vatican response](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19980625_cath-luth-joint-declaration_en.html)).
7. **Dossier overlap.** Official-layer objection 7.
8. **Verdict.** **PASS — 3 independent named speakers; Catholic answer heard; Lutheran and Reformed anchors; exact current Catholic target.**

#### D6 · Jordan B. Cooper with Jimmy Akin (`jkgsD6zNX2o`, 168K) — partial read through 51:05

- **Identity and scope [verified].** Cooper identifies himself as a Lutheran theologian and ordained Lutheran and describes his work in classical Lutheran theology (1:37–3:04): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jkgsD6zNX2o&t=97s. Akin identifies himself as a Catholic Answers senior apologist (3:04–4:04). This is an organized question-led dialogue specifically on justification, not a reupload or reaction.
- **Methodological delimiters.** Cooper explicitly rejects the caricature that Trent/Catholic teaching is Pelagian and the opposite caricature that Protestants regard good works or avoidance of mortal sin as optional (4:27–5:28): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jkgsD6zNX2o&t=267s. He also distinguishes his confessional Lutheran account from the U.S. Reformed majority framing (5:36–6:29). These caveats materially confirm the first pass's rule against a generic “Protestant view.”
- **J-2: forensic/imputed righteousness.** Cooper says justifying righteousness is Christ's righteousness imputed, counted, or clothing the believer; he acknowledges that no single verse states the whole formula and presents it as a synthesis of Pauline texts (12:04–14:21): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jkgsD6zNX2o&t=724s. He later distinguishes passive righteousness received for salvation from active righteousness performed for the neighbour under God's law (20:01–22:33): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jkgsD6zNX2o&t=1201s. This adds one independent, transcript-verified Lutheran person to J-2.
- **J-4 nuance: ongoing but forensic justification.** Cooper says historic Lutheran sources can call justification a daily or repeatedly given declaration, not only an entrance event; what remains excluded is progressive internal transformation as the basis of that verdict (22:41–26:02): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jkgsD6zNX2o&t=1361s. He uses Abraham and David in Romans 4 to argue that the cited justifications concern already-believing people (24:55–25:55). This supports J-4's forensic/transformative distinction but corrects the first-pass shorthand “definitive act”: it cannot be attributed without qualification to confessional Lutheranism.
- **Catholic answer heard so far.** Akin distinguishes initial forgiveness/renewal from growth in righteousness, uses Abraham's Genesis 12/15/22 sequence, and argues that Trent did not condemn every possible use of “faith alone” but faith severed from charity (14:21–19:53; 26:08–33:41). These are apologetic explanations. The official answer remains Trent VI.7–10 and canons 9/11/24, CCC 1991–2000, and the Joint Declaration; later portions of the transcript still need review before this entry is complete.
- **Romans 4 objection.** Cooper presses that Abraham in Romans 4 is already a believer, yet Paul's contrast with works and Psalm 32's forgiveness language still looks forensic rather than like internal growth (33:59–36:40): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jkgsD6zNX2o&t=2039s. This is a precise Lutheran form of J-2/J-4 rather than a new family. Akin answers with his own exegesis: “works of the law” are Mosaic-law obedience, David exemplifies reacquired righteousness after sin, and Genesis 15 exemplifies grace-enabled growth (36:49–43:18). The latter is an apologetic exegesis; it must not be presented as if every detail were defined by Trent.
- **J-1: faith alone means trusting reception of Christ, not bare assent.** Cooper defines faith as knowledge, assent, and especially trust/reliance; it justifies not by its virtue or merit but because it grasps Christ and receives his righteousness (43:41–48:12): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jkgsD6zNX2o&t=2621s. He expressly rejects the reduction to saying a prayer and being permanently safe regardless of life. This adds Cooper as an independent Lutheran person to J-1 while also delimiting the objection through Apology IV's living/trusting faith rather than a generic evangelical slogan.
- **Catholic response on kinds of faith.** Akin agrees Scripture can use “faith” for bare doctrinal assent, assent plus trust, or faith working through charity, and begins distinguishing these senses from James 2, the Gospels, Romans 14 and Galatians 5 (48:12–51:05). The response continues beyond the current read boundary; do not treat it as complete yet.
