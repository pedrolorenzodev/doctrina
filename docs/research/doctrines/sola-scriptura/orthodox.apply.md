## Conventions and source boundary

- **Tradition:** Eastern Orthodox, with the map's Greek and Slavic/Russian usages kept distinct. The precise common boundary is fixed in `eucharist/orthodox.md`: received councils and canons, later received councils with their reception caveats, approved seventeenth-century confessions with approval and Orthodox critique, and Philaret.
- **Relevant documents:** Nicaea II's horos and canon 1; Trullo 2 and the canon lists it receives; Dositheus II,XII and Questions I–III; Mogila I.4; Philaret Q.16–35 and the Scripture-reading directions. Jeremiah's Answers remain part of the mapped confessional universe, not replaced by private Gennadius/Kritopoulos texts.
- **Doctrinal title:** “Sola Scriptura” is the comparison route. The opening describes Scripture and Tradition in the Orthodox texts' own order rather than defining the tradition by opposition to a Protestant formula.
- **Greek/Slavic difference:** Dositheus's canon formulation, the Russian catechism's treatment of books outside the Hebrew list, and Russian omissions of Dositheus's reading prohibition remain distinct. Neither witness silently edits the other.
- **Scope controls:** Crete 2016 itself is not a doctrine-page voice, though its list/reception informs the map. The ROC 2020 project is mentioned only. Contemporary statutes and private theologians are not substitute confessions. The 1848 encyclical retains patriarchal-synodal rank.
- **Print:** Dositheus Greek in Schaff II; Philaret in Blackmore's English translation in that volume. [Digitized witness](https://archive.org/details/creedschristendo02scha). The catalogue gives 1877 and the Archive copyright note mentions 1896; the copy's title-page year is not collated [unverified]. Pages below are printed running heads.
- **Spanish:** mapped searches have not identified a reusable PD Spanish edition of the confessions. Translations labelled **[traducción propia asistida por IA]** are from the printed Greek, unreviewed by Pedro [unverified]. English Philaret is not translated into Spanish through English.
- **Tags:** `[verified: scan]` means only the cited wording was read on a page image during drafting. `[unverified]` includes translations, web/metadata witnesses and uncollated authority or edition details. No verification, critic or apply pass is performed.
- **Scripture:** only the documents' own citations are listed. A biblical allusion does not acquire a verse number from the drafter's memory. No new argument for the tradition is composed.
- **Reuse:** historical editions follow the mapped PD boundary, without a fresh worldwide rights audit [unverified]. Modern criticism receives a link and short excerpts only.

## Qué enseña

### Definition: Scripture taught by God

**Dositheus II**, opening, Greek, Schaff II p. 402:

> τὴν θείαν καὶ ἱερὰν γραφὴν εἶναι θεοδίδακτον [verified: scan]

> «La divina y sagrada Escritura es enseñada por Dios». [traducción propia asistida por IA] [unverified]

The excerpt begins after the source's introductory confessional verb. The complete opening continues with the obligation to believe it without hesitation and with the Church's interpretation and handing on.

### QE-1 · Books written by the Spirit through prophets and apostles

**Philaret Q.19**, Schaff II p. 449, Blackmore English:

> Certain books written by the Spirit of God through men sanctified by God, called Prophets and Apostles. These books are commonly termed the Bible. [verified: scan]

Q.20 says that these sacred books deserve attention before all others. Neither answer is recast as a concession extracted from a denial of Scripture's authority.

### QE-2 · Revelation transmitted and preserved

**Philaret Q.16**, p. 448:

> By two channels—holy tradition and holy Scripture. [verified: scan]

The question asks how divine revelation is spread and preserved in the true Church. This is the catechism's own two-channel language; it is not replaced with a Western taxonomy of two independent sources.

### QE-3 · What the catechism calls Tradition

**Philaret Q.17**, p. 448:

> By the name holy tradition is meant the doctrine of the faith, the law of God, the sacraments, and the ritual as handed down by the true believers and worshipers of God by word and example from one to another, and from generation to generation. [verified: scan]

Q.18 identifies the Church as its repository and prints 1 Timothy 3:15. The catechism's received Irenaeus quotation stays a patristic witness inside this document, not a modern confessional identity assigned to Irenaeus.

### QE-4 · Scripture preserved exactly

**Philaret Q.22**, p. 449:

> To this end, that divine revelation might be preserved more exactly and unchangeably. [verified: scan]

The answer continues that Scripture gives the prophets' and apostles' words as though the reader were hearing them. No claim about every manuscript, translation or later publisher is added to this catechetical purpose statement.

### QE-5 · Tradition agreeing with Scripture

**Philaret Q.23**, p. 449, after its introductory first-person phrase:

> that tradition which agrees with the divine revelation and with holy Scripture, as is taught us by holy Scripture itself. [verified: scan]

The next sentence prints 2 Thessalonians 2:15. This qualification is retained beside Q.16–17, rather than presenting every inherited custom as a divine command.

### QE-6 · Interpretation in the Church

**Dositheus II**, Greek, p. 402:

> οὐκ ἄλλως μέντοι ἀλλ' ἢ ὡς ἡ καθολικὴ ἐκκλησία ταύτην ἡρμήνευσε καὶ παρέδωκεν. [verified: scan]

> «Sin embargo, no de otro modo que como la Iglesia católica la interpretó y la transmitió». [traducción propia asistida por IA] [unverified]

“Catholic Church” here is the confession's own ecclesial term. Its historical polemical continuation is not imported as an unattributed description of current Protestant readers.

### QE-7 · Canon witnesses are not merged

The map locates Dositheus Question III and Philaret Q.31–35 and records a difference concerning books outside the Hebrew list. Trullo 2 receives more than one earlier canonical list. The dossier retains those separately; it does not choose a single list on behalf of the tradition (`traditions/orthodox.md` §§5.1–5.2; verification F1,F3,E6) [unverified: full canonical corpus not collated].

## Por qué

### PQ-1 · The same Holy Spirit

**Dositheus II**, Greek, Schaff II p. 403:

> Ἑνὸς γὰρ καὶ τοῦ αὐτοῦ ἁγίου πνεύματος ὄντος ἀμφοτέρων δημιουργοῦ, ἴσον ἐστὶ πάντως ὑπὸ τῆς γραφῆς καὶ ὑπὸ τῆς καθολικῆς ἐκκλησίας διδάσκεσθαι. [verified: scan]

> «Pues, siendo uno y el mismo Espíritu Santo el autor de ambas, es ciertamente igual ser enseñado por la Escritura y por la Iglesia católica». [traducción propia asistida por IA] [unverified]

The antecedents are the Scripture and the Church's testimony in the preceding sentence. The argument is Dositheus's; no extra inference about every individual cleric or every later custom is made.

### PQ-2 · The prior handing on

**Philaret Q.21**, p. 449:

> The most ancient and original instrument for spreading divine revelation is holy tradition. [verified: scan]

The same answer describes Christ's teaching by word and example and the apostles' earliest preaching. These are the catechism's own historical reasons; this drafting pass does not independently verify that history.

### PQ-3 · The purpose of writing

Philaret Q.22's reason is quoted under QE-4: more exact and unchanging preservation. Q.19 identifies the writers as prophets and apostles acting through the Spirit. The source does not force a choice between writing's purpose and the prior oral handing on.

### PQ-4 · Scripture's own instruction about Tradition

**Philaret Q.23**, p. 449:

> Therefore, brethren, stand fast, and hold the traditions which ye have been taught, whether by word or our epistle. [verified: scan]

The source prints 2 Thessalonians 2:15 immediately after this quotation. No other Pauline passage is imported to strengthen the answer.

### PQ-5 · Understanding, sacraments and rites

**Philaret Q.24**, p. 449:

> As a guide to the right understanding of holy Scripture, for the right ministration of the sacraments, and the preservation of sacred rites and ceremonies in the purity of their original institution. [verified: scan]

The continuation receives Basil's discussion of written and unwritten observances. It is kept as the catechism's patristic citation, not a freestanding quotation assigned to modern Orthodoxy.

### PQ-6 · Canon and received witnesses

**Philaret Q.32**, p. 451:

> Because, as the Apostle Paul says, unto them were committed the oracles of God; and the sacred books of the Old Testament have been received from the Hebrew Church of that Testament by the Christian Church of the New. [verified: scan]

The printed reference is Romans 3:2. This explains this catechism's enumeration; it does not settle the different wording of Dositheus Question III.

Mogila I.4 is located by Overbeck and the map's verification F2 as a witness to apostolic handing on and conciliar exposition. Its received Greek page was not read in this draft [unverified]; no additional quotation is translated through English.

## Cómo se vive

### Reading and hearing: the dated Greek discipline

**Dositheus Question I**, Greek, Schaff II p. 433, question and first answer:

> Εἰ δεῖ τὴν θείαν γραφὴν κοινῶς παρὰ πάντων τῶν Χριστιανῶν ἀναγινώσκεσθαι; Οὔ. [verified: scan]

> «¿Debe la divina Escritura ser leída indistintamente por todos los cristianos? No». [traducción propia asistida por IA] [unverified]

The following paragraph calls all Scripture inspired and useful, then restricts reading to those trained in its investigation, teaching and reading; p. 434 distinguishes everyone's hearing from untrained reading, especially of some Old Testament parts. The restriction is recorded, not silently softened into a modern recommendation.

**Status:** this is the Greek Jerusalem 1672 witness. The map records omission of Question I's prohibition in Russian editions (`traditions/orthodox.md` §9.3; verification C6). The Russian edition itself has not been collated here [unverified]; neither continued universal enforcement nor a universal repeal is asserted.

### Depth and teaching

**Dositheus Question II**, Greek, p. 434, question:

> Εἰ σαφής ἐστιν ἡ γραφὴ πᾶσι τοῖς ἀναγινώσκουσι Χριστιανοῖς; [verified: scan]

> «¿Es clara la Escritura para todos los cristianos que la leen?». [traducción propia asistida por IA] [unverified]

The answer names the command to search, Paul's gift of teaching and Peter's statement about difficult parts of Paul's letters. No verse numbers are inserted where the Greek does not print them. The source's clarity question is distinct from a claim that no Christian can understand anything in Scripture.

### Canon: Greek confession

**Dositheus Question III**, Greek, p. 435, concluding predicate:

> κανονικὰ βιβλία κρίνομεν, καὶ ταῦτα τὴν ἱερὰν γραφὴν εἶναι ὁμολογοῦμεν. [verified: scan]

> «Los consideramos libros canónicos y confesamos que son Sagrada Escritura». [traducción propia asistida por IA] [unverified]

The paragraph names Wisdom of Solomon, Judith, Tobit, the Dragon, Susanna, Maccabees and Wisdom of Sirach. Its appeal is to received ecclesial handing on; the dossier does not convert that named list into an unexamined modern book count.

### Canon: Russian catechetical witness

**Philaret Q.31**, p. 451, excerpt:

> reckon them at twenty-two, agreeing therein with the Jews, who so reckon them in the original Hebrew tongue. [verified: scan]

The named subjects are Cyril of Jerusalem, Athanasius and John Damascene; their names are retained as witnesses cited by Philaret, not members of a modern tradition.

**Philaret Q.34**, same page, on Sirach and certain other books absent from that enumeration:

> Because they do not exist in the Hebrew. [verified: scan]

**Philaret Q.35**, pp. 451–452, excerpt:

> appointed of the Fathers to be read by proselytes who are preparing for admission into the Church. [verified: scan]

Those categories are not silently replaced by Dositheus's wording or by a Western “apocrypha” definition.

### Liturgical and catechetical reading

**Philaret Q.42**, p. 452, on Psalms:

> It is a perfect manual of prayer and praise, and on this account is in continual use in the divine service of the Church. [verified: scan]

Q.22 describes hearing prophets and apostles in the scriptural text. Q.24 links interpretation with sacramental administration and received rites. These are documented forms of use, not statistics about Bible reading in Orthodox homes.

Neither the dossier nor one later catechism adjudicates their different enumerations. No complete canon-list table or silence finding is fabricated.

## Grado de autoridad

### Conciliar reception and the Spirit's teaching

**Dositheus XII**, Greek, Schaff II p. 417:

> οὕτω καὶ ἡ ἐκκλησία διδάσκεται μὲν ὑπὸ τοῦ ζωαρχικοῦ πνεύματος ἀλλὰ διὰ μέσου τῶν ἁγίων πατέρων καὶ διδασκάλων [verified: scan]

> «Así también la Iglesia es enseñada por el Espíritu que da vida, pero por medio de los santos padres y maestros». [traducción propia asistida por IA] [unverified]

The parenthesis that follows names the holy ecumenical synods as their rule. This qualification is retained; the confession does not make an isolated Father's sentence an independently infallible definition.

### Approval, critique and edition scope

Dositheus is the confession of Jerusalem 1672; Mogila is the Greek revision confirmed by the patriarchal act of 11 March 1643; Philaret is the 1839 Russian synodal catechism. Approval and publication particulars are mapped in `verification-orthodox.md` F1–F3, not freshly collated here [unverified].

Krivoshein's 1961 [Orthodox critique, §VI](https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Vasilij_Krivoshein/simvolicheskie-teksty-v-pravoslavnoj-tserkvi/) distinguishes the confessions' historical authority from irreformable universal doctrinal tests. It was fetched once for the Orthodox drafting pass. The host's modern introductory criticism of Krivoshein is not his own text; no site annotation is quoted as his judgment [unverified: web witness].

The reading prohibition's Russian omission and the canon-language differences are matters of documented witnesses, not a harmonization written by the drafter. Crete's reception does not confer one identical rank on every item of the map.
