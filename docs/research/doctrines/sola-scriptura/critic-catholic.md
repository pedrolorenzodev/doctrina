## A. Neutrality: the site's own voice

### 1 · Four Scripture rows state the Catholic reading as the event — **must-fix (wording)**

**Where:** §3 table, rows Jds 3, 1 Tim 3:15, 1 Tim 6:20 / 2 Tim 1:12-14, Col 3:16.
**Problem:** The papacy critic's 1, same section. Jds 3: «la Iglesia la guarda y no le añade nada; lo que crece es su comprensión»: an evangelical reader holds that the Church *has* added; "la Iglesia la guarda" is the Catholic claim stated as fact with the citation at the end. 1 Tim 3:15: «La Iglesia, "columna y fundamento de la verdad", guarda la memoria de las palabras de Cristo»: on a Catholic tradition page "la Iglesia" is read as the Catholic Church, and Westminster reads the same verse as a proof text for the Church's *testimony* to Scripture (WCF 1.5, proof k) `[verified in D]`. 1 Tim 6:20: «El "depósito" de la fe, contenido en la Tradición y en la Escritura»: "contenido en la Tradición y en la Escritura" is the Catholic reading of the deposit (CEC 84), not the verse. Col 3:16: «Por la Tradición, el Espíritu hace "que la palabra de Cristo habite…"»: "por la Tradición" is DV 8's gloss, stated as the verse's meaning. The rows that quote and attribute (Mt 28, 2 Tes 2:15, Hch 2:42, Lc 2:19, Jn 20:31, 1 Tes 2:13, Lc 24:45, Rom 12:6, Heb 1:1-2, Flp 3:8) and the two that already open «para la Iglesia» are fine.
**Fix:** Jds 3: «Para la Iglesia católica, "la fe transmitida a los santos de una vez para siempre" se guarda sin añadirle nada; lo que crece es su comprensión (Dei Verbum 8; Catecismo 171).» 1 Tim 3:15: «El Catecismo lee aquí a la Iglesia como "columna y fundamento de la verdad", que guarda la memoria de las palabras de Cristo (Catecismo 171).» 1 Tim 6:20: «El Catecismo lee el "depósito" como lo contenido en la Tradición y en la Escritura, confiado por los apóstoles al conjunto de la Iglesia (Catecismo 84).» Col 3:16: «Dei Verbum 8 cita este versículo para decir que, por la Tradición, el Espíritu hace "que la palabra de Cristo habite en ellos abundantemente".»

### 2 · Three answer sentences in the site's voice: a charge not made, a verdict on the objection, a frame the objector never states — **must-fix (wording)**

**Where:** §4 OB-1 answer, first sentence; OB-3 answer, first sentence; OB-5 answer, second paragraph.
**Problem:** (a) OB-1 opens «La Iglesia católica no pone a la Escritura en un lugar menor.» The objection (FC Ep RN 1, WLC 3) says Scripture is the *sole* judge; it does not say Rome demotes Scripture. Answering a charge not made, unattributed, is the Eucharist critic's 3 (b): a rhetorical move to gain the reader before the quotes begin. (b) OB-3 opens «La Iglesia católica distingue dos cosas que la objeción une.» "Que la objeción une" is the site judging the objection as a confusion; AC XXVIII.30–35 distinguishes rites kept "for good order" from traditions imposed as necessary for salvation `[verified: run copy ss-cat/dl/boc_ac28.txt, ¶30–35]`, so the objector does not "unite" them either. (c) OB-5: «Para la Iglesia, ese Espíritu no habla solo al lector individual». WCF 1.10 says the supreme judge is "the Holy Spirit speaking in the scripture", and WCF 31.3 (1647) gives synods authority "ministerially to determine controversies of faith" whose decrees "are to be received with reverence and submission" `[verified: OCR, 1894 print p. 97; finding 7]`. "Al lector individual" is a frame the objection never states; a Presbyterian reader sees his confession reduced to private judgment, which it expressly rejects.
**Fix:** (a) Start OB-1 with the second sentence: «Juan Pablo II la llama…» (and apply the expert's 6 on UUS 79), or «La Iglesia católica responde que no pone a la Escritura en un lugar menor: …». (b) «La Iglesia católica distingue la Tradición apostólica de las tradiciones eclesiásticas: la Tradición que enseña como palabra de Dios "es la que viene de los apóstoles…"». (c) «Para la Iglesia, el oficio de interpretar auténticamente la palabra de Dios "ha sido confiado únicamente al Magisterio vivo de la Iglesia"…», dropping the clause on the individual reader.

## B. Caricature: would each objector recognize the objection?

### 6 · OB-5 synthesis turns Westminster's perspicuity into «cualquiera lo entienda» — **must-fix (wording)**

**Where:** §4 OB-5, Síntesis, first sentence.
**Problem:** «Lo necesario para la salvación está dicho con claridad suficiente para que cualquiera lo entienda con los medios ordinarios.» WCF 1.7 says "not only the learned, but the unlearned, in a due use of the ordinary means, may attain unto a sufficient understanding of them" `[verified in D; OCR p. 20]`. "Cualquiera lo entienda" is the caricature of perspicuity ("anyone can understand the Bible") that Catholic apologetics usually answers and that a Reformed scholar would refuse to sign; the Confession's own sentence is conditioned (due use of the means) and bounded (a sufficient understanding of what is necessary).
**Fix:** «Lo necesario para la salvación está propuesto en la Escritura con tanta claridad que no solo los doctos, sino también los no instruidos, usando debidamente los medios ordinarios, alcanzan un conocimiento suficiente de ello.» The 1880 Spanish is already in D: «no solo los eruditos, sino aun los que no lo son, pueden alcanzar un conocimiento suficiente de ellas, por el debido uso de los medios ordinarios» `[verified in D]`.

### 7 · OB-5 shows "councils may err" without Westminster's and Augsburg's positive doctrine of church authority, so the objection reads as "examine everything yourself" — **must-fix (balance rule)**

**Where:** §4 OB-5, Síntesis and "Their own texts" (WCF 31.4 quoted; 31.3 absent; AC XXVIII.21–22 only in a trailing note).
**Problem:** The strongest honest form of the Reformed objection is not "councils err, so the believer judges"; it is "synods have real, ministerial authority under the Word, and still may err". WCF 31.3 (1647 numbering; 31.2 in the American text) `[verified: OCR, 1894 print p. 97]`: "It belongeth to synods and councils ministerially to determine controversies of faith, and cases of conscience; to set down rules and directions for the better ordering of the publick worship of God, and government of his church; to receive complaints in cases of mal-administration, and authoritatively to determine the same: which decrees and determinations, if consonant to the word of God, are to be received with reverence and submission, not only for their agreement with the word, but also for the power whereby they are made, as being an ordinance of God, appointed thereunto in his word." Spanish, Monterrey 1880, cap. XXXI, II, p. 155 `[verified: OCR]`: «Pertenece á los sínodos y á los concilios el decidir ministerialmente cuestiones con respecto de la fé, el resolver casos de conciencia, el asentar reglas [OCR: «regias»] é instrucciones [OCR: «instrueciones»] para el mejor gobierno en el culto público, y la disciplina de la iglesia de Dios, el oir y el determinar autoritativamente quejas respecto de los casos de mala administracion y sus decretos y decisiones cuando concuerden con la palabra de Dios, deben recibirse con reverencia y sumision, no solo por ser conformes á esta palabra, sino tambien porque la autoridad de dichos sínodos y concilios viene de Dios [OCR: «Dius»], pues así lo ordena en su palabra.» The Reformed map lists WCF 31.2–3 in this locus's row («synods "ministerially to determine"») `[verified in map, reformed.md l. 173]`; D read 31.4 only. On the Lutheran side AC XXVIII.21–22 says congregations "of necessity and by divine right must obey" bishops who teach the Gospel, citing Lk 10:16 `[verified: run copy boc_ac28.txt ¶21–22]`, the very verse the Catholic answer uses (CEC 87); D keeps it in a note. Without both, the Catholic answer's Lk 10:16 looks like a text the objectors ignore, and the objection looks like private judgment (finding 2c).
**Fix:** Add WCF 31.3 (1647) to "Their own texts" with the 1880 Spanish (the OCR readings above to be checked on the scan, p. 155), and AC XXVIII.21–22 as a quoted block, not a note. Synthesis: «Los sínodos y concilios deciden "ministerialmente" las controversias, y sus decretos, "cuando concuerden con la palabra de Dios, deben recibirse con reverencia y sumisión"; pero todos pueden errar y muchos han errado, por eso no son la regla de la fe sino una ayuda…». Locator per DECISIONS 2026-10-15: «31.3 (31.2 en el texto americano)».

### 8 · OB-2 quotes WCF 1.6 up to "traditions of men" and cuts the two clauses that condition it — **must-fix (clipped quote of the objector)**

**Where:** §4 OB-2, "Their own texts", WCF 1.6; echoed in the OB-2 and OB-3 syntheses.
**Problem:** 1.6 continues `[verified: OCR, 1894 print p. 20]`: "Nevertheless, we acknowledge the inward illumination of the Spirit of God to be necessary for the saving understanding of such things as are revealed in the word; and that there are some circumstances concerning the worship of God, and government of the Church, common to human actions and societies, which are to be ordered by the light of nature and Christian prudence, according to the general rules of the word, which are always to be observed." The first clause is what the Catholic answers in OB-4 (DV 5, expert 10) and OB-5 (the Spirit and the reader) are talking to; the second is the Reformed counterpart of CCC 83's «"tradiciones"… nacidas en el transcurso del tiempo» and of AC XV's rites kept "for good order": Westminster concedes that church order is settled by prudence under the Word. Cut, the Reformed position reads as "nothing but the text", which the Confession denies in the same paragraph, and the OB-2 synthesis («no se le debe añadir nada») and OB-3 synthesis stand on half a sentence.
**Fix:** Quote 1.6 whole (English and the Monterrey 1880 Spanish, p. 9, which continues after the sentence D read on the scan; to be read there). Synthesis OB-2, add: «…ni por tradiciones humanas; aunque la comprensión salvadora de lo revelado necesita "la iluminación interior del Espíritu", y las circunstancias del culto y del gobierno de la Iglesia se ordenan "por la luz de la naturaleza y la prudencia cristiana" según las reglas generales de la Palabra.»

### 9 · OB-3 synthesis puts the Lutheran merit clause in Reformed and Baptist mouths and the Westminster conscience clause in Lutheran ones — **must-fix (wording)**

**Where:** §4 OB-3, Síntesis; "Quién la plantea: luteranos y reformados; bautistas (por exclusión)".
**Problem:** The justification critic's 9 pattern. «quien enseña que con ellas se merece la gracia oscurece el Evangelio» is AC XV, Ap XV.4 and SA III.15 (finding 11) `[verified in D; sa3_15b.txt]`; WCF 20.2 and BF&M XVII say nothing of merit. «Solo Dios es Señor de la conciencia» is WCF 20.2 / BF&M XVII `[verified in D]`; the Lutheran texts say consciences are "not to be burdened", a different sentence. «Las ceremonias… pueden guardarse por buen orden» is AC XV; the Reformed form is WCF 1.6's "circumstances… ordered by… Christian prudence" (finding 8).
**Fix:** «…como si fueran necesarias para la salvación. Los luteranos añaden que quien enseña que con ellas se merece la gracia oscurece el Evangelio; los reformados y los bautistas, que "solo Dios es Señor de la conciencia".» Keep "Quién la plantea" as is.

## Verdict

The Catholic teaching is reported in the Church's words, graded on its own categories with the inferences tagged, and the Fathers stay inside the Catechism and Vatican I. The page's framing (the Church's position first, then what it teaches instead) works: the rejection is said once, in DV 9's words, and the column reads as a teaching, not as a list of "no". The breaches in the site's voice are small and few: four Scripture rows that state the Catholic reading as the event, one answer that rebuts a charge no objector made, one that calls the objection a confusion, one that reduces Westminster to the individual reader, and a heading that has the Church call evangelicalism a religion of the book. The fairness problems are larger and run mostly one way, against the objectors: perspicuity is caricatured as «cualquiera lo entienda»; Westminster's doctrine of synods (31.3) and the second half of 1.6 are absent, so the Reformed position looks like private judgment and "nothing but the text"; OB-6's synthesis argues beyond the Confession; the Lutheran texts reach the site as fragments in AI Spanish from English, without the status line the decision requires, and the Book of Concord's sharpest text for OB-3 (SA III.15) was not read; the Baptists are translated by a machine when the SBC has its own Spanish. Two cuts run against the Church: OB-1 never shows that Trent anathematizes contempt of the traditions, and OB-6 shows the anathema with that half removed. And one objection (OB-4) ends under a canon its objector does not fall under. Quote 31.3 and 1.6 whole, read SA III.15 and WLC 4, restore the anathema's two limbs where each belongs, move can. II.4 out of OB-4, translate the Lutherans from the Latin and the Baptists from the SBC, add the LCMS/ELCA status line, split the Dogma labels of QE-4 and QE-5; then a Presbyterian, a confessional Lutheran, a Southern Baptist and an AG pastor would each recognize their objection on the page, a Catholic theologian would find his Church neither softened (OB-1) nor sharpened (OB-4), and a secular reader would find no label wider than its canon.
