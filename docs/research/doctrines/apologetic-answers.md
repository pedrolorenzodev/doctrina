## Justification

### OB-4 · Works of supererogation (Westminster XVI.4)

**The point** (dossier `justification/catholic.md` §4 OB-4, the marked note): WCF XVI.4, "They who in their obedience attain to the greatest height which is possible in this life, are so far from being able to supererogate, and to do more than God requires, as that they fall short of much which in duty they are bound to do" (as quoted in the dossier, WCF-1647 p. 57 `[verified: OCR]` there).

**1. Official texts searched (2026-10-21).**
- CCC 1973–1974 (vatican.va es, `p3s1c3a1_sp.html`) `[verified]`: «1973 Más allá de sus preceptos, la Ley nueva contiene los consejos evangélicos . La distinción tradicional entre mandamientos de Dios y consejos evangélicos se establece por relación a la caridad, perfección de la vida cristiana. Los preceptos están destinados a apartar lo que es incompatible con la caridad. Los consejos tienen por fin apartar lo que, incluso sin serle contrario, puede constituir un impedimento al desarrollo de la caridad»; 1974: «Los consejos evangélicos manifiestan la plenitud viva de una caridad que nunca se ve contenta por no poder darse más.» CCC 1986 quotes LG 42 on «los múltiples consejos que el Señor propone en el Evangelio» `[verified]`.
- CCC 1476–1477 (vatican.va es, `p2s2c2a4_sp.html`) `[verified]`, quoting *Indulgentiarum doctrina* 5: the treasury includes «las oraciones y las buenas obras de la Bienaventurada Virgen María y de todos los santos […] de manera que, trabajando en su propia salvación, cooperaron igualmente a la salvación de sus hermanos».
- Trent VI ch. 11 and can. 25 (already in the dossier's OB-3 answer, `[verified: OCR]` there; not re-read here).
- The word «supererogación» does not occur in the CCC pages fetched (string count 0 in `p3s1c3a1`, `p3s1c3a2`, `p2s2c2a4`) `[verified]`; the dossier records that Trent VI does not use it (string search 2026-10-18).
- **Result:** no official text answers WCF XVI.4's claim as such (that no one can do more than God requires). **But CCC 1973 states the ground the apologists use (precepts vs counsels, «más allá de sus preceptos»)**: it is an official text close enough to go into the official answer itself `[inference]`; see "For Pedro".

**2. Apologists.**
- **Catholic Answers Staff**, «Can you help me explain transubstantiation, supererogation, and the temporal power of the pope?», catholic.com Q&A, 2019-02-23, https://www.catholic.com/qa/can-you-help-me-explain-transubstantiation-supererogation-and-the-temporal-power-of-the-pope `[verified]`: "Supererogation is doing more than what is required. For example, in 1 Corinthians 7 Paul counsels the Corinthians that he who marries does well, but he who does not marry does better (7:38)."
- **Arthur Barnes**, "Evangelical Counsels", *The Catholic Encyclopedia*, vol. 4 (New York: Robert Appleton, 1908), https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04435a.htm `[verified]` (public domain): names the objection ("The opponents of the Catholic doctrine base their opposition on such texts as Luke 17:10") and answers from Matt 19:16-21 ("That was all that was necessary in the strict sense of the word […] 'If thou wilt be perfect, go sell what thou hast'"), Matt 19:12 and 1 Cor 7 (Paul "gives his 'counsel' in favour of the unmarried state").
- Searched without a third independent source: catholic.com (site search), Spanish apologetics («obras de supererogación», «consejos evangélicos» + «siervos inútiles»), Dave Armstrong's blog `[not found]`. Not searched: YouTube debates (the point is not in the Justification debate files), Bellarmine *De monachis* (Latin, not opened).
- **Shared by both (verified 2026-10-21):** supererogation is doing more than what is required; Paul gives a counsel, not a command, on celibacy (1 Cor 7: Catholic Answers cites 7:38, Barnes gives no verse). **Not shared:** Matt 19:17, 21 (the rich young man) is Barnes only, Catholic Answers does not cite Matthew; 1 Cor 7:25 is cited by neither (the drafter's addition, removed from the draft).

**3. Contested by a Catholic?** `[not found]` (searched only the pages above; not proof). Not searched: post-conciliar theologians who criticise a "two-tier" reading of the counsels in the light of LG 40's universal call to holiness.

**4. Applies official teaching?** Yes: CCC 1973–1974 (counsels beyond the precepts) and CCC 1986/LG 42. Nothing new added. The draft leaves out the treasury of merit (CCC 1477), which neither apologist uses for this verse.

**5. Draft (es, debate layer: «Respuesta frecuente en la apologética»).** Two independent apologists only (below the usual three): Pedro to decide.

> Los apologistas católicos responden que la supererogación es hacer más de lo que se exige, y señalan que Pablo da un consejo, no un mandamiento, sobre el celibato: "el que la da en casamiento, bien hace; y el que no la da en casamiento, hace mejor" (1 Corintios 7:38).

Verses: RV1909 `[verified]` (1CO 7:38). Matt 19:17, 21 left out of the draft (Barnes alone); the sentence identifying the point with CCC 1973 was the site's own voice and is removed.

**Respuesta oficial encontrada:** CCC 1973–1974 (consejos evangélicos «más allá de sus preceptos») → pasar a la capa oficial del dossier (pendiente, tras la revisión de Pedro).

## Eucharist

### OB-6 · The 1895 encyclical's claim on «the ancient rituals of Rome and Gaul»

**The point** (`eucharist/catholic.md` §4 OB-6, «Respuesta apologética pendiente»): 1895 Encyclical §X, "the precious gifts are consecrated after the prayer of the invocation of the Holy Ghost by the blessing of the priest, as the ancient rituals of Rome and Gaul testify; nevertheless afterwards the Papal Church made an innovation" (as quoted in the dossier, `[verified: OCR]` there).

**1. Official texts searched.** CEC 1333, 1353, 1106, 1375; *Sacramentum caritatis* 13, 48; Florence, *Exsultate Deo*; PCPCU 2001 (all already in the dossier's answer). Re-read today: CEC 1375 (vatican.va es, `p2s2c1a3_sp.html`) `[verified]`: «Los Padres de la Iglesia afirmaron con fuerza la fe de la Iglesia en la eficacia de la Palabra de Cristo y de la acción del Espíritu Santo para obrar esta conversión», quoting Chrysostom («Esto es mi Cuerpo , dice. Esta palabra transforma las cosas ofrecidas», *De proditione Iudae* 1, 6) and Ambrose («La palabra de Cristo, que pudo hacer de la nada lo que no existía, ¿no podría cambiar las cosas existentes en lo que no eran todavía?», *De mysteriis* 9, 50.52). **Result:** no official text discusses the historical claim about the old Roman and Gallican rites `[not found]`; CEC 1375 gives the patristic ground the apologists use.

**2. Apologists.**
- **Adrian Fortescue**, "Epiklesis", *The Catholic Encyclopedia*, vol. 5, 1909, https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05502a.htm `[verified]` (public domain). **Concedes the historical fact:** "Nor is there any doubt that the Western rites at one time contained similar invocations"; "The Roman Rite too at one time had an Epiklesis after the words of Institution" (Gelasius I). **Answers its meaning:** "people were not much concerned about the exact instant […] They looked upon the whole Consecration-prayer as one simple thing"; "In the West there has been a great unanimity in speaking of the words of Institution as consecrating, especially since St. Augustine"; Chrysostom, *De prod. Iudae* I.6, "This word changes the offering"; "There is no hint of an Epiklesis at the Last Supper."
- **Peter Kwasniewski**, «East-West Disagreements about the Epiclesis and Transubstantiation», *New Liturgical Movement*, 4 May 2020, https://www.newliturgicalmovement.org/2020/05/east-west-disagreements-about-epiclesis.html `[verified]`. Answers an Orthodox article on the "pre-Schism West": "It is certainly true that the Eucharistic Prayer was understood in a more holistic sense by our predecessors […] but it is certainly going too far to claim that granting a special status to the words of Christ spoken over the elements was a medieval scholastic development"; quotes Ludwig Ott (*Fundamentals of Catholic Dogma*, Baronius ed., p. 416) for Tertullian, Ambrose (*De sacr.* IV 4,14, "The words of Christ bring about this Sacrament") and Chrysostom.
- Seen, not counted: Brother André Marie, catholicism.org, 10 Jul 2019, https://catholicism.org/ad-rem-no-346.html `[verified]` (explains the epiclesis, explicitly sets the historical proofs aside; publisher is the Saint Benedict Center, whose standing should be checked before use `[inference]`). Not searched: YouTube (no Catholic–Orthodox debate on this point in the Eucharist debate files); Erick Ybarra.
- **Shared by both (verified 2026-10-21):** the ancients took the whole prayer as one (Fortescue «one simple thing»; Kwasniewski «more holistic sense»); Ambrose and Chrysostom attribute the change to Christ's words. **Not shared:** that the old Western rites had an invocation of the Spirit is Fortescue alone; Kwasniewski does not concede it (he reports Mosebach and Hunwicke and defends Hunwicke's no-epiclesis reading for the Roman Canon).

**3. Contested by a Catholic?** On the answer: `[not found]`. On the historical premise Catholics disagree among themselves: Fortescue says the Roman rite had an epiclesis; Kwasniewski reports (note 2) that Mosebach and Fr John Hunwicke dispute whether the Roman Canon has or needed one `[verified: as reported there]`. So the draft does not take a side on the Roman Canon.

**4. Applies official teaching?** Yes: CEC 1375 (the same two Fathers), CEC 1353 (words and Spirit together), Florence *Exsultate Deo* (the form is the Saviour's words). Nothing new; the draft does not say "only the words" against CEC 1353.

**5. Draft (es, debate layer).**

> Los apologistas católicos responden que los antiguos veían toda la plegaria eucarística como una sola, sin buscar el instante exacto del cambio. Señalan que, en Occidente y en Oriente, los Padres ya atribuían la conversión a las palabras de Cristo: san Ambrosio de Milán y san Juan Crisóstomo.

Removed from the draft: the sentence on the Spirit invocation in old Western rites (one source, and a second source argues the opposite for the Roman rite) and «en el siglo IV» (the drafter's date, in neither source); CCC 1375 is the near official text (confirmed), not an answer to the point. Fathers' dates for the page: Ambrose, *Sobre los misterios* c. 387–391; Chrysostom, *Homilías sobre la traición de Judas* 1, 388 `[snippet]` (both as in the dossier's PQ-4; the Chrysostom date is still a snippet there).

**6. Source window:** Adrian Fortescue, *The Catholic Encyclopedia*, 1909, "Epiklesis"; Peter Kwasniewski, *New Liturgical Movement*, 4-5-2020.
