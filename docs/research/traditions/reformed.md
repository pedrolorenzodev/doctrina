## 1. Scope

**What "Reformed" means for the site.** Churches that confess the Reformed confessions of the 16th–17th centuries (or, in the mainline bodies, a "book of confessions" built around them), with presbyterian-synodal government. The tradition splits into families with different normative documents: two main ones (A, B) plus the mainline "book of confessions" (C) and the Hungarian/Second Helvetic line (D). ## 2. How authority works (in the tradition's own terms)

1. **Scripture alone is the "only infallible rule of faith and practice"** (OPC FG III.3 and vow 1; PCA vow 1 adds "as originally given, ... inerrant"; CRC: "the only infallible rule for faith and life"; URCNA: "complete subjection and obedience to the Word of God ... inspired, infallible, and inerrant"; INPM Art. 12: "la suprema autoridad"; PC(USA) W-4.0404b: "the unique and authoritative witness to Jesus Christ") [verified]. Its authority "dependeth not upon the testimony of any man, or church; but wholly upon God" (WCF 1.4; Scots XIX; Belgic V) [verified]. The final judge of controversies is "the Holy Spirit speaking in the Scripture" (WCF 1.10) [verified].
2. **Confessions are "subordinate standards" (Westminster vocabulary) or "forms of unity" (Continental).** They are human, fallible, and bind because (and insofar as) they agree with Scripture: PCA Preface III, the Constitution is "subject to and subordinate to the Scriptures"; OPC FG XXXII.1 "subordinate to the Scriptures"; PC(USA) F-2.02 "subordinate standards ... nonetheless, standards"; Church of Scotland Art. II "principal subordinate standard"; URCNA: "the Reformed confessions do fully agree with this Word of God and therefore [we] do subscribe"; CRC: confessions "whose doctrines fully agree with the Word of God" [verified]. The confessions say this of themselves: Scots preface invites correction "fra the mouth of God, that is, fra his haly scriptures"; WCF 31.3 in the 1788/American numbering used by PCA and OPC (31.4 in the 1647 text) councils "may err ... not to be made the rule of faith, or practice; but to be used as a help in both" [verified; numbering corrected 2026-10-15, see §4A].
3. **Creeds and councils.** The ancient creeds are received "because they are in accordance with the Word of God" (Gallican V) / "willingly receive the three creeds ... likewise that which, conformable thereunto, is agreed upon by the ancient fathers" (Belgic IX) [verified]. Councils are a help, not a rule (WCF 31; SHC II places "the decrees and canons of councils" in "the same order" as the Fathers) [verified].
4. **Fathers.** Honoured as interpreters, never normative: "we do not despise the interpretations of the holy Greek and Latin fathers ... as far as they agree with the Scriptures; but we do modestly dissent from them when they are found to set down things differing from ... the Scriptures" (SHC II); "all decrees of councils, opinions of ancient writers ... are to be examined" by Scripture (WCF 1.10) [verified]. The site must not present any Father as Reformed; it can quote these statements as the tradition's own rule for how it reads the Fathers.
5. **Tradition** in the Catholic sense is rejected as an independent authority: nothing is to be added "by new revelations of the Spirit, or traditions of men" (WCF 1.6); IEE art. I: "no reconocemos carácter autoritativo, independiente de las Sagradas Escrituras, a la llamada Tradición Eclesiástica" [verified].
6. **Who defines and revises.** No magisterium. Church authority is "only ministerial and declarative" (INPM Art. 11; PCA Preliminary Principles 7: "All church power ... is only ministerial and declarative since the Holy Scriptures are the only rule of faith and practice", and BCO 11-2: "The jurisdiction of Church courts is only ministerial and declarative" [verified in verification-lutheran-reformed.md R-log 1, BCO 2026 PDF]. Corrected 2026-10-15: this item placed the phrase in BCO 3-2 [memory], which is about the powers of order and jurisdiction and does not contain it). Confessions are amended only by the body's assemblies with supermajorities of presbyteries: PCA 3/4 GA + 3/4 presbyteries + 3/4 next GA (BCO 26-3); PC(USA) study committee + 2/3 presbyteries (G-6.03); OPC: GA acts adding to the standards don't bind until passed as amendments (FG XV.9); INPM Arts. 352–356 [verified]. Assembly decisions in the Continental family are "settled and binding, unless it is proved that they are in conflict with the Word of God or the Church Order" (URCNA CO 29) [verified].
7. **What binds, and whom.** Confessions bind **office-bearers** (ministers, elders, deacons) by vow or signature; members are bound by profession of faith, not by subscription (URCNA distinguishes "verbal assent" of members from officers' signed subscription [snippet, Synod 2014 via search]). Degrees of subscription (see §3): strict (URCNA Form of Subscription: "all the articles and points of doctrine ... do fully agree with the Word of God"; CRC "without reservation": officebearers sign the Covenant for Officebearers "without reservation", Church Order Supplement Art. 5, per https://www.crcna.org/Synod2025FAQ; the phrase is not on the Covenant page itself (verification-lutheran-reformed.md R-log 8, S-R3)), system / good-faith (PCA BCO 21-4: exceptions allowed if "neither hostile to the system nor strikes at the vitals of religion"; OPC vow "as containing the system of doctrine"), essential-tenets (PC(USA): "the essential tenets of the Reformed faith as expressed in the confessions", "instructed and led by" them) [verified].
8. **Proof texts are not part of the standards** (preface to the OPC-published edition of the standards, republished by the PCA as WCFPreface.pdf: proof texts "are not a part of the constitution itself"; the sentence is in this edition's preface, not in the OPC Form of Government) [verified; locator clarified per verification-lutheran-reformed.md R-log 2]. The site should not quote proof-text lists as if they were the confession.

## 4. The canon, by tiers

Tier logic (the tradition's own): **T0 Scripture** (only infallible rule). **T1 Confessional standards** of a body (subscribed by officers; amendable only by constitutional process). **T2 Constitutional order and worship documents** (Form of Government, Directory, church order, liturgical forms; binding on practice, subordinate to T1). **T3 Received but non-subscribed testimonies** (contemporary testimonies, "confessional-status" interpretations, synodical position statements). **T4 Historic Reformed confessions** no longer the standard of a living body checked (witnesses, not norms). The tier of a document **depends on the body**: list below says which body puts it where. This tier set is closed per body (each body's constitution enumerates its standards); new documents enter only by constitutional amendment, which is the membership rule a later agent should apply.

## 5. Scripture

- **Canon:** 66 books. WCF 1.2 lists them; 1.3 "The books commonly called Apocrypha, not being of divine inspiration, are no part of the canon of the Scripture, and therefore are of no authority in the church of God, nor to be any otherwise approved, or made use of, than other human writings" [verified]. ## 7. Representative non-normative voices (bounded, labelled non-normative)

Rule from the tradition: no theologian is normative (only confessions are named in the standards; WCF 1.10 subjects "opinions of ancient writers" and "doctrines of men" to Scripture) [verified]. **Exception: INPM lists Calvin's *Institución* and Geneva Catechism among its "Símbolos doctrinales"** [verified]. 2. **Zacharias Ursinus**, *Commentary on the Heidelberg Catechism* (Williard trans. 1851; PD, PM): exposition by a principal author [verified CRC intro credits Ursinus with the team].

5. **Francis Turretin**, *Institutio theologiae elencticae* (1679–85): Princeton's textbook [snippet].
6. **Charles Hodge**, *Systematic Theology* (1871–73), and A. A. Hodge, *Commentary on the Confession of Faith* (1869; Spanish Arellano 1897) [verified Spanish ed., PM].
7. **Herman Bavinck**, *Gereformeerde Dogmatiek* (1895–1901) [memory: standard Dutch Reformed dogmatics; English © 2003–08].
8. **Louis Berkhof**, *Systematic Theology* (1932/1938, CRC's Calvin Seminary) [snippet: named with Hodge as Turretin's successor textbook].

## 9. Disputed points

1. **Which WCF text is "the" Westminster Confession**: 1647, 1788, 1903, OPC 1956 (PCA follows), PC(USA) with further edits [verified]. Not a scholarly dispute but a real divergence among bodies; affects 20.4, 22.3, 23.3, 24.4, 25.6, 31 numbering, and the 1903 chapters 34–35.
2. **Strict vs system vs good-faith subscription**: PCA adopted good-faith subscription in 2003 [verified year: BCO 21-4 amendment approved at the 31st GA, https://www.pcahistory.org/pca/ga/actions/31stGA_2003_Actions.pdf (verification-lutheran-reformed.md S-R1); that report does not itself use "good faith", the label heads the 2002 proposal [snippet]]; URCNA scholars call their form "strict subscription" while denying it implies infallibility [verified, Mid-America]; CRC debate on "without reservation" (Synods 2022–2025) [verified]. Historical root: American Adopting Act of 1729 [memory].
3. **Anti-Roman Catholic passages** (HC Q80; WCF 25.6, 29; SHC II, III, XVII, XX; Scots XVIII, XXII): CRC brackets Q80 as not binding (2006); RCA keeps full text; PC(USA) asterisks them as "not the position" [verified]. The site must quote the text **and** state each body's status.
4. **Belgic Art. 36** (magistrate): CRC revised 1938/1985 [verified]; others keep the original [memory].
5. **Rejection of Errors in Dort**: not confessional in RCA [verified].
6. **INPM list inconsistency**: Art. 32 includes the Belgic Confession and Calvin's catechism; Art. 351 omits the Belgic [verified]. Also the 2002 constitution may since have been revised (not verified).
7. **Church of Scotland**: WCF still "principal subordinate standard" after the failed 1974 change [snippet; unconfirmed in verification-lutheran-reformed.md S-R2 (the cited PDF's text could not be extracted)]; "liberty of opinion" on points not of the substance of the faith, and the substance is undefined [verified Art. V; snippet Fergusson].
8. **Belhar**: standard in PC(USA) and RCA, only "contemporary testimony" in CRC [verified]; contested in conservative bodies [memory].

### How the confessions themselves describe their authority (PD texts)
- [verified] WCF 1.6 (PCA/OPC text, pca_wcf PDF; same in 1647): "The whole counsel of God concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man's salvation, faith and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture: unto which nothing at any time is to be added, whether by new revelations of the Spirit, or traditions of men." 1.10: "The supreme judge by which all controversies of religion are to be determined, and all decrees of councils, opinions of ancient writers, doctrines of men, and private spirits, are to be examined, and in whose sentence we are to rest, can be no other but the Holy Spirit speaking in the Scripture." 31.3 (1788/PCA/OPC numbering; 31.4 in 1647; corrected 2026-10-15, the numbers were reversed here, see §4A): "All synods or councils, since the apostles' times, whether general or particular, may err; and many have erred. Therefore they are not to be made the rule of faith, or practice; but to be used as a help in both." 1.3 Apocrypha "of no authority in the church of God". 1.4 Scripture's authority "dependeth not upon the testimony of any man, or church; but wholly upon God". 1.8 Hebrew OT and Greek NT "authentical; so as, in all controversies of religion, the church is finally to appeal unto them", to be translated into "the vulgar language of every nation".
- [verified] Belgic Confession Art. VII (Schaff III, ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds3.iv.viii.html, 19th-c. RCA English): "Neither may we compare any writings of men, though ever so holy, with those divine Scriptures; nor ought we to compare custom, or the great multitude, or antiquity, or succession of times or persons, or councils, decrees, or statutes, with the truth of God ... Therefore we reject with all our hearts whatsoever doth not agree with this infallible rule". Art. IX: "we do willingly receive the three creeds, namely, that of the Apostles, of Nice, and of Athanasius; likewise that which, conformable thereunto, is agreed upon by the ancient fathers." Art. VI: apocrypha listed (3–4 Esdras, Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, additions to Esther, Song of the Three, Susanna, Bel and the Dragon, Prayer of Manasseh, 1–2 Maccabees) "which the Church may read and take instruction from, so far as they agree with the canonical books; but they are far from having such power and efficacy as that we may from their testimony confirm any point of faith".
- [verified] Second Helvetic Confession ch. II (Schaff III appendix English, ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds3.v.ix.html): "we do not despise the interpretations of the holy Greek and Latin fathers, nor reject their disputations and treatises as far as they agree with the Scriptures; but we do modestly dissent from them when they are found to set down things differing from, or altogether contrary to, the Scriptures ... And in the same order we also place the decrees and canons of councils. Wherefore we suffer not ourselves, in controversies about religion or matters of faith, to be pressed with the bare testimonies of fathers or decrees of councils". → This is the Reformed family's own statement of how it treats the Fathers and councils (useful for the site's rule on Fathers).
