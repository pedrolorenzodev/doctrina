## 1. Scope

### 1.1 Why Pentecostal, and how big (reach figures) 

- Pew Research Center, *Religion in Latin America: Widespread Change in a Historically Catholic Region* (13 Nov 2014; >30,000 face-to-face interviews, 18 countries + Puerto Rico, Oct 2013–Feb 2014; Cuba not surveyed) `[verified, https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2014/11/13/religion-in-latin-america/ (markdown export)]`:
  - "Across all 18 countries and Puerto Rico, a median of nearly two-thirds of Protestants (65%) identify as Pentecostal Christians, either because they belong to a Pentecostal denomination (median of 47%) or because they personally identify as Pentecostal regardless of their denomination (median of 52%)."
  - 19% of Latin American adults "now describe themselves as Protestants"; "in Latin America 'Protestant' and 'evangelical' often are used interchangeably"; "Among those who belong to Pentecostal churches, Assemblies of God is one of the most commonly cited affiliations."
  - Chapter 4 (https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2014/11/13/chapter-4-pentecostalism/) `[verified]`: "in every country across Latin America, roughly half or more of Protestants … belong to a church that is part of a Pentecostal denomination or personally identify as a Pentecostal Christian"; "in three Latin American countries – the Dominican Republic, Brazil and Panama – about eight-in-ten Protestants" are Pentecostal; "in Puerto Rico, Nicaragua, Guatemala, Argentina, Honduras, Ecuador and Chile, roughly two-thirds or more"; "In the United States, by comparison, 18% of all Protestants, including 45% of Hispanic Protestants, are Pentecostal by one definition or another."
  - Caution for the site: Pew's "Pentecostal" includes self-identification regardless of denomination, so it measures a **movement**, not membership in bodies that hold a given statement of faith. Pew's examples of Pentecostal denominations: "Assemblies of God, the Pentecostal Church of God or the Quadrangular Evangelical Church". Speaking in tongues is reported by only "roughly a quarter or more" of Protestants in most countries (Ch. 4) `[verified]`: Pentecostal identity ≠ every member holding the "initial evidence" doctrine.
- **Newer data (supersedes the 2014 figures for six countries)**: Pew, "Catholicism Has Declined in Latin America Over the Past Decade", ch. 1 "Religious affiliation in Latin America" (21 Jan 2026; spring 2024 survey, 6,234 adults in Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru) `[verified, https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/01/21/religious-affiliation-in-latin-america/ + its table image]`:
  - "As of 2024, Pentecostal Protestants make up small percentages of the overall populations in the six countries surveyed, ranging from 4% of all adults in Mexico to 19% in Brazil."
  - Among Protestants, % Pentecostal, 2013-14 → 2024: Argentina 71 → 54; Brazil 80 → 65; Chile 67 → 54; Peru 52 → 40; Colombia 56 → 48 (Mexico: sample too small). Only Argentina and Brazil changes are statistically significant; Protestant samples are small (148 in Argentina … 313 in Brazil). "Today, Brazil is the only Latin American country surveyed where a majority of Protestants are Pentecostal (65%)."
  - Pew's 2024 question gave as examples of "a Pentecostal church": "Assemblies of God or the Universal Church of the Kingdom of God" — i.e. Pew counts neo-Pentecostal IURD as Pentecostal.
  - Pew's footnote: scholars suggest many Protestants "now call themselves simply 'Christians'" rather than "Pentecostal".
  - **Consequence for the project's premise**: the "two-thirds to eight in ten" figure is 2014 data. The 2024 data still show Pentecostals as roughly half or more of Protestants in Argentina, Brazil and Chile, and 40–48% in Peru and Colombia: still the largest single Protestant stream, but no longer "eight in ten" outside Brazil. Pedro should update the wording (see §10).

## 2. How authority works

Pentecostalism has no ecumenical council, no magisterium, no synod that binds the whole movement, and (with few exceptions) no historic confession older than 1910. Authority is **Scripture, read as the "all-sufficient rule"**, and below it the **statement of faith of each denomination**, adopted and revisable by that denomination's own assembly. The statements themselves say what they are and what they are not. The wording below is from the bodies' own constitutional texts.

### 2.1 Scripture is the rule; the statement is a "basis of fellowship" (AG family) `[verified]`

- General Council of the Assemblies of God (USA), *Constitution and Bylaws, updated as of 60th General Council, Columbus, Ohio, August 1–4, 2023*, Constitution Art. V preamble (p. 9): "The Bible is our all-sufficient rule for faith and practice. This Statement of Fundamental Truths is intended simply as a basis of fellowship among us (i.e., that we all speak the same thing, 1 Corinthians 1:10; Acts 2:42). The phraseology employed in this statement is not inspired or contended for, but the truth set forth is held to be essential to a full-gospel ministry. No claim is made that it contains all biblical truth, only that it covers our need as to these fundamental doctrines." PDF: https://ag.org/-/media/AGORG/Downloads/Constitution-and-Bylaws/2023-Constitution-and-Bylaws.pdf (downloaded 2026-10-14, `pdftotext` read; ag.org HTML pages are behind a Cloudflare challenge and return 403 to non-browser clients, the PDF is not).

- Same preamble wording already in the 1941 and 1947 editions of the Constitution (iFPHC scans: https://archives.ifphc.org/DigitalPublications/USA/Assemblies%20of%20God%20USA/Minutes%20General%20Council/Unregistered/1947/FPHC/1947%20Constitution.pdf) `[snippet]` — the 1916 original preamble is quoted in `docs/research/sources/free-church.md` §2.4 from the 1916 minutes `[verified there]`.

### 2.2 Who defines and revises (AG USA as the fullest documented case) `[verified]`

All from the 2023 *Constitution and Bylaws* (same PDF):

- **The General Council in session** (biennial assembly of ministers and church delegates) adopts and amends the Statement of Fundamental Truths (it is Art. V of the Constitution; the Constitution is amended by the General Council). Constitution Art. III "Prerogatives", g: "To approve scriptural teachings and practices, and to disapprove unscriptural teachings and practices. A list of disapproved doctrines and practices is set forth in Articles IX and X of the Bylaws."
- **The General Presbytery** is "the official policy-making body of the Assemblies of God" between General Councils (Constitution Art. IX §3). Position papers are adopted by the General Presbytery: each carries "(Adopted by the General Presbytery in session <date>)" (§4.A3) `[verified, list page via Wayback + 8 PDF headers]`.
- **The Executive Presbytery** "Interpret[s] policy originating in the legislation of the General Council or the General Presbytery. Such interpretations shall be final unless reversed at the next meeting of the body in which the legislation originated" (Bylaws Art. II §7 b), and as the General Council Credentials Committee "shall have the final authority in matters of doctrine and qualifications for ministry" (Bylaws, Discipline, §2).
- **Commission on Doctrines and Practices** (Bylaws Art. IX A): "established for the purpose of giving careful attention to preventing deviations from the Statement of Fundamental Truths and proliferation of unscriptural teachings. The commission shall receive its assignments from and make its reports to the Executive Presbytery." Ten ordained ministers, "recognized authorities in biblical knowledge", four-year terms. Resolutions "that involve scriptural interpretation" go to this commission for an assessment before the General Council votes (Bylaws, Resolutions, c).
- **Local churches**: "d. Subordinate in matters of doctrine and polity. A General Council affiliated assembly shall recognize that a district council or The General Council of the Assemblies of God has the right to approve scriptural doctrine and polity and to disapprove unscriptural doctrine and polity and the authority to withdraw its Certificate of Affiliation if deemed necessary." (Constitution Art. XI §1 d).
- **Ministers**: credential requirement "e. Doctrinal position. A thorough understanding of and agreement with our doctrinal position as contained in the Statement of Fundamental Truths" (Bylaws Art. VII); "A declared open change in doctrinal views" is a cause for discipline (Bylaws Art. X).
- **What binds whom**: the Statement binds credentialed ministers and affiliated churches of that denomination; it does not claim to bind other Christians or other Pentecostal bodies. The sanction is withdrawal of credentials or affiliation, not anathema.

## 4. The canon, by tiers and families

Tier names (the tradition's own logic, see §3): **T1 Scripture** (the only rule); **T2 denominational statement of faith** (constitutional; binds ministers and churches of that body; adopted/amended by its assembly); **T3 official doctrinal statements below the constitution** (position papers, doctrinal commitments, resolutions, teaching documents adopted by the body's presbytery/assembly: official but subordinate to T2 and revisable); **T4 historic founding texts** (not constitutional today but treated as the historical confession: 1916 SFT, Azusa *Apostolic Faith* 1906); **T5 fellowship-level statements** (WAGF, PWF, PCCNA: basis of cooperation, "not a legislative organ"). Each family is closed at T2 (one document per denomination) and open-ended at T3 (rule and index given per family).

## 6. Liturgical and devotional sources

## 9. Disputed points

1. **Two families on sanctification** (Holiness "second work" vs Finished Work), from W. H. Durham's 1910 "finished work" preaching; the AG (1914) formed largely from Finished-Work ministers `[memory; to cite from NIDPCM or AG Heritage]`. Visible in the texts: CoG 6/8 and IPHC 9–11 vs AG 9 `[verified texts]`.
2. **Oneness ("New Issue", 1913–16)**: the 1916 AG Statement was adopted against it; Oneness ministers left and formed bodies that became the UPCI (1945) `[memory/snippet]`. Whether Oneness belongs in a "Pentecostal" column is contested by Trinitarian Pentecostals (AG 2 (h) calls the denial of distinct persons a "transgression of the doctrine of Christ") `[verified wording of heading]`.
3. **"Initial physical evidence"**: AG, CoG, IPHC, UPCI, WAGF hold it; the AG added "physical" in 1917 and re-affirmed it in 1918 `[verified minutes]` amid internal dissent (F. F. Bosworth) `[memory]`; the current Chilean IMP text has no initial-evidence article at all `[verified, §4.F]`; Pew shows only about a quarter of Latin American Protestants report speaking in tongues `[verified]`.
4. **Authority of T3 documents**: AG position papers are "official statements" but the SFT preamble says only Scripture is the rule and the SFT is a "basis of fellowship"; whether a minister may dissent from a position paper without discipline is not stated in the C&B `[verified negative: no C&B clause makes position papers a credential requirement; Bylaws Art. VII cites only the SFT]`.
5. **Neo-Pentecostal / prosperity teaching**: AG position paper "The Believer and Positive Confession" (1980) criticises it `[memory]`; many Latin American "Pentecostal" self-identifiers attend independent neo-Pentecostal churches without a classical statement. Who speaks for "Pentecostals" is therefore a choice, not a fact (see §10).
6. **Which text is "the" AG SFT**: 1916 (17 articles, PD) vs current (16 articles, ©, revised several times): revision history now in §4.A1 (1917, 1918, 1920, 1927, 1933, 1961, 1969, 2005, 2009 changes); the site must date every quotation.
