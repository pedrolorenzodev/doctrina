## 1. Scope

### 1.1 Who "Orthodox" is for the site
The Eastern (Chalcedonian, Byzantine-rite) Orthodox Church: "The Orthodox Catholic Church comprises fourteen local Autocephalous Churches, recognized at a pan-Orthodox level" (Crete 2016, Encyclical §5; Greek «ἐκ δεκατεσσάρων κατά τόπους Αὐτοκεφάλων Ἐκκλησιῶν, πανορθοδόξως ἀνεγνωρισμένων») [verified, R1/R20]. Size: "nearly 260 million" Orthodox worldwide, of whom Eastern Orthodoxy is about 80% (≈ 200–210 million, derived); "In Russia alone, it has surpassed 100 million" (Pew 2017, 2010-based estimates) [verified, R6]. Pew also says "Eastern Orthodoxy is split into 15 jurisdictions": Pew's count, not the fourteen autocephalous churches of Crete; a site line must not mix the two [verified, ver. C4]. No church publishes a census-grade figure that could be fetched [sub-hispanic 1.3]. The Oriental (non-Chalcedonian) churches are outside the site's "Orthodox" column by project definition.

## 2. How authority works (in Orthodox terms)

The Orthodox Church has no single teaching office and no closed confessional book. It describes its own authority as the Holy Tradition in which Scripture, the councils, the Fathers, the liturgy and the canons are moments of one life guided by the Holy Spirit, verified by the Church's reception. Each point below is the Church's own wording (running log R-numbers give the source and tag).

### 2.1 Scripture and Tradition are one deposit, not two
- The Church's mission is to "transmit and preach all the truth contained in Holy Scripture and Holy Tradition" (Crete 2016, *Relations*, §2) [verified, R14].
- Philaret's Longer Catechism, the Russian Church's catechism since 1823/1839: revelation is "spread among men and preserved in the true Church … By two channels — holy tradition and holy Scripture" (Q.16); tradition is "the doctrine of the faith, the law of God, the sacraments, and the ritual as handed down … by word and example" (Q.17); the Church "is the sure repository of holy tradition" (Q.18); tradition is "the most ancient and original instrument" (Q.21), and remains necessary "as a guide to the right understanding of holy Scripture, for the right ministration of the sacraments, and the preservation of sacred rites" (Q.24) [verified, R12].
- Dositheus 1672, Decree II: Scripture is "God-taught … yet not otherwise than as the Catholic Church hath interpreted and delivered the same" [verified, R3]. Mogila I Q.4: the articles of faith "delivered unto the Church from our Lord Jesus Christ by the Apostles: And which have been expounded and approved by General Councils" [verified, R4].
- Nicaea II (787) states the principle in a conciliar horos: "we keep unchanged all the ecclesiastical traditions handed down to us, whether in writing or verbally" [verified, R9].

### 2.2 The ecumenical council is the highest organ; its authority is sealed by reception
- "The preservation of the true Orthodox faith is ensured only through the conciliar system, which has always represented the highest authority in the Church on matters of faith" (Crete 2016, *Relations*, §22, citing Canon 6 of Constantinople 381) [verified, R1/R14]. "The Orthodox Church … is the Church of Councils, from the Apostolic Council in Jerusalem … to the present day" (Crete, Encyclical §3) [verified, R1].
- Each council defines by first confessing the preceding ones: Chalcedon declares the creed "sufficed for the perfect knowledge and confirmation of religion" and teaches "that which is unchanged from the beginning" [verified, R11]; Trullo canon 1 recites the six councils and decrees "that the faith shall stand firm and remain unsullied until the end of the world" [verified, R10]; Nicaea II defines "following the royal pathway and the divinely inspired authority of our Holy Fathers and the traditions of the Catholic Church (for, as we all know, the Holy Spirit indwells her)" [verified, R9].
- Reception: the OCA's catechism states that "only seven such councils" have "received the universal approval of the entire Church in all times and places", that "not all such councils were automatically approved and accepted by the Church in its Holy Tradition", and that the seven councils' definitions and canons "are understood to be inspired by God" [verified, R15]. The 1848 Encyclical §17 locates the guardianship in the whole body: "the protector of religion is the very body of the Church, even the people themselves" [verified, R16]. Khomiakov's essay (non-normative) states the same idea [verified, R17].
- The Church's own list of later councils "of universal authority": 879–880 (Photius), 1341/1351/1368 (Palamas), 1484, 1638, 1642, 1672, 1691, 1872 (Crete, Encyclical §3) [verified, R1]. Their tier is contested at the margin (see §4 and §9): Crete itself was not signed by four of the fourteen churches [verified, R1–R2].

### 2.3 The Fathers as a consensus, the Church as infallible "through" them
- Dositheus, Decree XII: the Church "is taught indeed by the Life-giving Spirit, but through the medium of the holy Fathers and Doctors (whose rule is acknowledged to be the Holy and Œcumenical Synods …); and, therefore … it is impossible for the Catholic Church to err" [verified, R13]. The Fathers' authority is thus collective and measured by the councils, not individual.
- OCA catechism: "The writings of the Church Fathers are not infallible", yet "taken as a whole" they "have great authority … and are primary sources for the discovery of the Church's doctrine" [verified, R15]. Crete, Encyclical §2: the Church's identity "is confirmed in the teaching of the Fathers of the Church" [verified, R1].
- Doctrina rule (project invariant): a Father is cited as the Church's witness ("the Orthodox Church receives X as…"), never as a member of the modern Orthodox Church.

### 2.4 Liturgy as confession of faith (lex orandi)
- Crete, Encyclical, closing: "The holy Eucharist constitutes the innermost core also of the conciliar functioning of the ecclesial body, as well as the authentic confirmation of the Orthodoxy of the faith of the Church, as Saint Irenaeus of Lyon proclaims: 'Our teaching is in accord with the Eucharist and the Eucharist confirms our teaching'" [verified, R1].
- Philaret Q.17 includes "the sacraments, and the ritual" inside the definition of Tradition, and Q.24 quotes Basil on the unwritten rites (sign of the cross, facing east, the words of the epiclesis) as having "one and the same force for piety" as written doctrine [verified, R12]. Nicaea II counts "the Book of the Gospels, or the image of the cross, or the pictorial icons, or the holy reliques of a martyr" among the things "which the Church hath received" [verified, R9]. Detail in §6.

### 2.5 The canons
- Trullo canon 2 and Nicaea II canon 1 define the received canonical corpus (apostolic canons, councils, Fathers) — quoted in §4 Tier 1 (councils sub-stream). The canons are disciplinary-doctrinal: canons on heretics' baptism, the calendar, images, fasting and ordination carry doctrine. Their standard Greek collection is the Pedalion (1800; synodal examination under Neophytos VII; Church of Greece approval of the 1886 reprint) and, for the Russian Church, the Книга правил (1839) — see §4 Tier 4 and §9 [verified for the Pedalion front matter; secondary for the Книга правил].

### 2.6 Who defines, who revises, what binds
- Dogma is defined only by an ecumenical council received by the Church (2.2); no office defines alone. Dositheus, Decree X: "a mortal man cannot universally and perpetually be head" of the Church; "our Lord Jesus Christ Himself is head" and the Holy Spirit "hath appointed Bishops as leaders and shepherds" [verified, R3]. 1848 §20 rejects "every novelty as a suggestion of the devil" [verified, R5].
- Local churches bind their own members through their synods and statutes (ROC Bishops' Council; Church of Greece; etc.) — statute clauses in §4 Tier 5 [verified for ROC, Greece, OCA, Romania (ver. C2, C3, C5, I9); GOARCH unconfirmed in verification-orthodox.md I9]. Such acts bind one autocephalous church, not the whole Church.
- Confessions and catechisms (17th–19th c.) were synodally approved and are received as witnesses of the faith; the Church has never given them the status of the councils' horoi; Orthodox theologians dispute how much they bind (§4 Tier 3, §9) [verified: Krivoshein 1961 (ver. C6), Florovsky ch. II on Mogila (ver. C7; the "1937" date is not on the page read, unconfirmed), Pomazansky (ver. C8)].
- Theologoumena (θεολογούμενα): the Orthodox term for the opinions of the Fathers on questions the Church has not defined — "probable", not "truth" (Bolotov) [verified on Sanidopoulos' quotation, ver. I1; Bolotov's original unread, R18]. Private opinions of theologians carry no authority (R18).

The nearest Orthodox equivalents are rows 4 (horos) and 7–8 (synodal confessions/catechisms).

## 4. The canon, by tiers

Tiers follow the Orthodox Church's own gradation (§2–3): (1) the dogmatic definitions and canons received by the whole Church; (2) later councils "of universal authority" (Crete's phrase) and the Synodikon; (3) the synodally approved confessions and catechisms ("symbolic books"); (4) patriarchal-synodal acts and the canonical collections; (5) acts of a single autocephalous church. Detail, verbatim quotations and scan line numbers: scratch `sub-councils.md` (councils, statutes), `sub-confessions.md`, `sub-liturgy-scripture.md`, `sub-hispanic.md`; the editions already researched on 2026-10-09 are in `../sources/orthodox.md` (table §2) and `../sources/councils-creeds.md` and are only referenced here.

### Tier 1 — The Seven Ecumenical Councils and the received canonical corpus (closed)

**Membership rule (the Church's own):** the seven councils Nicaea I (325) … Nicaea II (787), with the Council in Trullo (691/2) counted as the canonical completion of the Fifth and Sixth ("Penthekte"; Crete 2016 *Relations* §20 calls it "the Quinisext Ecumenical Council") [verified, R14]; the canonical corpus is what Trullo c.2 enumerates and Nicaea II c.1 receives [verified, sub-councils §1.3]. ### Tier 3 — "Symbolic books": confessions and catechisms approved by synods (closed by the Church's own usage; status debated)

**Membership rule:** confessional texts that a patriarchal synod approved or sent in the Church's name (Mogila 1642/43; Dositheus 1672, re-sent 1723; Philaret 1839 by the Russian Holy Synod and "approved" by the Eastern patriarchs), plus the earlier confessions the Church later received as "monuments" (Gennadius; Jeremiah II, approved by Jerusalem 1672; Kritopoulos, never approved). The term "symbolic books" (συμβολικὰ βιβλία / символические книги) came from Protestant *Symbolik* (Kimmel's title *Libri symbolici Ecclesiae Orientalis*, 1843) and was adopted by Russian academic dogmatics (Pomazansky's "символические книги"); Karmiris' Greek collection prefers "monuments" (μνημεῖα) [verified for Kimmel and Pomazansky; memory for Karmiris' rationale; sub-confessions §12]. Crete 2016 speaks only of the *councils* of 1638, 1642, 1672, 1691, never of "symbolic books" [verified, R1]. Full detail per document: `sub-confessions.md` §1–§11.

## 5. Scripture

Detail, verbatim lists and URLs: scratch `sub-liturgy-scripture.md` A1–A4.

### 5.1 The canon: no single conciliar list; conciliar reception of several
- Trullo c.2 (received by Nicaea II c.1) "seals" documents that each contain a list, and the lists disagree: Apostolic canon 85 (Maccabees "three"; Sirach to be taught to the young; NT includes the two epistles of Clement and the Constitutions — which Trullo c.2 itself rejects — and lacks Revelation); Laodicea c.59–60 (22 books with Baruch and the Epistle inside Jeremiah; no Revelation); Carthage c.24 (Tobit, Judith, 2 Maccabees, "five books of Solomon"; Revelation included); Athanasius, Festal Letter 39 (22 books; "certain other books, not edited in the Canon, but established by the Fathers, to be read by those who have just come to us": Wisdom, Sirach, Esther, Judith, Tobit, Didache, Shepherd); Gregory the Theologian and Amphilochius (Hebrew-type lists; Revelation doubted) [verified, NPNF14 OCR, locators in sub-liturgy A1.1]. Percival's (Anglican editor's) conclusion that the approval "was not specific but general" is a Western scholarly reading, not an Orthodox statement [verified].
- **Dositheus 1672, Q.III** (full text; R3 abbreviated it): "we call Sacred Scripture all those which Cyril collected from the Synod of Laodicea, and enumerated, adding thereto those which he foolishly, and ignorantly, or rather maliciously called Apocrypha; to wit, 'The Wisdom of Solomon,' 'Judith,' 'Tobit,' 'The History of the Dragon,' 'The History of Susanna,' 'The Maccabees,' and 'The Wisdom of Sirach.' For we judge these also to be with the other genuine Books of Divine Scripture genuine parts of Scripture … all of which we also judge to be Canonical Books, and confess them to be Sacred Scripture." "Cyril" = Cyril Lucaris, whose condemned Confession (Q.III) had called them Apocrypha and kept "from the Old Testament twenty-two Books" [verified, Robertson pp. 155–156 and 211–212].
- **Philaret 1839, Q.31–35**: 22 books "agreeing therein with the Jews"; Sirach "and certain others" omitted "Because they do not exist in the Hebrew"; they are "appointed of the Fathers to be read by proselytes" [verified, R12]. NT: Q.49–55, Revelation "prophetical" (Q.53) [verified].
- Orthodox term for the Greek-only books: ἀναγινωσκόμενα ("read", after Athanasius), rather than "deuterocanonical" or "apocrypha" [verified for Athanasius' wording; the term's currency: secondary].
- Revelation: canonical in every Orthodox Bible, but "τὰ ἐπ᾽ ἐκκλησίας μὴ ἀναγινωσκόμενα" — the Apocalypse and parts of Acts are not read in church (1904 Patriarchal NT preface) [verified, IA scan].

## 9. Disputed points (status of documents, within the Church and among scholars)

1. **How many ecumenical councils.** Seven is the closed count stated by the Pedalion (later synods "loosely" so named [verified, ver. G1]; "no new ὅρος concerning dogma" at 879–880 [sub-councils §2.1; not searched in verification-orthodox.md G1, unconfirmed there]) and by the OCA catechism [verified, R15]. Yet the four patriarchs' 1848 Encyclical twice calls 879–880 "the eighth Ecumenical Council" (§5 item xi and §6; corrected 2026-10-15 from "§5, §14") [verified, ver. B1], Dositheus printed its acts as such in 1705 [verified via Dragas], and modern Greek theologians (Romanides, Dragas, Hierotheos of Nafpaktos) count 879–880 and 1351 as the eighth and ninth [verified for Dragas; memory for the others]. Crete 2016 deliberately chose «καθολικοῦ κύρους» ("of universal authority") [verified, R20]. **Site rule:** show "seven" as the Church's count; present 879–880 and 1341–1351 as "received as of universal authority; called the 8th/9th ecumenical by part of the Church", quoting both the 1848 wording and the Pedalion.
2. **Crete 2016.** Signed by ten of fourteen autocephalous churches; formally declared "non-binding" by Antioch [verified, official statement], "cannot be regarded as pan-Orthodox" by Russia [verified via secondary], rejected as "neither great, nor holy, nor pan-Orthodox" by Bulgaria, "not obligatory" by Georgia [secondary summaries]. The Bulgarian decision objects precisely to *Relations* §22 (the "conciliar system" as highest authority), holding that "the final criterion is the vigilant dogmatic conscience of the whole Orthodox body" [secondary] — i.e. an intra-Orthodox disagreement about where reception finally lies, echoing 1848 §17. **Site rule:** quote Crete only with the reception caveat (§4 Tier 4).
3. **The "symbolic books".** Synodally approved (1643, 1672/1723, 1839) and treated by 19th–20th-c. dogmatics (Makarii, Pomazansky) as "symbolic books" [verified for Pomazansky], but judged by Florovsky, of Mogila, a Latin "pseudomorphosis" and "crypto-Romanism" [verified, ver. C7; his chapter does not treat Dositheus], by Krivoshein (1961) historical monuments without "непреходящее значение" [verified], and by Ware of "lesser authority" [memory]. Specific Latinisms under dispute: Decree XVII's "substance … accidents" (Dositheus) — kept in 1723, qualified by the Russian 1838 translation and by Philaret Q.340 [verified]; Mogila's Latin sources [verified, Florovsky]; Q.I of Dositheus against lay reading of Scripture, dropped in Russian editions [verified, Krivoshein]. The Great Council's own 1961 agenda item on ranking these texts was never treated [verified for the agenda; inference for its dropping]. **Site rule:** quote them as "the Confession of the Synod of Jerusalem (1672)", etc., with the approval act and the 20th-c. critique side by side; never as "the Orthodox confession".
4. **Transubstantiation (μετουσίωσις) as a term.** Affirmed by 1672 (Decree XVII), by 1691 against Caryophylles (the tome recognised "равнозначность термина «пресуществление»", pravenc) [verified, ver. I5], by Mogila (Q.107 "Substance … changed into the very Substance") and Philaret Q.339; limited in meaning by Philaret Q.340 and by the 1838 Russian Dositheus [verified]. For the Eucharist pilot this is a documented intra-Orthodox range, not a contradiction.
5. **The Old Testament canon.** Dositheus Q.III calls the deuterocanonical books "Canonical Books … Sacred Scripture" [verified, R3]; Philaret Q.31–35 counts 22 books "agreeing therein with the Jews" and treats the rest as "appointed … to be read by proselytes" [verified, R12]; Kritopoulos excludes them [verified, Schaff]. Trullo c.2 receives lists that disagree (Laodicea/Athanasius vs Carthage) [verified list; sub-liturgy to detail]. **Site rule:** present the Greek and Russian usages separately (§5).
6. **Baptism of converts.** The 1755 Oros (Constantinople, Alexandria, Jerusalem) requires baptism [verified]; 1484 and Moscow 1667 receive Latins by chrismation [verified via oodegr]; the Church of Greece reinstated the 1484 practice in 1932 (Metallinos) [unconfirmed in verification-orthodox.md I7]; the Pedalion defends 1755 [memory]. A live difference of practice (akribeia/oikonomia) across churches.
7. **Authenticity questions:** the 1583 calendar "Sigillion" with anathemas (disputed; verification log) [memory; OrthodoxWiki lists a council of 20 Nov 1583 with a sigillion [snippet]; the authenticity dispute is unconfirmed in verification-orthodox.md I6]; Lucaris' Confession (authenticity now generally accepted; the Church's normative act is the condemnation either way) [verified for the condemnations]; the anti-Origenist anathemas of 553 (Percival's note on their conciliar status) [verified].
8. **Mogila's authorship and original language** (Latin draft; Isaiah Kozlovsky named; revised by Syrigos) [verified, Overbeck/Florovsky].
9. **The 1848 Encyclical's English text**: translator unknown, probably the 1860s Russo-Greek Committee; the Greek original (Constantinople 1848) has no open scan (only an Onassis Library cover record) [verified negative searches; sources/orthodox.md O2].
10. **Autocephaly disputes that affect which synods "speak"**: OCA (1970, not recognised by Constantinople), Ukraine (2019 tomos, rejected by Moscow), Ohrid (2022), and the 2018 Moscow–Constantinople break of communion — see §1 (sub-hispanic Part 1).

### R9. Nicaea II (787), the horos — the Church's own statement of Tradition as the ground of definition [verified, NPNF14 (Percival 1900) OCR of IA `sevenecumenicalc00perc`, scratch `npnf14n.txt` l.51960–52080, p. 550]

### R10. Trullo / Quinisext (691/2), canon 1 — the Orthodox self-definition by the preceding councils [verified, same scan, l.35137–35235, pp. 359–360]
Canon 1 "define[s] that the faith set forth by the God-chosen Apostles … shall be preserved without any innovation, unchanged and inviolate", then confirms in order: Nicaea (318 fathers, "consubstantiality"), Constantinople 381 (150 fathers, Macedonius; Apollinaris), Ephesus (200 fathers; "the immaculate Ever-Virgin … literally and in very truth the Mother of God"; Nestorius), Chalcedon (630 fathers; "of two natures, and must be glorified in these two natures"; Eutyches, Nestorius, Dioscorus), Constantinople 553 (165 fathers; Theodore of Mopsuestia, Origen, Didymus, Evagrius; the Three Chapters), Constantinople 680/1 ("two natural wills or volitions and two natural operations"; Honorius of Rome among the condemned); and closes: "we decree that the faith shall stand firm and remain unsullied until the end of the world as well as the writings divinely handed down and the teachings of all those who have beautified and adorned the Church of God and were lights in the world". Canon 2 (the list of received canons) is being quoted by the councils sub-stream.

- Q.31 OT books: "St. Cyril of Jerusalem, St. Athanasius the Great, and St. John Damascene reckon them at twenty-two, agreeing therein with the Jews, who so reckon them in the original Hebrew tongue." ### R13. Dositheus 1672, Decrees XI–XII (the Church's infallibility "through the holy Fathers and Leaders", whose rule is the ecumenical synods) [verified, Robertson 1899 OCR, scratch `robertson1899.txt` l.4860–4935, pp. 130–132]
Decree XI: members of the Catholic Church are "all the Faithful, and only the Faithful; who … having received the blameless Faith of the Saviour Christ, from Christ Himself, and the Apostles, and the Holy Œcumenical Synods, adhere to the same without wavering; although some of them may be guilty of all manner of sins". Decree XII: "We believe the Catholic Church to be taught by the Holy Spirit … The teaching of the Holy Spirit, however, doth not immediately, but through the holy Fathers and Leaders of the Catholic Church, illuminate the Church … the Church is taught indeed by the Life-giving Spirit, but through the medium of the holy Fathers and Doctors (whose rule is acknowledged to be the Holy and Œcumenical Synods; for we shall not cease to say this ten thousand times); and, therefore, not only are we persuaded, but do profess as true and undoubtedly certain, that it is impossible for the Catholic Church to err, or at all be deceived, or ever to choose falsehood instead of truth." Decree XIII: "We believe a man to be not simply justified through faith alone, but through faith which worketh through love, that is to say, through faith and works."

### R17. Khomiakov, "The Church is One" (Russian c. 1850; English *The Orthodox Doctrine on the Church*, 1864, IA `orthodoxdoctrin00khomgoog`, PD) — representative, non-normative statement of ecclesial infallibility residing in the whole Church [verified, scratch `khomiakov1864.txt` pp. 4 and 8]
"the Church herself errs not, for she is true, nor does she use craft, or fears for she is holy … after having once declared in a General Council and with one common accord, the possibility of error in the teaching of any individual person, or of any bishop, or patriarch whatever, she cannot afterwards admit, that this individual, or bishop, or patriarch, or his successors, cannot fall into any error of doctrine" (p. 4); "The Church does not prove herself either as Scripture, or as tradition, or as a work; but she herself is her own witness … The Church does not enquire what Scripture is true, what tradition is true, what Council is right" (p. 8); "every writing which the Church acknowledges as her own, is Holy Scripture. Such above all are the Confessions of faith of the Councils, and particularly the Niceno-Constantinopolitan creed" (p. 8). Status: a lay theologian's essay, not a church act; the 1848 Encyclical §17 is the synodal text that says something similar (R16).

### R19. Pilot-doctrine locators (Eucharist; intermediate state) in the three synodally approved confessional texts [verified in the scratch OCRs; page numbers from the scans]
- **Dositheus 1672, Decree XVII** (Robertson pp. 144–147): "we believe the Lord Jesus Christ to be present, not typically, nor figuratively, nor by superabundant grace, as in the other Mysteries, nor by a bare presence … or by impanation … but truly and really, so that after the consecration of the bread and of the wine, the bread is transmuted, transubstantiated, converted and transformed into the true Body Itself of the Lord" (Greek fn.: μεταβάλλεσθαι, μετουσιοῦσθαι, μεταποιεῖσθαι, μεταρρυθμίζεσθαι); "there no longer remaineth the substance (Οὐσίαν) of the bread and of the wine, but the Body Itself and the Blood of the Lord, under the species and form (Εἴδει καὶ τύπῳ) of bread and wine; that is to say, under the accidents of the bread (Ὑπὸ τοῖς τοῦ ἄρτου συμβεβηκόσι)"; communicants "whether pious or impious" receive; "Broken and distributed is He That is broken, yet not severed" quoted from the Liturgies of Chrysostom and Basil (Robertson's note points to his 1894 edition p. 319); whole Christ in every particle. Decree XVII also calls the Eucharist "the unbloody Sacrifice" (Decree XVIII) — sacrifice locus.
- **Dositheus, Decree XVIII** (pp. 149–151): souls "depart immediately either to joy, or to sorrow"; "neither their enjoyment, nor condemnation are complete" before the resurrection; those who repented without fruits "depart into Hades, and there endure the punishment due to the sins they have committed … and are delivered by the Supreme Goodness, through the prayers of the Priests, and the good works which the relatives of each do for their Departed; especially the unbloody Sacrifice"; "but when we know not" (no purgatorial fire named; Kimmel's note on the "status medius" is quoted by Robertson). → purgatory / intermediate state; saints and intercession (prayers for the dead).
- **Mogila, Orthodox Confession, Part I, Q.106–107** (Overbeck pp. 79–80): "The holy Eucharist, or the Body and Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, under the visible Species of Bread and Wine: Wherein, really and properly, and according to the Thing itself, Jesus Christ is present"; Q.107: leavened bread, wine with water; "the Priest must firmly and undoubtingly resolve within himself that the Substance of the Bread and the Substance of the Wine are changed into the very Substance of the very Body and Blood of Jesus Christ, by the operation or working of the Holy Ghost, whose Power and Influence let the Priest invoke in these Words … O Lord, send down from Heaven thy Holy Spirit upon us, and upon these Gifts now lying before thee; and make this Bread the precious Body of [thy Christ]" (the consecration is tied to the epiclesis). Also "the unbloody Sacrifice" (Q.107).
- **Philaret, Longer Catechism** (Schaff numbering; pp. 496–498): Q.315 "What is the Communion?"; Q.338 "the most essential act" = the words of institution "And after this the invocation of the Holy Ghost, and the blessing the gifts"; Q.339 "at the moment of this act the bread and wine are changed, or transubstantiated, into the very Body of Christ, and into the very Blood of Christ"; Q.340 "In the exposition of the faith by the Eastern Patriarchs, it is said that the word transubstantiation is not to be taken to define the manner in which the bread and wine are changed … but only thus much is signified, that the bread truly, really, and substantially becomes the very true Body of the Lord" + John Damascene (De fide orth. IV.13) quoted. Sacrament locators: Q.288 Baptism; Q.315 Communion; Q.351 Penitence; Q.361 Matrimony; Q.364 Unction with Oil; Q.263 communion of the Church on earth with the Church in heaven (saints' intercession).
→ Note for the Eucharist pilot: the three texts converge on "transubstantiation" (μετουσίωσις) as the word while Philaret Q.340 limits its meaning; Mogila ties the change to the epiclesis; the liturgical texts (§6) supply the epiclesis itself.
