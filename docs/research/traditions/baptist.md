## 1. Scope

**What "Baptist" means for the site.** Churches that baptize believers only (by immersion), hold the local congregation autonomous and Scripture as sole rule, and confess their faith in confessions adopted by churches, associations or conventions (§2). The tradition has **no single normative document and no body that speaks for all Baptists**: the SBC constitution says the Convention "does not claim and will never attempt to exercise any authority over any other Baptist body" (Art. IV) [verified]; the Baptist World Alliance is a fellowship of ~283 conventions and unions [snippet; unconfirmed in verification-baptist-pentecostal.md S-B5: the BWA home page says "A global network of 53 million Baptists in 138 countries and territories" and shows no body count]; it has a "Beliefs Statement" page (https://baptistworld.org/beliefs/, link verified, text not read) that is not a confession its members adopt [memory]. So the tradition splits into confessional families with different texts; they must be mapped separately (brief; `DECISIONS.md` 2026-10-14 "no sub-families mixed").

### 1.1 Open scope question for Pedro (not decided here)

Which family or families does the site present as "Bautista"? Options with their reach:

1. **Convention line only (C), anchored on BF&M 2000.** Largest by far: the SBC alone reports 12.3 M members [verified, verification B-log 12] and most Spanish-speaking conventions descend from Southern Baptist missions and use a BF&M text or New Hampshire. The 1963 text is PD-US (Wikisource) and close in wording for most articles.
2. **Particular/Reformed line only (A), anchored on the 1689.** Richest text (32 chapters, covers almost every locus), PD in English, and already the main Baptist source of the Eucharist pilot. Cost: much smaller in reach; ~42% of its words are identical to Westminster (`free-church.md`), so on many loci the Baptist column would repeat the Reformed one; Spanish translations circulating are © (own translation needed).
3. **Two sub-presentations (A and C) side by side** — what the Eucharist pilot already does implicitly ("corriente bautista reformada (1689)" vs "corriente memorial" with BF&M 2000) [verified in `data/site/eucharist.v2.json`]. Most faithful to how Baptists actually split on predestination and the Supper; doubles the per-doctrine work and must label each quote by family.
4. **A + B + C**, adding the Free Will line (NAFWB Treatise; Orthodox Creed). Only line with an Arminian confession, three ordinances (foot washing) and a confession that receives the three creeds (Orthodox Creed XXXVIII). Small reach; Treatise © NAFWB 2026 (1834 text PD in McGlothlin).

Whatever is chosen, each quote must say which document and which body holds it, and the site must say that **no Baptist confession binds all Baptists** (§2). Secondary scope questions: whether to quote the New Hampshire 1833 or 1853 text (§9); whether to present the 1925 "five points on confessions" as the tradition's own statement of authority (it is the closest thing Baptists have to a common statement, but it is an SBC document).

## 2. How authority works (in the tradition's own terms)

1. **Scripture is the sole rule.** "The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving Knowledge, Faith and Obedience" (2LCF 1.1) [verified, Creeds.json 1689 text]; "the supreme judge by which all controversies of Religion are to be determined, and all Decrees of Councels, opinions of antient Writers, Doctrines of men ... are to be examined ... can be no other but the Holy Scripture delivered by the Spirit" (2LCF 1.10) [verified, McGlothlin OCR]; nothing is to be added "whether by new Revelation of the Spirit, or traditions of men" (2LCF 1.6) [verified]. "The sole authority for faith and practice among Baptists is the Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments" (1925 statement, quoted in the BF&M 2000 preamble, point 4) [verified]; Scripture is "the supreme standard by which all human conduct, creeds, and religious opinions should be tried" (BF&M 2000 I, already NH 1833 art. 1) [verified]; "only sufficient, certain and authoritative rule" (Abstract I) [verified]; "our infallible rule of faith and practice" (NAFWB Articles of Faith 1) [verified]; "Las Sagradas Escrituras son la única norma de fe y práctica entre los bautistas" (CEBA Argentina 1987) [verified, scratch]. BUGB puts it Christologically: Christ "is the sole and absolute authority in all matters pertaining to faith and practice, as revealed in the Holy Scriptures" (Declaration of Principle 1) [verified].
2. **Confessions are witnesses, not creeds that bind conscience.** The tradition's most-quoted self-description is the SBC's 1925 "statement of the historic Baptist conception of the nature and function of confessions of faith", quoted in full in the 1963 and 2000 reports [verified]: confessions are "a consensus of opinion of some Baptist body, large or small, for the general instruction and guidance"; not "complete statements of our faith, having any quality of finality or infallibility"; revisable "at any time"; any group may draw up its own; "Confessions are only guides in interpretation, having no authority over the conscience"; "not to be used to hamper freedom of thought or investigation in other realms of life". The 2000 report adds that Baptists "deny the right of any secular or religious authority to impose a confession of faith upon a church or body of churches". Older forms of the same idea: 1644 conclusion ("we confesse that we know but in part ... if any shall doe us that friendly part to shew us from the word of God that we see not, we shall have cause to be thankfull"; but if any "impose upon us any thing that we see not to be commanded by our Lord Jesus Christ" they would rather "die a thousand deaths") [verified]; 1677 preface (the Bereans; "contention is most remote from our design") [verified]; SBC 1845: "a Baptist aversion for all creeds but the Bible" (as quoted by McGlothlin p. 299) [verified secondary]; CEBA: "Las confesiones son solamente guías para su interpretación y no tienen autoridad alguna sobre la conciencia" [verified, scratch]; BUGB "expressly disavowing and disallowing any powers to control belief" (adopted April 23, 1888, McGlothlin p. 291; the statement continues on p. 292) [verified; page re-checked on OCR page headers 2026-10-15].
3. **Yet confessions do function as boundaries of fellowship** ("instruments of doctrinal accountability", BF&M 2000 report) [verified]. They bind not the conscience but **membership in a voluntary body or employment in its institutions**: SBC constitution Art. III ("a faith and practice which closely identifies with the Convention's adopted statement of faith") [verified]; SBTS professors "engaging to teach in accordance with, and not contrary to, the Abstract of Principles" on pain of removal [verified]; SBC seminaries and mission boards require employees to affirm BF&M 2000 [snippet, Baptist Press]; NAFWB: affiliated organizations "shall be required to adopt the Treatise" [verified]; CBA (ex-ARBCA): churches "adopt as our subordinate standards ... the London Baptist Confession of 1689" with "full subscription" [verified]; UEBE (Spain): churches must "suscriban la Base Doctrinal y Principios Bautistas", "dentro de un margen interpretativo" [verified, scratch]; CIBSPR (Puerto Rico): "La iglesia debe afirmar la Fe y Mensaje Bautista 2000 en todas sus partes" [verified, scratch]. The tension between point 2 and point 3 is itself a theme of Baptist history (§9).
4. **The local church is autonomous; no Baptist body has authority over a church.** "A New Testament church ... is an autonomous local congregation of baptized believers ... Each congregation operates under the Lordship of Christ through democratic processes" (BF&M 2000 VI) [verified]; the power to receive and excommunicate "is given to every particular Congregation, and not one particular person" (1644 XLII) [verified]; SBC Art. IV [verified]; CNBV Venezuela: "Las organizaciones bautistas ... no tienen ninguna autoridad sobre una iglesia bautista" [verified, scratch]. Exception in the General Baptist line: the Orthodox Creed XXXIX makes general assemblies "of divine authority" with "lawful power to hear, and determine, as also to excommunicate" [verified]. Consequence for the site: **there is no "magisterium" and no body whose rulings bind Baptists as such**; a body's confession binds only that body's cooperation.
5. **Soul competency, priesthood of all believers, religious liberty.** BF&M 2000 report: "We honor the principles of soul competency and the priesthood of believers" [verified]; the phrase "competency of the soul under God in religion" is E. Y. Mullins's (*The Axioms of Religion*, 1908, p. 53–54; archive.org `axiomsofreligion00mull`) [verified in OCR]. "God alone is Lord of the conscience" (BF&M 2000 XVII; 2LCF 21.2 "God alone is Lord of the Conscience, and hath left it free from the Doctrines and Commandments of men") [verified both; 2LCF in Creeds.json, verification B-log 10].
6. **Ancient creeds and Fathers.** Baptist confessions do not as a rule receive the ecumenical creeds as such; Scripture tries "creeds" (BF&M I). The exception is the General Baptist **Orthodox Creed (1678/9) Art. XXXVIII**: the Nicene, Athanasian and Apostles' creeds "ought throughly to be received, and believed. For we believe, they may be proved, by most undoubted authority of holy scripture" [verified]. The 1677 preface affirms "consent with both [Westminster and Savoy], in all the fundamental articles of the Christian Religion, as also with many others, whose orthodox confessions have been published" [verified]. Grantham's 1678 edition of the Standard Confession added "the testimony of many ancient writers of Christianity ... to shew, that though the composition of these articles be new, yet the doctrine contained therein is truly ancient" (McGlothlin p. 110) [verified]. Fathers are thus at most witnesses examined by Scripture (2LCF 1.10 "opinions of antient Writers"); the site must never present a Father as Baptist.
7. **Who adopts and revises.** The body that adopted a confession revises it by its own vote: SBC messengers at an annual meeting adopted BF&M in 1925, revised it 1963, added XVIII in 1998, replaced it in 2000 and amended Art. VI on June 14, 2023 [verified: preamble + page note]; NAFWB has revised its Treatise at 21 sessions 1948–2026 [verified]; the 1689 text itself is closed (a 17th-c. document; modern bodies adopt it, they do not revise it).

## 3. Authority categories the tradition itself uses

Baptists have **few categories**, and the site's "Grado de autoridad" table should not invent more. Each is a term the documents themselves use:

| Term (EN / ES) | Meaning, in the tradition's words | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Scripture: "sole authority", "only ... rule", "supreme standard"** / «única regla de fe y práctica», «norma suprema» | The only binding authority for faith and practice | BF&M 2000 preamble pt. 4 and art. I; 2LCF 1.1; Abstract I; NAFWB Art. 1; UEBE Principios II; CEBA [all verified] |
| **Confession of faith / statement of faith / declaration** / «confesión de fe», «declaración de fe» | "A consensus of opinion of some Baptist body ... for the general instruction and guidance", "guides in interpretation, having no authority over the conscience", revisable | 1925 statement in BF&M 2000 preamble [verified] |
| **Adopted statement of faith (for cooperation)** / «confesión adoptada» | The text a convention or association uses to decide which churches are "in friendly cooperation" or members | SBC const. Art. III; NAFWB constitution; UEBE const. Art. VI [verified] |
| **"Subordinate standard(s)"**, "full subscription" | Used only by confessional (Reformed) Baptists: the 1689 adopted under Scripture "as a faithful expression of the doctrine taught in the Scriptures" | CBA/ARBCA founding agreement 1997 and constitution, quoted on cba1689.com [verified] |
| **Institutional confession ("fundamental laws")** / «principios fundamentales» | A doctrinal statement that binds teachers of an institution, e.g. Abstract of Principles at SBTS | SBTS charter 1858 as quoted at sbts.edu [verified] |
| **"Baptist principles / distinctives"** / «principios bautistas», «principios distintivos» | Short lists of non-negotiable identity marks (lordship of Christ, Bible as only rule, regenerate membership, congregational government, separation of church and state, religious liberty, autonomy) that some Spanish-speaking bodies place beside or above the confession | UEBE "Principios Bautistas" (13); CNBM (7) "no podemos diferir en cuanto a los principios y seguir siendo bautistas"; CNBV (9) [verified, scratch] |
| **Church covenant** / «pacto de la iglesia» | Mutual promises of a local church's members; not a doctrinal norm | NAFWB Treatise Part I [verified]; New Hampshire 1833 "Declaration of Faith and Covenant" (McGlothlin p. 300) [verified] |
| **Resolution** / «resolución» | A convention's statement of opinion at a given meeting; not a confession | SBC practice [memory: SBC resolutions are non-binding expressions of the messengers present; not checked on sbc.net] |

What does **not** exist: no "de fide" grades, no infallible magisterium, no "binding on all Baptists" category; even "heresy" is defined only by a body's own fellowship boundary.

## 4. The canon, by tiers

Tiers in the tradition's own logic (not imported from another tradition):
- **Tier 0 — Scripture** (§5): the only norm.
- **Tier 1 — Adopted confessions of a living body** ("confessional"): binding for cooperation/membership in that body, never for Baptists as such. Listed per family.
- **Tier 2 — Historic confessions** ("representative"): adopted by assemblies in the past, no longer the operative standard of a large living body, but recognised by Baptists as part of their heritage and cited by later confessions (e.g. 1644, 1660, NH 1833 for the SBC).
- **Tier 3 — Institutional and catechetical documents**: Abstract of Principles (binding on SBTS/SEBTS faculty), Baptist Catechism, church covenants.
- **Open-ended: convention resolutions, associational circular letters** — opinion, not confession (rule below).

The confessional universe is **closed per family**: the documents below are the complete list of confessions adopted by a general assembly, association or national convention that later Baptists treat as their family's standard. Private confessions (e.g. Gill's church 1764, Keach's 1697), local church statements and seminary statements other than the Abstract are **excluded**; the standard catalogues that enumerate the historic universe are W. J. McGlothlin, *Baptist Confessions of Faith* (1911, PD; archive.org `baptistconfessio00mcgl`) and W. L. Lumpkin, *Baptist Confessions of Faith* (1959; rev. 1969, 2011) [free-church.md, verified there].

## 5. Scripture

- **Canon: the 66-book Protestant canon.** 2LCF 1.2 lists the 39 OT and 27 NT books, "All which are given by the inspiration of God, to be the rule of Faith and Life"; 1.3: "The Books commonly called Apocrypha not being of Divine inspiration, are no part of the Canon (or rule) of the Scripture, and therefore are of no authority to the Church of God, nor to be any otherwise approved or made use of, then other humane writings" [verified, Creeds.json + McGlothlin OCR]. The Orthodox Creed XXXVII prints "the names of the books of the Old and New Testament, as acknowledged in all Protestant confessions" [verified, as described by Crosby's note in McGlothlin p. 152]. BF&M, NH and the Abstract speak of "the Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments" without listing books [verified]; CBA's constitution speaks of "the sixty-six books of the Bible" [verified, quoted on cba1689.com].
- **Inspiration and inerrancy wording**: NH/BF&M "truth, without any mixture of error, for its matter"; BF&M 2000 adds "Therefore, all Scripture is totally true and trustworthy" and replaces the 1963 sentence "The criterion by which the Bible is to be interpreted is Jesus Christ" with "All Scripture is a testimony to Christ, who is Himself the focus of divine revelation" [verified: 2000 text; 1963 sentence verified in scratch via comparison chart].
- **Original-language text**: no Baptist confession names a standard Hebrew/Greek edition [verified absence in 2LCF 1.8 as WCF: "The Old Testament in Hebrew ... and the New Testament in Greek ... being immediately inspired by God" — wording memory].
- **Translations: no official translation.** Baptist bodies do not adopt translations. In practice:
  - English: King James Version historically; Holman Christian Standard Bible (2004) and Christian Standard Bible (2017) are published by Holman/Lifeway, the SBC's publishing arm [memory, not checked].
  - Spanish: Reina-Valera 1960 is the common pulpit Bible across Latin American Baptists [memory]; the Baptist publishing house Casa Bautista de Publicaciones / Editorial Mundo Hispano (El Paso; roots in Toluca 1905) produced the **Reina-Valera Actualizada** (1989; revised 2015, "RVA-2015") [snippet: Baptist Standard, Logos and retailer listings]. The official Spanish BF&M quotes no translation by name [verified: none named on page]. For the site's own quotations of Scripture, the project's Bible decisions apply, not a Baptist choice.

## 6. Liturgical and devotional sources (lex orandi)

Baptists have **no liturgy with doctrinal authority**; worship is regulated by Scripture (2LCF 22.1: God "may not be Worshipped according to the imaginations, and devices of Men ... under any visible representations, or any other way, not prescribed in the Holy Scriptures" [verified]) and ordered by each church. Bounded list of texts that function as witnesses:
1. **Church covenants** (local, recited at the Lord's Supper in many churches [memory]): New Hampshire 1833 "Declaration of Faith and Covenant" (McGlothlin p. 300, covenant text not printed there) [verified that it existed]; NAFWB Treatise Part I "Church Covenant" ("Having given ourselves to God, by faith in Christ, and adopted the Word of God as our rule of faith and practice, we now give ourselves to one another ...") [verified]; Spanish «Pacto de la iglesia» in the NAFWB *Tratado* [verified].
2. **Hymnals of conventions** as witnesses of devotion only: *Baptist Hymnal* (Lifeway, 1956/1975/1991/2008) and *Himnario Bautista* (Casa Bautista de Publicaciones, 1978) [memory, not checked]. Not doctrinal norms.
3. **Ordinance practice** described in confessions (2LCF 30.3 the minister prays, blesses, breaks and gives; NAFWB XVIII.3 foot washing; Philadelphia XXXI laying on of hands) [verified]. Church manuals (Pendleton 1867; Hiscox 1890) describe practice (§7).

## 9. Disputed points

1. **Confession vs creed.** Baptists insist confessions do not bind conscience (1925 statement), yet bodies use them as tests of fellowship and employment (§2.3). The BF&M 2000 report's phrase "instruments of doctrinal accountability" and the 2000 decisions to require employees' affirmation were contested within the SBC (a 2000 motion asked institutions not to require signatures) [snippet, Baptist Press]; this is a live intra-Baptist dispute, not for the site to settle.
2. **Which confession "is" Baptist.** No document is accepted by all Baptists; even the 1689 was "owned" by the Particular Baptists only ("denying Arminianism") [verified].
3. **BF&M 1963 → 2000 changes** (Scripture criterion sentence removed; pastor office limited to men; 1998 Family article; 2023 "pastor/elder/overseer") divided Southern Baptists; the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship and Texas/Virginia conventions kept or returned to 1963 [memory; BGCT-related Hispanic convention adopts "1963 y del 2000", verified]. Site must state which text a quote is from.
4. **Official Spanish BF&M lags the English.** bfm.sbc.net/es (snapshot 2026-04-13; live page re-checked 2026-10-14, verification B-log 3) still has the pre-2023 Art. VI ("Sus oficiales escriturales son pastores y diáconos ... el oficio de pastor está limitado a los hombres") while the English page carries the 2023 text ("pastor/elder/overseer") [verified]. A quote in Spanish must note this.
5. **New Hampshire 1833 vs 1853.** The 1833 original had 16 articles and no surviving copy was found by McGlothlin; the commonly printed text (McGlothlin, Pendleton, UEBE) is Brown's private 1853 revision with 18 articles [verified]. Article numbers differ (e.g. Baptism and the Lord's Supper is XIV in 1853, not in the 1833 numbering). **The Eucharist pilot cites "New Hampshire Confession (1833), Art. 14"** (`data/site/eucharist.v2.json`, and `sources.extra.json` id `new-hampshire-1833`, edition "gracegems.org transcription", date 1833) [verified]. Verification B-log 14: the gracegems page linked there is itself titled "The New Hampshire Baptist Confession of **1853**" (18 articles, "14. Of Baptism and the Lord's Supper", spelling modernised), and McGlothlin's 1853 text has the same numbering ("xiv. Of Baptism and the Lord's Supper ... preceded always by solemn self-examination") [verified there]. So "Art. 14" is correct for the 1853 text and the label "1833" is wrong. In the 1833 sixteen-article numbering the article would be 12 (derived from the headings by removing "Repentance and Faith" and "Sanctification"; no 1833 copy seen, McGlothlin found none) [verification B-log 14, derived].
6. **Spiritual presence vs memorial.** 2LCF 30.7 ("spiritually present to the faith of Believers") and AP XVI ("confirm the faith", "bond, pledge and renewal of their communion") vs BF&M 2000 VII ("symbolic act of obedience ... memorialize"). Both are Baptist; the pilot already shows them as two strands [verified].
7. **"Ordinance" vs "sacrament".** 2LCF 28, BF&M, AP, NAFWB say "ordinance"; the Orthodox Creed XXVII calls Baptism and the Supper "two sacraments" and XXXIII "this holy sacrament" [verified]. Number of ordinances: two (most) vs three (NAFWB: foot washing) [verified].
8. **Autonomy vs assemblies.** OC XXXIX holds general assemblies "of divine authority" with power to "hear, and determine, as also to excommunicate" [verified]; Particular Baptists and the SBC deny any body authority over a church (1644 XLII; SBC Art. IV) [verified].
9. **Status of the Abstract of Principles**: binding only on SBTS (and other seminaries that adopted it) faculty, not on churches [verified for SBTS].

### Southern Baptist Convention (SBC)

- **Constitution** (https://www.sbc.net/about/what-we-do/legal-documentation/constitution/ , URL corrected per verification B-log 4: the `legal-documents` path first logged here now returns 404; local copy `sbc-constitution.txt`) [verified]:
  - Art. III: a church is "in friendly cooperation" only if it "Has a faith and practice which closely identifies with the Convention's adopted statement of faith. (By way of example, churches which act to affirm, approve, or endorse homosexual behavior would be deemed not to be in cooperation with the Convention.)" Further criteria on sexual abuse and ethnic discrimination.
  - Art. IV "Authority": "While independent and sovereign in its own sphere, the Convention does not claim and will never attempt to exercise any authority over any other Baptist body, whether church, auxiliary organizations, associations, or convention."
- **BF&M 2000 preamble** = "Report of the Baptist Faith and Message Study Committee", adopted June 14, 2000 (https://bfm.sbc.net/preamble/ ; read via Wayback 2025 snapshot, local `bfm-pre-wb.txt`) [verified]. Key exact text:
  - "Throughout our history we have been a confessional people, adopting statements of faith as a witness to our beliefs and a pledge of our faithfulness to the doctrines revealed in Holy Scripture."
  - History: 1925 first adoption; 1963 revision; "The Convention added an article on 'The Family' in 1998"; the committee "affirms the decision of the Convention in 1925 to adopt the New Hampshire Confession of Faith, 'revised at certain points and with some additional articles growing out of certain needs . . . .'"
  - The 1925 "statement of the historic Baptist conception of the nature and function of confessions of faith" is "quoted in full": (1) "a consensus of opinion of some Baptist body, large or small, for the general instruction and guidance of our own people and others ... not intended to add anything to the simple conditions of salvation revealed in the New Testament, viz., repentance toward God and faith in Jesus Christ as Saviour and Lord." (2) "we do not regard them as complete statements of our faith, having any quality of finality or infallibility. As in the past so in the future, Baptists should hold themselves free to revise their statements of faith as may seem to them wise and expedient at any time." (3) "any group of Baptists, large or small, have the inherent right to draw up for themselves and publish to the world a confession of their faith". (4) "That the sole authority for faith and practice among Baptists is the Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments. Confessions are only guides in interpretation, having no authority over the conscience." (5) "statements of religious convictions, drawn from the Scriptures, and are not to be used to hamper freedom of thought or investigation in other realms of life."
  - Then (2000 wording): "Baptists cherish and defend religious liberty, and deny the right of any secular or religious authority to impose a confession of faith upon a church or body of churches. We honor the principles of soul competency and the priesthood of believers, affirming together both our liberty in Christ and our accountability to each other under the Word of God. ### Particular Baptists

- **Second London Confession, 1677 preface** "To the Judicious and Impartial Reader" (CCEL copy, local `ccel-1689-preface.txt`) [verified]: the confession was first put forth "about the year, 1643. in the name of seven Congregations then gathered in London"; they kept the Westminster/Savoy order and "making use of the very same words with them both, in these articles (which are very many) wherein our faith and doctrine is the same with theirs, and this we did, the more abundantly, to manifest our consent with both, in all the fundamental articles of the Christian Religion ... we have no itch to clogge Religion with new words"; texts of Scripture affixed "in the margin for the confirmation of each article"; readers asked to follow "the noble Bereans"; "contention is most remote from our design"; "Our whole design is accomplished, if we may obtain that Justice, as to be measured in our principles, and practise ... according to what we have now published".

- **First London Confession 1644** (McGlothlin pp. 168–201) [verified in OCR]: by "the seven congregations" of London; title "The Confession of Faith, Of those Churches which are commonly (though falsly) called Anabaptists"; 1646 second edition title "A Confession of Faith of seven Congregations or Churches of Christ in London, which are commonly (but unjustly) called Anabaptists". But if any man shall impose upon us any thing that we see not to be commanded by our Lord Jesus Christ, we should in his strength, rather embrace all reproaches and tortures of men ... rather than to doe any thing against the least tittle of the truth of God, or against the light of our own consciences." Art. XLII (excommunication power "given to every particular Congregation, and not one particular person"). ### New Hampshire Confession

- McGlothlin pp. 299–307 [verified in OCR]: Baptist Convention of New Hampshire appointed a committee June 24, 1830; drafted by I. Person; revised by Baron Stow and J. Newton Brown; on January 15, 1833 the Board voted "that the Declaration of Faith and Covenant prepared by Bren. Stow and Brown ... are entitled to their unanimous approbation, and are by them cordially recommended to the adoption of the churches." "It contained sixteen articles, and was published with the authority of the Board". "In 1853 Rev. J. Newton Brown, who was then editorial secretary of the American Baptist Publication Society, on his own authority revised this Confession, adding two articles, one on 'Repentance and Faith,' and one on 'Sanctification,' making eighteen in all. In this form he incorporated it in 'The Baptist Church Manual'". McGlothlin calls it "non-committal on every point of difference between the Calvinistic and Arminian systems" and "perhaps the most widely used and influential statement of doctrine among American Baptists at the present time" (1911). 18 articles (1853): Scriptures; True God; Fall of Man; Way of Salvation; Justification; Freeness of Salvation; Grace in Regeneration; Repentance and Faith; God's Purpose of Grace; Sanctification; Perseverance of Saints; Harmony of the Law and the Gospel; A Gospel Church; Baptism and the Lord's Supper; Christian Sabbath; Civil Government; Righteous and Wicked; World to Come.
- Status today: basis of BF&M 1925 (preamble, verified above); its Spanish 18-article form is the UEBE (Spain) "Confesión de Fe" (scratch `spanish-bodies.md` §1, verified).

#### 21. Independent / fundamental Baptists in Spanish (brief)

- Only one sourced case found: **Seminario Bíblico Bautista de España (SBBE)**, Spain, https://sbbe.org [verified]. History page: founded 1986 by US missionary Julio Lyons (Primera Iglesia Bautista de Torrejón de Ardoz); stated goal 4: "Favorecer la cooperación y ayuda mutua de las iglesias bautistas independientes de España"; registered with the Ministerio de Justicia in 1990; name "Seminario Bíblico Bautista de España" adopted September 2001 [verified]. Distinctive points: "los 66 libros ... verbal y plenariamente inspirados por Dios y sin error en los autógrafos originales"; creation "sin ningún proceso de evolución"; "los dones como señales fueron restringidos al periodo de los apóstoles" (cessationist); atonement "sin límites en cuanto a su poder para salvar. Sin embargo, este sacrificio es limitado en su aplicación"; "Seguridad eterna del creyente"; "la venida inminente, personal, premilenial, y pretribulacional de Cristo"; "la Iglesia ... durante esta presente dispensación de gracia" (dispensational); local church "es autónoma ... libre de toda jerarquía externa ... y que no debe asociarse con ninguna influencia ecuménica"; baptism by immersion, Lord's Supper "sólo por creyentes bíblicamente bautizados" (closed communion) [verified]. It is a 16-point dispensational/independent-Baptist-type statement, not the 1689 (so the SBBE is not a Reformed Baptist school).
