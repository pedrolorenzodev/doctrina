## 1. Scope

Churches that confess the Augsburg Confession (1530) — the BoC's own self-description is "the churches of the Augsburg Confession" (SD RN 2, https://bookofconcord.org/solid-declaration/rule-and-norm/ [verified]). ### 1.3 Quia vs quatenus
- *Quia* ("because") subscription: the confessions are accepted because they agree with Scripture; *quatenus* ("insofar as"): accepted only insofar as they agree. Usage in confessional Lutheran literature (the essay uses the pair in passing, in a question, not as a formal definition: verification-lutheran-reformed.md S-L2): T. Tomczak, "Hermeneutics and the Confessions", WELS Wisconsin Lutheran Seminary essay file: "a quia ('because') from a quatenus ('in so far as') subscription" https://www.wls.wels.net/rmdevser_wls/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/Hermeneutics-and-the-Confessions-Tomczak.pdf [verified]. The CELC constitution writes the distinction into its basis ("not in so far as, but because") [verified].
- Classic statement: C. F. W. Walther, "Why Should Our Pastors, Teachers, and Professors Subscribe Unconditionally to the Symbolical Writings of Our Church?" (essay, Western District of the Missouri Synod, 1858; English in *Concordia Theological Monthly* 18 (1947), https://scholar.csl.edu/ctm/vol18/iss1, PDF https://scholar.csl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3017&context=ctm — blocked to scripts, not read). Quoted via https://lutheranchronicles.substack.com/p/walther-on-unconditional-subscription: "By a conditional subscription to the Symbols the subscriber does not pledge himself to accept every doctrine contained in the Symbols as in full agreement with Scripture and reserves the right to distinguish between the doctrines presented" … "Therefore an unconditional subscription is indispensable." [verified on the secondary quotation only: the substack page carries both strings and dates the essay to the 1858 Western District convention, republished April 1947 in CTM vol. XVIII (verification-lutheran-reformed.md S-L1); the primary CTM scan is still not read]
- The *wiefern / quatenus* vs *weil / cum* wording is already in the FC itself: SD RN 10 accepts the BoC "because (*cum, weil*) it has been derived from God's Word" and other books only "as far as (*wofern, quatenus*) they are consistent" with it (Bente, Hist. Intr. §6, citing Triglot 855, 10) [verified].
- Labelling a body "quatenus" is an outside, often polemical, description: no constitution checked here says "quatenus". ELCA/IELU use graded verbs ("true witness" / "further valid interpretations"; "correcta manifestación" / "en armonía"), not the word. The site should quote each body's verb, not assign a label. [verified: absence in the texts quoted above]

## 2. How authority works (in the tradition's own terms)

### 2.1 Scripture alone is the "rule and norm"; the confessions are "witnesses"
The Formula of Concord opens with a section that the BoC itself titles *Von dem summarischen Begriff, Regel und Richtschnur* / *De compendiaria regula atque norma* ("Comprehensive Summary, Rule and Norm"). It is the tradition's own statement of how its authorities relate. [verified: Ep Rule and Norm, German and Latin in Triglot e-texts (scratch `dl/tg.txt` l.14073ff, `dl/tl.txt` l.13240ff); English at https://bookofconcord.org/epitome/rule-and-norm/]

- Ep RN 1: "the sole rule and standard according to which all dogmas together with [all] teachers should be estimated and judged are the prophetic and apostolic Scriptures of the Old and of the New Testament alone" — Latin *unicam regulam et normam … nullam omnino aliam esse quam prophetica et apostolica scripta*; German *die einige Regel und Richtschnur*. [verified]
- Ep RN 2: other writings "of ancient or modern teachers" (Latin *sive patrum sive neotericorum scripta*) "must not be regarded as equal to the Holy Scriptures … and should not be received otherwise or further than as witnesses" (*nisi testium loco*; *denn als Zeugen*). This is the BoC's own rule for the Church Fathers: they are witnesses to where and how apostolic doctrine was preserved, never a co-norm. [verified]
- Ep RN 3–5: the three ecumenical creeds, then the "First, Unaltered Augsburg Confession … together with its Apology, and the Articles composed at Smalcald", then Luther's Small and Large Catechisms "as the Bible of the laity" (*quasi laicorum biblia*; *Laienbibel*). The AC is called "the symbol of our time" (*nostri temporis Symbolum*). [verified]
- Ep RN 7: "the Holy Scriptures alone remain the only judge, rule, and standard … as the only test-stone" — Latin *sola Sacra Scriptura iudex, norma et regula agnoscitur, ad quam ceu ad Lydium lapidem*; German *der einige Richter, Regel und Richtschnur … dem einigen Probierstein*. [verified]
- Ep RN 8: "the other symbols and writings cited are not judges, as are the Holy Scriptures, but only a testimony and declaration of the faith (*testimonium … eamque explicant*; *allein Zeugnis und Erklärung des Glaubens*), as to how at any time the Holy Scriptures have been understood and explained in the articles in controversy". [verified]
- SD RN 1–13 (https://bookofconcord.org/solid-declaration/rule-and-norm/, read 2026-10-14) repeats this in six steps ("First … Scriptures … the only true standard"; "second … three Ecumenical Creeds"; "third … the First, Unaltered Augsburg Confession … not because it was composed by our theologians, but because it has been taken from God's Word"; "fourth … Apology"; "fifth … Smalcald Articles"; "sixth … Small and the Large Catechisms"). SD RN 2: the common doctrine "should not be based on private writings, but on such books as have been composed, approved, and received in the name of the churches". SD RN 10: other good books are "useful expositions" only "as far as they are consistent with the above-mentioned type of doctrine"; the BoC is "a unanimously accepted, definite, common form of doctrine … from and according to which, because it has been derived from God's Word, all other writings should be judged". [verified]
- SD RN 9 on **Luther's other writings**: "to his full explanations in his doctrinal and polemical writings we wish to appeal, in the manner and as far as Dr. Luther himself in the Latin preface to his published works has given necessary and Christian admonition concerning his writings … that the Word of God alone should be and remain the only standard and rule of doctrine, to which the writings of no man should be regarded as equal". → Luther's works are appealed to as explanation, explicitly not as a norm (see §4 Tier 4). [verified]

### 2.2 *Norma normans* / *norma normata*
The pair "norming norm" (Scripture) / "normed norm" (the confessions) is the standard later vocabulary for the Rule-and-Norm relationship in confessional Lutheranism (and is also used by Reformed writers). **The Latin terms do not occur in the BoC itself**: a search of the full Triglot Latin and German e-texts finds 0 hits for "normans"/"normata" [verified by grep, 2026-10-14]. The BoC's own vocabulary is *regula et norma*, *iudex*, *lapis Lydius* / *Probierstein* (Scripture) vs. *testes*, *testimonium*, *Zeugnis und Erklärung*, *symbolum* (the confessions). Usage of the pair for the BoC: Wikipedia "Book of Concord" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Concord) [snippet]; Logos, "What You Need to Know to Study the Bible Like a Lutheran" (https://www.logos.com/grow/lutheran-bible-study/) [snippet]. Which 17th-c. dogmatician coined it was **not** established in this run [open; see §9]. Recommendation for the site: present the category with the BoC's own words (Ep RN 7–8) and give *norma normans / norma normata* as the conventional label.

### 2.3 "Symbol", particular vs ecumenical, and how a text becomes confessional
- The BoC calls its documents *symbola* / *Bekenntnisse*; SD RN 2 requires that they be "composed, approved, and received in the name of the churches", not private writings. [verified, SD RN 2]
- Bente, Hist. Intr. §1 classifies symbols as (1) ecumenical, (2) particular (adopted by a church body), (3) private (e.g. Luther's *Confession concerning Christ's Supper* 1528), and states "only such are symbols of particular churches as have been approved and adopted by them". [verified, PG #26909 §1; exact string re-found in Bente's introduction by verification L-log 16, which did not re-check the § number]
- Some BoC documents became symbols by formal act (AC 1530 read to the Diet; Tr and Ap subscribed at Smalcald 1537 by order of the estates; FC by the 1577–1580 subscription), others by reception: Bente §76 (SA) and §115 (catechisms) say they "achieved their symbolical authority by themselves, without resolutions of princes, estates, and theologians", and the FC "is merely chronicling actual facts" when adopting them. [verified, Bente §§60, 76, 78, 115]
- About 8,000 theologians, pastors and teachers subscribed the FC / BoC in 1577–1580; the subscribers' pledge (SD XII.40) reads: "this declaration … and no other, is our faith, doctrine, and confession … we will neither privately nor publicly speak or write anything contrary to it". [verified, Bente §6 quoting Triglot 1103, 40]
- The Preface to the BoC (1580): the signatories "determined not to depart even a finger's breadth either from the subjects themselves or from the phrases which are found in them (*vel a rebus ipsis vel a phrasibus*)". [verified via Bente §6 quoting Triglot p. 23; Preface itself at https://bookofconcord.org/preface/ fetched, not re-read line by line]

### 2.4 Who can define or revise
- The BoC knows no teaching office that adds to it. The FC presents itself not as a "new confession" but as a repetition and explanation of the AC (SD RN 2: "we will not make or receive a separate or new confession of our faith"; SD Preface 5 per Bente §6: "neither in this nor in any other writing, to recede in the least from that oft-cited Confession, nor to propose another or new confession"). [verified]
- Bente §1: "Self-evidently, only such are symbols of particular churches as have been approved and adopted by them." Today, which confessions bind is set by each church body's constitution (quia vs quatenus subscription, §2.5 and §4 Tier 1). There is no Lutheran body that can bind all Lutherans; the Lutheran World Federation and the International Lutheran Council are communions/associations, not magisteria (cited in §1). [verified for Bente; see §1 for the bodies]

## 4. The canon, by tiers

Tier logic (the tradition's own, per FC Rule and Norm): **Tier 0** Scripture (§5) → **Tier 1** the symbolical books of the BoC (closed) → **Tier 2** writings the BoC itself points to as explanation, chiefly Luther's (bounded) → **Tier 3** historical confessional texts outside the BoC (context; normative in no body checked) → **Tier 4** body-level statements and ecumenical agreements (bind only the bodies that adopted them; open-ended, rule given) → liturgy (§6) and representative voices (§7).

### Tier 1 — The Book of Concord (1580): closed, enumerated

**The book as a whole.** German *Concordia. Christliche, Wiederholete, einmütige Bekenntnüs …*, published officially at Dresden on 25 June 1580 (50th anniversary of the AC) [this date is not in Bente's text: unconfirmed in verification-lutheran-reformed.md S-L4; the rest of this paragraph re-confirmed there]; Latin *Concordia* first privately by Selneccer 1580, official Latin *textus receptus* Leipzig 1584. Contents of the official German edition (Bente's description of a Dresden folio copy): title; Preface (signed by the estates, Jüterbog 1579); three ecumenical creeds; AC; Apology; SA "with the appendix, 'Concerning the Power and Supremacy of the Pope'"; SC ("omitting the 'Booklets of Marriage and Baptism,' found in some copies"); LC; FC (Ep and SD, separate title pages dated 1580); signatures (~8,000); Catalog of Testimonies "with the superscription 'Appendix' (found in some copies only)". Paragraph numbers are not original: "Karl Hase, in his editions of 1827, 1837, and 1845, was the first to number the paragraphs". [verified, Bente §§2–3]

### Tier 2 — Luther's writings: honoured explanation, not norm (bounded)

## 5. Scripture

- **Status.** "The prophetic and apostolic Scriptures of the Old and of the New Testament alone" are the sole rule and norm (Ep RN 1); SD RN 3: "the pure, clear fountain of Israel, which is the only true standard by which all teachers and doctrines are to be judged" [verified]. LCMS: "the written Word of God and the only rule and norm of faith and of practice" (Const. art. II.1) [verified]; ELCA 2.03: "the inspired Word of God and the authoritative source and norm of its proclamation, faith, and life" [verified]; LWF art. II: "the only source and norm of its doctrine, life and service" [verified]; IELU: "única regla infalible" [verified].
- **Canon.** The BoC gives **no list of canonical books** [verified: Ep RN 1 and SD RN 3 name only "the prophetic and apostolic Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments"; no canon article in any BoC table of contents]. Practice since the 16th c.: the Hebrew-canon OT (39) + NT (27) as Scripture; the "Apocrypha" printed between the Testaments in the Luther Bible (1534) under the heading "Apocrypha: Das sind Bücher, so der heiligen Schrift nicht gleich gehalten, und doch nützlich und gut zu lesen sind" ("books not held equal to Holy Scripture, yet useful and good to read") [snippet: Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft, "Luther und die Apokryphen", https://www.die-bibel.de/luther-und-die-apokryphen; unconfirmed in verification-lutheran-reformed.md S-L3 (the page returns 403 to scripts)]. The Apology itself cites 2 Macc 15:14 once, as a "dream" and as the only testimony for the departed praying (Ap XXI.9, Latin: *praeter illud somnium sumptum ex libro Machabaeorum posteriore*) [verified, Triglot Latin `dl/tl.txt` l.5419–5423]. Luther's NT prefaces (1522) set Hebrews, James, Jude and Revelation apart [memory]; Chemnitz (*Examen* I) treats the NT antilegomena as of lesser authority for proving doctrine [memory]. No body checked has an article fixing the canon [verified absence in LCMS art. II, ELCA ch. 2, LWF art. II, IELU, IERP texts]. → For the site's "canon of Scripture" page: say the confessions do not define the canon, quote Ep RN 1 and Ap XXI.9, and present the Luther Bible heading as historical practice (Tier 2/3), not as a confession.
- **Original-language text.** No confessional rule. The BoC quotes the Vulgate (Latin texts) and Luther's German Bible (German texts) [verified by inspection, e.g. Ep RN 1 Ps 119:105 in both]. 
- **Translations by body** (none is "official" in a confessional sense): German churches: Lutherbibel (rev. 2017, EKD) [memory]; LCMS: ESV in *Lutheran Service Book* (2006) [memory]; ELCA: NRSV/NRSVue in *Evangelical Lutheran Worship* (2006) [memory]; WELS: NIV 2011 and the Evangelical Heritage Version (EHV, 2019) [memory]. **Spanish:** no Lutheran body has its own Spanish translation; Spanish Lutheran publications use Reina–Valera 1960 (the Misión Luterana PR catechism text appears to quote RVR1960 wording [memory, per protestant-magisterial.verify.md]) and Dios Habla Hoy / NVI [memory]. ## 9. Disputed points (status of documents and texts)

1. **Catalog of Testimonies**: in some 1580 copies only, sometimes headed "Appendix", "not regarded as a part of the confession proper" (Bente §2) [verified]; bookofconcord.org: "not a formal part of the Lutheran Confessions, per se" [verified]. Treat as witness, not confession.
2. **Treatise: separate confession or appendix?** FC and the BoC Preface treat it as an appendix to SA (Bente §79) [verified]; LCMS art. II omits it by name, ELCA 2.06 names it [verified]. Both include it in "the BoC"; no body excludes it.
3. **Which AC text.** Invariata vs Variata (1540) (§4 Tier 1) [verified]. Within the Invariata: German and Latin are both official and not identical; the 1584 Latin *textus receptus* "follow[s] the editio princeps of 1531" for AC and Ap (Bente §3) [verified]; the FC SD II.29 quotes in some 1580 copies the 1531 octavo (already altered) rather than the Mainz MS (Bente §2) [verified]. Bente §27 discusses the manuscripts [verified heading only].
4. **Apology: private or official?** Published under Melanchthon's name as a private work, recognised officially from 1532/1537 (Bente §60) [verified]. Quarto (April 1531) vs octavo (Sept 1531) differences (Bente §57 "Alterations of Apology" [verified heading]); the BoC follows the quarto editio princeps [verified Bente §3]. Plus the numbering problem (§4 concordance).
5. **Latin vs German priority** for SA (German original; Latin by Generanus [memory]) and catechisms (German original). Matters for Mary: SA I.4 German has "von der reinen, heiligen Jungfrau Maria" while the Latin has "ex Maria, pura, sancta sempervirgine nasceretur" ("ever virgin") [verified, `dl/tg.txt` l.8514, `dl/tl.txt` l.7142–7143. Corrected 2026-10-15: the e-text writes one word, *sempervirgine*, with no comma after *sancta*; this line had "sancta, semper virgine", and a search for "semper virgine" misses it (verification-lutheran-reformed.md L-log 21)]; Triglot English renders "pure, holy [and always] Virgin", the bracket marking the Latin-only phrase [verified]. SD VIII.24 (German original) has "truly the mother of God, and nevertheless remained a virgin" [verified English]. Cite the language explicitly on the Mary page.

7. **Reception of the FC.** Not adopted in Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel, Holstein, Hesse, Pomerania (later received), Anhalt, the Palatinate, Nuremberg, Strasbourg, Bremen, Danzig, Magdeburg, Frankfurt and others; Denmark forbade it in 1580 (Bente §284) [verified; the territory list re-confirmed in verification L-log 16, but the Denmark clause was not read to the end there: unconfirmed in verification-lutheran-reformed.md L-log 16]. Bente says the FC "was adopted 1593 by the Council of Upsala" in Sweden [verified as Bente's claim], whereas the Church of Sweden's own church order says Uppsala 1593 affirmed the creeds + AC and that the BoC "explains and comments" (*förklarad och kommenterad*) [verified KO 1 kap. 1 §]. Conflict between a 1921 LCMS historian and the body's own text: use the body's own text.
8. **Subscription: quia vs quatenus**, and whether graded wordings (ELCA, IELU, LWF "especially") amount to quatenus: contested between confessional bodies and LWF churches; no constitution checked uses the word "quatenus" [verified absence]. Report verbs, not labels.
9. **JDDJ (1999).** Binding for LWF member churches that affirmed it and the Catholic Church; rejected by ILC/CELC bodies [snippet]. Whether it changes what "the Lutheran view" of justification is: contested. The site must present it as an LWF–Catholic agreement, not as a Lutheran confession.
10. **Origin of *norma normans / normata*** not established (post-BoC; §2.2). [open]
11. **Luther's authority**: SD VII.41 calls him "the most distinguished teacher" and the best source for the AC's meaning, while SD RN 9 subordinates his writings to Scripture [verified]. Lutheran bodies differ in how much weight they give his non-confessional writings; none makes them normative [verified absence in §1.2 texts].
