## 1. Scope

**Who.** The Catholic Church: the communion of the Latin Church and the Eastern Catholic Churches *sui iuris* in full communion with the Bishop of Rome. Vatican II describes it as "made up of the faithful who are organically united in the Holy Spirit by the same faith, the same sacraments and the same government", forming "separate Churches or Rites" that are "of equal dignity" and "each as much as the others, entrusted to the pastoral government of the Roman Pontiff" (Orientalium Ecclesiarum 2–3, https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19641121_orientalium-ecclesiarum_en.html) `[verified]`.

**Size.** "In 2024, the Catholic population stood at just over 1.422 billion, compared with about 1.406 billion in 2023" (Vatican News, 28 Mar 2026, reporting the *Annuario Pontificio 2026* and *Annuarium Statisticum Ecclesiae 2024*, https://www.vaticannews.va/en/vatican-city/news/2026-03/new-data-from-annuario-pontificio-2026-shows-catholics-growing.html) `[verified]`.

**Eastern Catholic Churches.** Commonly counted as 23 Eastern Churches *sui iuris* besides the Latin Church (Catholic World Report, 20 Sep 2026, https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2026/09/20/the-23-eastern-catholic-churches-ancient-traditions-in-communion-with-rome/) `[snippet; CWR page not fetched in verification; the conventional count 23 matches Wikipedia's CCEO article, ver. C11; the Dicastery for the Eastern Churches profile page states no number]`; counting conventions vary (22/23/24) `[snippet]`. How they fit this map:
- Same doctrinal authority: OE 3 (above) puts them under the same Roman primacy; the 1998 CDF commentary and *Ad tuendam fidem* bind both codes in parallel (CIC can. 750 ↔ CCEO can. 598; CIC 752 ↔ CCEO 599) `[verified]` (see §2).
- Own discipline and liturgy: OE 5 "solemnly declares that the Churches of the East, as much as those of the West, have a full right and are in duty bound to rule themselves, each in accordance with its own established disciplines" `[verified]`. Canon law: CIC 1983 can. 1 "The canons of this Code regard only the Latin Church" `[verified]` (vatican.va CIC); the Eastern Churches have the *Codex Canonum Ecclesiarum Orientalium* (1990), promulgated by *Sacri canones*, 18 Oct 1990 ("Sacri Canones, die XVIII Octobris anno MCMXC"; AAS 82 (1990) 1033–1044) `[verified: ver. C9, https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/la/apost_constitutions/documents/hf_jp-ii_apc_19901018_sacri-canones.html]`.
- Decision for this map: Eastern Catholics are **not** mapped as a separate family. Their doctrinal sources are this same universe; their liturgical books (Byzantine, Syriac, Armenian, Coptic, etc.) are noted in §6 as lex orandi witnesses only.

**What this map covers.** The doctrinal sources of the Catholic Church as the Church itself grades them (§2–3): Scripture and Tradition; the creeds; the 21 councils it counts as ecumenical; papal definitions and the ordinary papal and curial magisterium (open-ended, indexed by Denzinger–Hünermann); the universal catechisms (Roman Catechism, CCC, Compendium); canon law (as discipline, not doctrine); the liturgy as a witness of faith. Particular-church documents (episcopal conferences, diocesan synods) are out of scope except where noted, because CIC can. 753 says bishops "do not possess infallibility in teaching" individually or in conferences `[verified]`.

**Open scope questions for Pedro** (see also §10):
1. Whether "Catholic" on the site means the universal magisterium only (recommended) or should also show regional voices (e.g. CELAM documents, Spanish/Argentine episcopal conferences) for the es audience.
2. Whether Eastern Catholic liturgical texts should ever be quoted under "Catholic" (risk of confusing users with the Orthodox column, whose liturgy is often textually identical).

## 2. How authority works (in the Church's own terms)

**One deposit, two modes of transmission, one interpreter.** Vatican II, *Dei Verbum* (18 Nov 1965), https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html `[verified]`:
- DV 9: Scripture and Tradition, "flowing from the same divine wellspring, in a certain way merge into a unity"; "it is not from Sacred Scripture alone that the Church draws her certainty about everything which has been revealed. Therefore both sacred tradition and Sacred Scripture are to be accepted and venerated with the same sense of loyalty and reverence."
- DV 10: "Sacred tradition and Sacred Scripture form one sacred deposit of the word of God, committed to the Church." "The task of authentically interpreting the word of God, whether written or handed on, has been entrusted exclusively to the living teaching office of the Church … This teaching office is not above the word of God, but serves it." "Sacred tradition, Sacred Scripture and the teaching authority of the Church … are so linked and joined together that one cannot stand without the others."
- DV 8: Tradition "develops in the Church with the help of the Holy Spirit", through contemplation of believers, experience, and "the preaching of those who have received through Episcopal succession the sure gift of truth"; "the words of the holy fathers witness to the presence of this living tradition"; "Through the same tradition the Church's full canon of the sacred books is known."
- Revelation is closed: "no new public revelation is to be expected before the glorious manifestation of our Lord Jesus Christ" (CCC 66, quoting DV 4) `[verified]` (https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PH.HTM); LG 25: pope and bishops "do not accept" "a new public revelation … as pertaining to the divine deposit of faith" `[verified]`.

**Who teaches with binding authority.** "On questions of faith and morals, the only subject qualified to fulfil the office of teaching with binding authority for the faithful is the Supreme Pontiff and the College of Bishops in communion with him" (CDF, *Doctrinal Commentary on the Concluding Formula of the Professio fidei*, 29 Jun 1998, §4, https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_1998_professio-fidei_en.html) `[verified]`.

**Infallibility and its three bearers.** *Lumen gentium* 25 (21 Nov 1964), https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html `[verified]`, mirrored in CIC can. 749 `[verified]`:
1. The Roman Pontiff "when, as the supreme shepherd and teacher of all the faithful … by a definitive act he proclaims a doctrine of faith or morals"; "his definitions, of themselves, and not from the consent of the Church, are justly styled irreformable" (LG 25; Vatican I, *Pastor aeternus* ch. 4 — DH 3074, cited by the 1998 commentary fn. 11) `[verified]`.
2. The bishops "gathered together in an ecumenical council … whose definitions must be adhered to with the submission of faith" (LG 25) `[verified]`.
3. The bishops "dispersed through the world" in communion with each other and the pope, when "they are in agreement on one position as definitively to be held" — the *ordinary and universal magisterium* (LG 25; CIC 749 §2) `[verified]`.
- Limit: "No doctrine is understood as defined infallibly unless this is manifestly evident" (CIC 749 §3; Latin "Infallibiliter definita nulla intellegitur doctrina, nisi id manifesto constiterit", https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/latin/documents/cic_liberIII_la.html) `[verified]`.
- Scope: infallibility "extends as far as the deposit of Revelation extends" (LG 25) `[verified]`.
- Defining vs non-defining acts: a doctrine is taught infallibly either by a defining act (ex cathedra or ecumenical council) or by a non-defining act of the ordinary and universal magisterium; the pope may "confirm or reaffirm" such a doctrine "without recourse to a solemn definition", which is "not a new dogmatic definition, but a formal attestation of a truth already possessed" (1998 commentary §9) `[verified]`. Fn. 17 adds that the ordinary universal magisterium is understood "in a diachronic and not necessarily merely synchronic sense", and its intention to teach definitively "is not generally linked to technical formulations of particular solemnity" `[verified]`.

**Non-infallible ("authentic") magisterium.** LG 25: "religious submission of mind and will must be shown in a special way to the authentic magisterium of the Roman Pontiff, even when he is not speaking ex cathedra … His mind and will in the matter may be known either from the character of the documents, from his frequent repetition of the same doctrine, or from his manner of speaking" `[verified]`. These three criteria (type of document, repetition, manner of speaking) recur in *Donum veritatis* 24 and the 1998 commentary §11 `[verified]`, and are the Church's own rule for weighting a non-definitive text.
- Encyclicals: "Nor must it be thought that what is expounded in Encyclical Letters does not of itself demand consent, since in writing such Letters the Popes do not exercise the supreme power of their Teaching Authority. For these matters are taught with the ordinary teaching authority … But if the Supreme Pontiffs in their official documents purposely pass judgment on a matter up to that time under dispute, it is obvious that that matter … cannot be any longer considered a question open to discussion among theologians" (Pius XII, *Humani generis* 20, 12 Aug 1950, https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_12081950_humani-generis.html) `[verified]`.
- Prudential interventions: "it could happen that some Magisterial documents might not be free from all deficiencies" in questions with "contingent and conjectural elements"; yet "the willingness to submit loyally to the teaching of the Magisterium on matters per se not irreformable must be the rule" (CDF, *Donum veritatis* 24, 24 May 1990, https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19900524_theologian-vocation_en.html) `[verified]`.
- Discipline: "magisterial decisions in matters of discipline, even if they are not guaranteed by the charism of infallibility, are not without divine assistance and call for the adherence of the faithful" (*Donum veritatis* 17) `[verified]`.
- Individual bishops and episcopal conferences: "do not possess infallibility in teaching" but "are authentic teachers"; the faithful adhere "with religious submission of mind" (CIC 753) `[verified]`.

**How a council's own texts are weighted.** For Vatican II the Council itself stated (Notification of the Secretary General, 16 Nov 1964, appended to LG, quoting the Theological Commission's declaration of 6 Mar 1964): "the sacred Council defines as binding on the Church only those things in matters of faith and morals which it shall openly declare to be binding. The rest of the things which the sacred Council sets forth, inasmuch as they are the teaching of the Church's supreme magisterium, ought to be accepted and embraced by each and every one of Christ's faithful according to the mind of the sacred Council. The mind of the Council becomes known either from the matter treated or from its manner of speaking, in accordance with the norms of theological interpretation" `[verified]` (LG page above). Paul VI, general audience 12 Jan 1966: the Council "ha evitato di pronunciare in modo straordinario dogmi dotati della nota di infallibilità; ma esso ha tuttavia munito i suoi insegnamenti dell'autorità del supremo magistero ordinario" (https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/it/audiences/1966/documents/hf_p-vi_aud_19660112.html) `[verified]`.

**Development.** "There is a growth in the understanding of the realities and the words which have been handed down" (DV 8) `[verified]`. A truth first held definitively (2nd paragraph) can later be defined as revealed (1st paragraph): the commentary's own example is papal primacy/infallibility before and after Vatican I (1998 commentary §11) `[verified]`. "Hierarchy of truths": "in Catholic doctrine there exists an order or 'hierarchy' of truths, since they vary in their relation to the foundation of the Christian faith" (CCC 90, quoting *Unitatis redintegratio* 11, https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PM.HTM) `[verified: ver. A8, CCC 90 is on __PM.HTM]`.

**Canonical consequences** (why grades matter in practice):
- Denial or obstinate doubt of a truth "to be believed by divine and Catholic faith" = heresy (CIC 751) `[verified]`; penalty CIC 1364 §1 `[verified]`.
- Rejecting 2nd-paragraph (definitive) or 3rd-paragraph (authentic) teaching after warning: "just penalty" — formerly CIC 1371 1° (as amended by *Ad tuendam fidem*, 1998), now **CIC 1365** after the 2021 revision of Book VI ("teaches a doctrine … or obstinately rejects the teaching mentioned in canon 750 § 2 or canon 752") `[verified]` (vatican.va CIC Book VI page; *Ad tuendam fidem* text). Eastern parallel CCEO 1436 §2 `[verified, via Ad tuendam fidem]`.
- Profession of faith obligatory for office-holders "according to the formula approved by the Apostolic See" (CIC 833) `[verified]`.

**Who may define or revise.** Only the pope, or the college of bishops with and under the pope (in council or dispersed) (LG 25; CIC 749; 1998 commentary §4) `[verified]`. Definitions are "irreformable" (LG 25) `[verified]`; disciplinary law can be changed by the legislator (e.g. CIC 1983 can. 6 §1 abrogated the 1917 Code) `[verified: cic_1_6 sample, vatican.va]`. The Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith assists the pope in doctrine (*Praedicate Evangelium*, 19 Mar 2022, arts. 69–78, https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/apost_constitutions/documents/20220319-costituzione-ap-praedicate-evangelium.html) `[verified: article range in the ToC]`.

**Category NOT magisterial: the manuals' "theological notes".** Classical manuals grade propositions with *notae theologicae* (*de fide divina et catholica*, *de fide definita*, *sententia fidei proxima*, *theologice certa*, *sententia communis*, *probabilis*, …) and *censurae* (heretical, erroneous, rash…). These are a school classification, not a magisterial one; the magisterium itself uses only some of the words (the 1998 commentary uses "erroneous", "rash or dangerous", "tuto doceri non potest"). Ott's scheme (*Fundamentals of Catholic Dogma*, §8 "The Theological Grades of Certainty", Eng. ed. pp. 9–10; Spanish *Manual de teología dogmática*, Herder 1958, pp. ≈36–37), with quotations, is in §7.3 `[unconfirmed: ver. I6 — read by the map agent in scans of an in-copyright book that may not be used; re-check in a licensed copy]`. Ott's six grades: (1) *de fide (definita)*; (2) *fides ecclesiastica* (Church doctrines finally decided by the infallible magisterium); (3) *sententia fidei proxima*; (4) *sententia ad fidem pertinens / theologice certa*; (5) *sententia communis*; (6) *probabilis, probabilior, bene fundata, pia*, and *opinio tolerata*. His grade 2 roughly corresponds to the Church's later grade #2 (*definitive tenenda*) `[inference]`; grades 3–6 have no magisterial counterpart.

**Implication for the site's "Grado de autoridad" table.** The Catholic column can use exactly four grades from the Church's own vocabulary: (1) *de fe divina y católica* (dogma), (2) *doctrina definitiva*, (3) *magisterio auténtico* (with the LG 25 weighting criteria as sub-notes: type of document, repetition, manner of speaking), (4) *disciplina*. Anything else (e.g. "sententia communis") must be labelled as a manual's classification (Ott), not the Church's.

## 4. The canon, by tiers

### 4.0 How the tiers are built (and why)

The Church does not grade *documents*; it grades *teachings* (§3). A single document can contain all four grades (e.g. Trent: dogmatic canons = grade 1, reform decrees = grade 4; Vatican II: mostly grade 3 by its own declaration). So the tiers below sort documents by **who issues them and how** (the Church's own criterion in LG 25: "the character of the documents"), and each entry says which grades it contains. A later per-doctrine agent must cite the **division** (canon, chapter, paragraph) and its grade, never "the document" as a whole.

| Tier | Name (es / en) | Membership | Closed or open |

#### 4.B.1 The rule: what grounds the Catholic count of 21

##### 4.B.1.1 Short answer
There is **no conciliar or papal act that fixes the list of 21**. The number is a received convention, built in three steps. This is stated by a Jesuit canonist at the Pontifical Oriental Institute in a peer-reviewed article: "Catholics generally exhibit a longer list of 21 ecumenical councils including the two Vatican Councils. But this is not an official list or canon fixed by any ecumenical council or papal definition or decree." and, after the Roman edition, "the number grew to 21, although no authoritative church magisterium established this canon" [verified: Nedungatt 2010, pp. 657-658, URL in table above].

##### 4.B.1.2 How the list was built
1. **Bellarmine (c. 1586-93), 18 councils.** Robert Bellarmine's "IV Controversia generalis, De Conciliis" lists 18 councils, omitting Constance but including Trent [verified: Nedungatt 2010 p. 657, citing Bellarmine, Opera omnia (Paris: Vivès, 1870) 2:199-204]. A Commonweal letter says he treated Constance as partly approved, partly rejected [snippet: https://www.commonwealmagazine.org/letters-popes-councils-scarcity-supermarket]. The count 18 matches nicely: 8 (Nicaea I - Constantinople IV) + 4 Lateran + 2 Lyon + Vienne + Florence + Lateran V + Trent = 18, i.e. Basel-Ferrara-Florence counted as one council (Florence) and Constance left out [inference].
   - Note: the dating "c. 1586-93" for De Conciliis is [memory] (Disputationes de controversiis christianae fidei, first printed Ingolstadt 1586-93); not checked.
2. **The Roman edition (1608-1612), 19 councils.** "A group of Roman scholars working under the patronage of Pope Paul V assumed Bellarmine's list but added to it the Council of Constance and published a complete collection of the decrees of the ecumenical councils". Title: *Tōn hagiōn oikoumenikōn synodōn tēs katholikēs ekklēsias hapanta: Concilia generalia Ecclesiae catholicae Pauli V pont. max. auctoritate edita*, 4 vols., Rome, Typographia Vaticana, 1608-1612. "This so-called 'Roman edition' did not, however, contain any papal decree and therefore was not an official Catholic collection. Nevertheless, with it a list of 19 ecumenical councils began to circulate in the West" [verified: Nedungatt 2010 pp. 657-658 and n. 13]. The edition omitted the acts of Basel (on Bellarmine's advice, per CE) [verified: CE art., "Bibliography" section: "On Bellarmine's advice Sirmond omitted the Acts of the Synod of Basle. This Roman collection is the foundation of all that followed"] and Tanner's note on Constance says the Editio Romana "ignores the council of Basel" [verified: pe_ecum16, https://www.papalencyclicals.net/councils/ecum16.htm].
3. **+ Vatican I (1869-70) = 20; + Vatican II (1962-65) = 21.**
   - CE 1908 lists "twenty Ecumenical councils" (ending with Vatican I = 20th; Constance 16th "ecumenical only in its last sessions (XLII-XLV inclusive) and with respect to the decrees of earlier sessions approved by Martin V"; Basle/Ferrara/Florence 17th "only ecumenical till the end of the twenty-fifth session" of Basel; Lateran V 18th; Trent 19th) [verified: CE art., section "Historical sketch"].
   - **John XXIII, opening address of Vatican II (Gaudet Mater Ecclesia, 11 Oct 1962)** counts 20 before it and calls Vatican II the 21st. Italian: "Tutti i Concili — sia i venti Ecumenici sia gli innumerevoli e da non sottovalutare Provinciali e Regionali" (I Concili Ecumenici nella Chiesa, 2.1) and "Il ventunesimo Concilio Ecumenico" (§6.2, "In che modo va sviluppata oggi la dottrina"); official Latin: "sive viginti Oecumenica" and "Concilium Oecumenicum primum et vicesimum" [verified: https://www.vatican.va/content/john-xxiii/la/speeches/1962/documents/hf_j-xxiii_spe_19621011_opening-council.html and .../it/speeches/1962/documents/hf_j-xxiii_spe_19621011_opening-council.html]. This is the clearest Holy See statement of the number 21; it is a papal speech, not a definition.
   - **DH 43rd edition** (the standard Catholic reference for magisterial texts) labels all 21 with their ordinals in its headers: "First Council of NICAEA (First Ecumenical) ... Fourth Council of CONSTANTINOPLE (Eighth Ecumenical) ... Council of FLORENCE (Seventeenth Ecumenical) ... Council of TRENT (Nineteenth Ecumenical) ... First VATICAN Council (Twentieth Ecumenical) ... Second VATICAN Council (Twenty-First Ecumenical)" [unconfirmed: DH; ver. F5 lists the DH ordinal headers as unconfirmable].
4. **Paul VI's 1974 letter (Lyon II).** Lateran I to Lyon II are described as "general synods held in the Western world". Quote (official Latin, vatican.va): "Hoc Lugdunense Concilium, quod **sextum recensetur inter Generales Synodos in Occidentali orbe celebratas**, anno MCCLXXIV a Decessore Nostro Beato Gregorio X convocatum est..." (4th paragraph). Addressed to Card. Johannes Willebrands, whom Paul VI named "Missum Extraordinarium" to preside the Lyon celebrations of the 7th centenary of "alterum Generale Concilium Lugdunense". Closing formula: "Datum Romae, apud Sanctum Petrum, die V mensis Octobris, anno MCMLXXIV, Pontificatus Nostri duodecimo" [verified: https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/la/letters/1974/documents/hf_p-vi_let_19741019_centenario-concilio-ecumenico.html ; local copy sc/pvi_lyon.txt].
   - Date caveat: the letter is dated **5 Oct 1974** in the text, but the vatican.va file name and index list it under **19 Oct 1974** (index heading in English: "Letter to Cardinal Giovanni Willebrands on the 7th centenary of the 6th Ecumenical Council of Lyon (October 19, 1974)") [verified: https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/letters/1974.index.html]. Only the Latin text is on vatican.va (the it and fr URL variants returned 200/404 respectively; I did not find an Italian/French translation) [verified for Latin; Italian/French presence not confirmed]. Published in AAS 66 (1974) 620 [verified via Nedungatt 2010 n. 18].
   - Reading: Paul VI does not call Lyon II "ecumenical" in the text; he calls it a "general synod of the West", "sixth" in that series. Counting from Lateran I (1123): Lateran I-IV = 1-4, Lyon I = 5, Lyon II = 6; so the Western series excludes Constantinople IV (held in the East) [inference]. Nedungatt reads this as Paul VI calling it "one of the 'general councils of the West' rather than a general council (without qualification) or an ecumenical council" [verified: Nedungatt 2010 p. 659]. The English index on vatican.va nevertheless uses "6th Ecumenical Council of Lyon" in its title [verified: same index].
5. **Current law.** CIC 1983 c. 338 §1: "It is for the Roman Pontiff alone to convoke an ecumenical council, preside over it personally or through others, transfer, suspend, or dissolve a council, and to approve its decrees"; c. 341 §1: decrees "do not have obligatory force unless they have been approved by the Roman Pontiff together with the council fathers, confirmed by him, and promulgated at his order"; c. 749 §2: the college of bishops "possesses infallibility in teaching when the bishops gathered together in an ecumenical council exercise the magisterium ... and declare ... that a doctrine of faith or morals is to be held definitively"; c. 749 §3: "No doctrine is understood as defined infallibly unless this is manifestly evident" [verified: CIC URLs above]. These are the canonical criteria (papal convocation/presidency/confirmation); they are not a list.
6. **Annuario Pontificio / vatican.va "official list".** I did **not** find an official Holy See table of the 21 councils (Annuario or vatican.va). Searches for an Annuario council list returned nothing; the Annuario is not freely available. Treat "Annuario lists 21" as [unverified]. What is verifiable from the Holy See: John XXIII's "XXI" (above), the Vatican II documents archive at https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/index.htm (Vatican II only), and the Latin texts of Lateran/Lyon etc. are not hosted by vatican.va.

##### 4.B.1.3 Scholarly debate (what to say, neutrally)
- **Not a closed list in scholarship.** Nedungatt: "even in this list the ecumenical status of certain second millennium councils like Pisa (1409) is disputed, and the addition of some general councils has been suggested" [verified, p. 658]. ##### 4.B.1.4 The disputed points, one by one

**Constantinople IV (869-70) vs. the council of 879-80.**
- Tanner (COD intro): "This council, designated as the eighth ecumenical council by western canonists, is not found in any canonical collections of the Byzantines; its acts and canons are completely ignored by them. Modern scholars have shown that it was included in the list of ecumenical councils only later, that is, after the eleventh century." Held at Hagia Sophia 5 Oct 869 - 28 Feb 870, legates of Hadrian II presiding; 10 sessions; Latin version (Anastasius) has 27 canons, the Greek only 14 [verified: Tanner intro https://www.papalencyclicals.net/councils/ecum08.htm; quote and 27/14 canons confirmed in ver. F5]. DH 43's header before DH 650 is reported to say the same: "recognized as ecumenical only by the Latin Church and not before the twelfth century" [unconfirmed: DH].
- The council of Nov 879 - early 880 (Photius restored; Roman legate Peter presiding; John VIII's letter annulling 869-70): Tanner quotes the letter and Ivo of Chartres and notes some scholars thought the text altered [verified: same Tanner intro]. Orthodox sources number 879-80 as the "Eighth" (or part of the "Eighth/Ninth") [memory for the general claim]; one Orthodox primary statement: the 1848 Encyclical of the Eastern Patriarchs, §5 item xi, says the Filioque "was subjected to anathema, as a novelty and augmentation of the Creed, by the eighth Ecumenical Council, congregated at Constantinople" [verified, https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/mod/1848orthodoxencyclical.asp, consistency pass 2026-10-15; same source as `orthodox.md` Tier 2 and §9.1, which also records that the Orthodox count of ecumenical councils stays at seven]. - Peri, "Postilla sul concilio ecumenico ottavo", Annuarium historiae conciliorum 1978, on the eighth council [snippet: https://katalog.dhi-paris.fr/vufind/Record/1644145057; not read].

**Lateran I, II (1123, 1139): "general" in the sources, ecumenicity doubted.** Tanner: of Lateran I, "It is reasonable, however, to doubt its ecumenicity... Moreover several other councils, similar to Lateran I, were convened in the 11th and 12th centuries but were not termed ecumenical. The ecumenicity of this council seems... to have been confirmed later by the tradition of the Roman church." Lateran II: "there is a doubt as to its ecumenicity for the same reasons that affect Lateran I." Lateran III: "We do not have the same reasons for doubting the ecumenical nature of this council as we have for Lateran I and II." [verified: Tanner intros ecum09, ecum10, ecum11; confirmed in ver. F5]. DH 43 is reported to say of Lateran I: "Its ecumenical character has been frequently questioned"; of Lateran II: "Its ecumenical character is disputed" [unconfirmed: DH]. Lateran V: Tanner: "there has been frequent dispute about whether the council was ecumenical" because few bishops, mostly Italian [verified: Tanner ecum18; ver. F5]. Vienne: acts mostly lost; decisions made in a consistory and committee rather than in the plenary (Tanner), political pressure from Philip IV (Tanner) [verified: Tanner ecum15].

**Constance (1414-18): which sessions, and Haec sancta.**
- Tanner: "With regard to the ecumenical nature of the sessions, there is dispute about those before the election of Martin V and also about the significance and force of the approval which he gave to the matters transacted by the council. The decrees notably those of sessions 3-5 and the decree Frequens (session 39), appear to proceed from the council's teaching. Objection has been made to them on the grounds of the primacy of the Roman pontiff." [verified: Tanner ecum16, "Introduction by Tanner in Decrees of the Ecumenical Councils" paragraph; ver. F5]. #### 4.B.2 One entry per council

How to read: "DH" = Denzinger-Hünermann 43rd ed. numbers (identical to Denzinger-Schönmetzer numbering up to DH 4000s [memory; ver. Part J found no contradiction]). DH numbers and everything attributed to DH headers below are `[unconfirmed: DH]` unless the entry says a Holy See text or Schatz confirms the number (ver. J1); "consistent" = a Holy See citation falls inside the range but its boundaries are unchecked (ver. J2). Dates, popes and ordinals: DH 43 headers (unconfirmed) unless another source is given. "Type": D = doctrinal/dogmatic (definition, creed, canons with anathema); R = disciplinary/reform; P = political/ad hoc (sentences, unions, crusade). "Mansi" volumes as cited in DH's apparatus (MaC = Mansi continued) or Tanner. Sub-numbering of canons is that of the edition named (it varies: Mansi/Percival vs COD vs Tanner).

Cross-cutting caveat on DH ranges: DH prints **only the doctrinally relevant extracts** of many councils (e.g. Lateran III has just one canon, DH 751; Lateran I three canons; Lyon I none). A DH range is therefore not a council's full text.

### 4.C T3 — Papal ex cathedra definitions

_Merged from subagent file `sub-papal.md` (2026-10-14). Its own legend applies: `[verified]`/`[snippet]`/`[memory]`; DH numbers are memory unless marked._


#### 4.C.1 The rule (Vatican I) and the fact that there is no list
- **Pastor aeternus ch. 4 (18 Jul 1870), DH 3074** — Latin, vatican.va [verified] https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-ix/la/documents/constitutio-dogmatica-pastor-aeternus-18-iulii-1870.html : "…sacro approbante Concilio, docemus et divinitus revelatum dogma esse definimus: Romanum Pontificem, cum ex Cathedra loquitur, id est, cum omnium Christianorum Pastoris et Doctoris munere fungens, pro suprema sua Apostolica auctoritate doctrinam de fide vel moribus ab universa Ecclesia tenendam definit, per assistentiam divinam, ipsi in beato Petro promissam, ea infallibilitate pollere, qua divinus Redemptor Ecclesiam suam in definienda doctrina de fide vel moribus instructam esse voluit; ideoque eiusmodi Romani Pontificis definitiones ex sese, non autem ex consensu Ecclesiae irreformabiles esse." (vatican.va also has /en/ and /it/ pages at the same path.) DH number: CDF 1998 Commentary fn 11 "Cf. DS 3074" [verified locally; ver. B1].
- **CIC 1983 can. 749 §3**: "No doctrine is understood as defined infallibly unless this is manifestly evident" [verified: vatican.va CIC Book III, EN and LA, read by the main agent]. This is why the defining intention must be explicit.
- **No official list exists.** The Holy See has never published a catalogue of ex cathedra acts [verified negatively: the CDF 1998 Commentary §11 gives examples "without any intention of completeness or exhaustiveness" and never uses "ex cathedra" for any specific papal document; no list found on vatican.va]. Klaus Schatz SJ (1985) concludes: "Die Theologie nach dem Ersten Vatikanum vermag nicht anzugeben, welche bisherigen päpstlichen Entscheidungen eigentlich »ex cathedra« sind" [verified, see 1.3].
- Catholic Encyclopedia 1910, "Infallibility" (P. Toner) [verified] https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07790a.htm : sets out the conditions (public capacity as pastor of all; faith or morals; evident intention "to define it in the technical sense"; intention to bind the whole Church, e.g. the *naufragium fidei* formula of Pius IX); "doctrinal decisions or instructions issued by the Roman congregations, even when approved by the pope in the ordinary way, have no claim to be considered infallible". Gives no list of definitions.

### 4.D T4 — Ordinary papal and curial magisterium (open-ended)

_Merged from subagent file `sub-papal.md` (2026-10-14)._

#### 4.D.0 Official texts on the weight of papal/curial documents


- **LG 25** (vatican.va, lg_en.txt) [verified]: religious submission of mind and will "must be shown in a special way to the authentic magisterium of the Roman Pontiff, even when he is not speaking ex cathedra … His mind and will in the matter may be known either from the character of the documents, from his frequent repetition of the same doctrine, or from his manner of speaking."
- **Humani generis 20** (Pius XII, 1950; humani_generis_en.txt) [verified]: "Nor must it be thought that what is expounded in Encyclical Letters does not of itself demand consent, since in writing such Letters the Popes do not exercise the supreme power of their Teaching Authority. For these matters are taught with the ordinary teaching authority … But if the Supreme Pontiffs in their official documents purposely pass judgment on a matter up to that time under dispute, it is obvious that that matter … cannot be any longer considered a question open to discussion among theologians."
- **Donum veritatis** (CDF, 24 May 1990; donum_veritatis_en.txt) [verified]:
  - §17: divine assistance also when the Pope exercises ordinary Magisterium "even should this not issue in an infallible definition or in a 'definitive' pronouncement"; "One must therefore take into account the proper character of every exercise of the Magisterium, considering the extent to which its authority is engaged."
  - §18: "the documents issued by this Congregation expressly approved by the Pope participate in the ordinary magisterium of the successor of Peter" (fn 18: CIC cc. 360–361; Regimini Ecclesiae Universae 1967; Pastor Bonus 1988).
  - §23: three assents — theological faith (solemn definition or ordinary universal Magisterium proposing as revealed); "firmly accepted and held" (definitive, connected truths); "religious submission of will and intellect" (non-definitive teaching).
  - §24: authoritativeness "becomes clear from the nature of the documents, the insistence with which a teaching is repeated, and the very way in which it is expressed"; prudential interventions "might not be free from all deficiencies".

#### 4.D.1 Document types and their weight


##### 4.D.1.1 What the Church itself says (official criteria)
- There is **no official canonical glossary of papal document genres** that assigns a fixed doctrinal grade to each [verified negatively: not in CIC, PE, or the 2025 Regulation; memory that none exists elsewhere]. The official criteria are functional, not by genre: LG 25 ("character of the documents, … frequent repetition … manner of speaking"), Donum veritatis 17, 23–24 ("nature of the documents, the insistence with which a teaching is repeated, and the very way in which it is expressed"), Humani generis 20 (encyclicals demand assent; a deliberate papal judgment on a disputed question closes it), CDF 1998 Commentary §§5–11 (three grades: revealed / definitive / authentic non-definitive) [all verified, §0].
- vatican.va's own classification of papal acts (Francis/Leo XIV pages) [verified https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en.html]: Apostolic Constitutions · Encyclicals · Apostolic Exhortations · Apostolic Letters · Motu Proprio · Bulls · Letters · Messages · Speeches · Homilies · Audiences · Angelus/Regina Caeli · Daily Meditations · Prayers · Travels.
- **Promulgation**: CIC can. 8 §1 [verified https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib1-cann7-22_en.html]: "Universal ecclesiastical laws are promulgated by publication in the official commentary, Acta Apostolicae Sedis, unless another manner of promulgation has been prescribed in particular cases." (AAS is the organ for laws; doctrinal acts are also printed there but can. 8 speaks of *laws*.) AAS volumes 1 (1909) onward on vatican.va as PDFs; whole-volume PDFs to 2002, issue-by-issue 2003–, indices to 2024 [verified https://www.vatican.va/archive/aas/index_en.htm]. 1865–1908 predecessor: Acta Sanctae Sedis [memory].

##### 4.D.1.4 Approval "in forma communi/generica" vs "in forma specifica"
- **Ordinary (generic) approval** = the formula "the Supreme Pontiff … approved … and ordered its publication": the act remains the dicastery's act; per Donum veritatis 18 such documents "expressly approved by the Pope participate in the ordinary magisterium of the successor of Peter" [verified]. CE 1910: congregational decisions "even when approved by the pope in the ordinary way, have no claim to be considered infallible" [verified].
- **Specific approval** makes the act the Pope's own. Rule now: **General Regulation of the Roman Curia (approved by Leo XIV 23 Nov 2025, published 24 Nov 2025, in force 1 Jan 2026, ad experimentum 5 years)** [verified, Vatican News https://www.vaticannews.va/en/pope/news/2025-11/vatican-publishes-the-new-regulations-of-the-roman-curia.html ; text https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/it/bollettino/pubblico/2025/11/24/0896/01618.html]:
  - art. 31 §4: "Affinché consti dell'approvazione in forma specifica si dovrà dire esplicitamente che il Romano Pontefice «in forma specifica approbavit»" [verified].
  - art. 31 §1: a curial institution must request it in writing with reasons and the final draft [verified].
  - art. 30 §2: curial bodies cannot issue laws or general decrees or derogate from papal law "senza la Sua specifica approvazione" [verified]; mirrors PE art. 30 [verified].
  - art. 37 §5: curial documents touching faith and morals must first go to the DDF (cf. PE arts 75, 179) [verified].
  - Recourse: "Non si da mai ricorso contro atti approvati dal Romano Pontefice in forma specifica (Cfr. cann. 1405 §2 e 333 §3 …)" [verified].
  - Predecessor: Regolamento Generale 1999 (approved by JPII 15 Apr 1999, in force 1 Jul 1999) [verified via Vatican News; ver. D4]; its art. 126 §4 had the same "in forma specifica approbavit" rule [memory, not re-read; unconfirmed: ver. D4].
- **None of the CDF/DDF doctrinal documents checked uses "in forma specifica"** (Mysterium Ecclesiae, Inter insigniores, eschatology Letter 1979, Dominus Iesus, Responses 2007, Placuit Deo, Fiducia supplicans, Gestis verbisque, Dignitas infinita, Mater populi fidelis, Una caro, A Journey of Hope) [verified, approval lines read]. Dominus Iesus's "certa scientia et apostolica sua auctoritate" ("with sure knowledge and by his apostolic authority") is the strongest formula among them [verified EN, ver. D3; the Latin wording not checked]; whether it equals forma specifica is a canonists' question [memory: debated].


#### 4.D.2 Membership rule for this open-ended tier (proposal)


**Rule (checkable).** A papal or curial text belongs to Doctrina's Catholic papal/curial tier if it meets **A and (B or C)**, and is not excluded by D:
- **A. Author and authority.** (i) An act of the Roman Pontiff in one of these forms: apostolic constitution, bull, encyclical, apostolic exhortation, apostolic letter (incl. motu proprio) — or (ii) a doctrinal document of the Holy Office / S.C. / CDF / DDF (declaration, instruction, letter, (doctrinal) note, notification, responsum ad dubium) whose closing formula states the Pope's approval (verified wording in 4.D.1.3) — or (iii) a pre-1971 decision of the Pontifical Biblical Commission approved by the Pope (historical layer, see the PBC status note below).
- **B. Indexed in Denzinger–Hünermann** (current reference edition: **DH 45th ed., Freiburg: Herder 2017**, ed. Hünermann with Hoping) [verified: Univ. Münster press release 26 Sep 2025, https://www.uni-muenster.de/Religion-und-Politik/en/aktuelles/2025/Denzinger-Lehramtstexte-Herausgeberschaft-Michael-Seewald.shtml (English page, confirmed in ver. C12): "current 45th edition, published by Herder"; the year 2017 and the co-editor Hoping are not in the release (unconfirmed: ver. C12)]. English: *Enchiridion symbolorum … Compendium of Creeds, Definitions and Declarations*, 43rd ed., ed. Hünermann, R. Fastiggi & A. Englund Nash, San Francisco: Ignatius 2012 [snippet]. Spanish: *El Magisterio de la Iglesia*, Barcelona: Herder 1999 (ISBN 84-254-2087-3) and a 2017 Herder edition (ISBN 9788425431210) [snippet]. Note: Michael Seewald (Münster) succeeded Hünermann in 2025; project CREDENZ (7 years) will produce a new edition cited "DS" (Denzinger–Seewald) [verified, same release] — so "DS" will become ambiguous with the older Denzinger–Schönmetzer; Doctrina should always write "DH" + edition.
- **C. Post-DH or not indexed, but doctrinal**: published in AAS (or on vatican.va where AAS is not yet out) and cited as a doctrinal authority by a later act of the Magisterium (CCC, a CDF/DDF document, or a later papal act) on the same locus. This lets in 2017–2026 texts (e.g. Mater populi fidelis, Una caro, Dilexit nos) and pre-2017 texts DH omits.
- **D. Exclusions**: speeches, homilies, general audiences, Angelus, messages, interviews, in-flight press conferences, daily meditations, prefaces to books — *unless* the Magisterium later cites them as doctrine (then they qualify under C, quoted as such); purely disciplinary/administrative acts (appointments, statutes, procedural norms, dispensations, decrees of excommunication on individuals or groups — e.g. DDF decree of 2 Jul 2026 on the FSSPX [verified listing]); documents of other dicasteries (DPCU, Liturgy, etc.) unless co-signed by the CDF/DDF or approved by the Pope as doctrinal (the JDDJ is an exception to be labelled: an ecumenical agreement signed by the PCPCU and LWF, not a DDF act); International Theological Commission and post-1971 Pontifical Biblical Commission documents (consultative; Sedula cura 1971 made the PBC "no longer an organ of the Magisterium" — Ratzinger 2003 [verified]) — allowed only as *commentary*, flagged "non-magisterial"; private writings of popes (e.g. Benedict XVI's *Jesus of Nazareth*, which he said was not a magisterial act [memory]).
- **Display rule**: each item shows author, type, date, AAS/ASS ref, DH number (if any), and the *self-stated weight* where the text states it (e.g. "definimus", "to be held definitively", approval formula). Doctrina never assigns an infallibility label of its own; ex cathedra is shown only for MD and ID, with the note that there is no official list (4.C).

### 5.1 The canon: Trent Session IV (8 April 1546), with antecedents

**Trent, Session IV, "Decree concerning the canonical Scriptures" (Decretum de canonicis Scripturis), 8 April 1546** [verified]
- English: Waterworth, *The Canons and Decrees of the Sacred and Oecumenical Council of Trent* (London 1848), pp. 17-19 in the scan (Cornell copy, archive.org `cu31924029369760`; "no known copyright restrictions in the United States", per the scan's cover note). - Latin: the same decree is printed as the Appendix of the Nova Vulgata on vatican.va: https://www.vatican.va/archive/bible/nova_vulgata/documents/nova-vulgata_appendix_decretum-can-script_lt.html [verified, fetched 2026-10-14]. (Useful: the Holy See itself publishes the Latin decree beside the Nova Vulgata.)
- Structure [verified]: (a) the Synod "receives and venerates with an equal affection of piety and reverence all the books both of the Old and of the New Testament, seeing that one God is the author of both, as also the said traditions" (written books + unwritten traditions); (b) list of books; (c) anathema; (d) second decree "concerning the edition and the use of the sacred books" (Vulgate "authentic"; interpretation against the sense of the Church / unanimous consent of the Fathers; licensing of printing).
- The list as printed (Latin, Nova Vulgata appendix, verbatim) [verified]:
  OT: "Quinque Moysis, id est Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numeri, Deuteronomium; Iosue, Iudicum, Ruth, quattuor Regum, duo Paralipomenon, Esdrae primus et secundus, qui dicitur Nehemias, Tobias, Iudith, Esther, Iob, Psalterium Davidicum centum quinquaginta psalmorum, Parabolae, Ecclesiastes, Canticum Canticorum, Sapientia, Ecclesiasticus, Isaias, Ieremias cum Baruch, Ezechiel, Daniel, duodecim prophetae minores [Osea ... Malachias]; duo Maccabaeorum, primus et secundus."
  NT: "Quattuor Evangelia ...; Actus Apostolorum a Luca Evangelista conscripti; quattuordecim epistulae Pauli Apostoli [... ad Hebraeos]; Petri Apostoli duae; Ioannis Apostoli tres; Iacobi Apostoli una; Iudae Apostoli una et Apocalypsis Ioannis Apostoli."
- Counting: the decree itself gives no total. Counting by its own entries gives 45 OT + 27 NT = 72 entries/titles if Lamentations is inside "Ieremias" and Baruch (with the Letter of Jeremiah as Baruch 6) is counted separately; the conventional "46 + 27 = 73" counts Lamentations separately [counting by me from the verified list; the figure 73 is standard usage, not in the decree]. Do NOT print "73" as a quotation from Trent. CCC 120 [verified, https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_one/chapter_two/article_3/iv_the_canon_of_scripture.html, fetched 2026-10-14]: "It was by the apostolic Tradition that the Church discerned which writings are to be included in the list of the sacred books. This complete list is called the canon of Scripture. It includes 46 books for the Old Testament (45 if we count Jeremiah and Lamentations as one) and 27 for the New." Use the CCC wording for the count, not an invented Trent quotation.
- Not in the Trent list (relevant to a neutral comparison) [verified by reading the list]: 1 Esdras (3 Ezra Vulgate numbering), 2 Esdras (4 Ezra), Prayer of Manasseh, 3-4 Maccabees, Ps 151. - Note the Ezra naming: Trent's "Esdrae primus et secundus, qui dicitur Nehemias" = Ezra + Nehemiah (Vulgate/Catholic usage; "1 Esdras" in Orthodox/LXX usage is a different book). [verified from Latin text; usage point is general knowledge]

**The anathema (English, Waterworth)** [verified, waterworth.txt]:
> "But if anyone receive not, as sacred and canonical, the said books entire with all their parts, as they have been used to be read in the Catholic Church, and as they are contained in the old Latin Vulgate edition; and knowingly and deliberately contemn the traditions aforesaid; let him be anathema."
Latin [verified]: "Si quis autem libros ipsos integros cum omnibus suis partibus, prout in Ecclesia catholica legi consueverunt, et in veteri vulgata latina editione habentur, pro sacris et canonicis non susceperit, et traditiones praedictas sciens et prudens contempserit, anathema sit."
Editorial caution: the anathema has two limbs ("and knowingly and deliberately contemn the traditions aforesaid"); quoting only the first half misrepresents it. Also "prout ... legi consueverunt ... et in veteri vulgata ... habentur" is the clause that ties the canon to liturgical use and the Vulgate (the "all their parts" = e.g. Mark 16:9-20, Dan 13-14, Esther additions) [the gloss on "parts" is the standard reading; not stated in the decree text; mark as interpretation].

### 5.2 The Vulgate, the Nova Vulgata, and approval of vernacular editions

**Trent: the Vulgate "authentica"** [verified]. Second decree of Session IV (Waterworth, `waterworth.txt` ~12600; Latin on vatican.va https://www.vatican.va/archive/bible/nova_vulgata/documents/nova-vulgata_appendix_decr-editione-usu_lt.html): the Synod "ordains and declares, that the said old and Vulgate edition, which, by the lengthened usage of so many ages, has been approved of in the Church, be, in public lectures, disputations, sermons, and expositions, held as authentic; and that no one is to dare or presume to reject it under any pretext whatever." Latin: "pro authentica habeatur". It also ordered that the Vulgate "be printed in the most correct manner possible" (hence the later revision).
- What "authentic" means, per the Church itself: Pius XII, *Divino afflante Spiritu* (30 Sept 1943) n. 21 [verified, https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_30091943_divino-afflante-spiritu.html]: the authenticity "is not specified primarily as critical, but rather as juridical"; it applies "only to the Latin Church and to the public use"; "nor does it ... in any way diminish the authority and value of the original texts." Use this to avoid the common misreading that Trent ranked the Vulgate above Hebrew/Greek.
- Dei Verbum n. 22 [verified, `dv_en.txt` line 57]: the Church accepted the Septuagint "from the very beginning", "has always given a place of honor to other Eastern translations and Latin ones especially the Latin translation known as the vulgate", and sees to "suitable and correct translations ... especially from the original texts of the sacred books"; translations made with separated brethren allowed with Church approval.

**Sixtine / Sixto-Clementine Vulgate** 
- The Holy See's own account (Latin "Praefatio ad Lectorem trium editionum Clementinarum", printed in the Nova Vulgata Appendix on vatican.va https://www.vatican.va/archive/bible/nova_vulgata/documents/nova-vulgata_appendix_praefatio-lectorem_lt.html) [verified, fetched 2026-10-14]: work begun under Pius IV and Pius V; revived by Sixtus V, printed, then ordered recast because of misprints; Sixtus died; Gregory XIV continued; Urban VII and Innocent IX died after very short pontificates; finished "sub initium Pontificatus Clementis VIII". Same preface says the edition was issued from the Vatican Press; that nothing non-canonical was placed in it; and that "Liber tertius et quartus Esdrae" and the "Oratio Manassae" were set "extra Canonicae Scripturae seriem" because Trent did not count them among the canonical books [verified, Latin]. It also says the editors did not intend a new translation but to restore the old Vulgate (and deliberately left some readings unchanged).
- Dates: Sixtine edition 1590; Clementine (Sixto-Clementine) printed 9 Nov 1592, with later 1593 and 1598 editions [snippet: Wikipedia "Sixtine Vulgate", opened in ver. H7: 1590, "replaced in 1592 by the Sixto-Clementine Vulgate", 1593 and 1598 variant editions in a bibliographic note; the day "9 Nov" not found there, unconfirmed: ver. G5/H7]. G5]. Bull name for the 1592 edition ("Cum sacrorum bibliorum" per my recollection) [memory, unverified; not found in ver. H7]; Bellarmine wrote the preface [snippet only].

### 6.1 The principle, and how the Church qualifies it

1. **The maxim.** "ut legem credendi lex statuat supplicandi" (so that the rule of prayer establish the rule of belief), attributed to Prosper of Aquitaine, 5th century; the passage is in the eighth chapter of the *Indiculus* / *Capitula Caelestini* (De gratia Dei), Patrologia Latina 51:209-210, and Pope Celestine I's letter to the Gallican bishops (ep. 21, PL 50:535) is said to quote it [snippet: Wikipedia "Lex orandi, lex credendi" via fetch, footnotes 1-2; secondary]. Denzinger number: **DS/DH 246** [memory; the page fetched does not give it, and the CCC footnote text was not available to me; verify in DH; unconfirmed: ver. I1, J3 — no legitimate source found]. (The Wikipedia footnotes, PL 51:209-210 and the Celestine I epistle 21 attribution, re-confirmed against Wikipedia only in ver. I1.) Authorship and the maxim's original meaning are debated by scholars (Wikipedia bibliography lists Paul de Clerck, 1994, "original sense and historical avatars of an equivocal adage") [snippet]. Editorial: describe it as "attributed to Prosper of Aquitaine", never "Celestine I taught".
2. **CCC 1124-1126** [verified, https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_two/section_one/chapter_one/article_2/iii_the_sacraments_of_faith.html, fetched 2026-10-14]: 1124: "The Church's faith precedes the faith of the believer who is invited to adhere to it. When the Church celebrates the sacraments, she confesses the faith received from the apostles - whence the ancient saying: lex orandi, lex credendi (or: legem credendi lex statuat supplicandi according to Prosper of Aquitaine [5th cent.]). The law of prayer is the law of faith: the Church believes as she prays. Liturgy is a constitutive element of the holy and living Tradition." 1125: "no sacramental rite may be modified or manipulated at the will of the minister or the community. Even the supreme authority in the Church may not change the liturgy arbitrarily, but only in the obedience of faith and with religious respect for the mystery of the liturgy." 1126: "the lex orandi is one of the essential criteria of the dialogue that seeks to restore the unity of Christians." (footnote numbers 45-47 are not on the vatican.va content page; the DS reference could not be read.)
3. **Pius XII, *Mediator Dei* (20 Nov 1947), nn. 46-48** [verified, https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_20111947_mediator-dei.html, fetched; `sub/lit/md.txt` lines 92-94]. n. 46 rectifies those who made the liturgy "a kind of proving ground for the truths to be held of faith" ("Lex orandi, lex credendi"). n. 47: the liturgy "has the Catholic faith for its content, inasmuch as it bears public witness to the faith of the Church". n. 48: "'Legem credendi lex statuat supplicandi' - let the rule for prayer determine the rule of belief ... The sacred liturgy, consequently, does not decide or determine independently and of itself what is of Catholic faith. More properly, since the liturgy is also a profession of eternal truths, and subject, as such, to the supreme teaching authority of the Church, it can supply proofs and testimony, quite clearly, of no little value, towards the determination of a particular point of Christian doctrine. But if one desires to differentiate and describe the relationship between faith and the sacred liturgy in absolute and general terms, it is perfectly correct to say, 'Lex credendi legem statuat supplicandi' - let the rule of belief determine the rule of prayer." n. 48 also cites Pius IX's use of liturgy in defining the Immaculate Conception. n. 45: the liturgy "must as a consequence conform to the decrees respecting Catholic faith". Footnote to the maxim in the encyclical: "De gratia Dei 'Indiculus'" [verified, md.txt line 303].
4. **Sacrosanctum Concilium (4 Dec 1963)** [verified, `sc_en.txt`]: n. 7 (Christ present "in the sacrifice of the Mass ... in the sacraments ... in His word ... when the Church prays and sings"; the liturgy "an exercise of the priestly office of Jesus Christ"; "no other action of the Church can equal its efficacy by the same title and to the same degree"); n. 10 ("the liturgy is the summit toward which the activity of the Church is directed; at the same time it is the font from which all her power flows"). SC 7 and 10 do not state the lex orandi formula; they ground the liturgy's weight. SC 21-23 (liturgy has immutable and changeable elements; "no other person, even if he be a priest, may add, remove, or change anything in the liturgy on his own authority" = SC 22 §3; "no innovations unless the good of the Church genuinely and certainly requires them" = SC 23) [verified lines 54-61]. SC 4: all lawfully acknowledged rites "of equal right and dignity" [verified line 16].
5. Presentation note for the site: the Church itself uses the maxim in two directions (Mediator Dei 48): prayer as witness to belief, and belief as the norm for prayer; liturgical texts are cited as witnesses and are not themselves a definition.

### 6.3 The 1962 books and their present status
- Benedict XVI, *Summorum Pontificum* (7 July 2007), Art. 1 [verified, https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/motu_proprio/documents/hf_ben-xvi_motu-proprio_20070707_summorum-pontificum.html, fetched]: "The Roman Missal promulgated by Pope Paul VI is the ordinary expression of the lex orandi (rule of prayer) of the Catholic Church of the Latin rite. The Roman Missal promulgated by Saint Pius V and revised by Blessed John XXIII is nonetheless to be considered an extraordinary expression of the same lex orandi of the Church and duly honoured for its venerable and ancient usage. These two expressions of the Church's lex orandi will in no way lead to a division in the Church's lex credendi (rule of faith); for they are two usages of the one Roman rite." The text calls the 1962 typical edition one "promulgated by Blessed John XXIII in 1962 and never abrogated" (Art. 1 text). Art. 9 §1 allowed the older ritual for baptism, marriage, penance and anointing; Ecclesia Dei Commission (Art. 11-12). Accompanying letter of 7 July 2007 to the bishops [verified, https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/letters/2007/documents/hf_ben-xvi_let_20070707_lettera-vescovi.html] says the 1962 Missal "was never juridically abrogated" and speaks of "Forma ordinaria" and "Forma extraordinaria".
- Francis, *Traditionis custodes* (16 July 2021) [verified, https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20210716-motu-proprio-traditionis-custodes.html]: "Art. 1. The liturgical books promulgated by Saint Paul VI and Saint John Paul II, in conformity with the decrees of Vatican Council II, are the unique expression of the lex orandi of the Roman Rite." "Art. 2. ... it is [the diocesan bishop's] exclusive competence to authorize the use of the 1962 Roman Missal in his diocese, according to the guidelines of the Apostolic See." "Art. 4. Priests ordained after the publication of the present Motu Proprio, who wish to celebrate using the Missale Romanum of 1962, should submit a formal request to the diocesan Bishop who shall consult the Apostolic See." "Art. 8. Previous norms, instructions, permissions, and customs that do not conform to the provisions of the present Motu Proprio are abrogated." Dated "Given at Rome, at Saint John Lateran, on 16 July 2021". The accompanying letter to bishops (https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/letters/2021/documents/20210716-lettera-vescovi-liturgia.html) fetched, not analysed [verified fetch].

Later Roman responses/rescripts (e.g., Dec 2021 Responsa ad dubia, Feb 2023 rescript) exist [memory, not read; the Dicastery site has a "responsa ad dubia" section: https://www.cultodivino.va/en/responsa-ad-dubia.html (link seen, not read)].

I6 — the quoted passages were not re-read; English text as reported: §8 "The Theological Grades of Certainty", pp. 9-10 of the English edition as paginated in the scan ("§ 8.   1. Immediately revealed truths (fides divina; with the Church's guarantee, fides catholica); if defined by Pope or General Council: "de fide definita".
  2. Catholic truths or Church doctrines finally decided by the infallible magisterium: "fides ecclesiastica".
  3. "A Teaching proximate to Faith (sententia fidei proxima) is a doctrine, which is regarded by theologians generally as a truth of Revelation, but which has not yet been finally promulgated as such by the Church."
  4. "A Teaching pertaining to the Faith, i.e., theologically certain (sententia ad fidem pertinens, i.e., theologice certa) is a doctrine, on which the Teaching Authority of the Church has not yet finally pronounced, but whose truth is guaranteed by its intrinsic connection with the doctrine of revelation (theological conclusions)."
  5. "Common Teaching (sententia communis) is doctrine, which in itself belongs to the field of the free opinions, but which is accepted by theologians generally."
  6. Opinions of lesser certainty: probable, more probable, well-founded (sententia probabilis, probabilior, bene fundata); "pious opinions" (sententia pia); least: "tolerated opinion (opinio tolerata)".

I6]: "not all the assertions of the Teaching Authority of the Church on questions of Faith and morals are infallible"; "Only those are infallible which emanate from General Councils ... and the Papal Decisions Ex Cathedra"; "The ordinary and usual form of the Papal teaching activity is not infallible"; decisions of the Roman Congregations (Holy Office, Bible Commission) "are not infallible. Nevertheless normally they are to be accepted with an inner assent". The grades are a scholastic tool still found in manuals; the magisterial categories today are those of CCC/Ad tuendam fidem/Professio fidei (see catholic-modern.md, local `ad_tuendam_fidem_en.txt`, `doctrinal_commentary_en.txt`).

## 9. Disputed points

Each item: what is disputed, who says what (cited), and what it means for the site. The site reports the dispute; it never settles it.

1. **Vatican II's weight and interpretation.** Official: no new infallible definitions; "supreme ordinary magisterium" to be received "according to the mind of the sacred Council" (Nota of 16 Nov 1964; Paul VI, 12 Jan 1966) `[verified]`. Interpretation contested: Benedict XVI, address to the Roman Curia, 22 Dec 2005, contrasts "a hermeneutic of discontinuity and rupture" with "the 'hermeneutic of reform', of renewal in the continuity of the one subject-Church" (https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/speeches/2005/december/documents/hf_ben_xvi_spe_20051222_roman-curia.html) `[verified; ver. I10]`. Site consequence: quote VII texts with grade 3 by default, grade 1 only where they restate an earlier definition (and cite that definition).
2. **Weight of individual CCC paragraphs.** *Fidei depositum* calls the CCC "a sure norm for teaching the faith" `[verified]`; Ratzinger's *Introduction* says each doctrine keeps "no other weight than that which they already possess" `[snippet; unconfirmed: ver. C6]`. The 2018 rewrite of n. 2267 (death penalty; rescript of 1 Aug 2018 `[verified: ver. A10]`) is the standard test case `[snippet]`. Site consequence: never cite "CCC n." as a grade by itself; trace it to its source (the CCC's own footnotes give it).
3. **Scope of the "ordinary and universal magisterium" and of paragraph 2 (definitive) truths.** The CDF placed *Ordinatio sacerdotalis* (1995 *Responsum*) and euthanasia, prostitution, fornication, canonizations, *Apostolicae curae* in the definitive category (1998 commentary §11) `[verified]`; CIC 749 §3 requires that infallibility be "manifestly evident" `[verified]`. Some theologians questioned whether that condition is met in the newer cases (e.g. Francis A. Sullivan's writings on the *Responsum*) `[memory; unconfirmed: ver. I10, not fetched]`. Site consequence: when the Church itself has classified a teaching (as here), use that classification and cite it; mention the scholarly dispute only in a "debate" note.
4. **Status of a papal text can be declared after the fact.** Francis' letter of 5 Sep 2016 to the Buenos Aires bishops on *Amoris laetitia* ch. 8 was ordered published in AAS "as authentic magisterium" by a rescript of the Secretary of State (5 Jun 2017; AAS 108 (2016) 1071–1074) `[snippet: NCRegister, CWR 5 Dec 2017, vaticannews.va/de; unconfirmed: ver. I10, not fetched]`; commentators disagreed on what that adds `[snippet]`. Site consequence: the membership rule (4.D) must look at AAS publication and declared status, not only at document type.
5. **Denzinger labels.** "DS" means Denzinger–Schönmetzer in older literature and in CCC/CDF footnotes, and will mean Denzinger–Seewald in the next edition (Münster 2025) `[verified]`. Site consequence: always print the edition with the number.
6. **The number 21 is a received convention, not a defined list.** "This is not an official list or canon fixed by any ecumenical council or papal definition or decree" (G. Nedungatt, *Theological Studies* 71 (2010) 657–658) `[verified: ver. F1; the page numbers are unconfirmed there]`; built from Bellarmine's 18, the Roman edition of 1608–12 (19), then Vatican I and II; John XXIII's opening address of Vatican II counts "viginti Oecumenica" before it and calls it "primum et vicesimum" `[verified; ver. F2]`. Paul VI (letter of 5 Oct 1974 to Card. Willebrands; vatican.va indexes it under 19 Oct) calls Lyon II "sextum … inter Generales Synodos in Occidentali orbe celebratas" `[verified; ver. F3]`; scholars read this as a distinction between ecumenical (first millennium) and Western general councils; the critical edition COGD adopts that distinction and adds Pisa 1409 and Pavia–Siena among "general councils" `[verified: Brepols]`; *L'Osservatore Romano* objected (3 Jun 2007) `[cited by Nedungatt, not read]`. Details and per-council disputes (Constantinople IV vs the council of 879–80; Lateran I–II; Constance and *Haec sancta*; Basel after session 25): 4.B.1.3–4.B.1.4. Site consequence: present the 21 as the Catholic count, say once that the count is traditional, and never call a medieval Western council "ecumenical" in the Orthodox column.
7. **Which papal acts are ex cathedra.** No official list (4.C.1). Only *Ineffabilis Deus* and *Munificentissimus Deus* have near-universal agreement; the 1927 Dublanchy list of 12 shrank over the 20th century (Schatz 1985) `[verified: ver. B5, I10]`; Schatz himself adds that since 1870 only the 1950 definition clearly meets the Vatican I criteria, the 1854 act predating them (ver.
