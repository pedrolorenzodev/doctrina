# English for the whole site: measured scope and estimate by block

Written 2026-11-05 (agent, coordinator session). **Purpose: a record to compare against the real spend when the blocks run** (Pedro: «guardaremos la estimación y luego compararemos cuánto es el gasto real»). Everything here is a measurement of the repo or an estimate; the estimate is marked as such. Weekly points = the integer counter of `usage_check.py` (one point = 1 % of the week).

## 1. What is already English (no work)
- UI strings: `src/content/ui-text/en.ts` (2,318 words against 2,472 in Spanish; parity maintained).
- History: `data/site/history.json` is bilingual (`{es, en}` pairs), 296 English history pages exist. Bible: English editions (BSB, KJV, WEB, DRA) exist; the 1,326 English Bible pages exist.
- The apologists' quotes: every quote stores its English `original` (111,867 words). **The English page shows the original; nothing to translate.**
- Word data: English glosses, Greek senses and Hebrew BDB definitions are English already.
- Citations: 247 of 854 registry excerpts already have an `en`.
- The architecture is ready: `pagesByLocale: { es, en: {} }` in `get-tradition-page.ts`, the same in `get-doctrine-page.ts`; the page objects are plain data of one type. English pages are a second record of the same shape.

## 3. Engineering (not translation)
1. **Translation pipeline that keeps structure:** extract the translatable strings of a page object with their paths, translate in batches, re-inject into a copy, write `*.en.ts`. Must protect inline tokens (`{v:Matt.26.26,…}` verse markers, citation ids, `«»` to curly quotes, markup) and check the result (same keys, same token counts, no Spanish left).
2. English loaders and validation: register the English page sets, validate them (`validateContent` today runs on Spanish only), English glossary, passages (apologists use `original`), objections, doctrine pages, search index, switcher (`pageExistsInLocale` from the QA fixes turns on by itself when a page exists), site tree names.
3. English verse texts for the `{v:…}` tokens (from the English Bible editions, programmatic).
4. English templates for the Baptist passages script.
5. Chat scripts and simplifier mocks in English; the drawer's answers and chips.
6. Tests: English routes, the inert-switcher specs that assert English 404s change, a Spanish-leak check on English pages (the QA sweep's `tools/qa/english-gaps.mjs` is the starting tool), a term-consistency pass (justification, transubstantiation, real presence …) against one glossary.

## 5. Risks that would move the number
- Doctrinal precision: a cheap model mistranslating a technical term costs a correction pass (the high end of block 7). Mitigation: one glossary of fixed English terms given to every batch.
- Official English texts: copyright (Catechism of the Catholic Church, BF&M): only short quotes with a link, as the Spanish ones are (the licence field is kept); if the fetch fails for an edition, the AI translation of the Spanish with the label is the fallback.
- The model may decline to translate full copyrighted texts (it did once, in Pasajes round 1); the apologists' quotes are not in this scope (English exists).
- Inline tokens and markup in the Spanish strings: a broken token breaks a page at build time (the validator catches it).
- Counter: one integer shared by every running session; measure a block when it runs alone.
