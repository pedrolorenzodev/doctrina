## sophiascriptura.com (the one that matters)

Read: home, `/about/`, `/data/`, `/fathers/`, `/glossary/`, `/llms.txt`, the work page and Markdown of `ign-romans`, the map page, the catalog and a Scripture index; screenshots with Playwright. It is **a free library of early Christian writings in Greek and Latin with English translations, a map of the early writers and a glossary, «produced by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic»** (its about page).

**What it holds (verified in its files, not from its text).** `/data/idx/catalog.json`: **678 works, 81,577 passages, 127 authors**; 42,665 Latin and 38,912 Greek passages; every work has title, author, date range (`from`/`to`), `city`, `region`, genre and an attribution flag (529 authentic, 83 disputed, 45 pseudonymous, 21 spurious); 627 works have a city. Each passage has a stable URL with an anchor, the edition and page («Lake, LCL 24:226»), Greek or Latin, a Claude translation stamped with the model and date («Claude (claude-opus-5-5), 2026-09-26»), neutral-looking translation notes and an SBL citation. Per work: Markdown, JSON, BibTeX and RIS. `/data/idx/scripture/<USFM>.json` (about 1.5 MB for John): one row per Father passage that quotes, alludes to or echoes a verse, with `t` (quotation / allusion / echo) and `c` (confidence high / medium / low). The coverage page lists known gaps (OCR misreadings, fragments, parts of Origen, Hippolytus) openly.

**Licence, read as asked.** About page, quoted: «The site's own content, including the translations, notes, and data, is in the public domain» (linked to CC0 1.0); «You may copy, change, and use it for any purpose, without asking permission»; the original-language texts «keep the license of their edition, shown on each work page». The work page shows the Greek of the Apostolic Fathers as Lake's 1912 text, **digitized by the First Thousand Years of Greek project (Open Greek and Latin), CC BY-SA 4.0**. So: the site's own layer is CC0 by its own statement; the Greek and Latin it rests on is not uniformly CC0. I did not find a separate terms-of-use page and I have not seen a bulk-download statement beyond the static URLs in `llms.txt`.

**What is useful, in order.**
1. **The map is the closest thing to what Pedro described, and it already exists** ([`/fathers/map/`](https://sophiascriptura.com/fathers/map/), screenshots in `docs/research/history-map-references/`): «Each circle is a writer, placed where he did his main or longest work, up to the Council of Chalcedon (AD 451). Diamonds are anonymous works, placed where they were probably written; squares are church councils.» A dark land map, filters by **year range («Active between» with two handles and «Play through time»)**, century, language, group and tradition; a table of every marker below the map; the coastlines are Natural Earth (public domain). This corrects what I wrote in `history-map-references.md` («no finished map exists»): my searches missed it.
2. **Data for the map.** The catalog's place, date range and attribution per work answer the gap I flagged («the place and date of each Father's main work»): a **candidate** dataset to verify against Codex's `docs/research/neutral/fathers-verification.md`, never to publish as it comes (it is AI-assembled; the site itself marks places «probable» or «uncertain», e.g. Ignatius' letter to the Romans «Smyrna (probable)», dates «ca. 110 (range ca. 105–135)»).
3. **Scripture → Fathers index** as candidates for the verse panel's «Lo que escribieron los Padres» and for each work's «Dónde aparece»: it tells where to look; every row would still need our own verification against our edition, and `t` and `c` are the site's judgement, not facts.
4. **Design to look at**: dark page, serif headings, a work page with a facts table («About this work»: author, attribution, date, written at, addressed to, text from, critical editions), a per-work «Read the whole work on one page». Close to our 09 and reader.
5. **A competitor and an analog.** Same subject, free, public domain, AI-made, much larger on Fathers (678 works against our 10 normalized ones); it has no tradition-by-tradition dossiers. Worth adding to `docs/research/analogs.md`.

**What does not fit, or needs a decision.**
- **«Standing»: the map colours each writer by the tradition that «claims» him** (venerated as a saint by Catholics and Orthodox, commemorated by Lutherans or Anglicans), with split circles for two traditions. That is exactly what the trust invariant forbids («Church Fathers are never assigned to a modern tradition»); it must stay out of any Doctrina map, whatever else is taken.
- **The translations are AI translations in CC0 over a CC BY-SA Greek text.** Doctrina's policy is that its translations come from the public-domain print and never from a BY-SA file, and BY-SA files are only witnesses (DECISIONS 2026-10-09). Whether a CC0 dedication holds for a translation made from a BY-SA digitization is a legal question I did not settle. The safe reading: use their translations at most as a **witness** to compare, not as text.
- **Its editions are single printed texts without apparatus**, as it says itself; our «no citation without an edition» rule is met only where the page names the edition and page (it does).
- Whether to contact the author: not needed for CC0 data; not recommended for the AI-made translations.
