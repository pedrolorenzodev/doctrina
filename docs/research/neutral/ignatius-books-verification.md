## Richard I. Pervo, *The Making of Paul* (2010), 134–135

- **Primary pages:** `[not found]`. Google Books exposes the book's metadata and table of contents but labels the other edition “No preview available”; its SearchWithinVolume endpoint reports the volume is not searchable. No legal full-text scan, publisher preview containing pp. 134–135, or open library copy was found in the searches performed on 2026-10-22. [Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books?id=RbGDwsknbdsC).
- **Exact wording available only through a secondary quotation:** “A date of c. 130–140 is the preferable date for Ignatius” (reported as p. 135) `[snippet]`. Roger Parvus reproduces that sentence while discussing the composition date of the Ignatian letters, but this does not substitute for seeing Pervo's page. [Parvus, 2011](https://vridar.org/2011/07/06/the-letters-supposedly-written-by-ignatius-of-antioch/).
- **What is dated:** `[not found]` in the primary work. The wording “for Ignatius” does not itself specify death, letters, or collection. Parvus's surrounding use treats it as a date for the letters, while later Wikipedia pages rephrase it as Ignatius' death; neither conversion can be attributed to Pervo until the page is opened.
- **Result:** do not display “death, 135–140” as book-verified. The most that can be retained is a hidden research note that a secondary source quotes Pervo's p. 135 as preferring c. 130–140 “for Ignatius”.

## Markus Vinzent, *Christ's Torah* (English edition)

- **Edition and print year:** the copyright leaf says “First published in English 2024”; the Library of Congress block describes the print edition as Routledge, 2024 and lists hardback ISBN 978-1-032-45702-4 and paperback ISBN 978-1-032-45703-1. The e-book DOI is 10.4324/9781003378303. `[verified]` [Legal publisher preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781003830986_A47339558/preview-9781003830986_A47339558.pdf).
- **Pages inspected:** printed pp. 28–31 were extracted and also rendered to images to confirm the printed pagination, headings, paragraph boundaries, and footnote markers. `[verified]`
- **Three-letter collection:** p. 31 places the probably pseudonymous production of this collection “in the years after 150 CE”. The dated object is explicitly the three-letter collection, not Ignatius' death. `[verified]`
- **Seven-letter collection, broad bound:** p. 30 reasons from the 177 letter of the churches of Vienne and Lyon and Theophilus of Antioch (c. 183), then says the collection seems to have originated “around 170–177 CE at the latest”, probably near Smyrna. The dated object is the origin of the seven-letter collection. `[verified]`
- **Seven-letter collection, lower bound:** the continuation on p. 31 says the earlier three-letter collection was expanded into seven letters “in the years after 170 CE”. This is a lower-bound formulation, not by itself a closed 170–177 interval. `[verified]`
- **Result:** the accurate compact wording is: three-letter collection after 150; expansion to seven letters after 170, with c. 170–177 as Vinzent's latest-origin window. These are collection/redaction dates, not a date for Ignatius' death.

## Robert Joly, *Le dossier d'Ignace d'Antioche* (1979)

- **Edition:** Brussels, Éditions de l'Université de Bruxelles, 1979, Publications de la Faculté de philosophie et lettres 69, 144 pages, ISBN 2-8004-0688-7. `[verified]` [WorldCat](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/Le-dossier-d%27Ignace-d%27Antioche/oclc/5900308); [Mollat catalogue](https://www.mollat.com/livres/709948/robert-joly-le-dossier-d-ignace-d-antioche).
- **Primary book:** `[not found]`. No legal scan or page preview of p. 115 or the back cover was located in ULB, BnF/Gallica, Internet Archive, Google Books, or Open Library discovery. Catalogue metadata verifies the edition but not the conclusion.
- **Date discrepancy in secondary witnesses:** a contemporary scholarly review says Joly considered the seven middle-recension letters written after Polycarp's martyrdom and treated 161 or shortly after as reasonable; later summaries give c. 165 and point to p. 115 and the back cover. `[snippet]` [Hadot review, 1982](https://www.persee.fr/doc/assr_0335-5985_1982_num_53_2_2252_t1_0308_0000_2); [later locator trail](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignace_d%27Antioche).
- **Authenticity position:** contemporary reviews agree that Joly rejects the seven letters as Ignatius' authentic work, assigns the corpus to an unknown author of Smyrna, treats Polycarp *Philippians* 13 as an interpolation used to launch the corpus, and also questions Ignatius' historicity. `[snippet]` [Bodenmann review, 1982](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rhpr_0035-2403_1982_num_62_3_5698_t1_0293_0000_1); [Hadot review, 1982](https://www.persee.fr/doc/assr_0335-5985_1982_num_53_2_2252_t1_0308_0000_2).
- **What is dated:** the seven-letter middle-recension corpus, not the death of a historical Ignatius. The proposed location is Smyrna. `[snippet]`
- **Result:** Joly's pseudepigraphic/Smyrna position is strongly corroborated by independent contemporary reviews, but the exact year must remain `[not found]` at book level. Do not silently harmonize “161 or shortly after” into c. 165 until p. 115 is opened.

## J. B. Lightfoot, *The Apostolic Fathers*, Part II, vol. 2 (second edition, 1889), 448–471

- **Edition and pages:** the Internet Archive copy is the public-domain second edition, London, Macmillan, 1889. Printed pp. 448–471 were read in the scan; printed pp. 448 and 471 were also rendered and visually checked against the OCR. `[verified: OCR]` [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/p2apostolicfathe02clemuoft/page/448/mode/1up).
- **What the ninth year dates:** p. 448 says the Antiochene and Roman martyrdom Acts agree on Trajan's ninth year and calls that year the fixed element in their common tradition. Lightfoot immediately asks whether this reflects an early independent tradition or a later common source; pp. 449–51 argue that the notices can probably be traced to an interpretation or recension of Eusebius' *Chronicon*. The object is the **martyrdom**, not the letters or their collection. `[verified: OCR]`
- **Why this is not a firm 105 or 107:** p. 448 equates Trajan's ninth year with A.D. 105 in the *Chronicon Paschale*. On pp. 451–52 Lightfoot says Eusebius gives only an approximate placement and calls Wieseler's attempt to derive a definite date from Eusebius unsatisfactory. `[verified: OCR]`
- **Lightfoot's conclusion:** after examining Harnack's proposed episcopal chronology, p. 471 explicitly refuses both Wieseler's precise A.D. 107 and Harnack's latitude as far as A.D. 138. Lightfoot accepts the Antiochene chronology only as a rough approximation whose value decreases farther back. `[verified: OCR]`
- **What is and is not dated:** these pages discuss the chronology of Ignatius' episcopate and martyrdom and the evidential value of the later Acts and episcopal lists. They do not supply a distinct date for the journey, letter composition, or later letter collection. `[verified: OCR]`
- **Result:** it is inaccurate to cite Lightfoot pp. 448–471 simply as book support for a fixed “c. 107–110”. His position is that the martyrdom belongs to a rough early Trajanic chronology, but the exact year 107 cannot be extracted from Eusebius and the evidence also does not justify extending the date to 138.

## *Clavis Patrum Graecorum* 1025

- **Edition and access:** CPG vol. 1, *Patres antenicaeni*, compiled by Maurice Geerard (Turnhout: Brepols, 1983), ISBN 978-2-503-05011-9. The printed volume's catalogue metadata is `[verified]`; its CPG data were checked in Brepols and KU Leuven's official open-access Clavis Clavium record, which does not expose the original printed page number. `[verified]` [Brepols volume record](https://www.brepols.net/products/IS-9782503050119-1); [CPG 1025 digital record](https://clavis.brepols.net/clacla/OA/Details.aspx?id=92454D22E91440C98A18D0BA6C792F51).
- **Exact catalogue object:** CPG 1025 is titled *Epistulae vii genuinae* and subdivides the seven letters as 1025.1–1025.7. It catalogues the seven-letter middle recension; it is not the martyrdom account, which the same database identifies separately as CPG 1036. `[verified]`
- **Chronology actually displayed:** the author record for “Ignatius episcopus Antiochenus martyr” gives the date only as **sub Traiano** and the century as **1–2**. It does not print c. 107, c. 110, or a 107–110 range. `[verified]` [Official author record](https://clavis.brepols.net/clacla/OA/Details.aspx?id=E74482854E894D3C92C7E2E1CB4ECD43).
- **Authenticity note:** the CPG 1025 record says that almost all had regarded the seven letters as genuine after major disputes, then records renewed dissent; it summarizes Joly as attributing them to a forger who composed them in 165–168. That is a report of Joly's alternative, not the Clavis's date for the traditional position. `[verified]`
- **Result:** the claim “traditional c. 107–110 in CPG 1025” fails exact-source verification. CPG 1025 identifies the seven genuine letters, while the linked author chronology says only “under Trajan”. A precise c. 107–110 must be sourced elsewhere and must state whether it dates the martyrdom or the letters.
