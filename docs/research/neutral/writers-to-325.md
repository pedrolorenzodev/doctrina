## 1. Scope and rule

### 1.1 Area

Christian writers and anonymous Christian texts whose works are mostly before 325, in Greek and Latin. Syriac writers of the period (Odes of Solomon, Bardaisan) go to B by the brief; Gnostic teachers, Marcion, the Montanist oracles, Paul of Samosata, Arius and the synods (Antioch 268, Elvira, Nicaea) go to D as movements or councils. A writer who reports a movement (Irenaeus, Hippolytus, Tertullian) is here.

### 1.2 The membership rule, as applied

1. **Clavis entry.** Greek: *Clavis Patrum Graecorum* vol. I, *Patres Antenicaeni* (CPG 1000–1943), plus vol. II for the two straddlers (Eusebius, Alexander of Alexandria). Latin: *Clavis Patrum Latinorum*, "Scriptores Antenicaeni" (CPL 1–93k). Apocrypha: *Clavis Apocryphorum Novi Testamenti* (CANT). **Source used:** the Clavis Clavium open-access interface of Brepols / Bollandists / KU Leuven (`https://clavis.brepols.net/clacla/OA/Link.aspx?clavis=cpg&number=N`, also `clavis=cpl`, `clavis=cant`), which serves the CPG/CPL entries (parent tree, editions, transmission, authenticity notes) without login through a guest ticket. I crawled the whole CPG I tree ("Patres Antenicaeni › Saecula I-III", 13 categories, 129 author or work nodes) and the CPL "Scriptores Antenicaeni" node, 2026-10-17 [verified: Clavis Clavium OA]. Raw text: scratch `neutral-a/clavis/`, extracted work list `neutral-a/works.txt`. Every CPG/CPL number below comes from that crawl unless tagged otherwise.
2. **Cited in doctrinal debate.** Standard patrologies give the writer a chapter or section of his own: J. Quasten, *Patrology* I–II (1950–53); B. Altaner – A. Stuiber, *Patrologie* (8th ed. 1978); H. R. Drobner, *Lehrbuch der Patrologie* (1994; Eng. *The Fathers of the Church*, 2007); C. Moreschini – E. Norelli, *Storia della letteratura cristiana antica greca e latina* I (1995). The section-by-section check is [memory] (these patrologies are in copyright and I did not open them); the Clavis author nodes cite Altaner–Stuiber page numbers for most writers, which supports it [verified: Clavis bibliography lines].
3. **Tiers (my reading of the brief, recorded as open question Q2).** Tier 1 = writers and anonymous texts with *substantial surviving works* that a doctrine page is likely to quote: all the Apostolic Fathers of the standard collections, the apologists whose apologies survive, and every author with his own patrology chapter whose works survive beyond fragments. Writers who have a patrology section but survive only in fragments or a single short letter are tier 2: one paragraph is all screen 09 could say about them.

### 1.4 Boundary calls (siblings: please cross-reference, not duplicate)

- **Eusebius of Caesarea** is in CPG vol. II (post-Nicene numbering, CPG 3465–3507 [memory; to verify]) but his major works (Chronicle, *Historia ecclesiastica* finished c. 324–325, *Praeparatio* and *Demonstratio evangelica*, *Martyrs of Palestine*) are before 325, so by the brief's straddle rule ("most of his works") he is A's. Full entry here; B should cross-reference him.
- **Alexander of Alexandria** (d. 328): his surviving letters on Arius are c. 318–324 → A, tier 2.
- **Lactantius** (d. after 317), **Methodius** (d. c. 311), **Arnobius** (fl. c. 300): A.
- **Adamantius, *De recta in Deum fide***: CPG puts it among pre-Nicene texts but its date is debated (c. 290–330s) → A, tier 2.
- **Eustathius of Antioch, Marcellus of Ancyra, Asterius the Sophist, Juvencus, Ossius of Córdoba**: most work after 325 → B or C (Asterius and Arius → D).

### 2.2 Didache

- **Names:** grc Διδαχὴ τῶν δώδεκα ἀποστόλων (full title in the manuscript: Διδαχὴ Κυρίου διὰ τῶν δώδεκα ἀποστόλων τοῖς ἔθνεσιν); lat *Doctrina duodecim apostolorum*; en *Didache*, *Teaching of the Twelve Apostles*; es *Didajé* / *Didaché*, *Doctrina de los doce apóstoles* [memory; §2.0.3].
- **Date of composition:** no author. Range c. 50–150; most scholars place the compiled text in the late 1st century, an older English-language consensus put it in the early 2nd, and it may have reached its present form as late as c. 150 [verified: WP]. Positions: J.-P. Audet (1958) c. 50–70; W. Rordorf – A. Tuilier (SC 248, 1978) end of 1st c.; K. Niederwimmer (1989/1993) c. 110–120; A. Milavec (2003) c. 50–70; A. Garrow ties its earliest layer to the Jerusalem decree of c. 49–50 [memory, except Garrow and Milavec: verified: WP]. Composite: most scholars think a redactor combined separate sources (Two Ways 1–6; ritual 7–10; ministry 11–15; eschatology 16) [verified: WP]. **Place:** Syria is the most common proposal; Palestine and Egypt also proposed [verified: WP; Egypt memory]. **Language:** Greek. **Function:** church order (the first known example of the genre) [verified: WP].
- **What it is:** A short manual (c. 2,300 words) for communities receiving Gentile converts: moral instruction ("the Two Ways", shared with Barnabas 18–20 and with Jewish sources), then baptism, fasting, prayer (the Lord's Prayer three times a day), meal prayers over the cup and bread, and directions on travelling apostles and prophets, bishops and deacons, and Sunday assembly, ending with a warning about the last days [verified: WP]. It is the oldest description of baptism outside the New Testament [verified: WP].

### 2.3 Ignatius of Antioch

- **Names:** grc Ἰγνάτιος ὁ Θεοφόρος (he calls himself "Theophorus" in each letter's greeting); lat Ignatius Antiochenus; en Ignatius of Antioch; es Ignacio de Antioquía [memory; §2.0.3].
- **Born:** unknown; no ancient source. Modern estimates c. 35 (some up to c. 50) are guesses (DECISIONS 2026-10-06 already records this; English Wikipedia's "c. 33" is uncited) [memory]. **Died:** martyred in Rome (thrown to the beasts, per Eusebius' report of tradition; Jerome is the first to say lions) [verified: WP]. Conventional date: under Trajan, c. 107–110; Eusebius' Chronicle (in Jerome's version) puts it in Trajan's tenth year (107/108) [memory]. Clavis: "sub Traiano" [verified: Clavis]. Dissenting dates: É. Decrept, July 116 (Malalas, Acts of Drosis); R. Pervo 135–140; T. D. Barnes 140s (an apparent echo of the Gnostic Ptolemy); and the forgery thesis (R. Joly 1979; R. M. Hübner 1997; T. Lechner 1999; M. Vinzent) places the letters under Marcus Aurelius, 161–180 [verified: WP]. As of 2020 most authors accept the letters and date them to the mid-late 110s or to c. 150 [verified: WP].

Luc.* 6) calls him second bishop after Peter [verified: WP for Eusebius; Origen memory]. **Region:** Syria; letters written in Asia Minor on the way to Rome. **Language:** Greek. **Function:** bishop, martyr, letter-writer.
- **Who he was:** Known almost only from his own letters, written while he was taken under military guard from Antioch to Rome to be executed. From Smyrna he wrote to the churches of Ephesus, Magnesia, Tralles and Rome; from Troas to Philadelphia, Smyrna and its bishop Polycarp [memory]. The letters urge unity around one bishop with presbyters and deacons, warn against teachers who denied that Christ truly suffered (usually called Docetists) and against Judaizing practice, and ask the Roman Christians not to prevent his martyrdom [memory]. Polycarp's *Letter to the Philippians* (13) speaks of collecting his letters, the earliest external witness [memory].

### 2.8 Shepherd of Hermas

### 2.19 Tertullian

- **Names:** lat Quintus Septimius Florens Tertullianus; en Tertullian; es Tertuliano [memory; §2.0.3; `data/site/sources.extra.json` uses "Tertuliano"].

### 2.20 Passion of Perpetua and Felicity

- **Names:** lat *Passio sanctarum Perpetuae et Felicitatis*; grc Μαρτύριον τῆς ἁγίας Περπετούας (Greek version); en *The Passion of Perpetua and Felicity*; es *Pasión de Perpetua y Felicidad* [memory; §2.0.3].

### 2.23 Origen

- **Names:** grc Ὠριγένης (surnamed Ἀδαμάντιος, Eusebius HE 6.14.10); lat Origenes; en Origen; es Orígenes [memory; §2.0.3].

## 3. Tier 2 entries

One paragraph each: dates, place, language, works with dates, best edition, PD route. Clavis numbers [verified: Clavis] unless tagged; everything else [memory] unless tagged. Chronological.

**3.1 Quadratus** (grc Κοδρᾶτος; es Cuadrato). Apologist, probably of Asia Minor (identification with the bishop of Athens of the same name is doubtful); presented an *Apology* to Hadrian, c. 124–125 (Eusebius HE 4.3.1–2; Chronicle). One fragment survives in Eusebius (those healed by Christ lived "until our own time"). CPG 1060. Some identify the *Epistle to Diognetus* with his apology (Andriessen 1947; §2.16). Editions: in Apostolic Fathers collections (Holmes); PD: Eusebius HE (Schwartz GCS); English in NPNF2 1 (Eusebius).

**3.2 Aristo of Pella** (Ἀρίστων ὁ Πελλαῖος). Jewish-Christian (?) author of a lost *Dialogue of Jason and Papiscus* (c. 135–140), a debate between a Christian Jew and an Alexandrian Jew, mentioned by Celsus (via Origen, *C. Cels.* 4.52) and Maximus the Confessor; the attribution to Aristo is in Maximus. CPG 1101. Only testimonies survive.

**3.3 Claudius Apollinaris of Hierapolis** (Ἀπολινάριος). Bishop of Hierapolis in Phrygia under Marcus Aurelius; addressed an *Apology* to him (c. 172–177) and wrote against the New Prophecy; all lost except two fragments *On the Pascha* in the *Chronicon Paschale* (CPG 1103), important for the Quartodeciman dispute (D). Editions: Otto IX (1872); English ANF 8 (fragments).

**3.4 Hegesippus** (Ἡγήσιππος; es Hegesipo). Christian writer probably from Palestine or Syria, who travelled via Corinth to Rome under Anicetus (c. 155–166) and wrote *Hypomnemata* (*Memoirs*, 5 books) under Eleutherus (c. 174–189). Lost; fragments in Eusebius (HE 2.23 on James the Just; 3.32; 4.8; 4.22, his succession list of Roman bishops, central to the history of apostolic succession). Clavis: "saec. II", CPG 1302; the Latin *Hegesippus* on the Jewish war (4th c.) is unrelated (CPL 190a, 1021). Edition: fragments in Routh, *Reliquiae sacrae* I (1846); English ANF 8.

**3.5 Dionysius of Corinth** (Διονύσιος Κορίνθου). Bishop of Corinth c. 170; wrote "catholic letters" to several churches (Lacedaemon, Athens, Nicomedia, Gortyna, Amastris, Knossos, Rome); Eusebius summarises them (HE 4.23) and quotes the letter to bishop Soter of Rome (c. 170), which says 1 Clement was still read at Corinth and that Peter and Paul taught in Corinth and Rome. CPG 1336. English: NPNF2 1 (Eusebius), ANF 8 (fragments).

**3.6 Acts of Justin and companions** (Μαρτύριον τῶν ἁγίων Ἰουστίνου…). Court record of the trial of Justin and six companions before the prefect Iunius Rusticus at Rome, c. 165; three recensions (A, the shortest and closest to an official record; B; C later). BHG 972z–974. Edition: Musurillo 1972 ©; PD: Otto III; Ruinart (Spanish 1776–1864, `greek-fathers.md` §4); English ANF 1 (one recension) [memory].

**3.7 Letter of the Churches of Lyon and Vienne** (es *Carta de las Iglesias de Lyon y Vienne*). Letter of the churches of Gaul to those of Asia and Phrygia describing the persecution of 177 (Blandina, Pothinus, Attalus, Sanctus); preserved in large part by Eusebius (HE 5.1–3); its author is sometimes guessed to be Irenaeus. Clavis groups it under "Pothinus et Socii (Ecclesia Lugdunensis et Viennensis)", "sub Marco Aurelio" [verified: Clavis]. PD: Eusebius' text (Schwartz); English NPNF2 1; Ruinart (Spanish).

**3.8 Acts of the Scillitan Martyrs** (*Passio sanctorum Scillitanorum*). Latin court record of the trial at Carthage of twelve Christians from Scilli, 17 July 180, under the proconsul Vigellius Saturninus; the oldest dated Christian text in Latin, and the first mention of a Latin copy of Paul's letters ("books and letters of Paul, a just man"). BHL 7527–7531. Edition: Musurillo ©; PD: J. A. Robinson, Texts and Studies 1.2 (1891, with the Greek version); English ANF 9 [memory].

**3.9 Rhodon**. Asian pupil of Tatian at Rome, wrote against Marcion and Apelles under Commodus (180–192); fragments in Eusebius HE 5.13. CPG 1300. English ANF 8.

**3.10 The Anonymous anti-Montanist** and **3.11 Apollonius (anti-Montanist)**. Two writers against the New Prophecy quoted by Eusebius: the anonymous treatise to Avircius Marcellus (c. 192–193; HE 5.16–17), our main source for Montanus, Maximilla and Priscilla; and Apollonius' work (c. 197; HE 5.18). CPG 1327, 1328. English ANF 7 (fragments), NPNF2 1. (Montanism itself: D.)

**3.12 Serapion of Antioch** (Σεραπίων). Bishop of Antioch c. 190–211; his letter to the church of Rhossus on the *Gospel of Peter* (Eusebius HE 6.12) is the earliest known case of a bishop examining and rejecting a gospel; also a letter against the New Prophecy. CPG 1333–1334. English NPNF2 1.

**3.13 Polycrates of Ephesus** (Πολυκράτης). Bishop of Ephesus; his letter to Victor of Rome (c. 190–195; HE 5.24.2–8) defends the Asian custom of keeping Easter on the 14th of Nisan and lists the "great luminaries" of Asia (Philip, John, Polycarp, Melito). CPG 1338. English NPNF2 1; ANF 8. (Easter controversy: D.)

**3.14 Gaius of Rome** (Γάιος). Roman churchman under Zephyrinus (c. 199–217), author of a *Dialogue against Proclus* (a Montanist), in which he points to the "trophies" (tombs or monuments) of Peter on the Vatican and of Paul on the Ostian Way (HE 2.25.6–7), and, per later sources, rejected the Johannine Apocalypse (perhaps the Gospel too), which Hippolytus answered (CPG 1891). CPG 1330–1331. English ANF 5, NPNF2 1.

**3.15 Inscription of Abercius** (Titulus Abercii). Greek funerary verse inscription of Abercius (Avircius), a Christian of Hieropolis in Phrygia, composed before 216 (it is imitated in a dated inscription of 216), describing a journey to Rome and a meal of "fish" and "mixed wine and bread" everywhere; Christian reading generally accepted, a pagan reading once argued (G. Ficker 1894). Two fragments found by W. M. Ramsay in 1883, now in the Vatican Museums; the full text from the *Life of Abercius*. CPG 1368 [verified: Clavis]. Edition: W. Wischmeyer (1980) ©; PD: Lightfoot AF II.1 (1885) [memory]. Cited in Eucharist discussions.

**3.16 Muratorian Fragment** (*Canon Muratorianus*). An 85-line Latin list of New Testament books with comments, in an 8th-century manuscript of Bobbio (Milan, Ambrosiana I 101 sup.), published by L. A. Muratori in 1740. Most date its original (probably Greek) to Rome c. 170–200 (it calls Hermas' time "very recent"); A. C. Sundberg (1973) and G. M. Hahneman (1992) argued for the 4th century and the East; debate open. CPG 1862; CPL 83a [verified: Clavis]. PD: S. P. Tregelles' facsimile edition (1867); English ANF 5. Central to "canon of Scripture" pages.

**3.17 Hermias** (Ἑρμείας). Author of a short satire, *Mockery of the Pagan Philosophers* (*Irrisio gentilium philosophorum*), date anywhere from the late 2nd to the 5th century; nothing known of him. CPG 1113. PD: Otto IX; English: none in ANF [memory]. Tier 2 only for completeness.

**3.18 Sentences of Sextus**. A Greek collection of some 450 moral maxims, a pagan collection Christianised c. 180–210; Origen cites it; Rufinus translated it into Latin as by Pope Xystus, which Jerome attacked; also in Syriac, Coptic (Nag Hammadi XII,1) and Armenian. CPG 1115 (and 1220). Edition: H. Chadwick (1959) ©; PD: J. Gildemeister (1873) [memory].

**3.19 Julius Africanus** (Σέξτος Ἰούλιος Ἀφρικανός; es Julio Africano). Christian scholar from Aelia Capitolina (Jerusalem), c. 160–post 240 (Clavis "† post 240"); friend of Origen and of the emperor Severus Alexander, for whom he organised a library at Rome. Works: *Chronographiae* (5 books, to 221; the first Christian world chronicle, fragments; CPG 1690), *Kestoi* (*Embroideries*, miscellany, 1691), *Letter to Origen* on the story of Susanna (1692), *Letter to Aristides* reconciling the genealogies of Jesus (1693). Edition: M. Wallraff et al., GCS NF 15 (2007) ©; PD: Routh II; English ANF 6.

**3.20 Alexander of Jerusalem** (Ἀλέξανδρος Ἱεροσολύμων). Bishop in Cappadocia, then coadjutor and bishop of Jerusalem (c. 212–250), founder of a library at Jerusalem; ordained Origen presbyter with Theoctistus; died in prison in the Decian persecution (c. 250–251). Letters in Eusebius HE 6 (to the Antinoites, to Antioch, to Origen; joint letter to Pontian of Rome). CPG 1698–1701; Clavis "s. III ineunte; martyr sub Decio". English NPNF2 1; ANF 6.

**3.21 Didascalia apostolorum** (es *Didascalia de los apóstoles*). Church order in the form of an apostolic teaching, written in Greek in Syria in the 3rd century (Clavis "s. III" [verified: Clavis]; most: first half or c. 230–250); survives complete only in Syriac, partly in Latin (Verona palimpsest LV 55) and reworked in *Apostolic Constitutions* I–VI. Deals with bishops' duties, penance and readmission of sinners, widows and deaconesses, and Christian relations with Jewish practice. CPG 1738. Editions: A. Vööbus, CSCO 401–402, 407–408 (1979) ©; A. Stewart-Sykes (2009) ©. PD: P. de Lagarde (Syriac, 1854); F. X. Funk, *Didascalia et Constitutiones apostolorum* (1905); E. Hauler (Latin, 1900); R. H. Connolly, *Didascalia Apostolorum* (Oxford 1929, `didascaliaaposto0000rhug`; English: US PD; Connolly d. 1948 → PD in Spain from 2029) [verified: helper]; M. D. Gibson (English from Syriac, 1903, PD) [memory].

**3.22 Apostolic Church Order** (*Canones ecclesiastici apostolorum*). Short church order (c. 300, Egypt or Syria), the Two Ways followed by rules attributed to individual apostles; Greek with Latin, Coptic, Syriac versions. CPG 1739 (Clavis "s. III"). PD: J. W. Bickell (1843); Funk (1905); English: Harnack–Owen, *Sources of the Apostolic Canons* (1895) [memory].

**3.23 Pope Cornelius** (Cornelius, bishop of Rome 251–253; Clavis "† 253"). Elected in 251; opposed by Novatian; exiled to Centumcellae, died there in 253. Letters: to Fabius of Antioch (Greek, Eusebius HE 6.43, with the count of the Roman clergy: 46 presbyters, 7 deacons…; CPG 1850) and two Latin letters to Cyprian (Cyprian *Ep.* 49–50). Edition: CCSL 3 (Cyprian's letters); English ANF 5. Cited for church order and the lapsed.

**3.24 Pope Dionysius of Rome** (bishop of Rome 259/260–268). His letter against those who divide the Godhead (against the Sabellians and the "tritheists"), quoted by Athanasius (*De decretis* 26), and his exchange with Dionysius of Alexandria; letter to Caesarea in Cappadocia. CPG 1860 [verified: Clavis "sedit 259–268"]. English ANF 7.

**3.25 Firmilian of Caesarea**. Bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia (c. 230–268; Clavis "† ca. 268"), friend of Origen; his letter to Cyprian (256) against Stephen of Rome on rebaptism survives only in Latin translation as Cyprian *Ep.* 75 (CPL 50). English ANF 5 (as Cyprian Ep. 74). (The rebaptism dispute: D.)

**3.26 Pseudo-Cyprianic writings.** Anonymous 3rd-century Latin works transmitted with Cyprian's: *De aleatoribus* (against dice-playing; by a bishop, perhaps of Rome or Africa, mid-3rd c.; quotes the Didache), *De rebaptismate* (c. 256, against Cyprian's position on rebaptism), *Ad Novatianum* (c. 253–257, against Novatian), *De laude martyrii*, *De montibus Sina et Sion*, *Quod idola dii non sint* (ascribed to Cyprian by Jerome and Augustine; authenticity debated), *Adversus Iudaeos*, and later items (*De singularitate clericorum*, *De duodecim abusivis saeculi* 7th-c. Irish). CPL 57–67, 75–76, 1106. PD Latin: Hartel CSEL 3.3 (1871); English: ANF 5 (several). Each needs its own date in its reader.

**3.27 Acts of Cyprian** (*Acta proconsularia* and Pontius' *Vita Cypriani*). Records of Cyprian's two hearings (257, 258) and execution, and a *Life* by his deacon Pontius (shortly after 258), the first Christian biography in Latin. CPL 52–53. PD: Hartel CSEL 3.3; English ANF 5; Spanish: Camino y Orella 1807 (prolegomena) [inference: check].

**3.28 Anatolius of Laodicea** (Ἀνατόλιος). Alexandrian scholar, bishop of Laodicea in Syria (c. 268–post 280; Clavis "† s. III exeunte"); a 19-year Easter cycle (*De Pascha*, CPG 1620; survives in a Latin version whose authenticity is debated) and an arithmetical work (1621). English ANF 6.

**3.29 Theognostus** and **3.30 Pierius**. Alexandrian teachers after Origen (late 3rd c.). Theognostus' *Hypotyposes* (7 books, CPG 1626) are known from Photius' summary and Athanasius' quotations; Pierius ("Origen the Younger", † c. 310, CPG 1630), homilies, fragments (Photius). English ANF 6 (fragments).

**3.31 Theonas of Alexandria**. Bishop of Alexandria 281/282–300 (Clavis). A letter against the Manichaeans in a papyrus (P.Ryl. 469, attribution conjectural, CPG 1632); the *Letter to Lucianus the chief chamberlain* (CPG 1633), printed in ANF 6, is generally considered a 17th-century forgery [memory: by J. Vignier; flag for verification].

**3.32 Peter of Alexandria** (Πέτρος Ἀλεξανδρείας; es Pedro de Alejandría). Bishop 300–311 (Clavis), martyred 25 November 311 under Maximinus Daia; the Melitian schism began during his exile (D). Works: *Canonical Letter* (14 canons on the lapsed, 306, in Byzantine canon law; CPG 1639), fragments *On the Godhead*, *On the Soul*, *On the Resurrection*, *On the Pascha*, letters (CPG 1635–1645); dubia and spuria 1650–1663. Edition: T. Vivian (1988) ©; PD: PG 18; Routh IV; English ANF 6.

**3.33 Phileas of Thmuis**. Bishop of Thmuis in Egypt, martyred at Alexandria c. 306/307 (Clavis "sub Diocletiano, † 307"); *Letter to the people of Thmuis* (Eusebius HE 8.10; CPG 1671) on the sufferings of martyrs; *Acts of Phileas* (Latin and Greek, the Greek on the Bodmer XX papyrus). PD: Eusebius; English NPNF2 1.

**3.34 Pamphilus of Caesarea** (Πάμφιλος; es Pánfilo). Presbyter of Caesarea, scholar who copied and corrected Bible manuscripts and Origen's works in the library of Caesarea; martyred 16 February 310 (Clavis "† 309"; Eusebius *Mart. Pal.* 11); wrote in prison, with Eusebius, an *Apology for Origen* (5+1 books), of which book 1 survives in Rufinus' Latin (CPG 1715 = 3476). Edition: R. Amacker – É. Junod, SC 464–465 (2002) ©; PD: PG 17; English: T. P. Scheck, FOTC 120 (2010) ©.

**3.35 Lucian of Antioch** (Λουκιανὸς ὁ Ἀντιοχεύς). Presbyter and teacher at Antioch, martyred at Nicomedia 7 January 312 (Clavis "† 312"); credited with a revision of the Greek Bible (the "Lucianic" or Antiochene text, CPG 1723); teacher of Arius and of Eusebius of Nicomedia ("Collucianists"); a creed attributed to him was quoted at the Dedication Synod of Antioch (341), attribution debated. Fragments: *Apology* (1720), letter to the Antiochenes (1721). English ANF 9? [memory: check].

**3.36 Victorinus of Pettau** (Victorinus Poetovionensis; es Victorino de Petovio/Pettau). Bishop of Poetovio (Ptuj, Slovenia), martyred c. 304 (Clavis "obiit 304"); the oldest Latin biblical exegete whose works survive: *Commentary on the Apocalypse* (CPL 80; millenarian in its original form; known mainly in Jerome's revision, the original found by J. Haussleiter and published 1916) and *On the Creation of the World* (CPL 79); *Adversus omnes haereses* sometimes ascribed to him. Editions: M. Dulaey, SC 423 (1997) ©; PD: J. Haussleiter, CSEL 49 (1916); English ANF 7.

**3.37 Commodian** (Commodianus; es Comodiano). Latin Christian poet of uncertain date and place: 3rd century (Africa, c. 250, the traditional view) or 5th century (Gaul or Italy); author of *Instructiones* (CPL 1470) and *Carmen de duobus populis* (*Carmen apologeticum*, CPL 1471) in rough accentual hexameters, millenarian in outlook. The Clavis files him under "Poetae Latini", not among the pre-Nicenes [verified: Clavis]. PD: B. Dombart, CSEL 15 (1887); English ANF 4. Kept here because his traditional date is pre-Nicene; flag for C to cross-reference.

**3.38 Pseudo-Clementine *Homilies* and *Recognitions***. A novel about Clement of Rome's travels with Peter and the recovery of his family, with long debates against Simon Magus; two 4th-century recensions (*Homilies*, Greek; *Recognitions*, Latin of Rufinus) of a lost 3rd-century "basic writing" (Grundschrift), with Jewish-Christian sources (*Kerygmata Petrou*) debated. CPG 1015 = CANT 209 [verified: Clavis]. Editions: B. Rehm, GCS 42 (1953) and 51 (1965) ©; PD: P. de Lagarde (1865); English ANF 8.

**3.39 Adamantius, *Dialogue on the Right Faith in God*** (*De recta in Deum fide*). Dialogue in which "Adamantius" (later taken for Origen) debates Marcionites, Bardesanites and Valentinians; c. 290–300 by most, early 4th c. by others (Clavis "s. IV" [verified: Clavis]); Latin version by Rufinus. CPG 1726. Edition: W. H. van de Sande Bakhuyzen, GCS 4 (1901, PD); English: R. A. Pretty (1997) ©. A source on Marcion's teaching (D).

**3.40 Alexander of Alexandria** (Ἀλέξανδρος Ἀλεξανδρείας; straddler). Bishop of Alexandria 312/313–328, who condemned Arius; his encyclical letter *Henos somatos* (c. 319–324; CPG 2000 [verified: Clavis]) and his letter to Alexander of Byzantium/Thessalonica (in Theodoret HE 1.4) are the first documents of the Arian dispute; attended Nicaea with his deacon Athanasius. Dubia in Syriac and Armenian (CPG 2010–2011 [verified: Clavis]). PD: PG 18; English ANF 6. The dispute itself: D.

**3.41 Early apocrypha cited in doctrine pages** (open question Q3 on whether they belong here). All anonymous or pseudonymous; CANT numbers [verified: Clavis] except where tagged:
- *Protevangelium of James* (CANT 50): birth and childhood of Mary, birth of Jesus, Joseph as an older widower with sons; c. 150–200 (Origen knows it); Greek, Bodmer V papyrus (3rd–4th c.). PD: C. Tischendorf, *Evangelia apocrypha* (1853, 2nd ed. 1876); English ANF 8. Cited in Marian pages (perpetual virginity, "brothers of the Lord").
- *Gospel of Peter* (CANT 13): passion and resurrection narrative; mid-2nd c.; rejected by Serapion (3.12); Akhmim fragment found 1886–87. PD: J. A. Robinson – M. R. James (1892); English ANF 9.
- *Apocalypse of Peter* (CANT 317): tours of heaven and hell; c. 135; in the Muratorian list "which some will not read in church"; Akhmim Greek and Ethiopic. PD: M. R. James (1924, US PD; James d. 1936 → PD everywhere) [memory].
- *Acts of Paul* (CANT 211), incl. *Paul and Thecla* and *3 Corinthians*: c. 160–190; Tertullian (*De baptismo* 17) says a presbyter of Asia wrote it and was deposed. PD: Lipsius–Bonnet (1891); M. R. James (1924); English ANF 8 (Thecla).
- *Epistula Apostolorum* (CANT 22): dialogue of the risen Christ with the apostles, c. 150–170, against Cerinthus and Simon; Coptic and Ethiopic, found 1895. PD: C. Schmidt (1919, German); M. R. James (1924) [memory].
- *Ascension of Isaiah* (Clavis Apocryphorum Veteris Testamenti, Jewish base with Christian parts c. 100–150) [memory]; English: R. H. Charles (1900; d. 1931, PD).
(Gnostic gospels and treatises are D's.)
