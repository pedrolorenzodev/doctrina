## Scope and evidence rule

The existing research names twelve modern scholars who materially anchor a date or an authenticity position: Étienne Decrept, Richard I. Pervo, Timothy D. Barnes, Markus Vinzent, Joseph Barber Lightfoot, Theodor Zahn, Adolf von Harnack, Kirsopp Lake, William R. Schoedel, Robert Joly, Reinhard M. Hübner, and Thomas Lechner.

James Ussher, Isaac Vossius, John Pearson, and William Cureton belong to the transmission and recension history but are not sources for a date displayed in the current research. Eusebius is an ancient witness, not a modern scholar. They should remain in the narrative rather than receive dating-scholar cards.

Dates below describe the object a scholar dates. A date for a persecution, a martyrdom, or a collection is not silently converted into a date for every letter. “Living” means that a current institutional or authority record remains open and no death record was found; it is not inferred from an old publication. When an authority record does not publish a requested fact, the gap is stated.

### Karl Gustav Adolf von Harnack

- **Identity:** Karl Gustav Adolf von Harnack (1851–1930), German Protestant theologian and church historian, born in Dorpat in the Baltic provinces of the Russian Empire, died at Heidelberg (Deutsche Biographie sfz49285) `[verified]`.
- **Field and position:** early church and history of dogma. His position changed. In 1878 he took as the view of most historians that the letters “nicht früher als in den letzten Jahren der Regierungszeit Hadrian's geschrieben sein können”, said that he would not prove the reliability of that impression there, and reported that Zahn had shown the letters to be authentic `[verified, with a nuance: 1878, pp. 1–2]`; the earlier wording “he argued that the letters could not be earlier” overstated this (corrected 2026-10-21). In 1897 he held that “die Ignatiusbriefe und der Polykarpbrief sind echt, und sie sind in den letzten Jahren Trajan's verfasst (110–117) oder vielleicht einige Jahre später (117–125)”, so the late value is 117–125, not open-ended `[verified: scan b1geschichtederalt02harn, p. 406; summary p. 700]`. The Schaff–Herzog encyclopedia summarises the change: he “has abandoned his former attempt to date the letters in the last years of Hadrian or the first of Antoninus Pius” and decided they are genuine, “toward the end of the reign of Trajan (110–117), or possibly, though not probably, a little later” `[verified]`.
- **Works behind the dating:** Adolf Harnack, *Die Zeit des Ignatius und die Chronologie der antiochenischen Bischöfe bis Tyrannus nach Julius Africanus und den späteren Historikern. Nebst einer Untersuchung über die Verbreitung der Passio S. Polycarpi im Abendlande* (Leipzig: J. C. Hinrichs, 1878), 1–9; and Adolf Harnack, *Geschichte der altchristlichen Litteratur bis Eusebius*, part 2, *Die Chronologie*, vol. 1 (Leipzig: J. C. Hinrichs, 1897), 400–406 (corrected 2026-10-21 from 400–404: the conclusion is on p. 406).
- **Fact sources:** [Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz49285.html) gives the full name, exact dates, profession, and offices; the [German Historical Museum biography](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/biografie/adolf-von-harnack) supplies the German career and church-history chair. The [1878 scan](https://archive.org/details/diezeitdesignat01harngoog/page/n19/mode/2up) contains the early statement; the [Schaff–Herzog summary](https://www.ccel.org/s/schaff/encyc05/htm/iii.xvii.xi.htm) records the later revision.

### Kirsopp Lake

- **Identity:** Kirsopp Lake (1872–1946), English New Testament scholar and church historian who later taught at Harvard: born at Southampton on 7 April 1872, died 10 November 1946; B.A. Lincoln College, Oxford; Leiden 1904–14; Harvard Professor of Early Christian Literature 1914–18, Winn Professor of Ecclesiastical History 1918–32 `[verified: Harvard Divinity faculty page]`.
- **Field and position:** early Christian literature, ecclesiastical history, and textual study. He reports Eusebius' year 108 and does not adopt it. His sentence on p. 166 reads: “Eusebius fixes the date of his [death] in Rome in the tenth year of Trajan, i.e. 108 A.D. The Trajan range is therefore Lake's report of a general tendency among modern critics, **not a dating of his own** (corrected 2026-10-21, `ignatius-dating-scholars.verify.md`, “Imprecise”, Lake).
- **Work behind the dating:** Kirsopp Lake, ed. and trans., *The Apostolic Fathers*, vol. 1, Loeb Classical Library (London: William Heinemann; New York: Macmillan, 1912) `[verified: archive.org apostolicfathers0001kirs]`, 165–171 (the introduction starts at pp. 165–166; the end of the range was not checked).

## Consistent Spanish dating titles

Use this grammar:

`Scholar · date — dated object`

The date segment preserves the source's precision; the object segment prevents unlike claims from appearing to be measurements of the same event.

| Source precision | Spanish form | Example from the actual datings |
|---|---|---|
| Month and year | `julio de 116` | `Decrept (2008) · julio de 116 — persecución en Antioquía` |
| Approximate year | `c. 110` | `Lightfoot (1889) · c. 110 — martirio` |
| Closed range | `105–117` | `Zahn (1873) · 105–117 — viaje, cartas y muerte` |

Rules:

- Use an en dash (`–`) for ranges and `c.` followed by a space.
- Spell out months in lower case: `julio de 116`; do not mix `jul. 116`, `116-07`, and prose.
- Do not turn “the 140s” into `c. 145`; render its real precision as `década de 140`.
- Do not turn a reign or a broad period into a point estimate.
- Always name the dated object: persecution, journey, martyrdom, letters, three-letter collection, or seven-letter corpus.
- Separate a scholar's life span from the dating title, in words (DECISIONS 2026-10-06, “Death dates: words, and † where space is tight”): «Nació 1959», «Murió 1889» (en: “Born”, “Died”), or «†» before the year where space is tight; unknown birth: «Nacimiento: desconocido»; never `n.` or `m.` (the abbreviations Pedro did not read), never `vive` (it asserts that he is alive; say nothing, or «sin fecha de muerte registrada»), never an invented end year such as `1959–2026`.
- Show the year of the work on every card, in parentheses after the name, and keep it with each view when a scholar changed position, rather than merging views into one range: Decrept 2008, Pervo 2010, Barnes 2008, Vinzent 2023 (online; the print year is unconfirmed), Lightfoot 1889, Zahn 1873, Harnack 1878 and 1897, Lake 1912, Schoedel 1985, Joly 1979, Hübner 1997, Lechner 1999 (one rule, replacing the earlier “year only for changed views”).
