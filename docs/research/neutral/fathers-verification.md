## Scope and method

The controlling universe is the tier-one entries in `writers-to-325.md`, `writers-greek-syriac.md` and `writers-latin.md`, plus any early writer present only in `data/raw/history.json`. Anonymous works such as the Didache, the Epistle of Barnabas and the Shepherd of Hermas remain in scope because the product presents them alongside named writers. Councils, movements and Reformation figures are outside this report.

Each row must separately establish:

1. birth and death, or a floruit/composition range when personal dates are unknown;
2. office and see, without retrojecting a later title where the evidence is disputed;
3. every work actually cited in the product, with a date and the scholarly source of that date;
4. one of the project verdicts: `CONFIRMED`, `WRONG` or `UNCONFIRMABLE`. An approximate date is labelled `estimate`; a traditional date unsupported by contemporary evidence is labelled `traditional`, not converted into an estimate.

Preferred authorities are CPG/CPL or the Clavis database, a critical edition's introduction, ODCC, Quasten and specialist scholarly studies. The neutral-universe files are the inventory, not self-authenticating evidence: their `[memory]` and Wikipedia-backed statements remain unverified here until replaced by one of those authorities. Page references identify the print pagination of a dated edition, not a mutable web transcription.

## E. Consolidated unresolved facts

These are genuine limits of the surviving evidence, not unfinished searches:

1. Ignatius's martyrdom and the absolute chronology of the middle-recension letters remain disputed; no single 107–110 range can be certified.
2. The Hippolytan corpus cannot be reduced to one author, and *Apostolic Tradition* cannot receive a simple c. 215 author/date label.
3. Cyril of Jerusalem versus John II for the Mystagogical Catecheses remains unresolved; every product citation must retain the attribution caveat.
4. John Chrysostom's birth year and exact episcopal-consecration day remain disputed even though his enthronement and death are secure.
5. Fulgentius of Ruspe's absolute life and episcopate chronology has incompatible modern reconstructions; the ranges in his row are intentional.
6. Benedict's conventional 480/529/547 framework rests principally on Gregory's later hagiographic narrative; the exact life and foundation years are not recoverable.
7. John Climacus has two live life chronologies, so neither his life nor the *Ladder* can be assigned a narrow year.

10. Andrew of Crete's death year and the Great Canon's composition year remain unresolved.
