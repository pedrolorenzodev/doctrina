## 1. Scope, source layers and editions

### 1.1 Entity rule

A council is an entity when its acts, creed, definition, canons or reception affect doctrine pages. A movement is an entity when ancient sources identify a network or teaching and a council, synod or writer explicitly opposed it. A label is not assumed to describe every person later placed under it.

Each movement entry distinguishes: **self-witness** (its own work/fragments), **opponent-witness** (a writer arguing against it), and **condemnation** (who used what judgment, in which text). “Semi-Pelagian,” “Nestorian” and “Monophysite” require special label histories.

### 1.3 Reception shorthand

“Ecumenical” is always attributed. The Chalcedonian Eastern Orthodox and Catholic churches receive seven through Nicaea II; Oriental Orthodox churches explicitly confess conformity with the first three in the [1973 Coptic–Catholic declaration](https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/speeches/1973/may/documents/hf_p-vi_spe_19730510_dichiarazione-comune.html); the Church of the East's modern declaration grounds common faith in the Nicene Creed without retroactively accepting Ephesus ([1994 Assyrian–Catholic declaration](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/speeches/1994/november/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_19941111_dichiarazione-cristologica.html)). Protestant reception belongs to each tradition map: many confessions receive early creeds while denying that councils are intrinsically infallible. `[verified for cited declarations; tradition detail deferred]`

## 2. Councils and synods

### 2.1 The seven commonly called ecumenical in Chalcedonian churches

| Council | Date, place, convocation/presidency | Texts and attributed decisions | Reception | Editions / licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nicaea I** | 20 May–late July 325, Nicaea; convoked by Constantine; Hosius of Córdoba and senior bishops are associated with leadership, but exact presidency is disputed. `[memory: exact close date]` | Creed; 20 canons; synodal letter; decisions concerning Arius, Easter and discipline. Arius and specified propositions were anathematized by the council. | First council across the principal ancient reception families; later creed commonly appears in its 381 form, so label the 325 text exactly. | ACO is not the main acts source because full acts do not survive; Mansi II; NPNF2 14; Tejada I. `[verified: reuse]` |
| **Constantinople I** | May–July 381, Constantinople; convoked by Theodosius I; presided successively by Meletius, Gregory of Nazianzus and Nectarius according to later narratives. `[memory: verify exact dates]` | Four securely conciliar canons in the Greek tradition (later collections number seven); doctrinal decisions against named groups. The creed's relation to the council and first documentary attestation at Chalcedon must be explained, not assumed as a verbatim session act. | Second for Chalcedonian and Oriental Orthodox churches; Church of the East also uses the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed, but reception wording needs its own source. | Mansi III; NPNF2 14; Tejada III; modern studies for creed/canon transmission. |
| **Ephesus** | 22 June–July 431, Ephesus; convoked by Theodosius II; Cyril opened/presided before the eastern delegation arrived; rival sessions followed. | Session acts; Cyril's letters, Nestorius' letter, the council's judgments and canons. It deposed Nestorius; “Theotokos” appears within its christological documents rather than as an isolated slogan. | Third for Chalcedonian and Oriental Orthodox churches; not accepted by the Church of the East. Modern 1994 declaration warns against projecting old anathemas/formulas onto current common confession. | ACO I; Mansi IV–V; NPNF2 14; Tejada. |
| **Chalcedon** | 8 October–1 November 451, Chalcedon; convoked by Marcian/Pulcheria; imperial commissioners directed sessions, with bishops/legates exercising ecclesial roles. | Acts; Definition; reception of creeds and specified letters; 28 canons in Greek transmission. It deposed Dioscorus and rejected Eutyches; distinguish persons, doctrines and later labels. | Fourth in Chalcedonian churches; not accepted as ecumenical by Oriental Orthodox churches. The 1973 declaration says post-451 divisions involved theological and non-theological factors and confesses shared christological language. | ACO II; Mansi VI–VII; NPNF2 14; Tejada I. |
| **Constantinople II** | 5 May–2 June 553, Constantinople; convoked by Justinian I; Eutychius of Constantinople presided; Pope Vigilius did not attend. `[memory: exact days]` | Eight sessions; sentence and 14 anathemas; condemnation of the “Three Chapters” (specified writings/person-related dossiers), not a generic erasure of Theodore/Theodoret/Ibas. | Fifth in Chalcedonian churches; reception was contested in parts of the Latin West. | ACO IV; Mansi IX; NPNF2 14; Tejada III. |
| **Constantinople III** | 7 November 680–16 September 681, imperial palace; convoked by Constantine IV, who attended/presided imperially at early sessions; papal and patriarchal delegations participated. | 18 sessions; dogmatic definition affirming two natural wills/operations; condemned named proponents and documents, including Honorius in the conciliar sentence. | Sixth in Chalcedonian churches. | ACO ser. II II; Mansi XI; NPNF2 14; Tejada III. |
| **Nicaea II** | 24 September–23 October 787, first at Nicaea, final session Constantinople; convoked by Irene and Constantine VI; Tarasius presided ecclesially. `[memory: exact first date]` | Seven sessions plus final; definition distinguishing honour/veneration of images from worship due to God; canons; refutation of Hiereia. | Seventh in Chalcedonian churches; its reception in the Frankish West was complicated by translation and the *Libri Carolini*, while Rome received it. | Mansi XII–XIII; NPNF2 14; Tejada III. |

## 3. Condemned movements and disputed labels

| Entity | Time/people and best self-witness | Opponent-witness and attributed condemnations | Neutral presentation / edition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Marcion and Marcionite churches** | Marcion, active at Rome c. 140s; his *Antitheses* and edition of Gospel/Apostolikon do not survive independently; wording is reconstructed from quotations and manuscript comparison. | Justin, Irenaeus, Tertullian *Against Marcion*, Epiphanius and Adamantius are opponents. Later legal/canonical condemnations vary. | Present Marcion's edited collection and creator/God distinction as reconstructed, never quote Tertullian as Marcion's unlabelled voice. | **Gnostic systems** | Umbrella, not one church. ### 3.2 Fourth- and fifth-century controversies

| Entity | Self-witness | Condemnation/opponent layer | Neutral presentation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Donatist movements** | Tyconius' *Book of Rules*; Donatist acts, letters and fragments of Parmenian/Petilian; martyr narratives. | Optatus and Augustine are opponents; Arles 314 and repeated African/Roman decisions opposed Donatist claims; imperial coercion is a separate political history. | Use plural/network where appropriate; do not define the group only by rebaptism. Edition of Gesta/collecta and opponent CSEL texts; transmission labels mandatory. |
| **Arian and “homoean/anomoean” families** | Arius' letters and fragments of *Thalia*; Asterius fragments; Eunomius' *Apology*; creeds of Antioch/Sirmium/Rimini-Constantinople. | Nicaea condemned Arius' propositions; Athanasius, Hilary, Basil and Gregorys are opponents; Constantinople I names later groups. | “Arian” is too broad for all non-Nicene fourth-century coalitions. | **Apollinarianism** | Apollinaris' fragments and works transmitted pseudonymously under accepted writers. | Gregory Nazianzen, Gregory Nyssa and Epiphanius oppose; Rome 377/382 and Constantinople I condemn attributed propositions/groups. | Reconstruct cautiously and name pseudonymous transmission. `[memory: verify exact synods]` |
| **Pneumatomachi / “Macedonians”** | Few secure self-texts; labels cover groups who disputed the status/language applied to the Holy Spirit. | Basil, Gregory Nazianzen, Athanasius and Epiphanius oppose; Constantinople I's later reception associates its creed/decisions with this controversy. | Do not make Macedonius the demonstrable founder without evidence; distinguish conciliar label from self-description. |
| **Priscillian and Priscillianists** | Eleven Würzburg tractates discovered in 1885 are major self-witness, though authorship of the collection requires work-level review. | Councils of Zaragoza (380), Bordeaux (384), imperial trial at Trier (385); opponents include Sulpicius Severus and later writers. | Separate ecclesial condemnations from the civil execution, which contemporaries including Martin of Tours opposed. | **Origenist controversies** | Origen's own extant works; later ascetic/Evagrian materials. | Epiphanius/Jerome/Theophilus; disputes c. 400; propositions attributed to 553 documents. | Later “Origenism” is not identical to Origen's full corpus. List proposition, source and date; never back-project 553 wording without textual history. |
| **Pelagius, Celestius and related debates** | Pelagius' Pauline commentary (with transmission under Jerome), *Letter to Demetrias*, confession and defense fragments; Celestius' propositions mainly through opponents. | African councils 411–418, Innocent I/Zosimus, Ephesus reception; Augustine and Jerome oppose. | Use own texts first. “Pelagianism” may denote different proposition sets across dates; name the document. Critical Caspari/de Bruyn references; public-domain Latin routes need verification. |
| **“Semi-Pelagianism”** | Not an ancient self-name; modern umbrella applied to Cassian, Faustus of Riez and southern Gallic debates. | Orange II (529) condemned specified propositions but did not use the 16th-century term. | Product label should be “debates on grace in southern Gaul”; mention the later historiographic label in a note. `[memory: verify first attestation]` |
| **Nestorius / “Nestorianism”** | Nestorius' letters, sermons/fragments and Syriac *Bazaar of Heracleides*; Theodore/Narsai are distinct authors, not automatic members of one “school.” | Ephesus deposed Nestorius; Cyril's writings and conciliar acts are opponent/judicial sources; 553 later treated Theodore/Three Chapters. | The Church of the East is not to be labelled “Nestorian.” The 1994 declaration says past anathemas concerned persons/formulas and divisions were largely misunderstandings, while expressing common christological faith. |
| **Eutyches and “Monophysitism/Miaphysitism”** | Eutyches' positions survive through acts; Dioscorus, Timothy Aelurus, Philoxenus and Severus have distinct documents and cannot be collapsed into Eutyches. | Constantinople 448, Chalcedon 451 and later councils condemn persons/formulas; Chalcedon is rejected by Oriental Orthodox churches. | WCC material records that Oriental Orthodox reject “Monophysite” and Eutyches and accept “miaphysite” as a modern descriptor. Use each writer's own formula; reserve “monophysite” for attributed historical usage. ## 4. Open questions for Pedro

1. **Council entity granularity.** Recommendation: one entity per historical assembly, not “Council of Carthage” as a merged page; shared landing pages may group 397/419.
2. **Reception display.** Options: one universal badge or a matrix by ancient communion/tradition. Recommendation: matrix with sourced cells and “creed received” distinct from “council received as ecumenical.”
3. **Movement names in navigation.** Recommendation: user-facing neutral headings (“debates after Ephesus,” “one-will controversy”) with historical labels as searchable aliases and explanatory notes.
4. **Later endpoint for iconoclasm.** Hiereia 754 exceeds the writer cutoff but is explicitly requested and inseparable from Nicaea II. Recommendation: councils/movements may extend through Nicaea II (787), while writer universe stays at c. 749.
5. **Own-voice threshold.** Recommendation: when fewer than substantial fragments survive, the page begins “No independent work survives” and separates every opponent source visually.
