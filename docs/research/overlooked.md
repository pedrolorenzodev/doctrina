# Overlooked writings: what the site doesn't have yet but should

_Research run of 2026-10-09 (overnight, agent-led). Synthesis of two judgment reports: `sources/overlooked-era.md` (2000 years swept by era: 180 works, each with doctrines, who cites it, rights route and priority A/B/C) and `sources/overlooked-doctrine.md` (16 doctrines × traditions: what each tradition actually cites; 52 "spine" texts ranked). Which tradition cites which passage is standard theological knowledge, mostly from memory: verify per citation before publishing. Rights routes are in `sources.md` and the per-area reports._

## 2. Then, the 30 writings to add first (ranked)

Ranking = weight on launch doctrines × number of traditions that cite it × how easily a legitimate full text exists. Spanish is our own translation for nearly all of them.

1. Justin, *First Apology* 61–67 (Eucharist, baptism) — EN READY.
2. Ignatius of Antioch, the seven letters — EN Lightfoot 1891 (Lake from 2027 in Spain).
3. Cyprian, *De unitate* (both versions of ch. 4) + Letters 63, 64, 73–75 — EN READY; **ES 1807 (PD)**.
4. Augustine on purgatory and the saints: *Enchiridion*, *De cura pro mortuis*, *City of God* 21–22, *Confessions* IX — EN READY; ES 1781/1893 PD.
5. Augustine, *The Spirit and the Letter* + the anti-Pelagian set (justification, grace).

9. Calvin's *Tracts* (Sadoleto ↔ Calvin, *Antidote to Trent*, *Short Treatise on the Supper*, *Consensus Tigurinus*) — EN READY, clean OCR.
10. Zwingli on the Supper and on baptism ↔ Hubmaier on believers' baptism.

## 3. Ideas that change how these texts are presented

- **Paired dossiers instead of single works.** The overlooked material is most valuable in pairs where each side answers the other in its own words — which is exactly the site's "never conclude" rule: Paschasius ↔ Ratramnus; Berengar 1059 ↔ 1079; Cyprian's two versions of *De unitate* 4; Sadoleto ↔ Calvin; Erasmus ↔ Luther; Zwingli ↔ Hubmaier; Trent ↔ Calvin's *Antidote* ↔ Chemnitz; Bellarmine ↔ Whitaker; *Confutatio* ↔ Apology; *Apostolicae curae* ↔ *Saepius officio*; Florence ↔ Mark of Ephesus; Leo XIII ↔ the 1895 Encyclical; Jeremias II ↔ Tübingen; Lucaris ↔ Dositheus.
- **"Battleground" patristic passages** — the texts both sides quote (Cyprian *De unitate* 4, 1 Clement 42–44, Nicaea 6, Chalcedon 28, Justin *1 Apol.* 66, Chrysostom *De proditione Judae* 1.6 (cited by both the CCC 1375 and the Formula of Concord SD VII.76), Augustine Sermon 272, Vincent, Basil *Spir.* 27…). A neutral site should show them side by side with each tradition's reading.
- **Short PD documents are cheap to translate ourselves** (Hormisdas, Berengar's oaths, *Benedictus Deus*, *Haec sancta*, *Unigenitus* 1343, Regensburg art. 5, Innocent I, *Sub tuum*, Abercius, Lucaris): 200–3,000 words each, under 25,000 words in total.

## 4. Spanish-speaking voices (no translation needed, high value for the audience)

- **Cipriano de Valera, *Dos tratados: del Papa y de la Misa*** (1588; ed. 1851, clean OCR) — papacy and Mass from the reviser of the Reina-Valera; with Golburne's 1600 English in EEBO-TCP (CC0) → a bilingual text with no OCR on the English side.
- ***Reformistas Antiguos Españoles*** (Usoz & Wiffen, 20 vols, 1847–65, PD): Juan de Valdés, Constantino Ponce de la Fuente, Pérez de Pineda — justification, Scripture.
- **Third Council of Lima** catechisms (1584–85) — the catechism of Catholic Spanish America.
- **Casiodoro de Reina's *Confessión de fe christiana*** (1560/61) — the confession of the Bible del Oso's translator; the 1601 Spanish–German edition is digitised by ULB Halle under Public Domain Mark (`sources/spanish-gaps.md`).
- Catholic Spanish classics: Juan de Ávila's Eucharistic treatises, Luis de Granada, Teresa, Juan de la Cruz, the Astete and Ripalda catechisms, Balmes.
