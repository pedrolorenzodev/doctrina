## Assessment of the report's conclusions

1. The safest core remains defensible as a **risk-managed product proposal**: use short, necessary quotations as evidence inside substantial original analysis; identify source, author, edition, language, and translation; do not reconstruct a source work. The statutes support those recurring factors.
2. The owner-summary sentence that Doctrina “can make a defensible no-permission launch” is not itself `[verified]`. It combines choice of law, territorial acts, contract formation, translation ownership, local procedure, and application to facts. It should be tagged `[inference]` and conditioned on counsel for the named gaps.
3. “Probably” and “Unclear” are useful triage labels, but none is a source fact. Every rating should be `[inference]`, with local case-law gaps kept visible.
4. The report supports no numerical quotation threshold. Options A–C are internal controls only. They should not carry `[verified]` tags merely because the report verified that they are not statutory thresholds.
5. The Vatican no-link clause is real. “Link only after the term is resolved” is a conservative product decision, not a legal conclusion that every link would be enforceably prohibited.
6. Passive browsewrap enforceability is genuinely uncertain, but the report overstates verification by combining a Ninth Circuit consumer case with Spanish incorporation rules without first establishing governing law and territorial application.
7. The two full-text religious-copying cases are useful boundary cases, not close factual matches for short analytic excerpts. They should not be used to imply that nonprofit/free analytic quotation is presumptively risky.
8. No reviewed source verifies the report's global conclusion about translation ownership for every official edition. Edition-page evidence is required document by document.
