## Scope, verdicts and legal gate

This report distinguishes four products: Spanish 66-book, Spanish 73-book Catholic, English 66-book and English 73-book Catholic Bibles. An edition is `FULL TEXT` only when Doctrina can host the whole text without requesting permission; `QUOTATIONS ONLY` means the publisher's standing allowance may cover limited excerpts but not the reader; `PERMISSION` means the desired use exceeds that allowance. Verification verdicts follow `verification-pass.md`: `CONFIRMED`, `WRONG`, `UNCONFIRMABLE`.

The country-by-country term analysis is not repeated here. `catholic-spanish-bibles.md` establishes the primary-law table for the United States, Spain and every Latin American country. It also leaves one owner decision open: Argentina, Uruguay and Bolivia retain statutory payment regimes for some public-domain uses. A work can therefore be copyright-public-domain everywhere yet still fail a product rule that interprets “free” as zero statutory charge. `[CONFIRMED: primary statutes; inference on product rule]`

### Immediate product answer

- Spanish 66 books can launch in full with RV1909. PDDPT is technically complete and openly offered, but its own official package states two incompatible Creative Commons licences; hold it until the licensor clarifies the controlling grant.
- English 66 books can launch in full with BSB and KJV.
- English 73 books can launch in full with DRA 1899 and WEB Catholic. DRA now has a matching dated print witness; WEB Catholic has a complete dated USFM release but its same-date VPL export must be rejected.
- Spanish 73 books have no ready provenance-safe digital production text. Torres Amat 1894 is the first OCR build; `spablm` is only a visibly labelled draft stopgap. The ordinary copyright terms permit Torres Amat and Scío, but the owner must define whether a paying-public-domain regime violates “free everywhere.” `[inference from confirmed evidence]`

### PDDPT licence-conflict verification, 2026-10-23

- The official 66-book USFM archive, with source files dated 2026-10-20, contains both claims in `01-INTspapddpt.usfm`: its Spanish publication paragraph names Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 and requires adaptations to use the same licence; the next metadata paragraph says `Permiso: CC BY` and links CC BY 4.0.
- The current official generated introduction reproduces that source text and then adds eBible's English copyright block declaring CC BY 4.0. The [official catalogue record](https://ebible.org/details.php?id=spapddpt) likewise declares CC BY 4.0 and provides the full text in multiple downloadable formats.
- Internet Archive captures of the official introduction dated 2023-06-02, 2024-07-05 and 2025-07-28 all contain the same BY-SA/BY contradiction. It is therefore neither a current-generation glitch nor merely an unverified print claim.
- The [Digital Bible Society record](https://arc.dbs.org/data/scripture/SPAPDDPT/) identifies the full Protestant canon, the Latinamerican Textual Bible Foundation and Asociación Bíblica Latinoamericana, but says only “Creative Commons license” and does not decide between the two grants. No separate public licensor statement or reproducible print-colophon scan resolving the conflict was found.
- Product rule: do not describe PDDPT simply as CC BY. A conservative fallback would obey CC BY-SA 4.0 because it is the stricter of the two grants, but choosing which conflicting notice controls is a legal-risk decision rather than a textual-research finding. `[CONFIRMED: official current archive plus dated archived official PDFs; product rule is inference]`

### Copyrighted Catholic Spanish translations

This does not mean permission cannot be granted; it means there is no public permission Doctrina can accept without asking. A statutory quotation exception is a separate legal basis and is not a publisher licence.
