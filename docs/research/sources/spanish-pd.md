# spanish-pd — Cross-cutting Spanish full texts of Christian primary sources (PD / open)

Status: DONE (2026-10-09). Agent: spanish-pd.

## 1. Scope and method
Scope: legitimate Spanish full texts for Fathers, councils (light; councils-creeds agent covers them), confessions, catechisms (light; catholic-modern covers Astete/Trent catechism), medieval and Reformation classics; legal frame Spain/Argentina; digital libraries' reuse terms; volunteer projects; AI-translation QC; OCR test on 18th–19th c. Spanish.
Method: official law texts (BOE, Infoleg, Boletín Oficial) read directly; reuse terms read on each library's own page (BNE, BVMC, BVMC Labs, es.wikisource, Memoria Chilena FAQ); catalog discovery via HathiTrust full-view catalog (real Chrome, ~45 queries), archive.org advancedsearch + metadata + djvu text (~25 queries), BNE Digital (Chrome, ~15 queries incl. the ePub line), es.wikisource author/category pages (Chrome; API rate-limited), UANL digital library, EEBO-TCP search; web search for context (budget then exhausted). Hands-on: downloaded 5 page images + 3 small PDFs/EPUB, ran Tesseract (spa / spa_old) with my own scoring script against hand-made ground truth. Councils (Tejada, Trent López de Ayala), catechisms (Astete, Roman Catechism) and Bibles are covered by the councils-creeds, catholic-modern and bible-66 reports — not repeated here.

## 2. Legal frame (VERIFIED on the official consolidated texts, 2026-10-09)

### Spain — TRLPI (RDLeg 1/1996), BOE consolidated text https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930 (saved: samples/spanish-pd/lpi.txt)
- Art. 26: life + 70. **Disposición transitoria cuarta**: "Los derechos de explotación de las obras creadas por autores fallecidos antes del 7 de diciembre de 1987 tendrán la duración prevista en la Ley de 10 de enero de 1879" = life + 80 (the 80-year figure is from the 1879 law art. 6 — memory, unverified on the 1879 text; universally cited). → In 2026, a Spanish-protected author/translator is PD in Spain if he died **in 1945 or earlier** (80 years counted from 1 Jan after death; died 1945 → PD 1 Jan 2026).
- Art. 11: translations are protected works of their own → **the translator's death date governs**, not the Father's.
- Art. 13: no copyright in legal texts, court decisions, acts of public bodies "y las traducciones oficiales" (relevant only to state documents, not to Church documents).
- Art. 32.1 (quotation): fragments, "a título de cita o para su análisis, comentario o juicio crítico", **only "con fines docentes o de investigación"**, with source and author. A paid commercial site is a weak fit; don't rely on it for long passages.
- Art. 41: PD works may be used by anyone respecting authorship and integrity (moral right: don't misattribute, don't mutilate — relevant to how we label modernized spellings and abridgements).
- **Art. 128 "meras fotografías"**: 25 years from the making of the photo/reproduction → a scan made in 2010 by a Spanish library can be protected *as an image* until 2035. Does **not** cover the text we transcribe/OCR from it (the text itself is PD); it covers republishing the scan images.
- **Art. 129.2 + 130.2 (editions of unprotected works)**: publishers of PD works have 25 years from publication over "dichas ediciones siempre que puedan ser individualizadas por su composición tipográfica, presentación y demás características editoriales". 19th-c. editions are long past 25 years → irrelevant for old books; relevant only to modern (post-2001) editions of PD texts (e.g., a 2010 critical re-edition of a 1600 Spanish text). Art. 129.1: whoever first lawfully publishes an unpublished PD work gets 25 years.
- Arts. 133–136: sui generis database right (15 years from creation/substantial update) → a modern digital library's *structured database* can be protected against substantial extraction even if every text in it is PD. This is why each library's own reuse terms matter (see §3).

### Argentina — Ley 11.723 (Infoleg consolidated text https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm, saved samples/spanish-pd/ar11723.html)
- Art. 5: life + **70** from 1 Jan of the following year → in 2026 PD if the author/translator died **in 1955 or earlier**.
- Art. 10: "hasta mil palabras de obras literarias" per quotation "con fines didácticos o científicos, comentarios, críticas o notas", only the parts indispensable.
- Art. 24: the translator of a PD work owns only his version and cannot stop others from translating anew → **our own new translation of a PD original is fully ours.**

### Argentina — "dominio público pagante" (NEW RISK, VERIFIED)
- Decreto-Ley 1224/58 (Fondo Nacional de las Artes) charges a fee for exploiting PD works in Argentina. **FNA Resolución 625/2022 (Boletín Oficial 24-08-2022, https://www.boletinoficial.gob.ar/detalleAviso/primera/270121/20220824, saved samples/spanish-pd/fna625.txt) added "Rubro 12.3. Derechos de edición – Entorno digital: 1. De obras literarias que se pongan a disposición, transmitan, difundan y/o comuniquen al público en el entorno digital ... Arancel: tres (3) MÓDULOS. El arancel aquí dispuesto deberá ser abonado anualmente y por cada obra puesta a disposición del público."**
- Art. 14 of that resolution: MÓDULO = value in art. 28 of Decreto 1030/2016 → **ARS 40,000** per Infoleg (Decreto 666/2024; https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/265000-269999/265506/texact.htm). So **ARS 120,000 per PD work per year** if it applies. Exemption (art. 31) only for primary/secondary school texts.
- Open questions for a lawyer (NOT answered here): (a) does it reach a site operated from Argentina but hosted in the US and run by a non-Argentine entity? (b) what counts as one "obra" (Augustine's whole corpus vs each treatise vs a single council's canons)? (c) does it apply to our own new translation (a new protected work, not a PD work) — by the text, a new translation is not "caída en el dominio público", so probably not; the Spanish *original* PD texts (Teresa, Granada, a 1790 translation) would be. (d) enforcement practice against websites (Wikisource / Cervantes Virtual / BNE are read in Argentina without paying; FNA collects mostly from publishers/broadcasters — memory, unverified). **This is a business/legal item for Pedro, not a blocker for research.** Note: it argues in favour of AI-assisted *new* translations (Art. 24) over republishing old PD Spanish translations, if the fee turns out to apply.

### US (hosting)
- Published before 1931 → PD in the US in 2026. A faithful transcription of a PD text gets no new US copyright. Spanish 18th–19th c. books are safe in the US.

### Practical rule for Spanish translations (all three jurisdictions)
A Spanish translation is safe everywhere when: **published before 1931 AND translator died ≤ 1945** (Spain is the binding constraint). For anonymous translations published before 1931: Spain art. 27 = 70 years from publication → PD.

## 4. OCR feasibility test (hands-on, VERIFIED; files in samples/spanish-pd/ocr/)

Method: page images fetched one page at a time (HathiTrust full-view via the real Chrome; IA BookReader page JPG). Hand-made ground truth (GT) of 114–172 words per page (`*.gt.txt`), scored with my own Levenshtein script (`samples/spanish-pd/ocreval.py`: lower-case, punctuation stripped, ſ→s, de-hyphenated, fuzzy alignment). Tesseract 5.5.3 with `spa`, `spa_old` (Tesseract's model for historical Spanish, includes ſ) and `spa+spa_old`; preprocessing = grayscale + autocontrast + 2× upscale + optional global threshold (PIL, `samples/spanish-pd/prep.py`).

I.14.8, on angels) | roman, **heavy long-s (ſ)**, ligatures, accents à/á, marginal refs | `spa_old`, thresholded | **3.9 %** | 19.5 % | `spa` alone: 6.4–7.1 % CER / 31–35 % WER. **IA's own djvu OCR of the same page: 12.0 % CER / 42.9 % WER** → redo OCR ourselves, don't use IA text for pre-1700 books. Conclusions:
- 18th–19th c. Spanish print: **~1–2 % CER** with Tesseract after thresholding + layout cleanup; i.e., ~1 error per 60–100 characters, mostly accents and punctuation. Fit for an LLM-assisted correction pass (OCR text + page image → corrected text) followed by human spot-check; fully feasible at S–M effort per volume.
- 16th–early-17th c. print with long-s: **~4 % CER** with `spa_old` (vs 12 % in IA's stock OCR). Needs an image-aware correction pass (vision LLM or human) — M–L effort per volume — but works.
- Toronto/Michigan scans on IA (e.g., BAE `bibliotecadeautoNNmadruoft`, Reformistas `per_reformistas-*`) already have usable stock OCR (Usoz 1858 sample: ~97–98 % character accuracy by eye; typical errors "4"→"á", "Sefior/Sehor"→"Señor").
- **HathiTrust dates can lie**: record 100211100 "Imitación de Cristo, Barcelona [1753]" is visibly a 20th-c. reprint (modern type, "según", modern accents). Always check the title page/typography before assuming PD by date.

## 6. Creative alternatives and workarounds

1. **BNE is the master key for Spain-printed PD books**: CC BY 4.0 for commercial use (verified), many records carry an ABBYY OCR layer (Summa 1880 verified), and the BNE ePub line gives corrected text (Tesoro de místicos verified). Workflow: search BNE Digital (https://bnedigital.bne.es/bd/es/simple; filter "Derechos"), download per-volume PDFs/ePubs, attribute "Biblioteca Nacional de España" (images) — text itself is PD.
2. **HathiTrust full-view as a finder for Spanish 18th–19th c. translations**: the Complutense–Google scans (`ucm.*`) cover most Spanish religious printing 1700–1850. Page images fetchable one by one from the browser session; full-volume PDF download needs a member login (we don't log in) → for bulk, find the same Google scan on Google Books / IA, or the BNE copy.
3. **Self-OCR beats stock OCR** for pre-1800 print: Tesseract `spa_old` + threshold gave 3.9 % CER vs IA's 12 % on Valera 1597 (§4).
4. **Spanish originals instead of translations**: for Reformed (Valera, Pérez, Constantino, Valdés) and Catholic (Ávila, Granada, León, Teresa, Juan de la Cruz) voices Doctrina can quote *native Spanish primary sources* — no translation, PD, scans + OCR available. These double as overlooked high-value writings (Ávila's 27 Eucharistic treatises; Valera's *Dos tratados* on papacy & Mass).
5. **English counterpart via EEBO-TCP (CC0)**: Golburne's 1600 English of Valera's *Two Treatises* is transcribed in EEBO-TCP → bilingual Valera with zero OCR.
6. **Second-hand PD translations as stopgap** (Gourcy→Ximeno 1792 apologists; Garnier→Duarte 1796 Basil): legal now; label "traducción castellana de 1792 sobre la versión francesa de Gourcy"; replace later with our own from Greek.
7. **es.wikisource as a collaboration channel**: its index pages for Valera 1597, Díaz de Beyral 1893, BAE 6/8/53/55 already exist with scans loaded but untranscribed. If Doctrina proofreads there, the community benefits — but **user contributions on es.wikisource are CC BY-SA 4.0** (footer + Wikisource:Traducciones: "toda obra traducida por los usuarios de Wikisource se encuentra publicada bajo la licencia Creative Commons"). Transcriptions of PD texts are arguably not copyrightable; *our own translations* posted there would be BY-SA (allows commercial use but forces share-alike on the translation) → keep our translations off Wikisource unless Pedro wants BY-SA.
8. **AI-assisted translation from PD originals (the only route for Didache, Justin Trypho, Irenaeus, Chrysostom homilies, Cyril, Theodoret, Damascene, Cabasilas, Philaret, Mogila, Lutheran confessions…)**. Legal basis: Argentina Ley 11.723 art. 24 (the translator of a PD work owns his version; nobody can stop new translations); Spain art. 11 (translation = own work). Practice elsewhere: Roger Pearse publishes AI-drafted translations of patristic texts with explicit "draft" framing and tests on rare languages, warning about failure modes (https://www.roger-pearse.com/weblog/2024/02/01/methodius-ad-theodorum-bhg-1352y-part-4-a-draft-translation-using-ai/ ; https://www.roger-pearse.com/weblog/2024/01/19/the-perils-of-ai-translation/ — titles/dates via WebFetch of his blog search, not read in full). **Recommended QC pipeline (my proposal, not an external standard)**:
   a. Source = PD edition named per passage (PG/PL/GCS/CSEL pre-1931; or Lake/Open Greek & Latin when licence fits); keep source segmentation (chapter.section) as the alignment key.
   b. Draft with an LLM from the original, giving it the PD English (ANF/NPNF) and any PD Spanish as *reference*, plus a fixed theological glossary (ὁμοούσιος→consustancial, μυστήριον→misterio/sacramento per tradition, sacrificium, transsubstantiatio, etc.).
   c. Automatic checks: segment-by-segment length ratio (omissions), Scripture references resolved against the original, proper names list, glossary compliance, numbers.
   d. Independent second-model review that flags divergences against the original (not against the draft), then back-translation sampling.
   e. Human review: 100 % of passages quoted on doctrine pages; sampling elsewhere; reviewer named.
   f. Label in UI: "Traducción de Doctrina (asistida por IA; revisada por …) a partir de [edición]", version/date; unreviewed = *borrador* (fits the "anything unverified is marked draft" invariant).
   g. If the Argentine FNA fee applies to PD works, our own translations are *new* works (not PD) → they fall outside "dominio público pagante" (by the text of Res. 625/2022, my reading, lawyer to confirm).
9. **Permission paths** (when a modern © Spanish translation is clearly better): BAC (Ruiz Bueno's *Padres Apostólicos*, *Padres apologistas*; Spanish Summa), Ciudad Nueva (*Biblioteca de Patrística*, *Fuentes Patrísticas*), FELiRe (Institución revision), Editorial Concordia/CPH (Libro de Concordia, Meléndez 1989, "all rights reserved", https://s3.amazonaws.com/cph-org-assets/media/pdf/161009.pdf per search snippet), BVMC (its HTML transcriptions; "Solicitud de contenidos" form on https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/marco-legal/). Draft request (Spanish, adapt per publisher):

> Asunto: Solicitud de licencia de uso — [obra/traducción] en Doctrina
> Estimados/as: Somos Doctrina, un recurso bilingüe (es/en) de estudio de la doctrina cristiana que presenta cada tradición con sus propias fuentes, citadas con edición y licencia. Quisiéramos publicar en nuestro sitio web [el texto completo / los pasajes X–Y] de [obra], en la traducción de [traductor] publicada por ustedes en [año], con atribución completa y enlace a su edición. El sitio [es gratuito en esta etapa / tendrá un plan de suscripción]. ¿Podrían indicarnos si es posible una licencia (gratuita o remunerada), sus condiciones (alcance territorial, duración, formato de atribución) y la persona de contacto? Quedamos a disposición para enviar más detalles. Atentamente, [nombre, contacto].

10. **Quotation exceptions** (fallback for © Spanish translations): Argentina art. 10 up to 1,000 words per quotation, didactic/scientific, only indispensable parts; Spain art. 32.1 only "con fines docentes o de investigación" → weak for a commercial product; use for short critical quotations only, never for the reader.
