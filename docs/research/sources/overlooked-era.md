# overlooked-era — Writings of 2000 years NOT yet in the registry (judgment report)

## 1. Scope and method
- Baseline checked in the repo: `data/site/sources.extra.json` (44 works) and `data/site/history.json` (56 Fathers, 41 councils, 18 heresies, 20 Reformation branches). "Overlooked" = the work itself is not in the 44-entry registry (a Father may be on the list while his key work is not, e.g. Leo the Great → Tome).
- Cross-read other agents' reports (councils-creeds, catholic-modern, protestant-magisterial, free-church, orthodox, greek-fathers, latin-fathers) so this list points to them for availability details instead of repeating them.
- Selection criterion: does the text get *cited by the traditions themselves* when they argue a launch doctrine (Eucharist, justification, papacy, sola Scriptura, Mary, baptism, purgatory, saints, canon, church authority)? Priority = (doctrinal weight) × (how many traditions cite it, for or against) × (how feasible a full legitimate text is).
- Rights column is a rough first pass. Ancient/medieval originals are PD everywhere; what matters is the translation. Modern (post-1931) documents were checked on the publisher's page where marked VERIFIED.
- Running log first (raw), ranked tables after.

## 4. Creative alternatives and workarounds found
1. **EEBO-TCP / Evans-TCP (CC0, hand-keyed)** for everything printed in English before 1700 (and American imprints before 1800): Cranmer, Jewel, Hooker, Homilies, Perkins, Ussher, Rheims NT notes + Wither's reply, 1536 Augsburg/Apology, Ratramnus 1688 (Latin + EN), 1 Clement 1647, Vincent, Luther in Elizabethan English, Second Helvetic 1568, Remonstrant texts, Edwards. Evidence: per-file `<availability>` CC0 (log L2, L5). Better than OCR; needs gap-filling and a spelling-normalisation layer.
2. **19th-century translation societies are all PD and on archive.org**: Calvin Translation Society (Beveridge *Tracts*, 1844–51, verified contents), Parker Society (Whitaker 1849, Jewel, Cranmer, Bullinger…), Wace & Buchheim 1883 (Luther 1520 treatises), Schaff's *Creeds* (cov. councils-creeds), Thatcher & McNeal 1905 (medieval papal documents), Reu 1930 (Confutatio), Brightman 1896 (Eastern liturgies incl. Addai & Mari), M. R. James 1924 (apocrypha incl. Protevangelium).
3. **"Paired dossiers" instead of single works**: the overlooked material is most valuable in pairs that let each tradition speak in its own words on the same question — Paschasius ↔ Ratramnus; Berengar 1059 ↔ 1079; Cyprian's two versions of *De unitate* 4; Sadoleto ↔ Calvin; Erasmus ↔ Luther; Zwingli ↔ Hubmaier; Trent ↔ Calvin's Antidote ↔ Chemnitz; Bellarmine ↔ Whitaker; *Confutatio* ↔ Apology; *Apostolicae curae* ↔ *Saepius officio*; Florence ↔ Mark of Ephesus; Leo XIII ↔ Patriarchal Encyclical 1895; Jeremias II ↔ Tübingen; Lucaris ↔ Dositheus. This fits the "never conclude" rule: the site sets texts side by side.
4. **Short Latin/Greek documents → own translation is cheap** (Hormisdas, Berengar's oaths, *Benedictus Deus*, *Haec sancta*, *Unigenitus* 1343, Regensburg art. 5, Innocent I, Sub tuum, Abercius, Lucaris): each is 200–3,000 words, PD original, translation labelled as Doctrina's. Total < 25k words.
5. **Pre-1931 publication date as the key**: BF&M 1925, AG 1916, Reu 1930, Coleman 1922, Robinson 1920, James 1924, Zwingli *Latin Works* 1912/1922/1929, Thompson 1919, Vassall-Phillips 1917, Connolly 1929, Driver–Hodgson 1925 are all US-PD now — but check the translator's death year before serving them in Spain (life+80 for deaths before 7 Dec 1987) and Argentina (life+70).
6. **Spanish PD originals that nobody uses**: Valera's *Dos tratados*, *Reformistas Antiguos Españoles*, Third Lima catechism (1584), Bellarmine's catechism in Spanish/Quechua (1649), Balmes. They give a Spanish-speaking reader authentic period voices instead of translations.
7. **Holy See's own law** (cov. catholic-modern F3: 70 years from publication / author's death) makes all pre-1939 papal documents PD at source — includes *Ineffabilis Deus*, *Apostolicae curae*, *Satis cognitum*, *Pascendi*, *Mortalium animos*.
8. **Positive licences hiding in plain sight**: US BCP 1979 (publisher: "in the public domain"), Cape Town Commitment (free reproduction, unchanged, with acknowledgement).
