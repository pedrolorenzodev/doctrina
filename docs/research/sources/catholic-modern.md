### F3. Vatican City copyright law n. CXCVII (1 Sep 2017, in force 1 Oct 2017) — KEY for term of protection (VERIFIED, full text fetched)
URL: https://www.ulsa.va/it/pubblicazioni/bollettini/bollettino-n--26---2018/atti-del-governatorato/legge-n--cxcvii-sulla-protezione-del-diritto-d-autore-sulle-oper.html (saved samples/catholic-modern/vat-law-197.txt). Replaces law n. CXXXII of 19 Mar 2011.
- Art. 1: Italian copyright law applies suppletively (incl. future amendments).
- Art. 2: copyright applies also to laws and official acts of the Holy See and SCV (unlike most states, where official texts are free).
- Art. 3 §1: writings and speeches of the Roman Pontiff are protected "salvo quanto Egli voglia disporre in casi specifici".
- Art. 5 §1–2: Holy See (Pope, Dicasteries, Curia, connected institutions) owns copyright in works "created or published under their name or made on their behalf".
- **Art. 5 §4: economic rights of these entities last "settanta anni a partire dall'anno di prima pubblicazione dell'opera ... ovvero dall'anno di morte dell'autore ove questi sia indicato nell'opera".** => The "in perpetuo" of the 2005 decree is about who exercises rights, not the term. Under the Holy See's OWN law, its documents fall into the public domain 70 years after publication (or after the named author's death).
- Practical reading (my analysis, not legal advice): a papal document names its author (the Pope) → conservative term = Pope's death year + 70. Leo XIII (d.1903), Pius X (1914), Benedict XV (1922), Pius XI (1939) → PD at source. Pius XII (d. 1958) → conservatively PD from 1 Jan 2029 (by publication-year reading, docs published ≤1955 already PD). John XXIII (d.1963) → 2034. Paul VI (d.1978) → 2049. Curial documents without a named author (e.g. Holy Office decrees) → publication + 70.
- Caveat: this is the term at SOURCE. In Argentina/Spain/US the law of the country where protection is claimed governs (Berne art. 5(2)); see jurisdiction analysis in §Analysis below. Translations made by others (e.g. 1900s English translations) have their own copyright.

### F4. USCCB guidelines for using the CCC text (US English, US Spanish, US French) (VERIFIED, page rendered in Chrome incl. collapsed sections; saved samples/catholic-modern/usccb-ccc-use.txt)
URL: https://www.usccb.org/committees/catechism/use-catechism-catholic-church ("Revised Statement of Principles and Guidelines for Use of the CCC"; approved by APSA 6 Sep 1996, revised Apr 2015)
- "No edition of the CCC, in any language, may be published in the United States ... without the written permission of the USCCB." Excerpts of EN/ES/FR texts may be used in the US only per these guidelines.
- Scholarly use (dissertations, articles, course materials) exempt if quotes acknowledged.
- I. General: "The text may not be published in parts or in serialized editions"; only translations approved for the US; excerpts complete and accurate; ellipses marked; footnoted material identified; mandatory copyright notice (exact wording on page; EN: "...Copyright © 1994, United States Catholic Conference, Inc. -- Libreria Editrice Vaticana. Used with Permission. ... Modifications from the Editio Typica copyright © 1997 ..."; ES (US Spanish): © 1993 USCC–LEV ...).
- **II. Fewer than 5,000 words, commercial or non-commercial: no permission needed** if section I is followed (incl. notice).
- III. Non-commercial >5,000 words: written permission, submit the work with excerpts highlighted, review by USCCB Communications + Subcommittee on the Catechism, 3 free copies; no royalties.
- **IV. Commercial >5,000 words: written permission + pro-rated royalty "calculated on the basis of ten percent (10%) of the list price"**, 3 copies to USCCB and 3 to the Holy See.
=> For Doctrina: (a) quoting individual CCC paragraphs inside doctrine pages, under 5,000 words TOTAL per "work" (ambiguous whether "work" = whole site or a page; conservative = whole site), is allowed with the notice — this covers the US English text. (b) A full CCC reader = "commercial use of more than 5,000 words" → permission + 10% royalty (how computed for a subscription website is undefined → must negotiate). (c) The 5,000-word rule is a USCCB policy for use in the US; it does not license the Spanish text published in Spain (Asociación de Editores del Catecismo) for Spain/LatAm readers.

### F7. Jurisdiction keys — rule of the shorter term (VERIFIED on official legal texts; useful for the WHOLE project, not only this area)
- **Argentina, Ley 11.723 art. 15** (InfoLEG consolidated text https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm, fetched): "La protección que la Ley argentina acuerda a los autores extranjeros, no se extenderá a un período mayor que el reconocido por las Leyes del país donde se hubiere publicado la obra." => A work first published in the US that is PD in the US (e.g. published before 1931) is not protected in Argentina either, even if the translator died < 70 years ago. Same logic for works first published in Vatican City (Vatican 70-year term).
  Also art. 8: anonymous works belonging to institutions/legal persons: 50 years from publication. Art. 10: quotation up to 1,000 words for didactic/scientific commentary ("sólo las partes del texto indispensables").
- **Spain, TRLPI art. 199.4** (BOE consolidated https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930, fetched): "Para las obras cuyo país de origen sea con arreglo al Convenio de Berna un país tercero y cuyo autor no sea nacional de un Estado miembro de la UE, el plazo de protección será el mismo que el otorgado en el país de origen de la obra sin que en ningún caso pueda exceder del previsto en esta ley". => Same effect for US-origin works by US authors (EU Term Directive art. 7(1)).
  Art. 13: "No son objeto de propiedad intelectual las disposiciones legales o reglamentarias ... y los actos, acuerdos, deliberaciones y dictámenes de los organismos públicos, así como las traducciones oficiales" — whether this reaches acts of a foreign sovereign like the Holy See is untested (my hypothesis only; do NOT rely on it). Art. 32: quotation for teaching/research, "en la medida justificada", with source. Art. 129.2: 25-year publisher's right in editions of unprotected works "individualizadas por su composición tipográfica" (protects the typeset edition, not the text: retyping/OCR into our own layout is the usual reading — verify with counsel).
- Consequence for this area: the US status of an English translation drives its status in AR and ES as long as the translator is a US national and the first publication was in the US (e.g. McHugh & Callan 1923, Baltimore Catechism, Benziger's 1903 "Great Encyclical Letters of Leo XIII"). For a US work by a non-US author, or first published in the UK/Ireland (e.g. Donovan, Dublin 1829), use life+70/80 at origin — trivially PD for 19th-c. translators.

### F10. Papal encyclicals — what is PD and where the PD text lives
Legal frame (my analysis from F3/F7; mark: needs counsel before relying on edge cases):
- Latin originals: Vatican law CXCVII art. 5 §4 → 70 years from publication or from the named author's death. Commons already treats them this way: `File:Pius XI - Casti connubii (Acta Apostolicae Sedis).pdf` is tagged {{PD-old-auto|deathyear=1939}} (checked wikitext via API).
- **Tier A — PD everywhere (US/AR/ES/VA): everything published before 1931** (Pius IX, Leo XIII, Pius X, Benedict XV, Pius XI up to 1930: e.g. Ineffabilis Deus 1854, Pastor Aeternus is a council text, Satis cognitum 1896, Providentissimus Deus 1893, Mirae caritatis 1902, Pascendi 1907, Mortalium animos 1928, Casti connubii 31 Dec 1930). US: published before 1931. VA/AR/ES: pope died ≥70 years ago.
- **Tier B — Pius XI 1931–1939** (Quadragesimo anno 1931, Mit brennender Sorge 1937, Divini Redemptoris 1937, Ad catholici sacerdotii 1935): PD at source (d. 1939), PD in AR/ES by shorter-term rules. US: likely PD — URAA (1996) restores only works still protected at source on 1 Jan 1996; Vatican's 1960 law received Italian law as it stood (static; the 2011 law notes the change to dynamic reception — Radio Vaticana 2011 https://www.archivioradiovaticana.va/storico/2011/03/30/nuova_legge_vaticana_sul_diritto_dautore_per_tutelare_l%E2%80%99autenticit%C3%A0/it1-474124, not fetched) and Italian law in 1960 was life+50 → Pius XI works PD at source by 1990 → not restored. (Hypothesis — verify with a US copyright lawyer; worst case they are PD in the US on 1 Jan of publication year + 96, i.e. 2027–2035.)
- **Tier C — Pius XII (d. 1958)**: Mystici Corporis 1943, Divino afflante Spiritu 1943, Mediator Dei 1947, Humani generis 1950, Munificentissimus Deus 1950. At source / AR / ES: conservatively PD from **1 Jan 2029** (death+70). US: probably restored by URAA (protected at source in 1996) → US term = publication + 95 (2039–2054). => For a Vercel/US-hosted site: quote only (fair use) until a licence or 2039+; for AR/ES audiences full text OK from 2029 if geofenced hosting were ever considered (not recommended).

## 3. Creative alternatives found
1. **Vatican's own law gives a 70-year term** (law CXCVII 2017 art. 5 §4) → combined with AR art. 15 and ES art. 199.4 (rule of the shorter term, verified texts), every papal Latin text whose Pope died ≥70 years ago is free in VA/AR/ES; the US layer adds the pre-1931 rule + URAA analysis.
2. **Rule of the shorter term for US translations**: a US-published, US-PD translation (McHugh–Callan 1923, Benziger 1903, Lasance 1924, Woywod 1925) is also free in Argentina and Spain, even when the translator died less than 70/80 years ago.
3. **Zorita 1819 bilingual Roman Catechism**: free Latin–Spanish alignment; tesseract per column ≈99% Spanish.
4. **Boyne Archives** (archive.org, CC0/PDM) AI English drafts of all ASS and AAS volumes → starting draft for translating pre-1931 papal acts (human review mandatory).
