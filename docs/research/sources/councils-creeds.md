## 1. Scope and method
Scope: creeds (Apostles', Nicene 325/381, Athanasian, Chalcedon), ecumenical councils 1–7, Catholic councils 8–21, Orthodox later synods (879–880, Palamite 1341–1351, Jerusalem 1672, Encyclical 1848, Constantinople 1872), regional councils (Carthage, Orange, Toledo III, Elvira, Arles), plus collections (NPNF 14, Schroeder, Tanner, Waterworth, López de Ayala, Denzinger, Mansi, Hefele, Tejada y Ramiro).
Method: archive.org advancedsearch/metadata/djvu text, Google Books API (filter=full), HathiTrust full-text catalog, Stanford Copyright Renewal DB, publisher terms pages, hands-on download + pdftotext/tesseract tests in samples/councils-creeds/.

### Vatican II and Holy See texts — LEGAL (VERIFIED on primary texts)
- Vatican City copyright law: **Legge N. CXCVII, 1 Sept 2017, in force 1 Oct 2017** (PDF fetched: https://www.vaticanstate.va/phocadownload/leggi-decreti/normativa-altre-materie/Legge%20N.%20CXCVII%20All.%2025.pdf ; text in samples/councils-creeds/vat_law_cxcvii.txt). Key articles: Art. 2 — copyright rules "si applicano anche ai testi delle leggi e degli atti ufficiali pubblicati, in qualunque forma, dalla Santa Sede" (i.e., unlike Italy, official acts ARE protected); Art. 3 §1 — writings and speeches of the Pope protected; Art. 5 §1 — copyright of works "create o pubblicate sotto il loro nome o realizzate per loro conto" belongs to Holy See/SCV; **Art. 5 §4 — economic rights last 70 years from the year of first publication** (or from author's death if the author is named). ⇒ Under Vatican law, Vatican II documents (1963–1965) stay protected until ~2034–2036; Vatican I (1870), Trent etc. long expired.
- LEV mandate: Decree of Card. Sodano, 31 May 2005 (https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/institutions_connected/lev/docs_lev/copyright_it.htm, fetched EN version): LEV holds "permanently and throughout the world, every moral copyright and all the exclusive financial rights — without any exception — over all the deeds and documents through which the Supreme Pontiff exercises his own Magisterium". 1978 notice: no "pubblicazioni, traduzioni o riproduzioni in qualsiasi forma" without LEV's explicit authorisation. No published free-use/quotation policy found on vatican.va or LEV's site.
- Permission contact (fetched https://www.libreriaeditricevaticana.va/en/content/16-foreign-rights): Foreign Rights Office, Dr. Francesca Angeletti, **diritti.lev@spc.va**, tel. +39 06 698 45363 — "manages the worldwide copyright of the texts of the Pontiffs, the Dicasteries of the Roman Curia, and the Vatican Institutions".
- Real cases: Jan 2006 LEV billed an Italian publisher ~€15,000 for a book of Benedict XVI texts (catholicculture.org, https://www.catholicculture.org/news/features/index.cfm?recnum=41978 ; UPI https://www.upi.com/Top_News/2006/01/24/Vatican-Papal-encyclicals-copyrighted/98141138106315/). LEV then denied it would charge for quotations in journals (same article, paraphrase). Enforcement targets publishers, mostly in Italy.
- Jurisdiction notes (not legal advice; to verify with a lawyer):
  - **Argentina, Ley 11.723 art. 8** (fetched infoleg text): "La propiedad intelectual de las obras anónimas pertenecientes a instituciones, corporaciones o personas jurídicas, durará cincuenta años contados desde su publicación." + art. 15: foreign protection can't exceed the origin country's term. If Vatican II texts count as anonymous institutional works, their Argentine protection would have ended 50 years after publication (i.e. 2014–2016). Arguable, untested; the Pope signs the promulgation, so "anonymous" is contestable. Also only Argentina; Vercel hosting in the US means US law governs the US-side act.
  - **Spain**: LPI art. 13 (VERIFIED, BOE consolidated text https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930): "No son objeto de propiedad intelectual las disposiciones legales o reglamentarias y sus correspondientes proyectos, las resoluciones de los órganos jurisdiccionales y los actos, acuerdos, deliberaciones y dictámenes de los organismos públicos, así como las traducciones oficiales de todos los textos anteriores." Whether it reaches acts of a foreign sovereign / the Holy See is unclear (no case found). Anonymous works: art. 27.1, 70 years from lawful disclosure; collective works art. 28.2, 70 years from disclosure. Art. 129.2/130.2: publishers of non-protected works get 25 years over the edition "siempre que puedan ser individualizadas por su composición tipográfica, presentación y demás características editoriales" → protects facsimile reproduction of a recent edition, not a re-typeset PD text (our reading). Art. 128: "meras fotografías" 25 years from the photo (relevant if we ever show page images of scans made <25 years ago — e.g. Google scans from 2006–2010 are protected in Spain until 2031–2035 under this reading).
  - **US** (VERIFIED, Compendium III ch. 300, §313.6(C)(2), https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap300/ch300-copyrightable-authorship.pdf): "the Office will not register a government edict issued by any foreign government or any translation prepared by a government employee acting within the course of his or her official duties" (after Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 2020). The Holy See is a sovereign recognised by the US; whether an ecumenical council's constitutions (or the Holy See's own official translations) are "government edicts" is untested — a creative argument for a lawyer, not something to rely on.
  - Translations: vatican.va Spanish/English versions are separate translations owned by LEV (or by bishops' conferences); Abbott 1966 / Flannery 1975 English are © (memory, unverified).
- VERDICT Vatican II: PERMISSION (LEV) for full text in es/en; meanwhile short quotations (Argentina art. 10: up to 1,000 words per quote for teaching/criticism; Spain art. 32 "cita") + deep link to vatican.va. Draft request email: see section 7.

## 3. Creative alternatives and workarounds found
1. **Wikisource as a contract-free mirror of CCEL-quality text**: NPNF14 (whole volume, canon-level pages) and Buckley's Trent (session-level pages) are on en.wikisource — PD text without CCEL's/Hanover's non-commercial terms.
2. **Renewal research unlocks 1937–1941 translations**: Schroeder's *Disciplinary Decrees* (1937) and *Trent* (1941) show no renewal (Stanford DB + manual grep of the 1964, 1965, 1968, 1969 CCE scans), HathiTrust marks Schroeder's Trent **pdus**, and Schroeder died 1942 so life-based terms in AR/ES have run out too → usable everywhere.
3. **Argentine and Spanish 19th-c. printings of Vatican I** (Goyena's *Digesto eclesiástico argentino*, 1880) give a PD Spanish Pastor Aeternus, nice for the AR audience.
4. **Tejada y Ramiro** gives a parallel Latin–Spanish text not only of Spanish councils but of the Hispana's general councils (Nicaea–Chalcedon, Carthage, Arles) and Spanish chapters on councils V–VIII.
5. ***Los sacrosantos concilios generales y particulares* (1793–96)**: PD Spanish for medieval councils 8–18 (via Richard's French) — fills the es gap for Lateran IV, Lyon, Constance, Florence.
6. **Tesseract grc on 19th-c. prints works**, so Greek originals (Schaff, Robertson, Mansi) can be produced in-house; IA's Greek OCR is useless.
7. **Vatican II legal angles to put to a lawyer** (not to rely on): Argentina Ley 11.723 art. 8 (50 years for anonymous institutional works); US Compendium §313.6(C)(2) (foreign government edicts not registrable); Spain LPI art. 13 (official acts). Practical route remains LEV permission; LEV has granted web permission before (papalencyclicals.net).
8. **Physical copy route** for PD-but-access-restricted scans (Schroeder Trent 1941, Rudder 1957): buy a used copy (TAN reprint is photographic) and scan it ourselves; or a US-based collaborator downloads HathiTrust pdus pages. Not: VPN tricks.
