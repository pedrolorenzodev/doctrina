## Decision boundary

Doctrina's rule is stricter than ordinary public-domain analysis: full text must be free in the United States, Spain, and every Latin American country. Both translators died well before the binding 1945 cutoff identified in the existing legal audit, but Argentina, Uruguay, and Bolivia retain public-domain payment regimes. Whether “free” in the product decision excludes a text that is copyright-public-domain but carries one of those statutory levies is a legal/business choice, not silently assumed here.

## Evidence already established

### Félix Torres Amat

- **Translation and edition:** the translation first appeared in Madrid in 1823–1825. The preferred OCR witness is *La Sagrada Biblia*, Barcelona: Subirana Hermanos, 1894, second edition “corregida con esmero”, two volumes. Internet Archive provides dated scans and OCR for the Old Testament (including the Catholic deuterocanon) and New Testament. `[verified]` [Old Testament scan](https://archive.org/details/lasagradabiblia0000dfel); [New Testament scan](https://archive.org/details/lasagradabiblia0000dfel_f1h6).
- **Translator and copyright baseline:** Félix Torres Amat died in 1847. On the country terms already checked against the statutes in `sources/legal-gaps.md`, that clears even the strictest 2026 death-year cutoff (Spain, Colombia, Panama: 1945; Mexico's transition: 1951). The 1894 edition is also pre-1931 in the United States. `[verified]`
- **Digital text:** no complete, provenance-safe digital edition with a reusable licence was found in the prior exhaustive search. Circulating modules and websites modernise the language, omit the deuterocanon, give no reliable provenance, or identify a modern “actualizada” edition. Wikisource has proofread portions of a different 1836 edition under CC BY-SA, but its Old Testament and deuterocanon are largely absent. Doctrina can use only its own scan-backed OCR of the 1894 print, not those ready-made files. `[verified]`
- **Contents:** the seven Catholic deuterocanonical books and the Greek portions of Esther and Daniel are present. It omits 3–4 Maccabees; no Clementine appendix (Prayer of Manasseh, 3–4 Esdras) was located in the checked editions. `[verified]`
- **Numbering:** Vulgate numbering. The test Psalm is printed as Psalm 96:7, which corresponds to RV1909 Psalm 97:7. `[verified: OCR]`

### Felipe Scío de San Miguel

- **Translation and editions:** first edition, Valencia, 1790–1793; second edition, Madrid, 1794–1797. Complete Catholic editions print the Latin Vulgate and Spanish in parallel with notes. The 1852–1854 Gaspar y Roig edition is a simpler Spanish-only OCR target; the Cambridge 1869 Bible Society edition is a clean protocanonical witness but omits the deuterocanon and therefore cannot supply 2 Maccabees. `[verified]`
- **Translator and copyright baseline:** Felipe Scío de San Miguel died in 1796, clearing every national term in the existing 2026 audit; the relevant editions are also pre-1931 in the United States. `[verified]`
- **Digital text:** CrossWire's `SpaScioNT` is a public-domain SWORD module for the second-edition New Testament, released 2026-03-22, but no complete digital Old Testament was found in CrossWire, eBible, GitHub, Gutenberg, Wikisource, or the earlier archive search. A full site edition therefore requires scan-backed OCR. `[verified]`
- **Contents:** Catholic editions contain the seven Catholic deuterocanonical books and Greek Esther/Daniel. Scío explicitly omits 3–4 Maccabees and 3–4 Esdras because the Catholic Church did not receive them as Scripture; no Prayer of Manasseh was found. `[verified]`

Doctrina must store and display each edition's own verse boundaries rather than splice Scío's two printed verses into a synthetic verse 7. `[verified: scan; inference]`

## Other pre-1946 candidates screened

### Vence/Galván Mexican Bible, 1831–1833

- **Edition and relationship:** *Sagrada Biblia en latín y español, con notas literales, críticas é históricas, prefacios y disertaciones…*, first Mexican edition, Mexico City: Galván, 1831–1833, 25 volumes. The Portal digital de Historia de la traducción en España describes its biblical text as a slight adaptation of Torres Amat, translated through the 1820 fourth French Vence edition; it is therefore a distinct historical edition but not a genuinely independent Spanish textual family. [Scholarly overview](https://phte.upf.edu/dhte/castellano-siglo-xix/sanchez-caro-2/) `[verified]`
- **Dated complete scan:** HathiTrust exposes all 25 volumes as full view under [catalog record 007328615](https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/007328615), each from the University of California; Google Books exposes the same complete set with scanned text. Internet Archive has only isolated volumes. `[verified]`
- **Copyright:** the named French contributors, Augustin Calmet (d. 1757) and Henri-François de Vence (d. 1749), are ancient enough under every term. The Mexican adaptation's contributors are not exhaustively named in the print metadata inspected, but publication in 1831–1833 is beyond every anonymous/corporate publication term in the country table as well as the US pre-1931 line. The underlying edition text is PD throughout the target territory. `[verified; inference]`
- **Canon:** the edition calls the Maccabean books canonical and prints 2 Maccabees in volume 18; the inspected page contains 2 Mac 15 itself. This directly confirms the relevant Catholic deuterocanon rather than inferring it from the title. `[verified: scan]`
- **Digital-text layer:** Google supplies OCR inside its reader and HathiTrust supplies the page scans, but neither discovery copy is treated as a reusable licensed digital edition. Doctrina would need its own scan-backed transcription/OCR, retaining volume and page provenance. The underlying words are PD; the access copy, OCR file, modern metadata and scan images are separate layers. `[verified; inference]`

The four checked readings show that this edition is not merely a photographic reprint of the selected Torres 1894 witness: Gn 14:18 adds «en sacrificio»; Sal 96:7 adds «Dios nuestro» and keeps «ó ángeles»; Mal 1:11 has «se me ofrecen sacrificios» where Torres has «se sacrifica y se ofrece al Nombre mío»; and 2 Mac 15:14 has «amigo» / «ruega mucho por este pueblo» where Torres prints «amante» / «ruega incesantemente por el pueblo». ### Production recommendation

1. **First Catholic Spanish full-text candidate: Torres Amat 1894.** It is complete for the 73-book Catholic canon, has a compact two-volume dated witness, clears every ordinary copyright term in the target territory and now has four scan-verified test readings. Doctrina would still have to build and proofread its own page-provenanced transcription. `[verified; inference]`
2. **Second selectable historical text: Scío, second edition (1794–1797).** Its edition layer also clears every ordinary term and the complete Catholic canon is present, but the many-volume witness and its edition-specific verse boundaries make production and alignment costlier. The reader must preserve those boundaries rather than synthesize RV1909-style numbering. `[verified; inference]`
3. **Vence/Galván 1831–1833: keep as a distinct collation witness, not the first production text.** Its underlying edition is public domain and the complete 25-volume scan could support a lawful Doctrina transcription, but it is a light adaptation of Torres Amat, has no reusable licensed digital text, and would require the largest OCR/proofreading effort of the three. Its four verified differences are worth retaining for textual history; they do not justify prioritising a third full reader before the two independent historical translations. `[verified; inference]`

**Full-text gate:** all three historical edition layers clear the ordinary copyright terms checked here. None should yet be described as unconditionally “free everywhere” under Doctrina's stricter rule: Argentina, Uruguay and Bolivia retain statutory payment regimes for some public-domain uses. If the owner decides that any such levy fails the rule, no candidate in this report passes until territorial applicability is resolved; if the rule means copyright permission rather than zero statutory cost, Torres Amat and Scío are the two production candidates above. `[verified; inference]`
