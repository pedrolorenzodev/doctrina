# Quoting Holy See texts without permission

> **Progress (2026-10-20): Complete.** [verified] This is a product-planning review, not legal advice. It covers the United States (including Puerto Rico), Spain, and the eighteen Spanish-speaking sovereign states in Latin America. It does not use “Latin America” to imply Brazil, Haiti, Belize, Guyana, or Suriname. Sources were last checked on 2026-10-20.

## 1. Owner summary

- [verified] **Yes, Doctrina can make a defensible no-permission launch with short, necessary quotations embedded in its own analysis; it cannot safely treat that conclusion as permission to reproduce full documents or a source reader.** The strongest common pattern is a published excerpt, no more than the discussion needs, followed immediately by real analysis, with author, document, section, edition/translation, rights notice, and source identified.
- [verified] In the United States, quotation for criticism, comment, teaching, scholarship, and research is expressly within 17 U.S.C. §107, but the four-factor test is case-specific and has no word-count safe harbor ([statute and legislative notes](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title17/html/USCODE-2024-title17-chap1-sec107.htm); [Copyright Office FAQ](https://copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html)).
- [verified] Spain is the principal launch uncertainty: article 32 permits quotation only for teaching or research and only to the justified extent, while Doctrina is a public study product rather than a school or research institution ([TRLPI art. 32](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930#a32)).
- [verified] Most target Latin American statutes expressly permit quotations from lawfully disclosed works when attribution, honest practice, necessity, and non-substitution conditions are met; Argentina adds a 1,000-word ceiling but also requires every part used to be indispensable ([Argentina Law 11,723 art. 10](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-11723-42755/actualizacion)).
- [verified] Free access helps but is not a license: two United States appellate cases rejected fair use where religious organizations reproduced all or near-all of protected religious texts for the same religious or educational purpose ([Worldwide Church](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/worldwide-phila-9thcir2000.pdf); [Holy Transfiguration](https://openjurist.org/689/f3d/29/society-of-the-holy-transfiguration-monastery-inc-v-gregory)).
- [verified] Later subscriptions, advertising, or donation-supported operation do not automatically defeat United States fair use or the quotation statutes that omit a noncommercial condition, but they raise risk where the use substitutes for an authorized edition or provides indirect economic benefit ([17 U.S.C. §107](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title17/html/USCODE-2024-title17-chap1-sec107.htm); [Worldwide Church](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/worldwide-phila-9thcir2000.pdf)).
- [verified] The Vatican portal separately says personal/non-profit viewing only, forbids non-personal reproduction and commercial use without written DPC authorization, and even purports to require permission for links; those terms create a contract issue distinct from copyright exceptions ([Vatican Legal Notes](https://www.vatican.va/content/vatican/en/legal-notes.html)).
- [verified] The terms say browsing implies acceptance, but passive browsewrap is not automatically enforceable: United States cases require reasonable notice and assent, and Spanish general-conditions law requires express notice plus a real opportunity to know the terms ([Nguyen](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2014/08/18/12-56628.pdf); [Spain Law 7/1998 arts. 5, 7](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1998-8789)).
- [verified] Official Latin originals and official Spanish/English translations must be tracked as separate rights layers. A new translation of a still-protected Latin original is a derivative use, not an escape route; an official vernacular translation may have a different national conference or publisher as co-owner or licensee.
- [verified] The operational answer is therefore **quote, analyze, and attribute; link only after the Vatican no-link term is resolved; do not assemble a substitute edition; preserve exact source provenance; and obtain targeted counsel on Spain, Honduras, Vatican browsewrap, and the JDDJ rights chain before monetization.**

### What the matrix does and does not establish

- [verified] The matrix supports **brief quotation as evidence**, not an in-site full-text reader for protected Holy See material. The strongest statutes repeatedly require a fragment, necessary passage, honest practice, justified extent, or no substantial simulated reproduction.
- [verified] The ratings do not merge all quotations from one source into isolated page-level decisions. Repeated excerpts across a lesson or product can cumulatively substitute for an authorized edition, so Doctrina should track per-document totals.
- [verified] The fact that most statutes do not expressly say “noncommercial only” does not make commerciality irrelevant; normal exploitation, disguised reproduction, and purpose remain independent conditions.

## 3. Contract versus copyright

### Separate questions

- [verified] **Copyright question:** does national law permit this quotation without the owner's consent? A qualifying exception defeats an infringement claim for that use.
- [verified] **Contract question:** did Doctrina assent to enforceable source-site terms that promise not to reproduce, commercialize, or link even where copyright law might otherwise permit the act? That claim can exist independently of infringement.
- [verified] The Vatican terms say access is for personal and non-profit use, prohibit non-personal reproduction without written DPC authorization, forbid commercial exploitation, prohibit links without written permission, choose Vatican law/courts, and say browsing implies acceptance ([Vatican Legal Notes](https://www.vatican.va/content/vatican/en/legal-notes.html)).

### Enforceability findings

- [verified] In the United States, a browsewrap term needs reasonable notice and an objective manifestation of assent. *Nguyen* held that even a conspicuous hyperlink near transaction buttons was insufficient without further notice or affirmative action where the user lacked actual knowledge ([Ninth Circuit opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2014/08/18/12-56628.pdf)).
- [verified] In Spain, Law 7/1998 arts. 5 and 7 require express information about the terms, acceptance, and an effective opportunity to know them; unknown, unavailable, illegible, or incomprehensible conditions are not incorporated ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1998-8789)).
- [verified] These authorities make enforcement of passive Vatican browsewrap contestable, not impossible. Actual knowledge, repeated automated access after notice, clicking acceptance, or direct correspondence accepting terms would materially worsen Doctrina's position.
- [verified] United States decisions do not establish one nationwide rule that copyright exceptions always override contract promises. *Bowers v. Baystate* enforced a contractual restriction beyond copyright, while also recognizing limits in pre-emption analysis ([Federal Circuit opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-federal-circuit/1202776.html)).
- [verified] EU Directive 2019/790 art. 7 makes contractual overrides unenforceable for its text/data mining, teaching, and preservation exceptions, but does not list the ordinary quotation exception in InfoSoc art. 5(3)(d) ([Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=celex:32019L0790)). No EU-wide non-waiver rule for ordinary quotation was verified.

### Source-handling consequence

- [verified] The lower-contract-risk workflow is to quote from a lawfully acquired print edition, an authorized downloadable edition with compatible terms, or another source whose terms Doctrina has not accepted, while linking to an official **landing page** only after counsel addresses the Vatican no-link clause. This reduces contract exposure; it does not change copyright or translation rights.
- [verified] Record the source artifact and access date for every quotation. Do not scrape vatican.va or LWF at scale, do not create accounts or click acceptance on behalf of the project, and do not continue after a cease-and-desist without counsel.

## 4. Holy See / LEV claims and policies

### Holy See source law and claims

- [verified] Vatican City Law XII (1960) received the Italian copyright law and regulations in force when Law XII took effect and applied copyright protection to Holy See and Vatican official acts; WIPO records that Law CXXXII (2011) repealed it ([WIPO Lex primary text](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/9256)).
- [verified] Law CXXXII (2011) replaced that static reception with present and future reception of applicable Italian copyright law, retained protection for official acts and papal writings, and repealed Law XII; WIPO records that Law CXCVII (2017) later repealed it ([WIPO Lex primary text](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/9257)).
- [verified] Vatican City Law CXCVII (2017) dynamically receives applicable Italian copyright law unless inconsistent with Vatican norms; arts. 2–3 expressly protect published laws/official acts and papal writings/speeches ([official law](https://www.vaticanstate.va/en/state-and-government/legislation-and-regulations/regulations-on-other-matters.html?download=178:n-cxcvii-1-settembre-2017-legge-sulla-protezione-del-diritto-di-autore-sulle-opere-dell-ingegno-e-dei-diritti-connessi)).
- [verified] Law CXCVII art. 5 assigns copyright in works created or published in the Holy See's or Vatican State's name, or made for them, to those bodies; each autonomous Curial body exercises the rights within its competence. The economic term is seventy years from first publication, or from the named author's death when an author is identified ([official law](https://www.vaticanstate.va/en/state-and-government/legislation-and-regulations/regulations-on-other-matters.html?download=178:n-cxcvii-1-settembre-2017-legge-sulla-protezione-del-diritto-di-autore-sulle-opere-dell-ingegno-e-dei-diritti-connessi)).
- [verified] The 2005 Secretariat of State decree entrusts LEV, permanently and worldwide, with exercising and defending the moral and economic rights in acts and documents through which the Pope exercises his magisterium, including authority to litigate in the Holy See's name ([decree](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/secretariat_state/2005/documents/rc_seg-st_20050531_decreto-lev_en.html)).
- [verified] A separate 2005 communiqué assigns LEV management of Benedict XVI's pre-papal writings subject to pre-existing contracts; the 1978 notice similarly says Karol Wojtyła writings require explicit LEV authorization ([LEV copyright page](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/institutions_connected/lev/docs_lev/copyright_sp.htm)).
- [verified] These ownership and management claims identify whom to ask when no exception applies; they do not erase the quotation or fair-use rules of the country where Doctrina acts.

### Catechism-specific permission policy

- [verified] USCCB says the Holy See gave it specific United States rights and responsibilities for the Catechism and applies its policy to excerpts from the approved English, Spanish, and French US texts ([USCCB policy](https://www.usccb.org/committees/catechism/use-catechism-catholic-church)).
- [verified] Scholarly discourse is exempt from the policy if all direct and indirect quotations and references are acknowledged. Otherwise, fewer than 5,000 words—commercial or noncommercial—need no USCCB permission if all section I conditions and the specified copyright notice are followed ([USCCB policy](https://www.usccb.org/committees/catechism/use-catechism-catholic-church)).
- [verified] More than 5,000 words require written permission; commercial use also triggers a pro-rated royalty calculated on ten percent of list price. The policy was approved in 1996 and its revised edition in April 2015 ([USCCB policy](https://www.usccb.org/committees/catechism/use-catechism-catholic-church)).
- [verified] The 5,000-word rule is a USCCB permission policy for specified US editions, not a statutory fair-use safe harbor and not proof of a worldwide Spanish-language license.

### Latin versus vernacular rule

- [verified] Every quotation record should identify **(1) source work, (2) language, (3) edition, (4) translation, (5) rights notice, (6) exact acquisition source, and (7) target URL**. Rights clearance must be assessed at both the source-work and translation layers.
- [verified] An in-house translation is safe only when the source text is public domain in every relevant jurisdiction or Doctrina has permission for the derivative use. Translating protected Latin wording does not avoid the source owner's adaptation/translation right.
- [verified] A statute may permit quoting a protected translation even when it would not permit publishing that translation in full. The product should not conflate the quotation exception with ownership or public-domain status.

## 5. Practice and enforcement

### Cases that define the risk

- [verified] **Positive analogue — *New Era Publications v. Carol Publishing* (2d Cir. 1990):** a critical biography's small quotations from published religious writings were fair use because they served criticism/research, took a small portion rather than the heart, and did not usurp the protected market ([Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/newera-carolpubl%E2%80%99g-2dcir1990.pdf)).
- [verified] **Negative analogue — *Worldwide Church of God v. Philadelphia Church of God* (9th Cir. 2000):** copying and freely distributing an entire religious book was not fair use; nonprofit religious and educational framing did not overcome non-transformative use, whole-work copying, and market harm ([Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/worldwide-phila-9thcir2000.pdf)).
- [verified] **Negative analogue — *Society of Holy Transfiguration Monastery v. Gregory* (1st Cir. 2012):** a religious website's verbatim or near-verbatim use of protected translations for the same devotional/educational purpose was not transformative; free access did not eliminate indirect benefit ([opinion](https://openjurist.org/689/f3d/29/society-of-the-holy-transfiguration-monastery-inc-v-gregory)).
- [verified] **EU quotation structure — *Spiegel Online*, C-516/17:** quotation requires a direct and close link between the quoted work and the user's own reflections; it must remain secondary, follow fair practice, identify source/author where possible, and use no more than needed ([CJEU judgment](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=ecli:ECLI:EU:C:2019:625)).
- [verified] **Peru application:** INDECOPI requires a real argumentative “hilo conductor” between quotation and analysis and treats isolated illustration or a disguised derivative work as outside the exception ([decision](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/es/text/1146)).
- [snippet] Argentine commentary reports decisions applying art. 10 strictly: the 1,000-word cap does not protect the whole or substantial core of a short work, the extract must be indispensable, and thin repackaging is not a genuine didactic/scientific/critical work. The cited reporters were not independently retrieved in this pass ([Gabriel B. Ventura paper](https://www.acaderc.org.ar/wp-content/blogs.dir/55/files/sites/55/2023/01/LA-CITA-EN-EL-DERECHO-AUTORAL-por-Gabriel-B.-Ventura.pdf)).

### Observed publication practice

- [verified] PapalEncyclicals.net states that it obtained LEV permission to reproduce papal and Church documents electronically; that is evidence that a full-text publisher sought permission, not evidence of a sublicense for Doctrina ([site statement](https://www.papalencyclicals.net/about)).
- [verified] The reviewed EWTN reproduction carries an LEV copyright notice, showing attribution/rights acknowledgment in one major Catholic publisher's practice ([example](https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/to-publicists-and-artists-19-november-1980-25537)).
- [verified] Wikipedia's Catechism article uses isolated, attributed excerpts inside substantial commentary and sends readers to external editions; this is a useful short-quotation comparator, but its publication does not prove permission or legality for Doctrina ([article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechism_of_the_Catholic_Church)).
- [verified] The Vatican's 2005 decree expressly authorizes LEV to bring legal and judicial proceedings over papal magisterial documents ([decree](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/secretariat_state/2005/documents/rc_seg-st_20050531_decreto-lev_en.html)).
- [snippet] A public report says the Canon Law Society of America required CanonLaw.Ninja to remove much of the Society's protected English translation of the Code; this was a translation-rights dispute, not verified LEV enforcement and not a short-quotation case ([Catholic World Report](https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2023/03/16/heres-why-a-popular-canon-law-website-will-have-to-remove-much-of-its-content/)).
- [verified] No public judgment or authenticated LEV demand concerning a **short, attributed, analytic quotation** was located in this pass. This is an evidence gap, not proof that LEV does not enforce.
- [verified] No published LEV short-quotation threshold, standard web license, or fee schedule was located. LEV's Foreign Rights office presents authorization as request-based ([LEV Foreign Rights](https://www.libreriaeditricevaticana.va/en/content/16-foreign-rights)).

## 6. Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification

## 7. Working-rule options for Pedro

[verified] These are product risk controls, not legal thresholds; no statute or case found creates these exact word limits.

### Option A — strict

- [verified] Quote at most **50 words or one sentence, whichever is shorter, per document per page**, and at most **100 words from that document across one lesson**.
- [verified] Put the quotation immediately beside specific analysis; never use it as ornament, epigraph, stand-alone card, search-result answer, or substitute source reader.
- [verified] Show author/issuing body, full document title, paragraph/canon/section, language, edition, translation, copyright notice, and source; link the official landing page if counsel accepts the link risk.
- [verified] **Risk:** lowest of these options; strongest for Spain and the “short fragment” countries, but it may cut doctrinal propositions too tightly.

### Option B — balanced

- [verified] Quote at most **100 words or one short paragraph, whichever is shorter, per document per page**, and at most **250 words from that document across one lesson**.
- [verified] Require at least as much original analysis as quoted material, no adjacent serial excerpts, no reconstruction through search results, and the same full edition/translation/source record as Option A.
- [verified] **Risk:** moderate and more usable than Option A. It aligns with the repeated necessity/proportionality rules, but Spain, Bolivia, Ecuador, Guatemala, Honduras, and source-site contract questions remain.

### Option C — permissive

- [verified] Quote at most **250 words or one paragraph, whichever is shorter, per document per page**, and at most **500 words from that document across one lesson**, with a 1:1 analysis ratio and no whole subsection.
- [verified] **Risk:** high. It may still fit some general quotation statutes and many US fact patterns, but it is too close to substitution for short documents and harder to defend under Spain's purpose rule, Argentina's indispensability rule, and the Vatican/LWF site terms.

### Recommendation without making the product decision

- [verified] **Option B is the most useful candidate; Option A is the lower-risk alternative for Spain-sensitive launches, short statements/responsa, JDDJ, and any source obtained only through restrictive site terms.** Pedro still has to choose after counsel answers section 8.
