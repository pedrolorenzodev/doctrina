# Quoting Holy See texts without permission

> **Progress (2026-10-20): Complete; independent verification applied 2026-10-20.** [verified] This is a product-planning review, not legal advice. It covers the United States, Spain, and the eighteen Spanish-speaking sovereign states in Latin America. Puerto Rico is included only as an unresolved scope item because federal applicability was not independently sourced in `holy-see-quotation.verify.md`, jurisdiction row 1. It does not use “Latin America” to imply Brazil, Haiti, Belize, Guyana, or Suriname. Sources were last checked on 2026-10-20.

## 1. Owner summary

- [inference] **Doctrina may be able to make a defensible no-permission launch with short, necessary quotations embedded in its own analysis; this is a risk-managed product proposal, not a verified legal conclusion or permission to reproduce full documents or a source reader.** The strongest common pattern in the reviewed statutes is a published excerpt, no more than the discussion needs, followed immediately by real analysis, with author, document, section, edition/translation, rights notice, and source identified. Counsel is still needed for the named choice-of-law, territorial, contract, translation, and local-procedure gaps.
- [verified] In the United States, quotation for criticism, comment, teaching, scholarship, and research is expressly within 17 U.S.C. §107, but the four-factor test is case-specific and has no word-count safe harbor ([statute and legislative notes](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title17/html/USCODE-2024-title17-chap1-sec107.htm); [Copyright Office FAQ](https://copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html)).
- [verified] Spain is a principal launch uncertainty: article 32.1 permits quotation only for teaching or research and only to the justified extent ([TRLPI art. 32](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930#a32)). [inference] Whether Doctrina's public study product has the required purpose remains unresolved; article 32.1 does not impose the formal-institution condition found in the separate article 32.3 teaching exception.
- [verified] Most target Latin American statutes expressly permit quotations from lawfully disclosed works when attribution, honest practice, necessity, and non-substitution conditions are met; Argentina adds a 1,000-word ceiling but also requires every part used to be indispensable ([Argentina Law 11,723 art. 10](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-11723-42755/actualizacion)).
- [verified] Free access is not a license: two United States appellate cases rejected fair use where religious organizations reproduced all or near-all of protected religious texts for the same religious or educational purpose ([Copyright Office summary of *Worldwide Church*](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/worldwide-phila-9thcir2000.pdf); [First Circuit opinion in *Holy Transfiguration*](https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca1/11-1262/11-1262-2012-08-02.pdf)). [inference] These are boundary cases about whole or near-whole nontransformative copying, not close factual matches for short analytic excerpts.
- [inference] Later subscriptions, advertising, or donation-supported operation do not automatically defeat United States fair use or quotation statutes that omit a noncommercial condition, but they can raise risk where the use substitutes for an authorized edition or provides economic benefit ([17 U.S.C. §107](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title17/html/USCODE-2024-title17-chap1-sec107.htm); [Copyright Office summary of *Worldwide Church*](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/worldwide-phila-9thcir2000.pdf)). Application outside the United States remains jurisdiction-specific.
- [verified] The Vatican portal separately says personal/non-profit viewing only, forbids non-personal reproduction and commercial use without written DPC authorization, and even purports to require permission for links; those terms create a contract issue distinct from copyright exceptions ([Vatican Legal Notes](https://www.vatican.va/content/vatican/en/legal-notes.html)).
- [inference] The terms say browsing implies acceptance, but passive browsewrap enforceability remains uncertain. *Nguyen* is Ninth Circuit consumer-browsewrap authority requiring reasonable notice and objective assent, not a nationwide rule for every setting. Spain Law 7/1998 arts. 5 and 7 require notice, acceptance, and a real opportunity to know the terms, but applying them here first requires establishing territorial scope and governing law ([*Nguyen*](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2014/08/18/12-56628.pdf); [Spain Law 7/1998](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1998-8789)).
- [inference] Track official Latin originals and official Spanish/English translations as potentially separate rights layers. The general adaptation/translation conclusion was not supported across every governing statute or edition in `holy-see-quotation.verify.md`, final Holy See row; exact edition notices and applicable statutes must be checked document by document.
- [decision] The conservative operational rule is therefore **quote, analyze, and attribute; link only after the Vatican no-link term is resolved; do not assemble a substitute edition; preserve exact source provenance; and obtain targeted counsel on Spain, Honduras, Vatican browsewrap, translation rights, and the JDDJ rights chain before monetization.** This is a product decision, not a verified legal conclusion that every official link is enforceably prohibited.

### What the matrix does and does not establish

- [verified] The matrix supports **brief quotation as evidence**, not an in-site full-text reader for protected Holy See material. The strongest statutes repeatedly require a fragment, necessary passage, honest practice, justified extent, or no substantial simulated reproduction.
- [verified] The ratings do not merge all quotations from one source into isolated page-level decisions. Repeated excerpts across a lesson or product can cumulatively substitute for an authorized edition, so Doctrina should track per-document totals.
- [verified] The fact that most statutes do not expressly say “noncommercial only” does not make commerciality irrelevant; normal exploitation, disguised reproduction, and purpose remain independent conditions.

## 3. Contract versus copyright

### Separate questions

- [verified] **Copyright question:** does national law permit this quotation without the owner's consent? A qualifying exception defeats an infringement claim for that use.
- [verified] **Contract question:** did Doctrina assent to enforceable source-site terms that promise not to reproduce, commercialize, or link even where copyright law might otherwise permit the act? That claim can exist independently of infringement.
- [verified] The Vatican terms say access is for personal and non-profit use, prohibit non-personal reproduction without written DPC authorization, forbid commercial exploitation, prohibit links without written permission, choose Vatican law/courts, and say browsing implies acceptance ([Vatican Legal Notes](https://www.vatican.va/content/vatican/en/legal-notes.html)).

### Enforceability findings

- [verified] In the Ninth Circuit consumer-browsewrap setting, reasonable notice and an objective manifestation of assent matter. *Nguyen* held that even a conspicuous hyperlink near transaction buttons was insufficient without further notice or affirmative action where the user lacked actual knowledge ([Ninth Circuit opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2014/08/18/12-56628.pdf)). This is not a nationwide rule for every factual setting.
- [verified] Spain Law 7/1998 arts. 5 and 7 require information about the terms, acceptance, and an effective opportunity to know them; unknown, unavailable, illegible, ambiguous, or obscure conditions are not incorporated ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1998-8789)). [inference] Before applying that law to Vatican terms and a US/Argentine operator, territorial scope under article 3 and governing law must be established.
- [inference] Taken together, these authorities make passive Vatican browsewrap enforcement contestable, not demonstrably impossible. Actual knowledge, repeated automated access after notice, clicking acceptance, or direct correspondence accepting terms may worsen Doctrina's position.
- [verified] United States decisions do not establish one nationwide rule that copyright exceptions always override contract promises. *Bowers v. Baystate* enforced a negotiated software reverse-engineering restriction as qualitatively different from copyright ([Federal Circuit opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-federal-circuit/1202776.html)). [inference] It establishes that some contracts can add restrictions, not that the Vatican's passive browsewrap is enforceable.
- [unconfirmed] The prior review read EU Directive 2019/790 art. 7 as invalidating contractual overrides for its text/data mining, teaching, and preservation exceptions but not ordinary quotation. The official page could not be directly inspected in `holy-see-quotation.verify.md`, priority row 13, so no `[verified]` conclusion is retained ([Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=celex:32019L0790)).

### Source-handling consequence

- [decision] The lower-contract-risk workflow is to quote from a lawfully acquired print edition, an authorized downloadable edition with compatible terms, or another source whose terms Doctrina has not accepted, while linking to an official **landing page** only after counsel addresses the Vatican no-link clause. This is a conservative product choice, not a verified conclusion that every link is enforceably prohibited. It reduces contract exposure; it does not change copyright or translation rights.
- [verified] Record the source artifact and access date for every quotation. Do not scrape vatican.va or LWF at scale, do not create accounts or click acceptance on behalf of the project, and do not continue after a cease-and-desist without counsel.

### Catechism-specific permission policy

- [verified] USCCB says the Holy See gave it specific United States rights and responsibilities for the Catechism and applies its policy to excerpts from the approved English, Spanish, and French US texts ([USCCB policy](https://www.usccb.org/committees/catechism/use-catechism-catholic-church)).
- [verified] Scholarly discourse is exempt from the policy if all direct and indirect quotations and references are acknowledged. - [verified] More than 5,000 words require written permission; commercial use also triggers a pro-rated royalty calculated on ten percent of list price. The policy was approved in 1996 and its revised edition in April 2015 ([USCCB policy](https://www.usccb.org/committees/catechism/use-catechism-catholic-church)).
- [verified] The 5,000-word rule is a USCCB permission policy for specified US editions, not a statutory fair-use safe harbor and not proof of a worldwide Spanish-language license.

## 5. Practice and enforcement

### Cases that define the risk

- [verified] **Positive analogue — *New Era Publications v. Carol Publishing* (2d Cir. 1990):** the Copyright Office's case summary reports that a critical biography's small published quotations used for criticism/research did not substitute for the protected market. The linked source is an agency summary, not the opinion ([Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/newera-carolpubl%E2%80%99g-2dcir1990.pdf)).
- [verified] **Negative boundary case — *Worldwide Church of God v. Philadelphia Church of God* (9th Cir. 2000):** the Copyright Office's case summary reports that copying and freely distributing an entire religious book was not fair use because nonprofit religious and educational framing did not overcome non-transformative use, whole-work copying, and market harm. The linked source is an agency summary, not the appellate opinion ([Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/worldwide-phila-9thcir2000.pdf)).
- [verified] **Negative boundary case — *Society of Holy Transfiguration Monastery v. Gregory* (1st Cir. 2012):** a religious website's verbatim or near-verbatim use of protected translations for the same intrinsic purpose was not transformative; cost savings and recognition supplied benefit despite free access ([First Circuit opinion](https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca1/11-1262/11-1262-2012-08-02.pdf)). These whole/near-whole-copying cases are not close factual matches for short analytic excerpts.
- [unconfirmed] **EU quotation structure — *Spiegel Online*, C-516/17:** the prior review summarized a direct link between quotation and the user's reflections, fair practice, attribution, and proportionality. ## 7. Working-rule options for Pedro

[decision] These are internal product risk controls, not verified legal thresholds; no reviewed statute or case creates these exact word limits.

### Option A — strict

- [decision] Quote at most **50 words or one sentence, whichever is shorter, per document per page**, and at most **100 words from that document across one lesson**.
- [decision] Put the quotation immediately beside specific analysis; never use it as ornament, epigraph, stand-alone card, search-result answer, or substitute source reader.
- [decision] Show author/issuing body, full document title, paragraph/canon/section, language, edition, translation, copyright notice, and source; link the official landing page if counsel accepts the link risk.
- [inference] **Risk:** lowest of these options; strongest for Spain and the “short fragment” countries, but it may cut doctrinal propositions too tightly.

### Option B — balanced

- [decision] Quote at most **100 words or one short paragraph, whichever is shorter, per document per page**, and at most **250 words from that document across one lesson**.
- [decision] Require at least as much original analysis as quoted material, no adjacent serial excerpts, no reconstruction through search results, and the same full edition/translation/source record as Option A.
- [inference] **Risk:** moderate and more usable than Option A. It aligns with the repeated necessity/proportionality rules, but Spain, Bolivia, Ecuador, Guatemala, Honduras, and source-site contract questions remain.

### Option C — permissive

- [decision] Quote at most **250 words or one paragraph, whichever is shorter, per document per page**, and at most **500 words from that document across one lesson**, with a 1:1 analysis ratio and no whole subsection.
- [inference] **Risk:** high. It may still fit some general quotation statutes and many US fact patterns, but it is too close to substitution for short documents and harder to defend under Spain's purpose rule, Argentina's indispensability rule, and the Vatican/LWF site terms.

### Recommendation without making the product decision

- [decision] **Option B is the most useful candidate; Option A is the lower-risk alternative for Spain-sensitive launches, short statements/responsa, JDDJ, and any source obtained only through restrictive site terms.** Pedro still has to choose after counsel answers section 8.
