# aggregators-legal — Aggregators' reuse terms, legal memo, permission route

Status: DONE (written incrementally, 2026-10-09). Not legal advice; a research memo for Pedro to take to a lawyer where flagged.

## 0. Key takeaways (for Pedro)
1. **Never ingest CCEL or New Advent files** for the paid site: CCEL asks commercial users to ask (and asserts © on its XML); New Advent claims © on everything and sells it. Same PD words are available cleanly from **Wikisource (CC BY-SA, attribute)**, archive.org/Google/HathiTrust scans (own OCR), Project Gutenberg (strip the PG name) and **Roger Pearse's tertullian.org "Additional Fathers", which are explicitly dedicated to the public domain**.
2. **Scan libraries' terms are contract, not copyright**: IA ("scholarship and research purposes only"), Google ("we request ... non-commercial"), HathiTrust (Google images/OCR: request NC) — but HathiTrust writes that "There are no restrictions on use of text transcribed from the images". → Always do **our own OCR/transcription**; never re-host their images or OCR. **Gallica charges for commercial reuse** (avoid Gallica-only sources). BNE is CC BY 4.0.
3. **US hosting means US law always applies**: pre-1931 = PD; US books 1931–63 PD if not renewed; **foreign books 1931–77 are protected 95 years from publication (URAA), renewal irrelevant** — this blocks most 20th‑c. Spanish translations in the US. Spain adds life+80 for authors who died before 1987; Argentina life+70 and a possible FNA levy on PD works (lawyer).
4. **Renewal check, tested with NYPL's CC0 data**: Fathers of the Church (CUA) and Tappert's Book of Concord and Luther's Works **were renewed** (permission needed); Benziger's *Summa* renewal covers only charts (translation PD); the **1941 Confraternity NT shows no renewal** (likely US PD).
5. **HF/GitHub licence labels are worthless as evidence** (CCC under MIT, CCEL dump under CC BY-SA, Creeds.json labels the © 2011 CRC Belgic as "Public Domain").
6. **Permission route**: ask for a *display-only licence on Doctrina* (Sefaria–JPS model), with buy-link and attribution; EN/ES templates in §4; rights-holder directory in §4.2. Translations are actively enforced (*HTM v. Gregory*, 1st Cir. 2012).

(The same page's older text says "Since the texts are public domain and no copyright will be asserted ..." — the 2006 paragraph supersedes it.)
- Per-file metadata contradicts the policy in the reader's favour but does not override it: the ThML XML of NPNF2-14 starts with the comment `<!-- Copyright Christian Classics Ethereal Library -->` **and** carries `<DC.Rights>Public Domain</DC.Rights>`; the .txt header says "Rights: Public Domain" (both fetched: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/s/schaff/npnf214.xml, https://www.ccel.org/ccel/s/schaff/npnf214/cache/npnf214.txt). The DC.Rights field describes the *print basis*, not CCEL's markup.
- Fees: **no published fee schedule found** (searched site + web). Contact is **a web form only** (https://www.ccel.org/info/email.html; no mailto on the page). Who: Harry Plantinga (director, copyright holder named on the policy page).
- Legal reading (US): CCEL cannot own the PD words. What it can plausibly own: its added introductions/summaries ("Tim Perrine, CCEL Staff Writer" blurbs), cover art, and arguably the selection/markup of the XML (thin). A plain-text transcription of a PD book is not copyrightable in the US (no originality: *Feist v. Rural*, 499 U.S. 340 (1991); for faithful reproductions of PD works *Bridgeman v. Corel*, 36 F. Supp. 2d 191 (S.D.N.Y. 1999) — memory, well known; not re-read tonight). But the request "contact us" plus large-scale copying of their files would be a bad look for a trust-first product and invites a dispute. 
- **Use:** do NOT ingest CCEL files for the paid site. Use them only (a) as a proofreading witness, (b) as deep links ("read on CCEL"). If we ever want their ThML (scripture-reference tagging is valuable), ask: draft in §4.5. Rebuild the same PD texts from Wikisource / archive.org scans (see 2.3, 2.10–2.12).

### 2.3 Wikisource (en, es, la, el)
- en policy (raw, https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Wikisource:Copyright_policy, fetched): "Unless otherwise noted, all user contributions to Wikisource are released under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0) and the GNU Free Documentation License"; "The copyright laws applicable to Wikisource are primarily those of the United States"; all hosted works must be "free content ... including commercial exploitation". **Original translations made by Wikisource users** (the "Wikisource translation" namespace) are CC BY-SA 4.0.
- es policy (https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/Wikisource:Derechos_de_autor, fetched): content "disponible bajo la Licencia Creative Commons Atribución-CompartirIgual 4.0"; NC/ND/fair use not admitted. **Trap:** es.wikisource admits any work published **before 1923 in any country** even if still protected in its country of origin ("Las obras anteriores a ese año se consideran en dominio público a los efectos de su publicación en Wikisource, pero pueden no estarlo en algunos países"). So **presence on es.wikisource ≠ PD in Spain/Argentina**: check the translator's death date ourselves (Spain: died ≤1945; Argentina: died ≤1955).
- What exactly is under CC BY-SA when we take a PD text from Wikisource? The PD words stay PD (a faithful transcription adds no original expression in US law). CC BY-SA can bite only on original contributions: Wikisource-made translations, editorial notes/headers written by editors, and (arguably, in the EU) the database. Safe practice: (1) take only the PD text + page scans' provenance, not Wikisource's own headers/notes; (2) credit "Transcription: Wikisource contributors (CC BY-SA 4.0)" anyway — attribution costs nothing and matches our trust invariant; (3) **any Wikisource *user translation* (or other CC BY-SA text) we display must itself stay CC BY-SA**, with attribution and licence link. Share-alike does **not** spread to the rest of the site: CC FAQ (https://creativecommons.org/faq/, fetched): "All Creative Commons licenses ... allow licensed material to be included in collections such as anthologies, encyclopedias ... You may choose a license for the collection, however this does not change the license applicable to the original material"; "Regarding ShareAlike, the condition only applies if a work is modified and if the work is shared publicly"; and CC licences "do[] not affect parts of the work that are unrestricted by copyright". So: keep CC BY-SA texts as separate, unmodified layers (our notes, alignments and commentary live in our own layer); if we correct typos in them, publish the corrected text under CC BY-SA too.
- **Use:** best clean base for ANF/NPNF/Schaff/Catholic Encyclopedia etc. **READY** with attribution. Respect API etiquette (bots: User-Agent with contact, rate limits; another agent hit HTTP 429 tonight).

### 2.10 Internet Archive
- Terms of Use (rendered in Chrome, https://archive.org/about/terms, "Terms of Service (last updated 12/31/2014)"): "**Access to the Archive's Collections is provided at no cost to you and is granted for scholarship and research purposes only.**" … "you certify that your use of any part of the Archive's Collections will be limited to noninfringing or fair use under copyright law. - **Use:** WORK (OCR) — main scan source. Keep: item id + page for provenance; prefer non-Google scans (Toronto/Cornell/Princeton) whose OCR is also better (spanish-pd agent's test).

### 2.12 Google Books
- The usage-guidelines page Google prepends to every PD PDF (read from IA mirror `canonsanddecree00buckgoog_djvu.txt`, fetched): "Public domain books belong to the public and we are merely their custodians. ... **We also ask that you**: + Make non-commercial use of the files ... we **request** that you use these files for personal, non-commercial purposes. + Refrain from automated querying ... + Maintain attribution ... + Keep it legal ...".
- Precise reading: it is phrased as a **request** about "the files" (Google's scans/PDFs), not a licence over the text; Google asserts no copyright in the PD book. Separately, downloading is subject to Google's Terms of Service (no automated querying; respect technical limits) — a contract matter. en.wikisource's Help:Google Books (fetched) tells contributors to delete Google's first page before re-hosting scans, i.e. Wikimedia treats the scans as PD (Commons challenges arise only over the Google cover page).
- **Use:** WORK. Download manually (no scraping); OCR ourselves; don't re-host Google's PDFs or images; don't serve Google's OCR. HathiTrust's note above is the cleanest written confirmation that text transcribed from Google images is unrestricted.

### 2.13 Gallica (BnF) — commercial reuse is PAID
- Conditions d'utilisation (rendered in Chrome, https://gallica.bnf.fr/accueil/fr/html/conditions-dutilisation-de-gallica): contents are "informations publiques" under the French Code des relations entre le public et l'administration (CRPA, arts. L.321-1 to L.327-1). "La réutilisation non commerciale de ces contenus est libre et gratuite ... mention de source ... « Source gallica.bnf.fr / BnF ». **La réutilisation commerciale de ces contenus est payante et fait l'objet d'une licence.** Est entendue par réutilisation commerciale la revente de contenus sous forme de produits élaborés ou de fourniture de service ou toute autre réutilisation des contenus générant directement des revenus." ## 3. Legal memo (research, not legal advice; items for a lawyer are flagged ⚖)

### 3.1 Which law applies to Doctrina
- Copyright is territorial: each country applies its own law to acts in its territory (Berne art. 5(2), "lex loci protectionis" — memory, standard). Hosting on Vercel (US) means **US law always applies** to the act of hosting/serving. Readers in Spain and Argentina mean the site also performs "communication to the public" there; EU courts accept jurisdiction where a site is **accessible** for copyright (CJEU *Pinckney*, C‑170/12, 2013; *Hejduk*, C‑441/13, 2015) and, for database right, where it is **targeted** (*Football Dataco v Sportradar*, C‑173/11, 2012) — memory, not re-read tonight. A Spanish-language site sold to Spaniards is clearly targeting Spain.
- Practical consequence: **a text is safe only if it is free in the US AND Spain AND Argentina** (and, for later expansion, Mexico/Colombia/UK…). The binding constraint differs per case: US for 1931–1977 foreign books (URAA), Spain for authors who died 1946–1986 (life+80), Argentina for the FNA levy (see 3.8).
- Enforcement reality (memory, unverified): rights holders in this niche who are known to act are publishers of modern Bible translations and official texts (LEV 2006 dispute and CLSA 2023 takedown — both documented in catholic-modern report; Crossway/Lockman/Biblica are known for takedown letters). Old-book heirs essentially never surface. Risk is concentrated in **modern translations and official texts**, not in 1880s books.

### 3.2 United States (hosting law) — terms, verified on Cornell's chart (Hirtle, "Updated January 1, 2026", https://copyright.cornell.edu/publicdomain, saved `cornell_pd.txt`)
- Published **before 1931** (anywhere): "In the public domain".
- US works **1931–1963**: "Published with notice but copyright was not renewed → None. In the public domain due to copyright expiration"; renewed → 95 years from publication.
- US works 1964–1977: 95 years from publication (renewal automatic).
- **Works first published abroad 1931–1977**: PD only if "in the public domain in its source country as of 1 January 1996"; otherwise "95 years after publication date" — **renewal is irrelevant for them** (URAA restoration). Statute: 17 U.S.C. §104A(h)(6) (fetched, law.cornell.edu): a "restored work" is one that "(B) is not in the public domain in its source country through expiration of term of protection; (C) is in the public domain in the United States due to— (i) noncompliance with formalities ... including failure of renewal ..."; §104A(a)(1)(B): restored copyright lasts "for the remainder of the term ... the work would have otherwise been granted in the United States". (*Golan v. Holder*, 565 U.S. 302 (2012) upheld this — memory.)
  - Worked example: Mingana's Theodore of Mopsuestia (Cambridge 1932/1933; Mingana d. 1937) → UK-protected on 1/1/1996 → US-protected until end of 2027/2028. Same logic hits **every Spanish/European translation published 1931–1977 whose translator died after ~1915–1925** (the source country's term must still have been running in 1996): e.g. a Madrid 1950 BAC volume is US-protected until 2045 regardless of renewal.
- Faithful transcription/scan of a PD book: no new US copyright (*Feist Publications v. Rural Telephone*, 499 U.S. 340 (1991), originality; *Bridgeman Art Library v. Corel*, 36 F. Supp. 2d 191 (S.D.N.Y. 1999), exact reproductions) — memory, standard cases. Editorial additions (introductions, new notes, *substantial* revision) are protectable: 17 U.S.C. §101 (fetched): a derivative work includes "a translation ... A work consisting of editorial revisions, annotations, elaborations, or other modifications which, as a whole, represent an original work of authorship"; §103(b) (fetched): copyright in a derivative work "extends only to the material contributed by the author ... and does not imply any exclusive right in the preexisting material".
- **Translations are derivative works** (same §101 text): a 1955 translation of Augustine is protected on its own clock even though Augustine is PD. Our own new translation from a PD original is our own work.

### 3.3 How to check a US renewal (and what I tested tonight)
- Tools: (1) **NYPL `cce-renewals`** (GitHub, **CC0**, tab-delimited renewals 1950–1991, https://github.com/NYPL/cce-renewals — README fetched; designed so "the *lack* of a match means the copyright wasn't renewed"); (2) Stanford Copyright Renewals DB (https://exhibits.stanford.edu/copyrightrenewals — **blocked by a captcha for scripts tonight**; works in a normal browser); (3) Catalog of Copyright Entries scans on archive.org via The Online Books Page (https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/cce/, fetched) for the original registration (and to see if the book was ever registered); (4) HathiTrust's Copyright Review (CRMS) rights codes (`pd`, `pdus`, `ic`) via the catalog API — a free second opinion done by librarians.
- Rule of thumb for 1931–1963 US books: renewal had to be filed in year 28 → look in renewal year = publication year + 27/28.
- **Hands-on test (samples/aggregators-legal/cce/*.txt):** streamed all NYPL renewal files 1957–1991 and grepped for patristic/confessional series. Results:
  - **Fathers of the Church (CUA/FOTC, 1947–1963): systematically renewed** by translators/estates (vol. 1 Apostolic Fathers 1947 → R606967, 1975; vols. 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, Augustine Letters 1951–53, City of God 1952/54, Basil Letters 1951, Cyprian 1958, Eusebius 1953, Clement *Christ the Educator* 1954 …). → **FOTC is NOT a PD route** (protected to 2042+).
  - **Tappert's Book of Concord (1959) renewed** (Fortress Press, 1987). **Luther's Works (American Edition) vols. renewed** (CPH/Fortress, 1984–1991). → PERMISSION.
  - Benziger *Summa theologica* (1947–48): renewal claims only "NM: synoptical charts / appendices, indices" → the English Dominican translation itself (1911–1925, London) is PD; only Benziger's added charts are protected. → the Summa English text is **READY** (from a pre-1931 printing to be safe).
  - Modern Library *City of God* (1950): renewal only for Merton's introduction → Dods translation PD (as expected).
  - Sheed's *Confessions* (1942/43) renewed 1970 → protected to 2037/38.
  - Ancient Christian Writers (Newman Press, 1946–): **mixed** — no series-level renewal found, but at least ACW 15 (Augustine, *Sermons for Christmas and Epiphany*, Lawler 1952) renewed by the translator (1980). → must check **volume by volume**; never assume a series is unrenewed.
  - Baltimore Catechism No. 2 (1961 edition) renewed 1989 by the Confraternity of Christian Doctrine; earlier (1885/1891/1941) editions need their own check.
  - **1941 Confraternity New Testament (St. Anthony Guild Press, revision of Challoner-Rheims): no renewal found** in the 1968, 1969 and 1970 renewal files (grep "new testament|BIBLE. N.T|Challoner|Rheims|St. Anthony"), although St. Anthony's Guild renewed dozens of its own 1940–42 pamphlets in exactly those years (R430995, R432743, R450907 …) and a 1942 *Chronological harmony* "using the rev. text of the Challoner-Rheims version" (R489783, 1970). → strong evidence the **1941 NT text was not renewed → US PD**; confirm by hand in Stanford DB and check the 1941 registration in the CCE (answers bible-66's open item). Under the rule of the shorter term (ES art. 199.4, AR art. 15) it would then be free in Spain/Argentina too (⚖ Falcon caveat).
  - Limits: NYPL data parse errors exist; a missing hit is strong but not conclusive. Always confirm in Stanford DB by hand + check the book had a notice.

### 3.4 Spain
(Articles quoted from the BOE consolidated TRLPI saved by the spanish-pd agent, re-read by me: `samples/spanish-pd/lpi.txt`, https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930.)
- Term: art. 26 "toda la vida del autor y setenta años después de su muerte"; for authors who died **before 7 Dec 1987**, DT 4ª keeps the 1879 law's **life + 80** (spanish-pd verified the DT wording). → In 2026 a translator who died **≤ 1945** is PD in Spain; died 1946–1986 → life+80; died ≥1987 → life+70. Anonymous/collective: 70 years from publication (art. 27/28).
- Art. 11: "Las traducciones y adaptaciones" are protected works → translator's dates govern.
- **Rule of the shorter term, art. 199.4** (verified): for works whose Berne country of origin is a non‑EU country and whose author is not an EU national, "el plazo de protección será el mismo que el otorgado en el país de origen de la obra sin que en ningún caso pueda exceder del previsto en esta ley". → A **US** translation by a US translator that fell into the US PD (pre-1931, or 1931–63 unrenewed) is **also PD in Spain**. ⚖ Caveat: the EU Term Directive's transitional rule (works protected in any member state on 1 July 1995; CJEU *Sony Music v Falcon*, C‑240/07, 2009 — memory) can keep some such works alive; ask a Spanish lawyer for any high-value 1931–63 US translation.
- **Art. 128 "meras fotografías"** (verified): "Quien realice una fotografía u otra reproducción obtenida por procedimiento análogo ... goza del derecho exclusivo ... veinticinco años computados desde el día 1 de enero del año siguiente a la fecha de realización". → A scan made in Spain in 2015 is protected **as an image** until 2040. It does not reach the PD *text* we transcribe. Don't republish Spanish-library page images unless the library licenses them (BNE does: CC BY 4.0).
- **Art. 72 RDL 24/2021** (transposes DSM Directive 2019/790 art. 14; verified on BOE https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2021-17910): "Cuando hayan expirado los derechos de explotación de una obra de **arte visual**, cualquier material resultante de un acto de reproducción de dicha obra no estará sujeto a derechos de propiedad intelectual, a menos que ... sea original". Limited to *visual art* (icons, paintings, illuminated miniatures) — **not to book pages**; for page scans art. 128 still applies.
- **Art. 129.2 / 130.2** (verified): publishers of unprotected works have a 25‑year right in "dichas ediciones siempre que puedan ser individualizadas por su composición tipográfica, presentación y demás características editoriales", counted from publication. Irrelevant for 19th‑c. books; relevant to **modern critical editions of PD texts** (e.g. a 2010 Spanish edition of a 16th‑c. text) and to BVMC's digital editions. Art. 129.1: first lawful publisher of an unpublished PD work gets 25 years.
- **Art. 32.1 quotation** (verified): fragments "a título de cita o para su análisis, comentario o juicio crítico. Tal utilización solo podrá realizarse **con fines docentes o de investigación**, en la medida justificada por el fin ... indicando la fuente y el nombre del autor". A paid study site has a decent argument for "docentes", but the exception is narrow; keep each quote short and commented.
- **Art. 41**: PD works may be used by anyone "siempre que se respete la autoría y la integridad de la obra" → label modernised spelling, abridgements and our corrections.
- **Database right arts. 133 & 203** (verified): protects "la inversión sustancial ... para la obtención, verificación o presentación de su contenido" against extraction/re-use of "la totalidad o de una parte sustancial", and also "repetidas o sistemáticas de partes no sustanciales"; **only for makers who are EU nationals/residents or EU companies (art. 203)**. → US databases (CCEL, New Advent, Wikisource/WMF, IA, HathiTrust) have **no** EU sui generis right; BVMC (ES), BNE (ES, but licensed CC BY), Gallica (FR), PTA (DE) do. Their PD *texts* can still be taken from other copies of the book.

### 3.5 Argentina (Ley 11.723, InfoLEG consolidated text saved by spanish-pd, re-read by me)
- Art. 5: life + 70 from 1 January after death → translator died **≤ 1955** = PD in 2026.
- **Art. 10 (quotation)**: "Cualquiera puede publicar con fines didácticos o científicos, comentarios, críticas o notas referentes a las obras intelectuales, incluyendo **hasta mil palabras** de obras literarias o científicas ... y en todos los casos sólo las partes del texto indispensables a ese efecto. ... **Cuando las inclusiones de obras ajenas sean la parte principal de la nueva obra, podrán los tribunales fijar equitativamente ... la cantidad proporcional** que les corresponde a los titulares". → Per work quoted, ≤1,000 words, only what is indispensable, inside our commentary. A study page that is mostly quotation risks the compensation clause. Not a route to "read in full".
- **Art. 15 (shorter term)**: "La protección que la Ley argentina acuerda a los autores extranjeros, no se extenderá a un período mayor que el reconocido por las Leyes del país donde se hubiere publicado la obra." → US-PD US books are PD in Argentina too; a Spanish book is protected in Argentina at most for the Spanish term (and at least... the shorter of the two applies, i.e. life+70 vs Spain's life+80 → Argentina's own life+70).
- Art. 24: "El traductor de una obra que no pertenece al dominio privado sólo tiene propiedad sobre su versión y no podrá oponerse a que otros la traduzcan de nuevo." → **Our own translation of a PD original is clean in Argentina.**
- Art. 23: translation contracts must be registered within a year — historical quirk, not useful to us (⚖ if ever relying on an unregistered 20th‑c. translation contract).
- **Dominio público pagante** (FNA, Decreto-Ley 1224/58, Res. FNA 625/2022 "Rubro 12.3 ... entorno digital ... tres (3) MÓDULOS ... anualmente y por cada obra"): verified by spanish-pd (see its §2). ⚖ Big open question for an Argentine lawyer: whether an Argentine-operated paid site publishing PD works owes ARS 120,000 per work per year. It does **not** obviously reach our own new translations (new protected works) — another argument for translating PD originals ourselves.

### 3.7 Contract terms that are not copyright
- Browsewrap/ToS conditions (IA "scholarship and research purposes only"; Google "we request ... non-commercial"; HathiTrust datasets agreement; Gallica CRPA licence) bind **access**, not the PD text. Risks: account/IP blocking, contract claims (weak for browsewrap in the US), and for Gallica a statutory French fine. Mitigation: download single volumes by hand, OCR ourselves, keep provenance, avoid Gallica-only sources, never sign HathiTrust/Google dataset agreements for this commercial project.
- CC BY-SA from Wikisource/Perseus: keep the SA texts as separate, unmodified layers; attribute; republish any corrections under the same licence.

## 4. The permission route

### 4.1 What to ask for (learned from how others did it)
- **Sefaria (closest analogue: a free library of a religious corpus with original texts + translations):** accepts PD, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC (case by case). Two models that worked: (a) a **platform-only grant** — JPS: "The JPS Tanakh translations are only free for use on Sefaria" (JPS announcement, fetched https://jps.org/resources/jps-bible-translation-enters-digital-era-with-sefaria/; partnership by June 2016); (b) a **funded open release** — Koren/Steinsaltz Talmud under "Creative Commons Non-Commercial license ... CC BY-NC 4.0", keeping explanatory notes and the vocalised Koren-font text proprietary (Koren blog, fetched https://korenpub.com/blogs/blog/technology-partnership-with-sefaria). Lesson: **ask for a display licence scoped to Doctrina** (no redistribution, no download, attribution + buy-link), not for an open licence — it is what publishers say yes to. A CC BY-NC grant would not work for us if Doctrina is paid, so ask explicitly for "commercial use on doctrina.app/... including behind a subscription".
- **Catholic precedents** (detailed in catholic-modern report): USCCB refused a daily-CCC site three times and sent a cease-and-desist to FlockNote, later licensing the **Compendium** instead; the Picayune parish got Vatican permission by mail (1999); papalencyclicals.net states LEV permission to reprint electronically. App developers (CCCSeries quiz apps) avoid hosting the CCC and **link to vatican.va** (search snippet of App Store descriptions; not opened).
- **Litigation precedent (why we never "just post" a modern religious translation):** *Society of the Holy Transfiguration Monastery v. Gregory*, 689 F.3d 29 (1st Cir. 2012): the monastery's English translations of liturgical/patristic Greek texts are copyrightable; an archbishop who posted ~1,000 pages lost (summaries: Justia https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca1/11-1262/11-1262-2012-08-02.html, US Copyright Office fair-use index https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/soc%E2%80%99yhtm-gregory-1stcir2012.pdf — search snippets; opinion not read in full tonight). Fair use failed even for a religious, non-profit use.
- CPH (verified on https://www.cph.org/copyrights-permissions, rendered): for *Concordia: The Lutheran Confessions* (2005/2006): quoting "up to and inclusive of **200 sentences** without express written permission ... providing that the words quoted do not amount to a complete work, nor account for 25 percent or more of the total text of the work in which they are quoted", **but** "Publication of any work intended for commercial sale that uses Concordia ... is strictly prohibited without the express written permission". Contact copyrights@cph.org, 800-325-0191; web form. CPH Small Catechism (1986): "Other than reproduction ... for noncommercial personal, congregational, or classroom use ... without prior written permission". → For the site use the PD Triglot (Bente/Dau 1921) and ask CPH only if we want the modern text.
- CRC/Faith Alive (verified): free "for study, education, review, or worship use up to 100 copies ... distributed free of charge" → a paid site needs permission: Permissions@crcna.org (decoded from the page), Faith Alive Christian Resources, 1700 28th Street SE, Grand Rapids, MI 49508.
- USCCB Permissions Policy page (rendered, https://www.usccb.org/media/permissions-policy): per-text rules (Lectionary one-time print use free with notice); Bible permissions: https://www.usccb.org/offices/new-american-bible/permissions (search snippet: NABRE >5,000 words or >40 % of a book needs permission; nabperm@usccb.org — verify before writing).

## 5. Creative alternatives and workarounds found
1. **Roger Pearse's "Additional Fathers" are explicitly PD-dedicated** (2.4): Jerome's Vulgate prologues (canon doctrine; Edgecomb 2006 PD), Optatus on the Chair of Peter (papacy), Pacian (church), Theodore of Mopsuestia's baptismal/eucharistic homilies (Eucharist pilot; US-usable from 2029), Cyril of Alexandria on Luke, Irenaeus *Proof*. Clean HTML, ready to ingest.
2. **Renewal mining with NYPL's CC0 data** (3.3): a script can check every 1931–63 US candidate in seconds; tonight it settled several questions (FOTC renewed; Tappert renewed; Benziger *Summa* renewal covers only the charts → translation PD; Modern Library *City of God* renewal covers only Merton's intro). Turn this into a pipeline step: `title/translator → renewal hit? → verdict`, then confirm by hand in Stanford's DB.
3. **HathiTrust's written statement** that "There are no restrictions on use of text transcribed from the images, or paraphrased or translated using the images" (2.11) is the cleanest documentary cover for building our texts from Google-scanned PD books: OCR the page images ourselves, never ship Google's OCR or images.
4. **Rule of the shorter term works in our favour for US books** (Spain art. 199.4, Argentina art. 15): an American translation that is PD in the US (pre-1931, or 1931–63 unrenewed, US translator) is PD in Spain and Argentina too — so US-PD English is usable for the whole audience. (Doesn't help Spanish translations published in Spain: there the US URAA term is the problem.)
5. **Ask for a platform-only display licence, not an open licence** (Sefaria–JPS model), and offer a buy-link and usage reports; for high-value texts consider a **funded release** (Sefaria–Koren model, CC BY-NC — only useful if Doctrina stays free; otherwise ask for a commercial grant).
6. **API.Bible "Express Licensing"** (American Bible Society; help article fetched, "Last updated July 22, 2026"): "allows eligible developers to obtain commercial licenses for many copyrighted Bible translations directly through API.Bible"; price set per translation by the IP holder and monthly users; "Some Bible translations are only available for commercial licensing up to certain monthly user thresholds"; NIV excluded. A legitimate paid route for modern Bibles (check which Spanish ones are offered — not visible without an account; brief forbids sign-up).
7. **Our own translation from the PD original** is the universal fallback and is legally the cleanest (17 U.S.C. §101 derivative = ours; AR art. 24 "no podrá oponerse a que otros la traduzcan de nuevo"); it may also sidestep the Argentine FNA levy, which targets PD works, not new ones (⚖).
8. **Public-domain calendar**: record the date each blocked text becomes free in each country (e.g. Mingana's Theodore: US 1 Jan 2029; Mingana 1932 Creed commentary: US 1 Jan 2028; Straubinger AR 2027 per bible-66) and schedule re-checks — some "permission" items solve themselves within the roadmap horizon.
9. **Wikisource as a contract-free mirror of CCEL-quality text** (also noted by councils-creeds): NPNF/ANF, Schaff, Catholic Encyclopedia — same PD words, licence we can comply with.
10. **Use aggregator files only as collation witnesses**: diffing our OCR against CCEL/New Advent to *find* our OCR errors is not copying their text, as long as each corrected reading is checked against the scan.
11. **Geo-scoped display** (only if ever needed): a text PD in US/AR but protected in Spain (translator died 1946–55) could be shown outside Spain using Vercel's geo headers. Lower risk, but complexity and imperfect (VPNs); prefer another translation.
